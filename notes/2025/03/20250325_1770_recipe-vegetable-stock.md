# Recipe — vegetable stock

Stopped buying cartons of stock after making a batch from kitchen scraps. The flavor difference is meaningful.

## What goes in

A good vegetable stock can be made almost entirely from vegetable trimmings you'd otherwise compost. I keep a zip-lock bag in the freezer and add to it over the course of a week:

- Onion and leek trimmings (not the very outer papery layers — too bitter)
- Celery leaves and ends
- Carrot peels and tops
- Parsley stems
- Mushroom stems
- Garlic peels
- Bay leaves (2-3)
- Black peppercorns (a small handful)
- Thyme sprigs if available

**Avoid:** Cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower (too sulfurous), beets (turns everything red and sweet), starchy vegetables like potato (clouds the stock), anything rotting.

## Method

1. If time allows, roast the vegetables first at 200°C for 30-40 minutes until lightly caramelized. This adds a deeper, richer base. Skip if in a hurry — still good without.

2. Put everything in a large pot. Cover with 3 liters of cold water. Bring to a gentle simmer.

3. Simmer uncovered for 45-60 minutes. Do not boil hard — a rolling simmer extracts better flavor and keeps the stock clearer.

4. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve. Press lightly but don't force it (forces fine particles through, makes it cloudy).

5. Cool quickly, portion into 500ml containers, freeze.

Keeps in the freezer for three months. Use within 5 days if refrigerated.

Good for risotto, soup, braising. Also a good base for the miso soup from [[20250310_1758]] if you don't have dashi ingredients.
