# Film review — All of us strangers

Watched *All of Us Strangers* (2023, dir. Andrew Haigh) last night. Based loosely on a 1987 Japanese novel by Taichi Yamada.

The premise: a screenwriter living alone in a nearly empty London tower block begins an affair with his downstairs neighbor (Harry, played by Paul Mescal), and simultaneously starts revisiting his dead parents — who appear to him as they were when they died, as if living in the same suburb where he grew up, willing to engage with the adult version of their son.

The film is quiet and oblique in the way that grief and longing are oblique. Adam (Andrew Scott) is always slightly out of step with every scene he's in — present but not quite anchored. The scenes with his parents (played by Claire Foy and Jamie Bell, both extraordinary) have the quality of recovered memory: overly clear in some details, hazy around the edges.

The final act is devastating and in retrospect well-prepared. I didn't see it coming in the way it arrives, which is a compliment to the script.

Scott and Mescal have remarkable chemistry. The central relationship is tender in a way that doesn't feel performed.

Technically: the cinematography uses a lot of overexposed backgrounds, burning out windows and exterior light. I read that this was intentional and thematic — the outside world is secondary to the interior space of Adam's consciousness. It works.
