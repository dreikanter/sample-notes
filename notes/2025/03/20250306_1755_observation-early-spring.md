# Observation — early spring signals

March 6 and there are snowdrops in the front beds. First ones I've noticed — they could have been there for two or three days. They're very small, smaller than I remember from last year, and they're pushing up through what's left of the dead leaf mulch from the fall.

The crows have gotten louder. This happens every year around this time — the pair that nests in the maple behind the house starts getting territorial. I can hear them from inside in the morning, a sustained argument with other crows or possibly with themselves.

The light at 5pm is perceptibly different from a month ago. Still not what I'd call evening light — more like late afternoon — but there's more of it, warmer in tone. The clocks haven't changed yet but the shift is already visible.

I saw what I think was a Cooper's hawk in the backyard this afternoon, sitting in the lower branches of the maple for about five minutes before something startled it. Slate-backed, barred belly, relatively long tail with a rounded end. Could have been a sharp-shinned hawk — the two are hard to distinguish in the field, especially at a distance. The size suggested Cooper's but I'm not confident.
