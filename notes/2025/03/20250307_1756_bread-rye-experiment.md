# Rye bread experiment — March 7

Wanted to try a rye-forward loaf. Standard sourdough is 10-20% rye; this is 40% rye, which changes things considerably.

## Formula

- Light rye flour: 200g
- Bread flour: 300g
- Water: 375g (75% hydration — high for this flour mix)
- Salt: 10g
- Levain: 100g (fed with equal parts rye and bread flour for two days before baking)

## What changed

**Mixing:** Rye has very little gluten structure, so the dough feels completely different — sticky, almost paste-like when the rye content is high. Standard stretch and fold doesn't do much. Switched to a series of coil folds every 45 minutes.

**Fermentation:** Rye ferments faster because the enzymatic activity is higher. Shortened bulk from 4 hours to 3 hours at 24°C. Watched for 75% rise rather than 50%.

**Shaping:** Nearly impossible to do a proper tension build. Used a loaf pan (Pullman pan without the lid) instead of a banneton. This is a compromise — the structure is looser than a freestanding boule.

**Bake:** 250°C for 15 minutes with steam (lid on pan), 230°C for 35 minutes lid off, then 10 minutes out of the pan to crisp the sides.

## Result

Dense but not gummy. The rye flavor is pronounced — slightly sour, earthy. Good with butter and salt, excellent with smoked salmon. Crumb is closed (expected at 40% rye). Will try 30% rye next time for comparison.
