# Garden — first direct sow attempt, March 13

Soil thermometer read 7°C this morning, which is on the edge. Cool-season crops want at least 7-10°C for germination. Decided to push ahead with the spinach since it's the most cold-tolerant of the planned crops.

## What I sowed

- **Spinach (Bloomsdale):** Two short rows in the shadier north end of bed 2, as planned in [[20250203_1722]]. Sowed 1cm deep, 5cm apart. Covered with a light layer of compost.
- **Arugula (not in original plan):** Added on impulse. Found a half-empty packet in the seed tin. Put it in between the spinach rows. Arugula is arguably even more cold-tolerant.

## Soil conditions

Still slightly compacted from winter. Loosened with a fork to about 15cm depth before sowing. Added a 2cm layer of homemade compost to the top, raked in lightly.

## Protected with row cover

Put a single layer of agricultural fleece over the bed, secured with stones. This should add 2-3°C of warmth at ground level and protect from any late frost events.

## Timeline expectations

At 7-10°C, germination will be slow: 14-21 days for spinach. It will speed up as the weather warms. First harvest of baby leaves probably 6-8 weeks out.

Saved the beet and salad mix sowing for when temperatures are more consistently above 10°C — probably 3 weeks from now.
