# Observation — cherry blossoms, March 28

The cherry trees on the avenue came out this week. Full bloom today — about 2 days after the buds fully opened. The timing lines up with last year almost exactly.

These are Yoshino cherries, I think — pale pink, nearly white, in clusters of 5-6 flowers per stem. The Japanese aesthetic of cherry blossom viewing (hanami) is specifically tied to this window when the trees are fully out but before the petals begin to drop. That window here is probably 4-5 days given the forecast.

Walked through at noon and the light through the canopy was remarkable. The trees are planted every 6 meters or so along the block, and the branches meet overhead to form a partial tunnel. The effect when walking toward the sun is like looking through layers of translucent paper — the flowers backlist.

There was a group of people who had set up a blanket directly beneath one of the larger trees. Not a picnic exactly — they were sitting quietly, drinking coffee from a thermos, occasionally looking up. I understood what they were doing.

By evening a few petals were already falling. A week from now the sidewalk will be covered in them and they'll look like wet confetti, which is less poetic but still good.

This might be the clearest seasonal marker of the year for me. More than the equinox, more than the first frost.
