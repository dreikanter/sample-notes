# Review — All Fours by Miranda July

Miranda July's [All Fours](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/722871/all-fours-by-miranda-july/) (2024) is a novel about a woman in her mid-forties who drives away from her Los Angeles home, intends to go to New York, and instead pulls off the highway and stays in a motel in Monrovia for three weeks. That's the first act. What follows is harder to describe without making it sound either more chaotic or more symbolic than it actually is.

The book is about perimenopause in a direct, clinical-detail way that I haven't seen handled with this lack of ceremony before. The narrator tracks her hormone levels, describes the quality of her sleep changes, and treats the body's shift as neither tragedy nor liberation — just a thing that is happening, with its own logic. That alone is worth something.

What July does structurally is interesting: the novel refuses to build toward the kind of self-knowledge payoff that this type of book (middle-aged woman, road trip, deviation from routine) usually promises. Things happen. Some of them matter. The ending is genuinely strange rather than earned-strange.

The prose is extremely controlled, which is at odds with the subject matter in productive ways. A few scenes in the motel room made me put the book down for ten minutes to think, which is a reasonably good sign.

Weaknesses: some of the secondary characters feel diagrammatic. The husband especially.

I'd give it 4 out of 5. The ambition and the execution are mostly in alignment, and the perimenopause material alone makes it worth reading.
