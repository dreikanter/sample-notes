# Film notes — Anatomy of a fall

Watched *Anatomy of a Fall* (2023, dir. Justine Triet) with high expectations after its Palme d'Or win. The film met them.

The premise: a German woman living in a French chalet finds her husband dead on the snow outside. A fall from the attic — accident, suicide, or murder? The film follows the subsequent trial, which gradually becomes less about what happened and more about what the marriage was, what truth is in a courtroom, and what a child (their partially sighted son, Daniel) can know and testify to.

Sandra Hüller is extraordinary as Sandra, the defendant. She's defending herself against accusations organized around facts she can't deny and is forced to explain in court in a second language. The scene where she explains to Daniel why she can't tell him whether she killed his father — because she's genuinely not entirely sure what the truth is, and telling him something simple would be dishonest — is one of the finest pieces of acting I've seen.

The courtroom scenes are long and formal in a way I expected to find tedious but didn't. They function as epistemological theater: each witness's account slightly different, each lawyer's framing revealing the frame's agenda.

The film trusts the audience to sit with ambiguity. There is no resolution that resolves cleanly. What we get instead is Daniel's decision, which is its own kind of truth.
