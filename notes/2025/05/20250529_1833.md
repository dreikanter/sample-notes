# Thinking about deep work — notes from Cal Newport

Reading *Deep Work* again, second time around. The first time I read it in 2020, it felt aspirational and slightly impractical. Reading it now, after several years of trying different approaches to focused work, it lands differently.

Newport's core claim is that the ability to perform cognitively demanding work in distraction-free stretches is both increasingly rare and increasingly valuable. The economy rewards it; the typical office environment erodes it. This seems straightforwardly true.

What I find more nuanced is his typology of depth schedules. The monastic approach (permanent isolation from shallow work) is only available to a specific kind of academic or writer. The bimodal approach (multi-day blocks of deep work alternating with normal work) is more realistic but requires a job that tolerates it. The rhythmic approach (daily fixed deep work blocks) is what most knowledge workers can actually do.

What I use: the rhythmic approach, 90 minutes in the morning before email. This works when I protect it and doesn't work when I don't. The honest failure mode is that "before email" gets compressed to "before second coffee" which becomes "between the first Slack ping and the standup call."

Newport's advice on measuring depth in hours per week is practical. I track this loosely in weekly reviews — if I'm consistently below 10 hours of uninterrupted focused work in a week, something has slipped.
