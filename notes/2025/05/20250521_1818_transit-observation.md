# Transit observation — morning commute notes

The 8:14 train is reliably the most interesting. Not the busiest — that's the 7:52 — but the crowd is more varied. The 7:52 is almost entirely suited commuters staring at phones. The 8:14 catches people on flexible schedules: a nurse in scrubs reading a paperback thriller, two teenagers speaking rapid French to each other, an older man with a rolled newspaper under his arm who actually reads it, folding it into quarters the way people used to.

The carriage smells like coffee. Not unpleasant. Everyone seems to have a cup in a different kind of vessel: paper cups with cardboard sleeves, stainless tumblers, a ceramic mug in a silicone carrier, one person with an actual French press travel mug that looks unwieldy to clean.

I timed it once: from Westfield to Central takes exactly 11 minutes on a good day, 17 on a bad one. The variance is almost entirely the station dwell time at Merton — some driver holds the doors for late passengers, others don't.

The light through the east-facing windows at this time of morning is low and gold. It catches the dust in the air above the seats. People who are awake enough to notice it look briefly content.

More on how transit environments shape mood and behavior: https://cityobservatory.org/transit-and-wellbeing/

There's something specific to this hour that feels like the city hasn't fully decided what kind of day it's going to be yet.
