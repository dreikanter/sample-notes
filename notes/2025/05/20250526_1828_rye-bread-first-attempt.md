# Rye bread — first attempt notes

Made a 60% rye loaf today using a recipe adapted from a Hamelman formula. Rye behaves completely differently from wheat: no gluten network forms the same way, so you can't knead it. Instead you mix until just combined and then it goes straight into a tin.

**The process**

Made a rye soaker the night before — 100g coarse rye meal soaked in 200g cold water. This softens the bran and helps with moisture retention. The main dough the next morning was 240g dark rye flour, 160g bread flour, 300g water, 100g active starter, 9g salt.

Mixed everything including the soaker. It looked like a dense, wet paste — nothing like a wheat dough. Poured it into an oiled pullman loaf tin, smoothed the top with a wet spatula, and covered.

Proofed at room temperature for about 3 hours. The rise was modest — maybe 25% increase. Baked at 220°C for 15 minutes then 190°C for 40 minutes. Internal temp reached 98°C.

**Result**

Dense, moist, slightly tacky crumb. Strong malty flavor with good sour notes. The crust is thin and crisp. The loaf needs 24 hours to set before slicing — cutting too early gives a gummy texture.

Not the most photogenic bread but the flavor is excellent, especially with strong cheese or smoked fish.
