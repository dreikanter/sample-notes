# Observations on heat — the first hot week of the year

Temperature hit 31°C on Saturday, the first proper hot day since last September. There's a particular texture to the first hot week that doesn't repeat itself — the city isn't adapted to it yet. People dress wrong, slightly too warm for the conditions because the mental model hasn't updated. Ice cream shops have queues out the door in a way that won't happen in August, when people are more normalized to the heat.

The light changes too. In late May at this latitude, the sun angle is still tilting toward midsummer but hasn't arrived. The shadows are long in a way they won't be in July. The evening sky holds color until past 9pm — a pale yellow at the horizon, then orange, then the blue deepening overhead while there's still light in the west.

I sat outside after dinner for the first time this year. The neighbors were in their garden. The sound from the park three streets over — children, someone's stereo — carried further than it would have in colder weather. Hot air is denser, or maybe it was just the absence of wind.

The tomatoes I planted last week look happier. They seemed slightly stressed after transplanting, which they always do, but with warmth they've straightened up and the newest leaves are a brighter green.

This is the payoff of winter, I think — not summer itself but this first week when the sensory contrast is sharpest.
