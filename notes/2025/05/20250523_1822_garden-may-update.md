# Garden update — late May

The tomato seedlings went into the raised bed on the 20th, after a two-week hardening-off period on the back step. Three varieties this year: Stupice (an early Czech heirloom), Black Krim, and one cherry variety called Sweet Million. Stupice is already noticeably more vigorous than the others — it was the first to develop its second set of true leaves and is standing upright without staking.

The garlic planted in October is starting to put up scapes. I cut one to taste and it was mild, almost buttery — good raw in a salad dressing. The full scapes will come off in another week or two, which redirects energy to the bulb rather than the flower.

Lettuce situation: the cut-and-come-again planting from April is producing faster than we can eat it. We've been giving away bags to neighbors. The Forellenschluss (trout's back) variety is the one people always remark on — it has red freckling on pale green leaves and looks almost decorative.

Pest notes: some aphid activity on the nasturtiums along the bed edge. Leaving it alone for now since the nasturtiums are functioning as a trap crop — the aphids seem to prefer them over the tomatoes. A few ladybug larvae visible, which should help.

Next tasks: install tomato cages, direct-sow beans in the gap on the north side, check the rain gauge (looked like 12mm last night).
