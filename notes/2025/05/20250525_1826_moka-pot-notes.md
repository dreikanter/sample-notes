# Moka pot technique — what actually changed the cup

After two years of using the moka pot the same way and getting a reliably bitter result, I finally changed some variables and documented what made a difference.

**What I changed**

1. Pre-heated water. Instead of starting with cold water in the base and heating it on the stove, I now fill the base with near-boiling water from a kettle. This shortens the time the grounds spend heating, which is when bitterness tends to develop.

2. Grind size. I was grinding too fine — closer to espresso than the medium-fine recommended for moka. A slightly coarser grind slowed the extraction and gave a less harsh result.

3. Heat setting. Moved from medium-high to medium-low. The pot takes longer but the extraction is more even. When you hear the loud gurgling splutter, you've already gone too far — the ideal is to pull it off the heat just as the coffee starts to flow, then let the residual heat finish the job.

**What didn't matter as much**

Tamping the grounds (don't tamp — it restricts flow and increases pressure). Pre-wetting the filter paper (no paper filter here, so irrelevant). Water-to-grounds ratio within a reasonable range.

The result is now noticeably less bitter with more chocolate and nutty notes coming through.
