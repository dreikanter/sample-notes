# Reading notes — The Dispossessed by Ursula K. Le Guin

Le Guin writes utopia without making it a sales pitch, which is the only way utopia is bearable. Anarres is the anarchist moon colony in this novel and it has shortages, social pressure, and conformism alongside its genuine goods — mutual aid, absence of property, freedom from hierarchy. Urras, the capitalist home planet, is lush and beautiful and also corrupt and brutal.

The novel alternates chapters between the two worlds. Shevek, a physicist, is the viewpoint character in both timelines — growing up on Anarres, then visiting Urras as the first person to make the crossing in generations. The structure is clever because it lets Le Guin show both worlds as genuinely flawed without requiring her to resolve them.

What I found most interesting on this reread: the way Anarres produces its own conformism. The community exerts social pressure without laws. There's no government but there is ostracism, exclusion, and the slow suffocation of anyone who diverges from consensus. Shevek experiences this as a physicist whose theoretical work is threatening to the existing order. Le Guin refuses to make the oppression cartoonish — it's diffuse and social, which is somehow more claustrophobic.

The physics sequences are genuinely attempting to describe a unified theory of time. Le Guin didn't have a science background but read extensively in physics before writing. The result is speculative but plausible-sounding.
