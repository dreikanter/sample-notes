# Crete food notes

Spent two weeks in Crete in May and kept eating variations of the same five things, which is not a complaint. Dakos everywhere: barley rusk softened with tomato juice, topped with crumbled mizithra and olive oil. The rusk matters more than you'd think — the cheap tourist ones dissolve into paste, whereas the real ones hold texture for twenty minutes.

The olive oil situation is genuinely different from mainland Greece. Pressing is done locally in small mills, and you can taste the difference between oil from Chania and oil from Heraklion the same way you'd distinguish wine regions. Bought a 500 ml tin from a co-op outside Rethymno; it's grassy and slightly bitter in a way that supermarket "Greek olive oil" never achieves.

Lamb with stamnagathi (wild chicory) showed up at a taverna in Apokoronas. The chicory is blanched until the bitterness softens, then dressed with the lamb braising liquid. Simple enough to replicate at home if I can find the right greens — endive or dandelion would probably work.

Worth noting that kalitsounia (small cheese pastries, either fried or baked) vary village by village. The baked version with honey is served at breakfast in some places, which sounds wrong and tastes right. Recipe variation archived at https://www.david-tanis.com for reference, though it's not identical.
