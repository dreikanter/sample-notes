# Kyoto travel notes — Fushimi and Arashiyama

**Fushimi Inari**

Go early. I mean genuinely early — the approach gates are busy by 7am on weekdays. At 6am, with a light rain falling, the lower tunnels of torii were nearly empty. The rain caught the orange paint and the stone steps smelled like wet cedar. It was one of those places that earns its reputation.

The crowds start at the lower shrine. By the time you're 20 minutes up the mountain, they thin considerably. Most visitors turn back at the first viewing point. Continuing to the summit (about 90 minutes each way) means you'll have long stretches to yourself among the older, more weathered gates where the paint has faded to a pale orange-red.

**Arashiyama bamboo grove**

The bamboo grove itself is small — maybe 200 meters of path, very narrow, flanked by dense bamboo that sways and clicks in the wind. It photographs like a place outside time and exists in reality as a slightly managed tourist corridor.

What's genuinely worthwhile is the area around Tenryu-ji and along the Oi River. The temple garden is excellent — a well-preserved Muromachi-period garden with the Arashiyama hills as borrowed scenery. The Oi River path in the late afternoon had herons standing in the shallows.

Practical: rent a bicycle. The two areas are 7km apart and the cycling paths are flat and scenic.
