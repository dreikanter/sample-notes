# Fermentation log — first lacto batch

Started the first batch of lacto-fermented carrots and daikon on May 20. Used a 2% salt brine by weight — 20g of kosher salt dissolved into 1 liter of filtered water. The vegetables were sliced into matchsticks, roughly 3mm thick, and packed tightly into a 1-liter mason jar with a few cloves of garlic and a small dried chili.

The key thing with lacto-fermentation is keeping vegetables submerged. I improvised a weight using a small zip-lock bag filled with brine, which sits nicely on top without introducing any outside contamination.

After 48 hours the brine was already cloudy — a good sign that the lactobacillus bacteria are active. Small bubbles cling to the sides of the jar. Temperature in the kitchen is hovering around 21°C, which is close to ideal. Too warm and things ferment too fast and can go mushy; too cold and it drags out over weeks.

By day four I'll start tasting. The flavor should shift from raw-vegetable to tangy and slightly fizzy. Once the acidity is where I want it, the jar goes into the fridge to slow things down.

Good reference for this process: https://www.wildfermentation.com/making-it-is-a-snap/

The goal is to use these in banh mi-style sandwiches — quick pickled daikon and carrot are traditional but the lacto version has a more complex flavor. Will document tasting notes in a follow-up.
