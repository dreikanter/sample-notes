# Sourdough hydration experiment — 78% vs 85%

Ran a side-by-side bake on Saturday using the same starter, same flour (50% bread, 50% whole wheat), same timeline, but two different hydration levels. The goal was to see how much the crumb actually differs in practice.

**78% hydration loaf**

Easier to handle during shaping. Held its shape better during the bench rest. Scored cleanly. Baked at 250°C with lid on for 20 minutes, then 230°C uncovered for 20 minutes. Result: a reasonably open crumb, good crust color, decent ear on the score.

**85% hydration loaf**

Noticeably stickier. Required wet hands throughout shaping and the bench rest was shorter (15 minutes instead of 25) because it was spreading more. Scored messily — the blade dragged slightly. Baked same profile. Result: more irregular, slightly more open crumb, thinner crust overall.

The taste difference was minimal. Both loaves had a mild sour note with the whole wheat providing some nuttiness. The 85% crumb was technically more open but also less even — big holes next to dense patches, which isn't ideal for spreading things.

For everyday bread, 78% is clearly more manageable without meaningful sacrifice. For baguette-style bread where openness matters more and shaping is different, the higher hydration might be worth the difficulty.

Good deep dive on hydration tradeoffs: https://www.theperfectloaf.com/guides/bakers-percentages/

Will try 80% next as a compromise point. Might also experiment with a longer cold retard to develop flavor without relying on starter acidity.
