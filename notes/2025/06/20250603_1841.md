# Restaurant notes — dinner at Isolette

Had dinner at Isolette on Tuesday with two friends. It's a small French-adjacent bistro that opened in March in the old pharmacy building on Calder Street. About 30 covers, no reservations for parties of fewer than four, good luck if you show up after 7pm without one.

**What we ate**

Shared a charcuterie board to start — competent, not exceptional. The house-cured duck prosciutto was the standout; the pâté was good but the toast was slightly stale at the edges.

Main courses: one person had the duck breast with lentils and mustard-cream sauce, which drew unprompted praise. I had the pan-fried gurnard with fennel and saffron broth — the fish was beautifully cooked, crisp skin, the broth was clear and deeply flavored. Someone ordered the mushroom risotto, which was rich and properly loose (not the gluey compressed version that places put on plates).

Dessert: a shared tarte tatin. The caramel was excellent, the pastry a bit soft — it might have been made too far in advance. The crème fraîche alongside was very good.

**Drinks**

House white by the glass was a Picpoul de Pinet, correctly cold, good value. The wine list overall is short and well-chosen without being precious about it.

**Overall**

Comfortable, confident cooking without the need to be trendy. Service was warm and unobtrusive. Prices in the reasonable range — €35 per person with wine.

Menu and bookings: https://www.isolettebistro.example.com
