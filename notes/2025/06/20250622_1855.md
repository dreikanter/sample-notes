# Midsummer — solstice notes

The longest day was yesterday. I stayed up late enough to notice the sky never fully went dark — at midnight there was still a faint bruise of blue at the northern horizon, the last light from the sun that had set two hours before but was still only just below the horizon at this latitude.

I've been tracking the sunset time since January: it moves by larger increments around the solstices and smaller increments near the equinoxes. Right now we're at the turnaround point where the rate of change is slowest. For the next two weeks the sunsets will barely shift before starting their long retreat toward autumn.

There's a melancholy in the solstice that I find easier to acknowledge than to explain. Summer has arrived but summer's decline has also begun. The light is at its maximum and therefore diminishing. It's the kind of paradox that doesn't respond to reason — you can note that there are months of warm weather ahead and it doesn't quite touch the feeling.

The garden at 9pm was bright enough to read in. A couple of bats were out, catching insects over the beds. The tomatoes have set their first fruit — small, green, hard. The French beans that finally went in last week have germinated and are standing upright with their first seed leaves open.

Someone wrote that the solstice is the year's hinge point — I think of it as the year's longest breath, held at maximum expansion before the exhale begins.
