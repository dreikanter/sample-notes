# Walk notes — canal towpath, east section

Walked the eastern section of the canal towpath for the first time. About 9km from the lock at Millhouse to the basin at Coalfield Wharf and back. Set out at 8am, back by 10:30.

The towpath at that hour had a pleasing mix of serious cyclists (lycra, aero bars, nodding at each other in acknowledgment) and people walking dogs with no particular agenda. The canal water was still, reflecting the trees on the far bank so precisely it was hard to tell where water ended and reflection began.

There's a section around the 3km mark where the towpath passes under a railway bridge and then opens into a wide stretch where the canal bends south. The light was coming in low from the east and the water was silvered by it. Two swans were on the far side with a brood of cygnets — still the grey-brown colour they have before they turn white, moving in a loose cluster between the adults.

The old lock-keeper's cottage at Brentwick is now private but the garden visible over the wall is extraordinary — completely informal, tall meadow grasses spilling out between the path and the waterside, what looked like a fig tree against the south wall, climbing roses on every surface.

At the basin: the narrowboats moored there have been there a while judging by the plant life on their roofs. Herb gardens, a couple of solar panels, a cat asleep on one bow.

Towpath conditions were good. Some muddy patches near the third lock but manageable in trail shoes.
