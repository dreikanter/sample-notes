# Monitoring and observability — notes on the distinction

There's a distinction between monitoring and observability that gets collapsed in most discussions but matters for how you instrument systems.

**Monitoring** is checking known things. You decide in advance what matters — CPU usage, request latency at the 95th percentile, error rate — and you set thresholds. An alert fires when a threshold is crossed. This is necessary and useful. It also only catches failures you anticipated.

**Observability** is the property of a system that lets you understand what's happening *inside* it from the outside, using outputs. A system is observable if you can answer arbitrary questions about its state at any point in time without deploying new instrumentation. Logs, metrics, and traces are the typical outputs.

The distinction matters because complex distributed systems fail in ways you didn't anticipate. If your only tool is monitoring, you can tell that something is wrong (alert fired) but you may not be able to understand why. If your system is observable, you can explore.

**What this looks like in practice**

Good observability starts at the code level. Structured logging with consistent field names (request_id, user_id, duration_ms) across services means you can correlate events across systems. Distributed tracing (OpenTelemetry) lets you follow a request through multiple services and see where time is spent.

Metrics from logs are often better than pre-aggregated metrics because they retain cardinality — you can slice by user, endpoint, or region after the fact.

The four golden signals (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) are a good baseline for monitoring. Observability is what you build on top of that.
