# June 1 — early morning notes

Woke before the alarm at 5:40. Not unusual in summer — the light comes in around the edge of the curtains even with them closed. Lay still for a few minutes listening. A wood pigeon was calling from the large pine at the end of the street; also a blackbird, further away, doing that liquid improvisation that sounds like someone very good warming up on a saxophone.

Made tea without turning on any lights. Stood at the kitchen window. The neighbor's cat was crossing the garden wall with the deliberate slowness cats use when they want you to know they're not hurrying on your account.

I find I can think more clearly at this hour than at any other time. Not because the morning is inherently special — I've read the literature on this and there's nothing biologically unique about 5:40am versus any other quiet period. It's the absence of accumulated friction from the day. No decisions made yet, no conversations to replay, no tasks already behind schedule.

I used to try to capture these early-morning thoughts in detail. Now I usually let them go. The thinking happens; most of it doesn't need to be preserved. The notes I do make at this hour tend to be more honest than ones made later, when I know they might be read.

The tea finished, the birds still at it, the light strengthening by perceptible degrees. A good way to start June.
