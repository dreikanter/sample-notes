# Dentist appointment — overdue by several months

Had the dentist appointment today. Six months overdue. It went fine — two small areas flagged for monitoring, one filling that's reached the end of its useful life and will need to be replaced at the next visit. No extractions, no drama.

The appointment itself was unremarkable. The dentist was businesslike and thorough, talked me through what she was seeing as she went. The hygienist spent 25 minutes on a cleaning that would have been unnecessary if I'd come in when I was supposed to.

What I find interesting is the psychology of deferred healthcare. The longer I wait for an appointment I know I should make, the more I assume the news will be proportionally worse. By the time I booked this one, I'd constructed an imaginary scenario involving multiple procedures. The reality — a monitoring note and a future filling — is not nothing but it's considerably less than the fictional version.

The avoidance is self-reinforcing: avoid → build up catastrophic expectations → avoid more to not confirm the worst → eventually go → outcome is fine → feel silly for avoiding. The cycle resets and I still don't immediately book the follow-up appointment.

Going to put the next one in the calendar before I leave next time. The dentist recommended every six months; the real number in my history is closer to 14 months.
