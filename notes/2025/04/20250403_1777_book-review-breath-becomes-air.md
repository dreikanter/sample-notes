# Book review — When breath becomes air

Read *When Breath Becomes Air* by Paul Kalanithi in two sittings. I'd been putting it off because I knew it would be hard — Kalanithi wrote it while dying of lung cancer at 36, a neurosurgeon who got the diagnosis during his residency, weeks before he was to become chief resident.

The book is in two parts. The first is a memoir of his formation: literature student who became a doctor because he wanted to understand what made life meaningful and thought both literature and medicine offered different windows into that question. The prose here is careful and genuinely literary — not doctor-writing-memoir but someone who has spent real time with language.

The second part is written after diagnosis. He continues to operate for as long as he can, then stops, then starts writing. He is trying to understand how to live when you suddenly cannot presume a future. The question he keeps returning to: what makes a life meaningful when time cannot be assumed?

The epilogue is written by his wife, Lucy, and it is worth reading.

What stays with me: the idea that identity is constituted partly by the projects we take ourselves to be engaged in. Kalanithi loses the project of becoming a senior surgeon. He finds, slowly, a different project. The book is that finding.

Not cathartic exactly — too honest for catharsis. But genuinely clarifying.
