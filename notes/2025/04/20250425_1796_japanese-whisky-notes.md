# Japanese whisky — tasting notes

Attended a whisky tasting at a bar that was doing a Japanese whisky flight. Four pours, good progression from lighter to more complex.

## The pours

**Nikka from the Barrel:** Blended, 51.4% ABV. Punchy, full-bodied. Honey, dried fruit, some smoke underneath. Finishes with a heat that dissipates slowly. Good value for the quality.

**Suntory Toki:** Lighter, fresher. Meant as a highball base. Apple and floral notes, gentle sweetness. On its own it's a bit thin; with good ice and maybe a splash of soda water it makes sense. Not one to sip neat.

**Hakushu 12-year:** Single malt. This is the one I'd seek out. Clean and green-herb forward, slight peat, cucumber note, very dry finish. Unlike anything Scottish. Less weighty than Scotch — feels like it belongs outdoors.

**Yamazaki 12-year:** Single malt, Suntory's flagship expression. Rich, complex, lots of fruit — plum, dark cherry. More oak influence than the Hakushu. Best of the evening by a small margin.

## Notes on Japanese whisky more broadly

The style tends toward precision and restraint compared to Scottish expressions. Less peat in most expressions (with exceptions). The climate in Japan produces faster maturation than Scotland, which changes the spirit in ways that are only partially understood.

The counterfeiting problem in the category is real — there are many "Japanese" whiskies on the market that contain non-Japanese spirit. Look for brands that are transparent about their production.
