# Reading notes — Entangled life by Merlin Sheldrake

*Entangled Life* (2020) is about fungi — mycorrhizal networks, lichen, psychedelic mushrooms, fermentation, the role of fungi in shaping the history of life on earth. It is one of those books that rewires how you see ordinary things.

The central revelation for me: trees in forests are connected to each other through underground fungal networks (mycorrhizal networks) through which they exchange nutrients, water, and chemical signals. The "wood wide web" framing is now somewhat contested in the literature as oversimplified, but the basic fact of these networks is well-established.

Sheldrake is a rigorous scientist who writes gracefully. He's honest about what's known, what's speculative, and what's been overstated in popular coverage. The chapter on lichens is particularly careful — lichens are not a single organism but a multi-species relationship (fungus + algae and/or cyanobacteria), and even this turns out to be more complicated than previously thought.

The chapter on psychedelic mushrooms and research on psilocybin is interesting as science but also as a meditation on how categorically weird it is that an organism evolved a compound that so profoundly affects mammalian consciousness. Sheldrake doesn't oversell the therapeutic angle — he stays in the science.

I read most of this outside, which was the right context. Looking at soil differently afterward.
