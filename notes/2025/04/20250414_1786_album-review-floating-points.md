# Album review — Floating Points, Pharoah Sanders

Returned to *Promises* (2021, Floating Points with Pharoah Sanders and the London Symphony Orchestra) this week. I first heard it when it came out and have returned to it irregularly since.

The album is nine movements of a single piece. Sanders plays saxophone; Floating Points (Sam Shepherd) provides an ambient electronic foundation; the LSO provides orchestral depth. There is almost no rhythm — no drums, no pulse in the conventional sense. It's built on texture, resonance, and a slow harmonic evolution.

The saxophone work is unlike Sanders's more familiar fiery playing. He's in his mid-80s on this recording, and the sound has the quality of someone who has made peace with their instrument over decades — unhurried, the phrases going where they want to. Some of the highest notes have a slight imperfection that sounds more honest than perfect execution would.

The string writing from Floating Points is patient in a way that's hard to achieve — the LSO is used for color and warmth rather than melodic statement. They function like a warm room rather than a presence.

Best listened to in full, in a quiet space, ideally when you have nowhere to be for an hour. It is not background music. It asks for attention.

One of the genuinely unusual albums of recent years.
