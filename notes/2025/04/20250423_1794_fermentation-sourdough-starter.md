# Sourdough starter maintenance notes

My starter (unnamed — I resist the naming convention) has been going for about three years. These are the maintenance notes I follow.

## Feeding ratio

I maintain at a 1:5:5 ratio: 1 part starter, 5 parts water, 5 parts flour. So 10g starter : 50g water : 50g flour. Using a 50:50 blend of bread flour and whole wheat.

The whole wheat accelerates fermentation (more native yeast and bacteria in the bran). Adjust ratio based on temperature and timing needs.

## Temperature and timing

- At 24°C: peaks in about 6-8 hours
- At 21°C: 8-12 hours
- At 18°C: 12-18 hours (good for overnight fermentation)

For baking, I feed 8-12 hours before I want to use it, targeting the peak rise (when it domes at the top and hasn't begun to fall).

## Signs of a healthy starter

- Doubles or more between feedings at room temperature
- Plenty of bubbles visible through the jar sides
- Smells pleasantly sour — tangy, yeasty, slightly fruity. Not acetone or very harsh

## What to do if it's neglected

If it's been in the fridge and neglected for weeks: discard all but 20g, feed 1:5:5, leave at room temperature. Repeat twice before baking. A starter that's been maintained for a long time is very resilient.

## Hooch

The gray liquid that forms on top after a long time between feedings is "hooch" — alcohol produced by fermentation. It means the starter is hungry, not dead. Pour it off or stir it in (more sour if stirred in), then feed.
