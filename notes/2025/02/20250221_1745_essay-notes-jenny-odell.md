# Essay notes — Jenny Odell on doing nothing

Working through *How to Do Nothing* by Jenny Odell. I started this months ago and keep setting it down, which is mildly ironic given its subject.

The book is explicitly not a productivity guide, which it announces early and then somewhat undermines by being somewhat useful. Odell's argument is against the attention economy — the systems that capture and monetize attention — and for a different kind of engagement with place, community, and time.

The section that hit hardest is about birdwatching as a practice of attention. Odell describes how learning to distinguish birds forced her to actually look at where she was — really look, not glance. The birds were an occasion for paying attention to the world rather than to content delivered through a screen. This is not anti-technology as such; it's a case for noticing things at the scale of direct perception.

The chapter on "manifest dismantling" applies the same logic to time and productivity. Not all refusal is possible or privileged, she acknowledges, but some forms of disengagement from the optimization frame are necessary for human sanity and political clarity.

It reads better in short doses than long sessions — the ideas need time to settle. Good for 20-30 pages, then rest.
