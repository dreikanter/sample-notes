# Film review — Past lives

Watched *Past Lives* (2023, dir. Celine Song) on a weeknight when I was expecting something light. Got something else entirely.

The film follows Nora and Hae Sung across three periods: childhood in Seoul before Nora's family emigrates to Canada, young adulthood when they reconnect via social media, and a final meeting twelve years after that. It's about memory, immigration, in-yun (the Korean concept of fate across lifetimes), and the life you didn't live.

What strikes me most is the restraint. The film trusts the audience to feel things without underlining them. The early sections are filmed with a kind of washed-out daylight quality that makes everything feel like a memory even as you're watching it. The final scene — Nora crying on the doorstep after saying goodbye to Hae Sung — lands because the film has earned it without manufactured drama.

Greta Lee and Teo Yoo both give performances that communicate volumes without much dialogue. There's a dinner scene where all three main characters sit together and the silences are doing most of the work.

The script is semi-autobiographical. Song's description of writing it is worth reading: [https://variety.com/2023/film/news/past-lives-writer-director-celine-song-autobiographical-1235620014/](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/past-lives-writer-director-celine-song-autobiographical-1235620014/)

Rating: one of the best films I've seen in several years. Not rewatchable in the conventional sense — it's too much — but worth seeing once very carefully.
