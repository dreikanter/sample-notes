# Reading notes — On photography by Susan Sontag

Working through *On Photography* (1977) slowly, a few essays at a time. It is the kind of book that demands rereading rather than reading, because Sontag packs so much into compressed paragraphs that a first pass misses most of it.

The central argument: photography is not simply documentation. It transforms its subject by making it a possession, a trophy, a fragment pulled from time. The tourist who photographs everything is not really experiencing the place — the photograph becomes a substitute for the experience rather than a record of it.

What I find most interesting is the chapter on photography and surrealism. Sontag argues that the camera's ordinary function is surrealist — every photograph takes a slice of reality and isolates it, defamiliarizes it. The mundane becomes strange when frozen. A parking lot photographed by an artist and a parking lot photographed by a surveyor are formally identical but culturally utterly different.

Her skepticism about documentary photography has aged interestingly given Instagram. She worries that the proliferation of images numbs rather than sensitizes. I think she was right about the numbing but underestimated how the proliferation would also produce new forms of visual literacy.

The essay "Melancholy Objects" is the best one in the collection, dealing with photographs as stand-ins for memory and loss. Worth reading independently.
