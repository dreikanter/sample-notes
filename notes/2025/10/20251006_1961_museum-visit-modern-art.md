# Museum visit — modern and contemporary wing

Free evening at the museum—they open late on Thursdays and it's less crowded than weekends. Spent about ninety minutes, mostly in the modern and contemporary wing.

## Pieces I spent time with

**Agnes Martin — Untitled #10 (1990):** One of her late grid paintings. Horizontal bands of pale color, pencil lines barely visible beneath the wash of paint. In photographs it looks like nothing. In person it vibrates. The light in the gallery was right—northern exposure, no direct sun.

**Franz Kline — Mahoning (1956):** Enormous black brushstrokes on white, displayed at the end of a gallery so you walk toward it for fifteen feet. The strokes look clumsy in reproduction but are precise and decisive up close. The scale does what scale is supposed to do.

**Joan Mitchell — La Grande Vallée XIV (1983):** The most intense of the three. Colors overlapping almost violently, but there's structure underneath. Mitchell was working from memory of landscape, which is clear here—this is what countryside looks like through emotion rather than observation.

## Thoughts

I default to the modern wing and keep not engaging with the contemporary section, which I notice as a bias worth addressing. Tomorrow's project: spend thirty minutes deliberately with the works I'd normally walk past.

The museum has a useful [audio guide app](https://www.moma.org/audio/) for major collections, though I mostly prefer silence.
