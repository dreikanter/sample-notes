# Autumn hike — ridge trail, October 14

Left the car at the lower trailhead at 8:30 a.m. Clear sky, temperature around 48°F (9°C). The kind of morning that makes you wonder why you ever stay inside on a weekend.

The ridge trail runs about 9 kilometers round-trip with 280 meters of elevation gain. Most of the climb is in the first 3 km, then the ridge walks are gradual. Today took three and a half hours including stops.

## Foliage

At or near peak. The sugar maples are extraordinary—they go through yellow, orange, and red simultaneously, sometimes on a single tree. The oaks are more restrained, transitioning from green through bronze over a longer period.

The phenomenon I always try to explain and can't fully: the way a forest in peak foliage looks different depending on whether you're looking toward the sun or away. Backlit leaves glow; the same leaves viewed from the other direction are darker and more saturated.

The beech trees haven't turned yet. They tend to hold green later and keep pale golden leaves into winter.

## Wildlife

A large hawk circling the ridge—too far to identify confidently, probably a broad-winged or red-tailed. Seven or eight turkeys crossing the trail at the lower section, moving with the particular purposefulness of turkeys. A woodpecker working a dead snag; the sound carried surprisingly far in the still air.

## General

Brought a thermos of coffee and two Macoun apples from last week (see [[20251003_1958]]). Both were right.
