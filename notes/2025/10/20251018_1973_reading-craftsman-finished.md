# Reading notes — The Craftsman (finished)

Finished The Craftsman by Richard Sennett. Started these notes at [[20250921_1944]].

## Final thoughts

The book improved in its later sections. The chapter on ethical craftsmanship—using the example of bomb designers who couldn't disengage their skills from their moral consequences—is genuinely disturbing and well-argued. Sennett's point: skill doesn't carry ethical content by default. The craftsperson's pride can be deployed in the service of terrible things.

The closing argument about the relationship between craft and citizenship is ambitious and not entirely persuasive, but it's interesting. The claim is that the habits of mind developed through craft (patience, attention to material, revision, self-criticism) are also the habits of good democratic participation. I buy it partially. I think the problem is that many skilled people are narrow in exactly the ways that good citizenship requires breadth.

## What I took from it

The most lasting idea for me: the dialectic between problem-solving and problem-finding. Good craftspeople don't just solve the problem they're given; they identify, through engagement with the material, a more interesting or more fundamental problem. This applies directly to software work, where the feature request is often not the actual problem.

The book is from 2008 and some of it shows its age (the Linux example is dated). But Sennett is attempting something broad and the thinking holds up.

**Rating:** 7.5/10. Better than I expected; not quite the masterpiece some reviews suggest.
