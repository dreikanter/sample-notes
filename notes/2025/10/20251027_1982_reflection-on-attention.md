# Reflection on attention and how I spend it

Been thinking about this since the concert (see [[20251020_1975]]). Two hours of paying attention without interruption, and the particular quality of that attention compared to how I pay attention most of the time.

Most of my attention is fractured. Even when I'm working on a single thing, there's a background hum of other things demanding notice—a notification I haven't cleared, a task I pushed back, a message thread I know I should respond to. The attention is nominally on the thing in front of me but it isn't fully there.

The concert enforced singleness. You can't really do anything else while listening to Shostakovich in a small hall. The attention consolidates. And then there's a quality of experience that doesn't happen otherwise.

I don't think this is just about doing one thing at a time, exactly. I can do one thing at a time and still be distracted by the shape of the task, by anticipation of what comes next, by self-consciousness about how well I'm doing it. What I noticed at the concert was the absence of that.

Burkeman (again—I keep coming back to [Four Thousand Weeks](https://www.oliverburkeman.com/books)) calls this "the opposite of alienation." Being fully in the thing you're doing rather than hovering above it.

I can't manufacture this state on demand and I'm suspicious of systems that claim to produce it reliably. But I can notice the conditions that seem more compatible with it and create more of them.

Less: fractured evening screen time.
More: walking, cooking, music, books that require sustained attention.
