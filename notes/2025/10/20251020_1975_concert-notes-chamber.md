# Concert notes — chamber music series

A Monday evening concert at a small hall, part of a subscription series. About 200 seats, nearly full.

## Program

**Haydn — String Quartet in G major, Op. 33 No. 5 ("How do you do?")**
Played by the Alder Quartet. The nickname comes from a descending figure in the first movement that sounds like a greeting. Light and good-humored throughout; Haydn's jokes land even 240 years later. The ensemble's tuning in the slow movement was extraordinarily clean.

**Debussy — String Quartet in G minor, Op. 10**
I hadn't heard this live before. On recording it can sound like impressionist wash; in a small hall you hear the structural argument underneath. The scherzo is genuinely fast and precise. The cellist, Miriam, was particularly impressive—the inner voice work requires real endurance.

**Shostakovich — String Quartet No. 8 in C minor, Op. 110**
The reason I bought the subscription. Written in three days in 1960 while Shostakovich was in Dresden for a film score assignment. He later claimed it as his own memorial—a farewell note in music. The DSCH motif (D-E♭-C-B in German notation, his initials) runs through all five movements.

The hall went completely quiet between movements. No one coughed. That particular quality of attention is why live music matters.

**Rating:** 9/10. The Shostakovich alone justified the ticket.
