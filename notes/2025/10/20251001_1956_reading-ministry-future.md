# Reading notes — The Ministry for the Future

**Author:** Kim Stanley Robinson
**Published:** 2020

Started this after finishing Demon Copperhead (see [[20250913_1928]] for the haul). The comparison people make to a policy document is accurate: this is less a novel with characters than a sustained thought experiment about how we might actually solve climate change.

The chapter variety is striking. Some chapters follow specific characters. Some are monologues by abstract voices—the market, the biosphere. Some are short essays or transcribed meetings. The form is trying to represent the scale and multiplicity of the problem.

The opening chapter is genuinely harrowing. A massive heat wave kills several hundred thousand people in India in a few days—a wet-bulb temperature event that makes outdoor survival physiologically impossible. An American aid worker survives when almost no one else around him does. Robinson doesn't dwell; the rest of the novel is the response to this event.

What I find compelling: the book takes seriously that the problem requires both technical solutions (carbon-negative technologies, halting ice sheet slide) and political/economic transformation (the shadow money system Robinson calls the "carbon coin"). No single protagonist. No single solution.

What I find less compelling: the characters feel like argumentative functions rather than people. It's a feature for some readers and a flaw for others.

The [book's website](https://www.orbitbooks.net/) has some supplementary climate science links.

Still in progress—about a third through.
