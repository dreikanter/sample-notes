# Apple picking at Hyland Orchard

Drove about an hour out of the city on Saturday. The orchard has been operating since 1913 according to the sign at the entrance. Rows of trees on a hillside that goes up steeper than you expect, with a view of the valley from the top.

We picked two varieties: Honeycrisp (already a favorite) and Macoun, which I hadn't had before. The Macoun is smaller, tarter, and softer-fleshed—better for eating out of hand than for storage, apparently. The woman running the stand said it bruises easily and doesn't ship well, which is why it's mostly an orchard-direct variety.

The picking itself: peaceful in the specific way that repetitive physical tasks are peaceful. Pick, lower into bag, move along the row. The trees had been picked over somewhat by earlier visitors in the week, so we had to go higher. The orchard provides wooden stepladders every few rows.

Left with about 22 pounds of apples, which is more than two people can reasonably eat before some turn. Plan:
- Eat the Macouns first (they won't last)
- Make applesauce with the second bag of Honeycrisps
- Try a French-style apple cake (not tarte tatin—too fussy)

The orchard has a cider press running through October. [Hyland Orchard](https://www.hylandorchard.com/) has a website with seasonal hours.

The drive back was slower. Worth it.
