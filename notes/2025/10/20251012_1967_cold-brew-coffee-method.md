# Cold brew coffee — method and ratio notes

Been making cold brew at home for two summers. Settled on a method.

## Ratio

1:5 coffee to water by weight for a concentrate that I dilute 1:1 with water or milk. So: 100g coffee to 500g (ml) water, which yields approximately 400ml concentrate after filtering.

If you want a ready-to-drink batch rather than concentrate, use 1:8 or 1:10.

## Coffee

Medium roast works best for cold brew. Light roasts tend to taste thin and acidic without heat extraction; dark roasts can go sour and muddy. I've had good results with a single-origin Colombian or Ethiopian washed process, both medium.

Grind coarse — roughly the texture of raw sugar. A coarser grind means slower extraction, which means fewer bitter compounds.

## Process

1. Combine ground coffee and room-temperature filtered water in a large jar or pitcher
2. Stir once to ensure all grounds are wet
3. Cover and refrigerate for 18–24 hours
4. Filter through a paper coffee filter in a funnel — this takes 20–30 minutes; don't rush it
5. Store filtered concentrate in a sealed jar, refrigerator

## Shelf life

Ready-to-drink: up to 2 weeks refrigerated
Concentrate: up to 2 weeks

The flavor actually improves slightly over the first 3 days.

## Notes

Tried a coarse cloth filter once; the resulting cold brew was noticeably more bitter than paper-filtered. The paper removes more of the lipid compounds that add bitterness.
