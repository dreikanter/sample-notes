# Sourdough bake — attempt four, 78% hydration

Following up on attempt three (see [[20250908_1918]]). Increased hydration to 78% as planned.

## Parameters

- **Flour:** 70% bread flour, 30% whole wheat
- **Hydration:** 78% (up from 75%)
- **Starter:** 18% inoculation, 10-hour levain
- **Bulk ferment:** 4.5 hours at 74°F (23°C)
- **Cold retard:** 14 hours

## Process notes

The higher hydration was noticeable immediately during mixing. The dough was considerably wetter and stickier; I needed to wet my hands rather than flour them during the stretch-and-fold sets. By the fourth fold it had developed significantly more structure than expected.

Shaping was harder—had to work quickly. Used a well-floured surface and floured banneton.

## Result

**Crust:** Excellent. Even better than attempt three.

**Crumb:** Noticeably more open. Large irregular holes distributed through the crumb, which is what I was after. Not as gaping as many bakery loaves but a clear improvement.

**Flavor:** The shorter bulk (4.5 hours vs. 5.5) reduced sourness slightly. I think I actually preferred three's flavor, but the crumb structure is better here.

## Conclusion

78% hydration is a better hydration for the open crumb I want. Will keep the shorter bulk from this attempt but push starter temperature slightly cooler to recover some of the sour flavor.

**Next attempt:** 78% hydration, 12-hour levain at 72°F instead of 74°F.
