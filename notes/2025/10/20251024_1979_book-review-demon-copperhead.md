# Book review — Demon Copperhead by Barbara Kingsolver

**Published:** 2022
**Pulitzer Prize:** 2023

The premise is Dickensian in the literal sense: this is a retelling of David Copperfield set in the Virginia Appalachian opioid crisis, narrated by a boy named Damon Fields (Demon). Kingsolver's fidelity to Dickens is playful rather than mechanical—you can track the parallels if you've read the original, but you don't need to.

The voice is the achievement. Demon narrates with a specificity of place and idiom that feels earned rather than performed. He knows the names of mountains and hollers, the brand of cola that comes in the biggest bottle for the least money, the particular logic of a life lived in genuine poverty without the romanticization that often afflicts fiction about it.

Kingsolver is unsparing about the pharmaceutical companies and the prescribers who enabled the opioid epidemic—this is partly the Dickens point, made explicit. The villains are systemic as well as personal, and the novel doesn't offer easy redemption.

At 548 pages it's long and occasionally it shows. The middle section sags slightly. But the final hundred pages are some of the most powerful Kingsolver has written.

The [Pulitzer Prize page](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/barbara-kingsolver) has a good description of the judges' reasoning.

**Rating:** 8.5/10. The best novel I've read this year. Read it if you can handle difficult material.
