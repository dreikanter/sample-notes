# Reading notes — Lonesome Dove by Larry McMurtry

Started this on a whim after finishing Demon Copperhead. Long novel (945 pages); I'm about 300 pages in.

The reputation is fully deserved. What McMurtry does is make you forget you're reading a "Western"—the landscape is vivid, the cattle drive plot is functional structure, but the real material is character. Gus McCrae and Woodrow Call are among the most fully realized fictional companionships I've encountered. They've known each other for 40 years and disagree about nearly everything; the relationship is so established that they barely need to complete sentences.

The prose style is unfussy and direct. McMurtry doesn't linger over sunsets when there's plot to advance. This should feel like a limitation but it creates velocity.

What I wasn't expecting: the emotional range. The novel is funny in ways that genuine American humor often is—specific, deadpan, arising from character rather than situation. And then brutal in ways that feel earned rather than gratuitous.

The Pulitzer Prize was 1986. [McMurtry's website](https://larrymcmurtry.com/) doesn't tell you much but the Pulitzer page does.

Three weeks of reading left at my current pace. Not rushing it.

**Progress:** ~300/945 pages
**Rating so far:** 9.5/10
