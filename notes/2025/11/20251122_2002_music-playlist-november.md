# Music for November — playlist notes

November is for certain kinds of music and not others. Tracking what I've been listening to.

## Heavy rotation

**Bill Evans — Waltz for Debby (1961)**
The Village Vanguard recordings. Evans, Scott LaFaro, and Paul Motian playing live with the sound of the room—glasses clinking, muffled conversation. LaFaro died in a car accident 10 days after this was recorded. The album has that quality of unknowing finality.

**Nils Frahm — Spaces (2013)**
Solo piano live recordings. Minimal and patient; the room acoustics are part of the music. Good for late evenings.

**Arthur Russell — World of Echo (1986)**
Strange solo cello and voice recording, one of the more unusual albums in the catalog of a very unusual musician. Hard to describe. The [Drag City page](https://www.dragcity.com/artists/arthur-russell) has background.

**Nick Drake — Bryter Layter (1970)**
"Hazey Jane II" in particular. Drake's production on this album is denser than Pink Moon but more intricate than Five Leaves Left. The arrangements (by Robert Kirby) don't get enough credit.

**Caroline Shaw — Orange (2019)**
A Pulitzer-winning composer writing for string quartet. Dense, technically demanding, not easy to listen to for the first twenty minutes, then suddenly you're inside it.

## What I played during cooking on Thanksgiving

A playlist of Italian standards—Dean Martin, Connie Francis, old-school Italian holiday music. The kitchen needed something warmer and more forgiving.

Reference: [Pitchfork's Arthur Russell retrospective](https://pitchfork.com/features/in-the-studio/arthur-russell-revisited/)
