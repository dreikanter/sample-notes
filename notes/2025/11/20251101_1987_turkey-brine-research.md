# Turkey brine research — wet vs. dry

We're hosting Thanksgiving this year (see [[20251030_1985]]). 8 people. I've done a wet brine twice before; thinking about trying dry this year.

## Wet brine

The traditional approach: submerge the turkey in a salt-water solution (usually 1 cup salt per gallon of water) for 12–24 hours. Often includes aromatics—bay leaves, peppercorns, citrus, herbs.

**Pros:** Significantly increases moisture retention during roasting. Hard to mess up the salt level if you follow the ratio.

**Cons:** Requires a container large enough to hold a 14+ lb turkey submerged. Means clearing most of the refrigerator. Wet skin can make crisping the exterior harder.

## Dry brine (salt cure)

Rub the turkey generously with kosher salt (about 1 tbsp per 5 lbs of turkey), plus optional spices and baking powder for crispier skin. Let rest uncovered in the refrigerator for 48–72 hours.

**Pros:** No logistics of a large brining vessel. The uncovered rest in the refrigerator actually dries the skin, which crisps better. The salt draws moisture out, then it reabsorbs along with dissolved flavors.

**Cons:** Requires more planning (48–72 hours vs. 12–24 for wet). Over-salting is easier to do with dry.

## Verdict

Going with dry brine this year. Starting Sunday night (Nov 23) for a Thursday dinner gives 4 full days—plenty of time.

The recipe I'm adapting from: [Samin Nosrat's dry-brined turkey method](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/11/14/dining/dry-brined-turkey-recipe.html) in NYT Cooking.

Key additions: paprika, thyme, and orange zest in the brine.
