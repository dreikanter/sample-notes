# Thanksgiving debrief — 2025

8 people, 4:30 p.m. table time. Everything came together—barely.

## What worked

**The dry brine (see [[20251101_1987]]):** Noticeable improvement in skin crispiness. The paprika gave the skin a deep mahogany color that looked impressive and tasted good. Several people asked what I did differently.

**Spatchcocking:** The turkey was done in 85 minutes. For context, a traditional roasted 14 lb turkey takes 3–3.5 hours. The breast and dark meat were done simultaneously, which almost never happens with traditional roasting. Will do this every year.

**Cranberry sauce:** Made two days ahead. The homemade version is so much better than canned that it's not a fair comparison. The orange zest and a bit of port were the right additions.

**Kale from the garden:** A surprise hit. The frost-sweetened kale sautéed with garlic converted at least one confirmed kale-skeptic.

## What could improve

**The Wellington:** Technically successful (didn't leak, cooked through) but slightly under-seasoned. The duxelles needed more salt and maybe some thyme. The vegetarian guest was gracious.

**Timing:** The mashed potatoes were done 20 minutes early and had to be kept warm, which made them slightly less fluffy than they should be. Next year: coordinate better or make the potatoes last.

**Table:** Not enough room for all the serving dishes. We improvised with a folding card table, which worked but wasn't elegant.

## Next year

Dry brine, spatchcock, make the Wellington more aggressively seasoned, buy a larger serving board.
