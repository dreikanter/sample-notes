# Winter morning walk — November 26

The day after Thanksgiving. Cold and grey, temperature around 34°F (1°C). Walked for about an hour before anyone else in the house was awake.

The neighborhood is quiet in a particular way on long holiday weekends—fewer cars, no school buses, the particular silence of a community in collective rest. The coffee shop on the corner was open; its window was lit up warm orange against the grey morning. I went in and got a pour-over to drink on the walk.

The leaves are almost entirely down now. What's left on the trees is mostly the brown remnants on the oaks—they hold their leaves longer and release them more slowly than maples. The contrast has shifted from autumn's color drama to winter's structural interest: the shapes of bare branches, the geometry of telephone wires against white sky, the way the light goes low and long all day without ever feeling like it's going to get higher.

Three dogs I haven't seen before, being walked by a woman in a bright red coat. A pair of runners who nodded. A crow working at something on the sidewalk, unhurried until I was five feet away.

There's something useful about walking without a podcast or music on mornings like this. The thoughts that arrive when there's nothing else arriving are usually the better ones—slower, less anxious, less concerned with what I should be doing. More just noticing what's there.
