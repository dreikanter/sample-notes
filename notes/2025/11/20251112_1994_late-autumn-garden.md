# Late autumn garden — November check

First frost of the season came three days ago—two nights of 29°F (-2°C). The garden has changed abruptly.

## What happened

The remaining basil, which had been hanging on in the shelter of the south bed, blackened overnight. The tomato cages are now empty structures over bare soil. The cosmos, which I'd been watching for weeks, finally collapsed.

What survived: the kale is thriving. A hard frost sweetens the leaves by converting starches to sugars, and this one seems to have done exactly that. I cut a large bunch this morning and it was the best kale I've had from the garden.

The garlic I planted in October (see [[20251007_1962]]) hasn't shown yet, which is normal. Garlic sets roots in autumn and doesn't emerge until late winter or early spring. The straw mulch looks intact.

## Winter interest

The garden isn't beautiful in winter, but there's interest. The seed heads on the dried rudbeckia are covered with goldfinches this week. The ornamental grasses still have their seed plumes. The bare branches of the Japanese maple are a good shape against grey sky.

## What to do before real cold

- Pull remaining spent annuals before the ground freezes hard
- Protect the rosemary with a cloche or thick mulch (borderline hardy here)
- Mulch the perennials that were divided last month

## Looking forward

Seed catalogs will arrive in January. That's the real start of the gardening year.
