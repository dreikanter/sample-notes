# Reading notes — The Ministry for the Future (finished)

Started in [[20251001_1956]], finished this week.

The back half of the book was better than the front. The plot, such as it is, resolves in ways that feel neither triumphant nor defeated—things get somewhat better, not enough, but the machinery of change is in motion. Which is probably closer to the truth of how large problems get solved than either utopian or dystopian resolutions.

## What I'm taking from it

**The carbon coin idea** is interesting. Robinson imagines a new global currency backed by carbon kept in the ground—countries and entities that sequester carbon receive coins that are legally mandated as acceptable for debt payment. It's designed to make "doing nothing" economically competitive with fossil fuel extraction. I don't know enough economics to evaluate whether it's workable, but the political economy logic is coherent.

**The chapter structure** is a formal argument for complexity. The problem can't be seen from one vantage point; Robinson refuses to give us one.

**The geoengineering sections** on pumping meltwater from beneath Antarctic glaciers were the most surprising. This is real science being actively researched—[Nature paper on subglacial pumping](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-021-01165-7). Robinson took the idea seriously enough to explain it in detail.

## Overall assessment

Recommended if you care about climate solutions and can tolerate fiction that's more essay than narrative. Not recommended if you need strong characters or emotional throughline.

Rating: 7/10.
