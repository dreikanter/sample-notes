# Pasta from scratch — first attempt

From the November task list (see [[20251030_1985]]). Made tagliatelle.

## Ingredients

- 200g 00 flour (plus extra for dusting)
- 2 large eggs
- Pinch of salt

## Process

Mound the flour on a work surface, make a well in the center, crack eggs into the well. Beat eggs gently with a fork, then gradually incorporate flour from the inner walls of the well. When it becomes too stiff for the fork, switch to hands.

Kneaded for about 10 minutes—the dough went from shaggy and sticky to smooth and supple. The 10-minute timer feels arbitrary but the texture change is real. Wrapped in plastic and rested 30 minutes at room temperature.

Rolled with a rolling pin to about 2mm. This is harder than it sounds. The dough is elastic and wants to spring back. The advice to roll from the center outward helps. Eventually got a rough rectangle about 60x40cm.

Folded into a loose roll and sliced into strips—tagliatelle width, approximately 6mm. Shook out into loose nests.

## Cooking and sauce

Cooked in well-salted boiling water for 2–3 minutes. Fresh pasta cooks much faster than dried.

Served with a simple butter-sage sauce: melt 60g butter in a pan, fry 6 sage leaves until crisp, toss with pasta and Parmigiano. Finished with a little pasta water to bind.

## Result

Genuinely better than any dried pasta I've used in this preparation. The texture is silky and toothsome simultaneously. The nests weren't perfectly even but they cooked evenly.

**Will do again.** Reference: [Marcella Hazan's pasta principles](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Marcella-Hazan-Essentials-of-Classic-Italian-Cooking/Marcella-Hazan/9780394584041).
