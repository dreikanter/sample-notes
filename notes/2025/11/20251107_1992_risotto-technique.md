# Risotto technique — what actually matters

Made mushroom risotto twice this week while Thomas is out of town (not related). The first batch revealed what I was doing wrong; the second fixed it.

## What actually matters

**The rice:** Arborio or Carnaroli. Carnaroli is harder to find but holds its shape better and absorbs the broth without going mushy. Use Carnaroli when you can.

**The stock must be hot:** Adding cold stock to hot rice causes the grains to seize and release starch unevenly. Keep the stock in a small pot on the burner beside the risotto.

**The toast:** Before adding liquid, toast the dry rice in the soffritto fat for 2 minutes until the edges of each grain turn translucent. This sets the outer starch and slows absorption slightly, giving you more control.

**Adding liquid:** Add one ladle at a time, stirring until almost absorbed before adding the next. The stirring is important—it releases surface starch from the grains, which is what creates the creamy consistency.

**The mantecare:** The final step. Remove from heat, add cold butter cut into cubes and Parmigiano, and stir vigorously until the butter is fully incorporated and the risotto is glossy and flowing. Most recipes underdescribe this step.

## Quantities (serves 2 generously)

- 200g Carnaroli rice
- 1.2 liters stock, hot
- 1 small onion, finely diced
- 3 tbsp dry white wine
- 40g cold butter
- 50g Parmigiano, grated

## The mushroom variation

Sauté 200g mixed mushrooms (cremini, shiitake, porcini if budget allows) separately until deeply colored. Add at the end, after the mantecare.
