# Film review: The holdovers

Watched *The Holdovers* (2023, dir. Alexander Payne) last night. It's been out for over a year and I'm late to it, but it turns out timing matters — watching a film set over Christmas break during Christmas break changes the light on it.

Paul Giamatti plays a despised prep school classics teacher stuck minding a handful of students who can't go home for the holidays. Da'Vine Joy Randolph plays the school cook who has recently lost her son in Vietnam. Dominic Sessa, a real find, plays the troubled student who stays behind. They orbit each other for two weeks in a Massachusetts winter, and by the end something has genuinely happened to all of them, though the film is too honest to call it healing.

What's remarkable is the texture. Payne and his cinematographer shot on film stock and degraded it to look like a 1970s movie — it's set in 1970, and you'd be forgiven for thinking you were watching an actual artifact. The grain, the color temperature, the aspect ratio: all precise. But it never feels like a gimmick because the performances are warm enough to work against the formal cold of the era.

Giamatti is predictably excellent. Randolph won the Oscar for Supporting Actress and absolutely deserved it. Her scene near the end in the diner is one of the best pieces of screen acting I can think of recently.
