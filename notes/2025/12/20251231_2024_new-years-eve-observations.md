# New Year's Eve observations

The city does a thing every December 31 where the streets empty by 10 PM and then fill back up at midnight with people who seem genuinely surprised the year is over. I walked to the corner bodega around 11:30 and the avenue was quiet except for a few cabs and one guy pushing a shopping cart full of flattened cardboard.

By 12:05 there were fireworks from two separate directions — illegal ones from the park blocks away, and what sounded like a professional show somewhere over the water. The neighbor upstairs played something with a thumping bass that I couldn't identify. Someone in the building was cooking onions; I could smell it in the hallway.

I've been doing a version of this for enough years that the ritual is comfortable. I stay home. I make a decent meal — tonight, roasted chicken thighs with olives and lemon, a simple green salad. I think about the year. I make a short list of things I want to do differently, not resolutions in the formal sense but more like course corrections.

This year's list is short: read more slowly, reply to more emails from people I care about, learn to make one thing in the kitchen I've been intimidated by (croissants, probably).

The fireworks went on until about 12:20 and then it was quiet again. The city settled back into itself. Good enough.
