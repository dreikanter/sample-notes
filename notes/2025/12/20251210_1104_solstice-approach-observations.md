# Solstice approach observations

Twelve days from the winter solstice. Sunset here is now at 4:52 PM, which means darkness arrives before the working day officially ends. This does something odd to perception of time — the evening feels longer, but it isn't; the day just ends earlier.

Went out two nights ago with the 8x42 binoculars. The Pleiades are high in the south by 8 PM, easy naked-eye target even in the city if you look slightly off-center (averted vision). Orion is fully clear of the eastern trees by 9. The seeing was mediocre — 3/5 at best — but Jupiter was still worth the look, two bands visible with steady moments.

Light pollution here is Bortle 7 from the backyard. From the hill in the park it drops to maybe Bortle 5 on a good night, which changes the experience considerably — the Milky Way isn't spectacular but it's present. [[20240905_1009]] has notes from the clearer autumn session.

## Seasonal notes

The cold has a different quality in December than October. October cold is sharp and dry; December cold here tends to be damp and still, which makes it feel deeper than the thermometer suggests. Dressing in layers with a wind-resistant outer shell works better than insulation alone.

Coffee consumption is noticeably up. I've started keeping the kettle on the counter instead of the cabinet, which is a reliable indicator of the season. [[20250120_1010]] still applies — the light-roast Ethiopian is better than anything from the supermarket shelf.

A good resource for planning observation sessions around moon phase and twilight times: https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/

The year feels like it's decelerating. Good time to start drafting the year-end note before the 25th rather than scrambling on the 31st.
