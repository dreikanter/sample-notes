# Red lentil soup variations

Following up on yesterday's solstice soup (see [[20251226_2016]]): I've made this three times in the last two weeks and finally understand what I was doing wrong in earlier attempts.

The critical step is blooming the spices in oil before adding anything wet. One teaspoon cumin seeds, half a teaspoon smoked paprika, a pinch of cayenne, and a bay leaf — all into the pan with two tablespoons of olive oil over medium-high heat for about ninety seconds. The kitchen changes. This is not metaphorical.

Then onion and garlic, soft but not colored. Then the rinsed lentils (red, split — they cook fast). Then stock or water, about four cups. Simmer twenty minutes. The lentils dissolve entirely; there's no need to blend. A squeeze of lemon before serving is mandatory, not optional. Fresh parsley helps if you have it.

Variations I've tried:
- **Turkish style**: Add diced tomato with the onion, finish with dried mint and Aleppo pepper
- **Egyptian-ish**: More cumin, finish with a separate pan of sliced onion fried dark in butter
- **Greener version**: Wilt in two big handfuls of spinach in the last minute

The Serious Eats recipe at https://www.seriouseats.com/red-lentil-soup-recipe is a good baseline but undersalts at every stage. Salt as you go.

One pot, thirty minutes, genuinely restorative. That's the bar for winter cooking.
