# Christmas day reflection

Quiet morning. Nobody visiting until the afternoon, which gave about three hours of unstructured time — rare enough to feel slightly disorienting. Made a proper breakfast instead of the usual hurried coffee, read for an hour without checking anything, and sat with the year for a while.

## What changed this year

The shift to a lighter tech stack at work — fewer services, simpler deployments — took until October to feel settled. The Docker Compose work [[20250830_1012]] was part of that, but the harder part was the organizational inertia. Simpler is genuinely harder to sell than complex.

Started cooking more deliberately rather than just efficiently. The lentil soup [[20251117_1102]] is now a monthly staple. The sourdough habit from a few years ago [[20230503_1004]] is still running — currently baking every ten days or so, which feels sustainable.

Reading was inconsistent. Good stretches in spring and a strong run in November, thin in the summer months. The Dostoevsky from May [[20250508_1011]] and Le Guin from November [[20251108_1101]] are the two that will stay with me.

## What I want to carry forward

Less context-switching during working hours. The weeks where I blocked mornings for deep work were measurably more productive and less depleting. The year-end review I started in [[20251212_1013]] outlines this in more detail.

The backup restore test still hasn't happened. Carrying it as the first concrete task of January.

## A note on the holiday itself

There's something to be said for a slow Christmas. The pressure to make it feel significant can crowd out the actual quiet that makes it feel like a pause. Easier said than practiced.
