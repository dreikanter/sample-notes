# Coffee advent calendar — notes

Got a specialty coffee advent calendar—24 small bags of different single-origin coffees, one for each day until Christmas Eve. It's the kind of thing that sounds excessive but is genuinely educational.

## Method

Using a V60 pour-over for all of them to keep the variable constant. 15g coffee, 250ml water at 93°C (200°F), 2:30–3:00 total brew time. Recording tasting notes loosely.

## Days 1–4

**Day 1 — Colombia, Huila, washed process:**
Bright, clean, red fruit. Almost tart. I'd describe it as raspberry-adjacent, which sounds overreaching until you smell it directly.

**Day 2 — Ethiopia, Yirgacheffe, natural process:**
Opposite of Day 1: heavier, fruit-forward in a jammy way rather than tart. Wine-like aroma. Natural process coffees tend toward this profile because the fruit dries around the bean and imparts flavor.

**Day 3 — Guatemala, Antigua, washed:**
More balanced than the first two. Chocolate and brown sugar. This is the coffee that would convert someone who thinks they don't like black coffee.

**Day 4 — Kenya, Kiambu, washed:**
Most unusual so far. A sharp, savory quality alongside the fruit—like tomato and black currant. "Tomato in coffee" sounds wrong but works.

## Notes

The variation across these four is larger than I expected. The processing method (washed vs. natural) and origin produce genuinely different cups from what is nominally the same product.

Reference: [James Hoffmann's coffee YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@jameshoffmann) for method detail and taste training.
