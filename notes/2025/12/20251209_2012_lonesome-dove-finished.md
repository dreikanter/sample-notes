# Lonesome Dove — finished

Started in [[20251124_2003]], finished December 9. 945 pages in about 15 days, which is fast for me.

The ending is right. It doesn't give you what you want, which is what it should do. The last 100 pages prepare you for the ending without making it feel inevitable; when it arrives, it feels both surprising and like the only thing that could have happened.

What McMurtry accomplishes that I couldn't fully articulate while reading: he makes the epic feel intimate and the intimate feel epic without effort. The cattle drive from Texas to Montana is a framework; the actual subject is the relationship between two men who have lived their lives in a particular way and will die in that way, and whether that way of living was worth it. Neither of them can answer the question. Neither can the book. That's the honesty.

Gus McCrae is one of the great characters in American fiction. I'll argue about this if pressed.

The novel won the Pulitzer in 1986. [Larry McMurtry went on to write sequels](https://larrymcmurtry.com/)—I won't read them immediately, maybe ever. Some endings shouldn't be extended.

**Rating:** 10/10. Read it.

Added to my mental list of books I want to reread when I've forgotten enough of it to experience it fresh. That list: five books. This one is the newest addition.
