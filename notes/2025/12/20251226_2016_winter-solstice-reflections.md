# Winter solstice reflections

The solstice passed yesterday, technically, at 10:02 AM. I stepped outside at dusk and watched the light drain out of the sky faster than seems fair — by 4:15 the street was dark and the neighbors' holiday lights had already kicked on automatically. There is something almost violent about December's early evenings.

I've been thinking about what darkness is actually for. Not metaphorically — literally. The circadian biology of it: melatonin onset, the way core body temperature drops to prime the body for sleep, how shift workers and night-owl teenagers are fighting against millions of years of mammalian wiring. A paper I read last month in *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* (https://www.nature.com/nrn) made the case that "social jetlag" — the mismatch between biological and social clocks — is a genuine public health problem, not just a productivity complaint.

But philosophy aside: I made lentil soup today. Red lentils, cumin-tempered oil, a squeeze of lemon at the end. The apartment smelled like something worth coming home to. Small anchors matter on the darkest days.

The days get longer starting tomorrow. Six months of incremental recovery. There's an optimism in that, if you want it.
