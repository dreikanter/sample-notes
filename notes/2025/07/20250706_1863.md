# Composting — starting a new bin

Set up a second compost bin today. The first one is essentially full and in its final maturation phase — I'm not adding anything to it anymore, just letting it finish. The new bin is a standard plastic dalek type, positioned at the shadier end of the garden where the first one is.

Materials I've been accumulating to get it started:
- Several bags of shredded paper and cardboard (brown material — the "carbon" input)
- Coffee grounds from the past few weeks kept in a bag in the freezer
- Grass clippings from yesterday's mow (green — nitrogen input)
- Kitchen scraps: vegetable peelings, eggshells, fruit cores

The ratio to aim for is roughly 25-30 parts carbon to 1 part nitrogen by weight. In practice this means alternating layers — a layer of brown stuff, a layer of green stuff — and keeping the pile moist but not wet.

Things I'm leaving out: meat, fish, dairy, cooked food. Also citrus in large quantities (it can slow decomposition). Dog waste is a separate system.

The first bin took about seven months from start to usable compost. The second will ideally go faster because I have more confidence about the moisture level and layering now. The grass clippings will be the main risk — in large quantities they mat together and become anaerobic. I need to mix them with enough brown material.

Practical composting guide with troubleshooting: https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/resources/composting
