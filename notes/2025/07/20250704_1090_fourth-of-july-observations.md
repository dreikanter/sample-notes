# Fourth of July observations

Went to the neighborhood block party for the first time in three years. The street was closed off from 4 PM, and by 6 PM there were maybe 200 people between the barricades — a lot of families with young kids, a handful of older residents who've been on the block since the 1980s, and a new wave of people who moved in during the pandemic remote-work shuffle.

The interesting tension: the longtime residents organized around tradition (same potluck structure, same DJ with the same playlist anchored around the same ten songs), while the newer arrivals kept suggesting modifications — a cocktail station, a shared Spotify queue, a photo wall. The organizers were politely immovable. The result was that both groups sort of co-existed without really mixing, which is its own kind of data point about how neighborhoods integrate or don't.

Fireworks over the park at dusk. The city officially cancelled the municipal display due to budget cuts, so three separate neighborhoods ran competing illegal-but-tolerated private shows. The overlap from about 9:05 to 9:20 PM was genuinely spectacular by accident.

Temperature was 91°F at 7 PM, dropping to 79°F by 10 PM. Too hot for most of the afternoon; the actual gathering didn't cohere until after 7. Worth noting for next year if I'm planning anything outdoors in early July.
