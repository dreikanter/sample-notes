# Notes on long-form writing — what makes it hard

I don't do much long-form writing — mostly notes and documentation — but I've been attempting something longer for the past two months, a personal essay about a trip I took in 2019. Notes on what's been difficult.

**The specific problem of compression**

A trip that lasted three weeks contains more material than I could process in a year of essays. The first draft problem isn't blank page syndrome — it's which 2% of experience to include and which 98% to leave out. The omissions are where the judgment happens.

Chekhov [[20250607_1845]] on this: the moon doesn't need describing; you only need the glint on the glass. The difficulty is trusting that the detail does the work.

**Sequence versus meaning**

The trip happened in time order, but the interesting thing about it isn't the chronology. The interesting thing is a kind of realization that accumulated slowly and then arrived suddenly on a particular afternoon. The essay wants to be structured around that shape, not around Tuesday-Wednesday-Thursday.

**Voice stability**

Over a piece of several thousand words, the voice needs to hold. My notes naturally drift in tone depending on mood. What sounds good on Tuesday reads as slightly different from what I wrote on Friday. Revision is partly about making the voice feel like one person throughout.

**What seems to be working**

Getting a complete bad draft first, then revising. Not trying to revise and draft simultaneously. The first draft is permission to be rough.
