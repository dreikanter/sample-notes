# Rain after drought — garden notes

It finally rained properly last night. About 18mm according to the rain gauge, which is more than we had in the previous four weeks combined. The garden this morning looks different — colors more saturated, everything visibly more upright.

The beans were the most obviously stressed by the dry spell. The leaves had been developing a slightly matte quality and the lower ones were yellowing. This morning they're glossy and the yellowing seems to have halted. Whether they'll fully recover depends on whether the rain continues.

The tomatoes, counterintuitively, seemed okay through the drought because I was watering them by hand every other day specifically at the root. The risk now that it's rained heavily is blossom end rot or skin cracking on fruit that was water-stressed and then suddenly well-watered. I cut open one cherry tomato this morning and it was fine, but I'll keep watching.

The grass doesn't need me to mourn it — it looked brown and dead and has now gone back to green within hours. Grass is extraordinarily resilient in this way. What appeared to be permanent damage turns out to be dormancy.

Interesting observation: the earthworm activity after the rain was visible. Several came to the surface on the paths, which they do when the soil is saturated and they can't breathe underground. A few blackbirds were working the lawn systematically.

The smell of petrichor — rain on dry earth — is caused by geosmin, a compound produced by soil bacteria. Strong this morning.
