# Reflections on a long meeting-heavy week

Last week was unusually meeting-heavy — 11 distinct meetings over four days. This is more than double my usual load. Notes on what that felt like and what it disrupted.

The obvious thing: the focused work time contracted sharply. I had at most two 90-minute blocks of uninterrupted time across the whole week, down from a usual five or six. Some work just didn't happen. The API architecture decision I needed to think through carefully got pushed to this week.

Less obvious: the meetings themselves degraded in quality as the week went on. By Thursday's planning session I was less sharp, less engaged with the material, and contributed less than I should have. This isn't just tiredness — it's a specific kind of cognitive depletion from switching contexts repeatedly. Each meeting requires a different kind of attention and the switching itself is expensive.

What I'll do differently: I should have declined two of the 11 meetings. One was a status update I could have gotten as a written summary; one was a meeting I attended as a courtesy where I wasn't needed. Both consumed time without producing anything I couldn't have gotten another way.

The question I'm sitting with is about the right meeting load as a structural matter, not just this anomalous week. I think my sustainable level is 5-6 meetings per week. Above that and the quality of both meetings and independent work drops.
