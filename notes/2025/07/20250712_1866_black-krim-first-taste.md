# Black Krim tomatoes — first taste

The first Black Krim came off the vine today. It had been sitting there getting darker and heavier for two weeks and was finally soft enough to the touch.

Sliced it while still warm from the sun — this matters, cold destroys tomato flavor — and ate it standing at the kitchen counter with just salt and a small pour of olive oil.

The flavor is exceptional. Very different from the Stupice [[20250701_1860]], which is bright and slightly sharp. The Black Krim is darker in flavor too: rich, almost meaty, with a sweetness that doesn't tip over into the saccharine quality of commercial greenhouse tomatoes. There's something almost wine-like in the complexity — I know that sounds affected but I don't have a better description.

The color is remarkable. The exterior is deep red with green-black shoulders, and sliced open the flesh is a dark red-brown. In bright light you can see the gelatinous seed cavities clearly against the denser flesh.

Texture: denser and meatier than most tomatoes, not watery. Fewer seeds than expected. The slices hold their shape.

This is why I grow tomatoes. The gap between a good homegrown tomato and a bought one is large in theory but it's still surprising every year to experience it directly.

Next week there should be five or six more ready. I'll use them in a simple caprese and save the rest for a roasted sauce.
