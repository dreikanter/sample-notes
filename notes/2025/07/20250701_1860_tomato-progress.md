# Tomato progress — July update

Six weeks after transplanting, the three varieties are diverging noticeably.

**Stupice**

Already has ripe fruit — two small red tomatoes on July 1, which is early by my usual expectations. The flavor is excellent: properly tomato-y with some acidity, not bland like the supermarket alternatives. The plant itself is compact and bushy, not reaching for height the way the indeterminate varieties do. This is a keeper for next year.

**Black Krim**

About two weeks behind the Stupice in ripening. The fruit that's set is large and irregular, starting to show the dark shouldering that gives it the name. These are the ones I'm most looking forward to — they're reportedly rich and slightly sweet.

**Sweet Million (cherry)**

The outlier in growth — this plant is enormous. It's twice the height of the Stupice despite being the same age. Fruit is setting in huge clusters, hundreds of small green cherries. At this point in the season the yield will be enormous and I'm already thinking about what to do with all of them: preserved in oil, roasted for sauce, dried.

**Maintenance notes**

Removing suckers (side shoots in the V between stem and branch) from the indeterminate varieties weekly. Left a few lower leaves on but stripped the ones touching soil to reduce blight risk. Giving a diluted tomato feed weekly now that the plants are in fruit-setting mode.
