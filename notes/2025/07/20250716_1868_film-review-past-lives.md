# Film review — Past Lives (2023)

Celine Song's debut film is almost aggressively restrained, and that restraint is what makes it devastating. Nora and Hae Sung were childhood sweethearts in Seoul who were separated when Nora's family emigrated to Canada. The film picks up their story in three sections: childhood, twelve years later, and twenty-four years later.

The central device — two people who might have had a life together encountering each other across decades — could easily become melodrama. Song refuses that. The film is full of conversations that are careful, emotionally precise, and aware of how much is being left unsaid. The bar scene near the end, where Hae Sung and Nora's husband Arthur talk while Nora is in the bathroom, is one of the best-written scenes I've seen in years. They're strangers with nothing obviously in common except that they both love the same person, and they treat each other with a careful, slightly bewildered kindness.

Greta Lee is extraordinary. She plays a woman who has chosen her life deliberately and doesn't regret it, while also feeling exactly the weight of what that choosing foreclosed. The two things are true simultaneously and she doesn't resolve the tension.

The Korean concept of *in-yeon* — a kind of fate or connection that follows people across lives — is introduced early and does real work throughout the film. It's not supernatural; it's more like a way of taking seriously the improbability of any particular person's significance to another person.

Rating: 5/5. One of the better films in recent memory.
