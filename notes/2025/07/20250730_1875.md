# End of July — heat notes and the garden update

July was hot. Four days over 30°C, which is unusual but becoming less unusual. The city doesn't handle heat well — the housing stock wasn't built for it, there's not enough shade in the streets, the parks dry to a pallid yellow-brown.

**Garden at end of July**

The tomatoes are in full production now. The cherry tomatoes are the main event — they're coming in faster than we can eat them fresh, so I've been roasting them in large batches. Half a tray of olive oil, salt, and a few garlic cloves, 160°C for 90 minutes until they're collapsed and slightly caramelized. Freeze in portions for pasta sauce through winter.

The Black Krim [[20250712_1866]] produced about eight large fruits. All excellent, eaten in simple preparations — sliced with good oil, salt, sometimes a few basil leaves. The last one was slightly overripe on the vine, which gave it an almost jammy sweetness.

The French beans are now producing properly. About 300g per picking, picking every three days. We've been eating them blanched with butter and a few almonds fried in the same pan, which is the best treatment I know for fresh beans.

**The thing I failed at**

The lettuce, which had been producing so well in June, bolted in the heat. All three plants sent up tall flower spikes and the leaves became bitter. I pulled them and have sown a more heat-tolerant variety for a late summer cut.

Overall the garden has been the most rewarding it's been in several years. The raised beds and dedicated watering made the difference.
