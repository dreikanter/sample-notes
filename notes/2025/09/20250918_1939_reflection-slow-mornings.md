# Reflection — on slow mornings

There's a version of the morning I've been trying to protect. Coffee before the phone, ideally before 7, when the apartment is quiet and I'm not yet performing for anyone.

I read somewhere—I think it was in [Oliver Burkeman's Four Thousand Weeks](https://www.oliverburkeman.com/books)—that the first hour of the morning is one of the few periods most people have any genuine discretion over, because obligations haven't accumulated yet. The rest of the day tends to be reactive.

I've noticed that the mornings I check my phone first thing tend to color the rest of the day. Nothing about a 7 a.m. phone check actually changes anything, but something is consumed that I can't recover.

On the mornings I get it right—coffee, either reading or just sitting with the window open—I feel like the day is mine in a way I don't feel otherwise. This might be psychological self-deception but I don't think the source of the feeling matters if it's real.

The challenge is that slow mornings require slow evenings: getting to bed at a reasonable time, not staying up to "decompress" from the day with another hour of screen. The mornings and evenings are connected.

I don't have a system for this. The attempts I've made to formalize it (tracking it, setting alarms, etc.) always collapse. The thing that works is just wanting to do it, which is not advice and can't be systemized. But I notice it when it works.

This is an attempt to articulate it clearly enough to remember what I'm aiming for.
