# TV notes — Dispatches from Elsewhere

**Network:** AMC
**Year:** 2020
**Episodes:** 10

Came to this one late. A friend described it as "what if an ARG became a TV show that was also about loneliness" and that's mostly accurate.

The premise: four strangers in Philadelphia are recruited into a mysterious game run by an organization called The Jejune Institute. Each episode focuses on one character and is stylistically distinct from the others—episode 3, told from the perspective of Simone, uses a different visual grammar than the others.

Jason Segel created and stars in it, and his performance as Peter—the blankest, most passive of the four—is better than you'd expect from someone who built his career on comfortable likability. The show asks him to carry silence, and he can.

The production design is extraordinary on what must have been a modest budget. Philadelphia becomes a city of secret passages and coded meanings, which is a good way to make a city feel alive in fiction.

Not everything works. The final two episodes reach for emotional transcendence in a way that's a little too telegraphed. But the mid-season episodes—especially "Simone" and "Fredwynn"—are genuinely moving and strange.

The show is in conversation with ideas about meaning-making in adult life, which sounds pretentious but is handled gently. The [official AMC page](https://www.amc.com/shows/dispatches-from-elsewhere) has some supplementary material.

Rating: 7.5/10. Worth your time if you have patience for quiet television.
