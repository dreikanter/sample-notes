# Restaurant notes — El Rincón Oaxaqueño

**Type:** Taqueria / Oaxacan
**Location:** 14th and Valencia area
**Visit:** Friday evening

A small counter-service spot I walked past twice before finally going in. Eight stools, a steam table, a handwritten menu over the register.

Ordered the tlayuda and a mezcal negroni from the tiny drinks menu. The tlayuda is a large, somewhat stiff tortilla (not crispy, not soft—something in between) topped with black bean paste, Oaxacan cheese, and your choice of protein. I got the tasajo, a salt-dried beef that's slightly chewy and deeply savory. The whole thing is probably 40 cm across and feeds two people, which I didn't know until it arrived.

The beans were extraordinary—black, silky, cooked with what I suspect was avocado leaf, which gives Oaxacan black beans that specific anise-adjacent aroma. I asked the woman at the counter about it and she confirmed avocado leaf. She said they also sell dried leaves on weekends, which I'll go back for.

The mezcal negroni was good but not better than a home version with the same ingredients.

Total for one (which was truly too much food): $22.

The place doesn't have a website, which is correct for what it is. There's a brief mention in a [Eater SF neighborhood guide](https://sf.eater.com/) if you know where to look.

**Would return:** Yes, with one other person, to share the tlayuda properly.
