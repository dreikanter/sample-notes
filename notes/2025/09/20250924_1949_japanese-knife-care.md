# Japanese knife care notes

Picked up a Nakiri from a Japanese kitchen supply shop last month. This is what I've learned about maintaining it properly.

## What I'm working with

**Knife:** Nakiri (vegetable knife), 165mm blade
**Steel:** White steel #2 (Shirogami), carbon steel, not stainless
**Hardness:** ~62 HRC

## The key differences from Western knives

White steel takes a sharper edge than most stainless steel but is significantly more reactive. It will rust if left wet or in contact with acidic food for more than a few minutes. It will patina — develop a grey-blue surface layer — which is normal and actually protective.

The bevel geometry is also different: my Nakiri has a nearly flat grind compared to the convex grind of European knives. It cuts differently; you feel the difference immediately on hard vegetables.

## Daily care

- Wipe dry immediately after use — a damp towel, then dry
- Don't leave in the sink or wet on the cutting board
- Apply a thin coat of camellia oil every few weeks to prevent rust

## Sharpening

Currently using a set of whetstones: 400 grit for profile work, 1000 grit medium, 3000 finishing, 6000 polishing. For regular maintenance (no chips) the 1000 and 6000 are sufficient.

Angle: maintaining the original bevel angle, approximately 12–15 degrees per side.
