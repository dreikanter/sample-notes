# Farmers market finds — late September

Went early this week—7:30 a.m.—and the light was still low and golden. The market runs through October but this feels like the peak of the autumn transition: summer squash going out, hard squash and apples coming in.

## What I bought

**Honeycrisp apples** from the orchard stall near the north entrance. $4/lb, which is steep, but they're the real thing—firm enough to echo when you knock them. I bought two pounds and already ate three.

**Delicata squash** — two of them, medium-sized. Easy to cook; the skin is edible. Plan to roast halved with butter and maple.

**Shishito peppers** — last of the season apparently. Got half a pound to blister in a dry pan with flaky salt. Ten percent of them are legitimately hot.

**Dried beans** — a bag labeled "calypso" (black-and-white marbled) from a farm I hadn't noticed before. The farmer said they cook faster than most dry beans and hold their shape well. Will try in a soup this week.

**Raw honey** — the wildflower variety. The vendor explained the flavor shifts with the season; this late-summer batch tastes of goldenrod and clover. He links to a university extension article on [wildflower honey varietals](https://extension.psu.edu/honey-bees) on his card.

## What I didn't buy

Passed on the $12 wheel of fresh chèvre. Passed on three varieties of specialty mushrooms that I would cook badly and waste.

## Next week

Early arrival again. It's worth it.
