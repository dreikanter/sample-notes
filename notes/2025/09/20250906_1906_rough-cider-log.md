# Rough cider — first attempt log

Starting a rough cider from the Bramley apples brought back from the orchard visit [[20250905_1898]]. "Rough cider" in the sense that I'm making no attempt at clarity or consistency — this is wild-yeast fermentation from backyard apples in a demijohn.

**Starting materials**

About 4kg of mixed Bramley and one Orleans Reinette. Juiced using a steam juicer (borrowed — they extract well without a press). Yield: approximately 2.4L of juice. Brix reading (sugar content) with a refractometer: 12 Brix, which suggests about 6-7% potential alcohol if fermented dry.

**Process**

Added one Campden tablet (sodium metabisulfite) dissolved in a small amount of water to the juice, to knock back wild yeast and bacteria on the apple skins that might produce off-flavors. Will wait 24 hours before pitching yeast.

Yeast: a Cider M02 strain, a dried yeast designed for fruit wines and ciders. Rehydrated per packet instructions.

Pitched into a 3L demijohn with an airlock. Starting specific gravity: 1.048 (consistent with the Brix reading).

**Goals**

Something bone dry, perhaps 6%, that tastes recognizably of apples but not of commercial cider. Rough is fine. Strange is fine. I'm not expecting this to be excellent but I want to understand the process.

Fermentation started by the next morning — airlock bubbling once every 3-4 seconds.

**Next check:** one week, gravity reading.
