# Notes on the neighborhood — what changes and what stays

I've lived in the same neighborhood for seven years. Some observations about what the seven years have changed and what hasn't moved.

**What's changed**

The coffee shop on the corner has cycled through three incarnations: a café that was there when I moved in and closed in 2020 (lockdown didn't kill it — it was already struggling), a bakery that was good but overpriced and lasted 18 months, and now a small specialty coffee place with a serious roaster relationship and a short, confident menu. The current iteration is the best of the three and seems stable.

The supermarket on the main road converted from a mid-range chain to a discount retailer about three years ago. This is probably a signal about the demographics of shopping patterns but I haven't worked out quite what it means.

Property prices have increased dramatically. The terraced house two doors down sold for £120k more than a comparable house in the same street sold for in 2019. The people moving in are broadly younger and seem to have money. The people moving out are broader demographic mix.

**What hasn't changed**

The Turkish restaurant at the far end of the high street has the same menu, the same décor, and possibly the same staff as when I moved in. It's never empty and never has a queue. It occupies a niche so stable that change would feel wrong.

The park. Same paths, same pond, same geese. The geese were established here long before me and will be here long after.

The sound of the city at 2am, which is specific to this particular intersection of roads and hasn't changed.
