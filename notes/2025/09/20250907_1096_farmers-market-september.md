# Farmers market, early September

Went to the Saturday market on the corner of Clement and 4th. Smaller than the Ferry Building crowds but more relaxed — vendors actually have time to talk. Picked up a flat of small yellow plums labeled "Shiro," a bunch of Chioggia beets, and a sourdough loaf that I later realized was nearly identical in crumb texture to what I've been baking at home. [[20230503_1004]] might have been right that the hydration sweet spot is higher than most recipes suggest.

The pepper vendor had a tray of dried Calabrian chilies at $8 for a small bag — steeply priced, but one or two flakes go a long way in pasta. Bought them anyway.

Observed that the honey vendor now labels by foraging radius ("urban 3-mile radius") instead of flower type. This seems like a response to customer skepticism about varietal claims, since urban bees visit whatever blooms and monofloral labeling requires testing most small operations can't afford. Interesting marketing pivot.

One stand was selling "koji-cured" everything: salt, butter, even a spice rub. Koji as a retail trend has been building since 2020 but seems to be hitting the mainstream-curious tier of farmers market shoppers now. The butter was genuinely good — umami-forward, slightly grainy, worked well on the sourdough at home.

Useful background on koji fermentation: https://www.seriouseats.com/koji-fermented-foods-guide

Temperature was about 62°F with morning fog burning off by 10:30. Best time to shop before the bread and mushroom tables sell out.
