# Autumn gardening tasks — what needs doing before October

The summer annuals are finishing and there's a window in September and early October to set the garden up for next year. List of what needs doing, roughly in priority order.

**Urgent (before first frost, likely late October)**

- Lift and store the dahlia tubers. They won't survive frost in the ground. Clean off the soil, let them dry for a week, pack in slightly damp compost and store in a frost-free shed.
- Take pelargonium cuttings before the parent plants die. Root them in small pots on a warm windowsill.
- Empty and clean the tomato cages and store for next year.

**High priority**

- Plant spring bulbs. The tulips and alliums need to go in by mid-October. Daffodils can wait until November.
- Sow sweet peas in root trainers for overwintering in the cold frame. Earlier start means better root development.
- Plant the garlic for next year. The bed is already cleared [[20250523_1822]]. Variety: Solent Wight and Lautrec Wight.

**General tidying**

- Cut back the herbaceous perennials once they've had their first frost damage — I leave them slightly longer than some people recommend because the seedheads provide food for birds.
- Add a layer of compost mulch to the beds before the ground hardens.
- Empty the water butts and turn them upside down to prevent algae over winter.

**The compost project [[20250706_1863]]**

The first bin should be ready to use by October. Turn it once more to check.
