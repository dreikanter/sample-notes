# Rome travel notes — day two

Up at seven, breakfast at the corner bar—cornetto and a macchiato standing at the counter like everyone else. It cost €2.20. The same breakfast at a table would have been €5.00. Rome teaches this rule quickly.

## Trastevere morning

Spent two hours walking Trastevere before the tour groups arrived. The neighborhood is genuinely lived-in: laundry overhead, scooters parked sideways on the cobblestones, a tabacchi with a hand-lettered sign advertising lottery tickets. Stopped at the Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere—12th-century mosaics in the apse, gold tessera catching the early light. No queue, no ticket. Just walked in.

The [official Rome tourism page for Trastevere](https://www.turismoroma.it/) oversells it a bit ("a neighborhood out of time"), but the mosaics really are staggering.

## Borghese Gallery

Pre-booked the 11 a.m. slot. Two-hour maximum visit; they enforce it. Worth every minute. The Bernini sculptures—Pluto and Persephone, Apollo and Daphne—are the reason to come. Photographs don't communicate scale or the impossible softness of the marble. Persephone's flesh indents under Pluto's fingers. You stand a foot away and can't understand how it's done.

Canova's Pauline Bonaparte as Venus Victrix is in the same room. She reclines on a marble chaise longue; the cushion beneath her reads as fabric. Two artists, decades apart, doing impossible things with the same stone.

## Afternoon

Negroni on the Campo de' Fiori. Feet hurt. Happy.
