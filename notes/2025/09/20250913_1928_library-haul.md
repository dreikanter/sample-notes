# Library haul — September 13

Picked up four holds today. Brief notes on why I requested each.

## Demon Copperhead — Barbara Kingsolver

2022 Pulitzer winner. A retelling of David Copperfield set in Appalachian Virginia during the opioid crisis. I'm skeptical of novels with a thesis but Kingsolver usually earns it. About 550 pages—ambitious for my current pace.

## The Ministry for the Future — Kim Stanley Robinson

Climate fiction, near future, published 2020. Recommended twice in the last month by different people. The [publisher's description](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/kim-stanley-robinson/the-ministry-for-the-future/9780316300131/) makes it sound like a policy document in novel form, which might be accurate. People seem to find it either essential or exhausting.

## Checkout: The Role of Libraries in a Democracy — various authors

Short essay collection. Pulled it because of a conversation I had at the library talk last Tuesday (noted in [[20250909_1921]]). Curious whether the arguments hold up outside of self-interested advocacy.

## The Warmth of Other Suns — Isabel Wilkerson

This has been on my list for three years. Narrative nonfiction about the Great Migration of Black Americans from the South. Wilkerson follows three individuals across decades. Around 600 pages. Not rushing it.

## Currently reading

Still in the middle of The Dispossessed (Ursula Le Guin)—borrowed from a friend, not the library. Plan to finish before diving into any of the above.

**Due date for all four:** October 11.
