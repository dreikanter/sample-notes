# Mushroom foraging — first outing of the season

Went out foraging this morning with someone who knows what they're doing. I am not experienced enough to forage alone safely — identification mistakes with mushrooms can be lethal — but I'm learning.

The location was a mixed woodland about an hour's drive out, predominantly oak and beech with some birch at the edges. The conditions were ideal: the rain earlier in the week had been followed by warm days, which is what mushrooms need.

**What we found**

Ceps (Boletus edulis, porcini) — seven of them, ranging from button-sized to one large mature specimen. This was the prize. The underside of the cap has pores rather than gills; ceps have a distinctive bulbous stem with a white net pattern. The smell is immediately recognizable.

Chanterelles (Cantharellus cibarius) — a good scattered cluster under the oaks. Orange-yellow, wavy-edged cap, forking false gills. No toxic lookalikes if you're careful about distinguishing from false chanterelle (which has true gills and is typically on dead wood).

Bay bolete (Imleria badia) — several, recognized by the dark brown cap that bruises blue when cut or damaged. Good edible mushroom, less prized than ceps.

Horn of plenty (Craterellus cornucopioides) — a patch near a beech trunk. Small, black, funnel-shaped. Excellent flavor, easy to dry.

**What we left**

Several mushrooms we weren't certain about. The rule: if uncertain, leave it. There is no shortcut.

Identification came home with us: all the ceps were checked against multiple sources before cooking.
