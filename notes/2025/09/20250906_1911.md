# Piano practice — returning after a long gap

Started playing piano again last week, first time in about four years. I played seriously from ages 8-18, got to about Grade 7 standard, and then stopped when I went to university and didn't have access to an instrument.

Four years ago I bought a weighted-key digital piano and played occasionally but not consistently. Then life contracted during a busy period and I stopped again.

Starting again after a gap is a specific kind of humbling. The muscle memory is partially there — my hands know where to go for basic patterns — but the coordination and the sight-reading that came fluently before require active thought now. I can play things I used to play automatically, but slowly, attending to each hand separately before combining them.

The piece I'm working on is a Bach Two-Part Invention (No. 4 in D minor). It's relatively simple but teaches contrapuntal playing — two independent melodic lines, one in each hand. When both lines are running independently at the same time, it's exactly the kind of exercise that rebuilds coordination.

I'm practicing 20-30 minutes per day, which is short enough to be consistent and long enough to see progress. The first session felt like swimming in mud. By session five, the first eight bars are going reliably.

What I've remembered: practice works by repetition of small sections, not by playing through the whole piece repeatedly. Taking four bars, playing them slowly until they're accurate, then gradually increasing tempo — this is still how it works.
