# Observation — the sound of rain on different surfaces

A heavy rain this morning, starting around 4 a.m. and still going at 8. Lay awake for a bit just listening.

The apartment has three distinct auditory registers for rain depending on where you are in it. In the bedroom, which faces the interior courtyard, rain sounds distant and undifferentiated—a hiss. In the kitchen, the sound hits the window glass directly and is sharper, more percussive, individual drops audible when the rate slows. From the bathroom, which is under an older section of roof, there's a hollow drumming that's entirely different in character—resonant, like a large hand on wood.

The petrichor smell came through the kitchen window when I opened it a crack: that compound combination of geosmin from actinobacteria in the soil and volatile organic compounds from plant oils. [Smithsonian Magazine has a good piece](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/what-causes-that-smell-after-it-rains-13806085/) on the chemistry of it. The word petrichor was coined in 1964 by two Australian researchers for a paper in Nature.

I find I sleep better in rain. Not because of the white noise particularly—I've tested white noise apps and they don't do the same thing. I think it's the suggestion of shelter: weather outside, roof overhead, no need to go anywhere.

The rain was still going when I left at 9:30. I took an umbrella I haven't used since April and walked slowly. The streets in this neighborhood drain badly and there were lakes at two intersections, the kind where you commit to the crossing or don't.
