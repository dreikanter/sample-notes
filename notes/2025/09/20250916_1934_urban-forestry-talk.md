# Urban forestry talk — library notes

Attended a free lecture at the branch library, organized by the parks department. Speaker was a certified arborist who manages the city's street tree program. About 30 people in the room.

## Key points

**Why street trees fail:** The failure rate within the first five years is around 30% for city-planted trees. The main culprits are wrong species choice, soil compaction, and insufficient watering after establishment (city budgets rarely cover the 3-year establishment period properly).

**Soil volume matters more than tree species:** A tree in 200 cubic feet of soil will never reach the same canopy size as one in 600 cubic feet, regardless of how well it's cared for. The speaker showed photos of the same species planted in parking lot islands vs. greenways—the difference was stark.

**The equity dimension:** Wealthier neighborhoods have significantly more tree canopy than low-income neighborhoods in almost every American city measured. The correlation between tree cover and household income is well-documented. This is partly historical redlining and partly ongoing political neglect.

**Heat islands:** A single mature tree can reduce surface temperature by 2–5°C in its immediate vicinity. City-wide canopy targets of 30% are increasingly common in climate adaptation plans.

**What residents can do:** Advocate for appropriate planting (not just numbers), water newly planted street trees, report damaged trees, avoid damaging roots by compacting soil around the base.

Resource recommended by speaker: [American Forests Tree Equity Score](https://www.americanforests.org/our-work/tree-equity-score/)
