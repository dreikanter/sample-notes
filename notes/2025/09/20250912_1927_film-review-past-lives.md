# Film review — Past Lives

**Director:** Celine Song
**Year:** 2023
**Runtime:** 106 minutes

I watched this alone on a Sunday afternoon, which was exactly the right context. Past Lives is a quiet film, but it carries extraordinary weight.

The premise: two childhood friends in Seoul are separated when one family emigrates to Canada. The film follows them across three time periods—childhood, early adulthood, and their early thirties—as the connection between them is strained, rekindled via video call, and finally confronted in person in New York.

Song's direction is restrained almost to the point of severity. There are no dramatic gestures. Characters say what they mean, mostly, and what they can't say stays visible in the space between them. The cinematography by Shabier Kirchner frames people against backgrounds that dwarf them: the Seoul skyline, the Staten Island Ferry water, the street outside a bar at night.

The final thirty minutes are among the most emotionally precise I've seen in recent memory. What makes the ending devastating isn't what happens—nothing much does—but the recognition of what it costs to live a life rather than the one you almost had.

Greta Lee and Teo Yoo are both exceptional. The [A24 page for Past Lives](https://a24films.com/films/past-lives) has some good supplementary material including a conversation with Song.

Rating: 10/10. See it with someone you have a complicated history with, or alone.
