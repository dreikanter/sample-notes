# Fermentation notes — first sauerkraut batch

**Date started:** 2025-09-11
**Cabbage weight:** 1.2 kg (one medium head)
**Salt:** 24 g (2% by weight)

## Method

Shredded the cabbage finely with a mandoline—consistent thickness matters for even fermentation. Mixed with salt in a large bowl and massaged for about eight minutes until the cabbage released enough liquid to submerge itself when packed into the jar. Packed tightly into a 1-liter wide-mouth mason jar, pressed down hard so brine came above the cabbage. Used a small zip-lock bag filled with brine as a weight to keep everything submerged.

Set on the counter at room temperature (~70°F/21°C).

## Day-by-day log

**Day 1:** No activity visible.
**Day 2:** Bubbles forming. Smells pleasantly tangy already.
**Day 3:** Active bubbling. Brine overflowed slightly—good sign. Wiped the jar neck clean.
**Day 5:** Bubbling slowed. Tasted: salty, crisp, mild sour. Not quite there.
**Day 7:** Tasted again—better. Moving to the refrigerator to slow fermentation and hold at this acidity level.

## Result

Good texture, pleasingly sour without being aggressive. The caraway seeds I added on a whim (maybe 1 tsp) were a good call.

## Next time

Try a red cabbage version. According to [Wild Fermentation by Sandor Katz](https://www.wildfermentation.com/), the pH should drop to around 3.5 for long-term preservation. May get a pH strip to monitor this more precisely.
