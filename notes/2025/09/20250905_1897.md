# Notes on attention — what I've changed

A year ago I wrote down a concern about reactive Slack usage (roughly every 20 minutes without intention, [[20250524_1824]]). Having tracked this informally since then and tried a few changes, here's what actually moved the needle.

**What worked**

Closing Slack during focused work blocks. Not silencing it — closing it. The difference is that "silenced" means I still know it's there and might still reflexively switch to it. Closed means it's not a tab to drift to.

Batching communication to three times a day: morning, after lunch, end of day. This required telling my team explicitly and asking them to use email or phone for urgent matters. The reality: almost nothing that arrives on Slack is actually urgent. Over six months, three things came up that needed immediate attention. Everything else could wait.

Turning off most phone notifications. I now receive calls, texts, and one app. No email push, no social apps. My phone is for communication and navigation, not entertainment.

**What didn't work**

Strict time-blocking in my calendar. I blocked out 9-11 as "focus time" and added it to shared calendars. Meetings got scheduled over it anyway and I didn't enforce it. The visual commitment wasn't enough; I needed behavioral enforcement.

Desk positioning — turned the desk to face away from the window, on the theory that distracting views would reduce focus. Made no measurable difference.

**Where I am now**

I think I average 4-5 hours of genuinely focused work on a good day, which is about where Newport [[20250529_1833]] says the ceiling is for knowledge workers.
