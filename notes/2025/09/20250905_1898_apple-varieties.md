# Apple varieties — notes from the orchard visit

Visited a heritage apple orchard open day — a working farm about 25 miles out of town that opens for one weekend in September when the main crop varieties are ripening. They have over 80 varieties, most of them old English ones that aren't commercially grown.

**Tasting notes from the afternoon**

*Egremont Russet* — the classic. Pale gold skin with the distinctive rough, russet texture. Flesh is dense and dry, nutty, with a faint anise note. Outstanding for eating fresh or in cheese boards.

*Cox's Orange Pippin* — I've had supermarket Cox before but this was a different experience. Complex flavor: honey, apricot, a slight floral note. The skin has beautiful orange-red striping. This is why people talk about it as a benchmark.

*Blenheim Orange* — very large apple, the sort that looks too big. Flavor is milder than Cox but very pleasant: sweet with a light acidity. Good for cooking as well as eating fresh.

*Orleans Reinette* — new to me. Yellow-gold with orange flushing. Flavor has a rich, almost tropical quality and a pleasant bitterness in the skin. The best apple I tried on the day.

*Bramley's Seedling* — tasted raw out of curiosity. Barely edible fresh — eye-wateringly tart. Cooked into a crumble that afternoon and it was extraordinary, collapsing completely into a fluffy purée with intense apple flavor.

Bought a mixed case to take home. Planning to make cider from the Bramleys.
