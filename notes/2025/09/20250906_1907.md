# Essay on keeping notes — why I do it and what it's become

I've been keeping notes systematically for about three years. The habit started as a productivity tool — capturing things I needed to remember, tasks to track. What it's become is harder to categorize.

The notes I wrote at the start of this year are primarily about external things: recipes, code patterns, garden tasks. As the months have gone on, there's a gradual drift toward something more internal — reflections on how I'm working, what I'm noticing, the texture of specific moments.

I'm not sure when "note-taking" became "keeping a record of a period." The solstice observation [[20250622_1855]], the bittern note [[20250829_1891]], the summer reflection [[20250906_1903]] — these aren't notes in the functional sense. They're attempts to fix moments that feel worth keeping before they become unavailable.

What notes can and can't do: they can preserve the content of a moment — the specifics, the sensory detail, the reasoning. What they can't do is preserve the feeling of the moment. Reading back the June solstice note gives me the information about what I observed but not the experience of sitting outside in the long light.

This might be why writing the notes matters beyond their utility as records. The act of writing is the act of paying attention. The note is the byproduct; the attention is the thing.

I'm also aware that notes can become a substitute for experience — noting the thing instead of doing the thing. I try to write notes from memories rather than in real time, precisely because writing in real time interrupts the very experience I'm trying to preserve.
