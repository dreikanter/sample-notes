# Music review — Mk.gee, Two Star & the Dream Police

**Release date:** March 2024
**Label:** Self-released / Interscope

Heard about this album through a single offhand sentence in a music newsletter and listened immediately. That's the best way to encounter it—without expectations.

Mk.gee (Michael Gordon) is a guitarist first. The album is built on a specific guitar tone: slightly too warm, slightly compressed, with a tremolo that sounds like it's being processed through gear that's almost failing. There are obvious debts to Prince and D'Angelo but also to some corner of 90s alt-rock that doesn't have a clean name.

"Are You Looking Up" runs on a groove that sounds simple until you notice the rhythm guitar is doing something extremely strange underneath. "Candy" is the most accessible track and the obvious single, but it's also the one that reveals the least. The album rewards patience.

What's unusual is the emotional register: sad but not moping, erotic but not explicitly, nostalgic for something the record seems to know you never quite had. Several tracks seem designed to be listened to alone at 1 a.m.

The [NPR review](https://www.npr.org/music/genres/pop/) praised the guitar work and mostly left the lyrics alone, which is wise—they're impressionistic to the point of opacity.

Best tracks: "Are You Looking Up," "Candy," "Alesis."

Rating: 8/10. The kind of record I'll still be putting on in three years.
