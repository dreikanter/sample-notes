# Morning light in the east garden

The angle of the sun has shifted noticeably this week. By 7 a.m. the light hits the raised beds at a low diagonal that it didn't manage in July, casting long shadows from the tomato cages across the lettuce patch. The cosmos are still going strong—probably another three weeks before frost ends them—but the basil is looking tired, leaves yellowing at the base.

I pulled the last of the summer squash yesterday. Four plants gave us more than we needed from June through August, and now the vines are papery and collapsing. The soil underneath was dark and loose, which I wasn't expecting given how dry August was. I'll work in some compost before planting the garlic in October.

The cardinal flower by the north fence peaked two weeks ago but still has a few spikes. It's attracted at least two ruby-throated hummingbirds this season, which I counted while drinking coffee on the steps. According to the [Cornell Lab's hummingbird tracking page](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/home/), the fall migration typically passes through this region in mid-September, so they'll be gone soon.

The kale I planted in late July is ready to harvest. I've been cutting outer leaves and leaving the center to keep producing. A light frost actually sweetens kale, so I'm in no hurry to clear it.

Next steps: order garlic bulbs from Keene Organics, pull spent annuals, work compost into beds 2 and 4.
