# September — arrival of autumn

September 1 and the change is perceptible if not yet dramatic. The tree at the end of the street has a few yellow leaves — not the tree dying, just the earliest ones deciding it's time. The temperature this morning was 13°C at 7am, which is the first time since April that I needed a jacket at that hour.

There's a particular smell that arrives with September: not the wet-earth smell of rain (which has no season) but something more specific, a slight mustiness from leaf litter beginning, a drying out of the summer growth. The cut-grass smell that dominated June and July is fading.

The light continues to shift in ways that feel surprising even when you've watched it happen many times before. At 7am there's a low golden light that catches the dust particles in the air above the park path, turning an ordinary walk into something that looks like a painting.

I've started running in a light jacket, which I resisted for too long in August out of stubbornness. The cooler morning temperatures are better for running — I go faster without noticing it, stay out longer without heat fatigue.

The swallows are gathering. I counted 40 on the wire along the southern edge of the park — they'll be gone within a month, following the swifts south. Swallows leave later than swifts and not as abruptly; they tend to build up in numbers first, gathering and departing over the course of several weeks.

The season turns. It's still summer by the calendar but it's autumn in the air.
