# Film review — Past Lives (2023)

Watched this for the second time, which confirmed that the first viewing was not an accident of mood. It genuinely is as good as the response suggested.

Celine Song wrote and directed, and it's her debut feature, which is difficult to believe. The film is built on restraint: long pauses, looks held a beat too long, conversations that circle what they actually mean without landing on it directly. In lesser hands this becomes precious or evasive. Here it feels like the only honest way to handle material this close to the bone.

The structure is clean — three sections, each roughly a decade apart, each showing a version of the relationship between Nora (played by Greta Lee) and Hae Sung (Teo Yoo). The film doesn't treat any of the three timeframes as more real or more important than the others. The twelve-year-old versions of the characters are accorded the same gravity as the adults.

What's strange about the final act is how it refuses to let the audience reduce anyone. Nora's husband Arthur (John Magaro) is written with real generosity — he understands exactly what he's witnessing at the bar table, and his response is neither theatrical jealousy nor saintly patience. It's just a person feeling something complicated in real time.

The line that landed hardest: "If you leave something behind, you gain something too, but you lose something too." This is said plainly, not as an epiphany.
