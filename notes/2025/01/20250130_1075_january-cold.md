# January cold

Managed to catch whatever was going around in the second week of the month. A week of low-grade misery: headache, fatigue, the specific dullness of sinuses that won't clear. Nothing serious, just inconvenient. Spent most of three days on the sofa reading and watching things I wouldn't normally make time for.

Finished the rest of *Anna Karenina* during this stretch. Being sick and immobile is apparently the ideal condition for it — no competing obligations, nothing better to do. The final third went quickly. Tolstoy handles Levin's religious crisis with a directness that I found surprising: no mysticism, no epiphany, just a gradual settling into something liveable. Anna's end is brutal but not sensationalised.

Also watched two films I'd been putting off: *The Holdovers* (warmer than I expected, Alexander Payne doing something gentler than his usual register) and *All of Us Strangers* (unsettling in a way that stayed with me, the kind of film where the logic of what you're watching shifts slowly under you).

The cold meant I couldn't run for nine days, which broke the habit I'd been building since November. Getting back to it today felt strange — the rhythm was gone and I had to consciously remind myself of the route. Three kilometres, nothing fast.

The particular silence of the streets at 7am in January is something I forget about over summer. Almost no traffic, frost still on car roofs, light just arriving but uncertain about it.
