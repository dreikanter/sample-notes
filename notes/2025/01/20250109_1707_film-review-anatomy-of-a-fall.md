# Film review — Anatomy of a Fall

Director: Justine Triet. Palme d'Or winner at Cannes 2023. Watched on a cold January evening.

A man falls from the window of his chalet and dies. The question the film pursues across two and a half hours: accident, suicide, or murder? The suspect is his German-Swiss wife Sandra, who is French, a novelist, and the film's central intelligence.

What Triet is doing is more interesting than the courtroom drama genre suggests. The trial framework is a device for examining how we construct narrative from fragmentary evidence, how the same facts yield radically different interpretations depending on the story frame you apply. The prosecution's story and the defence's story are both coherent. Neither is obviously wrong. This is not a whodunit with a solution; it's a film about epistemology.

Sandra Hüller is extraordinary — possibly the best single-actor performance I've seen in years. Her German accent in French and the slight remove it gives her functions as ambiguity made physical: you can never quite read her. The courtroom scene where she describes her marriage in clinical French while the subtitles translate what she's *actually* saying about her life is one of the more technically daring moments I've seen in mainstream drama.

The dog is the best-written animal in contemporary cinema.

The [New Yorker review by Richard Brody](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/anatomy-of-a-fall-is-a-great-legal-drama-but-also-much-more) is worth reading alongside viewing.

A film that doesn't tell you how to feel about it and trusts you to work it out. I thought about it for a week.
