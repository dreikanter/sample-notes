# January garden tasks

The "nothing to do" month that is actually quite busy if you're doing things right.

## What's happening in the garden

The garlic is continuing to grow — Solent Wight about 20cm high now, Elephant variety has caught up to about 14cm. Both looking healthy despite a hard week of frost before Christmas.

The broad beans survived the freeze in December well. They're small and low — ideal for overwintering. The plants that are too lofty at this stage tend to suffer from frost damage. These will start growing more rapidly once the days lengthen in February.

First signs of snowdrops below the apple tree. Just the tips so far — a few centimetres of pale green pushing through the leaf mulch. Always earlier than expected and always welcome.

The tulip bulbs in the front bed have not emerged yet, which is fine. They'll come up in February-March.

## Seed catalogues

Working through the [Sarah Raven catalogue](https://www.sarahraven.com) and the [Real Seeds catalogue](https://www.realseeds.co.uk). Real Seeds focuses on varieties that are open-pollinated and suitable for seed-saving — philosophically appealing. The tomato selection is good.

## Forcing

Thinking about forcing a pot of rhubarb this year. Pull the old pot over the crown in January, and by March you get pale, sweet forced rhubarb that's quite different from the outdoor crop. Need to buy a pot or a terracotta forcer.
