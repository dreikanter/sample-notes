# Reading notes — Invisible cities

Finished Calvino's *Invisible Cities* last night. Bought it on a whim after seeing it mentioned in an essay about architecture and fiction — the essay itself was mediocre but the recommendation was good.

The structure is remarkable: Marco Polo describes cities to Kublai Khan, each city named after a woman and organized by theme — cities of memory, of desire, of signs, of the dead. No city description is longer than two or three pages. The Khan occasionally pushes back, suspects Marco is describing the same city (Venice) each time, and Marco neither confirms nor denies this.

What stays with me is the city of Leonia, which redefines itself daily by the things it throws out. The excess of consumption as identity. Calvino wrote this in 1972 and it reads as contemporary as ever.

Also Valdrada, built on a lake, so every act is performed twice — once above the surface, once reflected below. The reflection, Calvino notes, is always more resentful and harder.

The book sits in an interesting space between philosophy, poetry, and fiction. It is not trying to tell a story in the conventional sense. It is more like a sustained meditation on the relationship between a place and its name, between memory and geography.
