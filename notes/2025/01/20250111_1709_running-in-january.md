# Running in January — cold weather notes

Picked up running again after a six-week gap over December (social occasions, weather, excuses). First run back was harder than expected — the legs remember but the lungs apparently do not.

## Current state

Three runs this week: 4km, 5km, 4km. All easy pace, no pressure on times. The goal for January is just to re-establish the habit before thinking about any progression. The [Couch to 5K approach](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/running-and-aerobic-exercises/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/) is good for returning runners too — treating it as if you're building from scratch prevents the ego error of going too fast too soon and injuring yourself in week two.

## Cold weather logistics

The main adjustment for winter running: dressing in layers that work for the actual temperature, not the temperature you feel standing still. The rule of thumb I've seen: dress for 10°C warmer than the actual temperature, because you'll generate that much heat within five minutes.

What I'm wearing at 3–5°C:
- Base layer: lightweight merino long-sleeve
- Mid layer: light running jacket with a bit of wind resistance
- Tights rather than shorts
- Running gloves — hands get cold faster than anything else
- Hat sometimes — only if it's below 2°C

## Ice

Ice is the real winter running risk. The canal towpath that I normally use is safe — packed earth. The pavements in the residential streets near home can be icy after overnight frost, especially in shadow. I've moved to the park for icy days because grass doesn't freeze the same way.

## Mental aspect

Running in the cold is psychologically harder to start and considerably easier once you're moving. The first five minutes are unpleasant; the rest is fine. The [Runner's World winter motivation piece](https://www.runnersworld.com/uk/training/a773339/winter-running-motivation/) makes the point: the hardest part is always putting on the shoes.

January running: unglamorous, necessary, quietly satisfying.
