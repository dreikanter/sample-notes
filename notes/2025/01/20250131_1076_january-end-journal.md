# January end journal

The month closed with an unusual cold snap — three consecutive nights below minus eight, which is rare here. The radiator in the hallway started knocking again around 2 a.m., so I stuffed a folded magazine under the bracket. Solved it for now.

Spent most of the afternoon finishing the ledger for Q4 freelance invoices. I keep meaning to move to a proper tool but the plain spreadsheet still does the job faster than anything with a login screen. One client was 47 days overdue; sent a second notice. These things always resolve eventually but the waiting is irritating.

Read about half of a long essay on the psychology of procrastination at [Nautilus](https://nautil.us/the-real-reasons-we-procrastinate-236897/) — the argument that procrastination is primarily an emotion-regulation failure rather than a time-management failure is convincing. It explains why better scheduling rarely fixes the habit on its own.

In the evening I rewatched the first episode of a documentary series on urban water infrastructure. Cities spending billions on century-old pipe systems is one of those slow-moving crises that barely makes news. The episode covered Philadelphia's 1800s brick sewers still in active use.

Noticed the coffee plant on the windowsill has two new leaves, bright green against the older waxy ones. It won't ever produce enough cherries to roast, but it's a decent houseplant. See earlier notes on roasting at home [[20250120_1010]].

Tomorrow I want to start clearing the desk corner that became a staging area for everything since November. It's reached critical mass.
