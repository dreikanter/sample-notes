# Coffee tasting log — January batch

Ran a proper cupping session today with three bags I picked up from Onyx Coffee Lab last week. The format was simple: 8.25g of grounds per 150ml water, four-minute steep, then break the crust and evaluate.

**Ethiopia Yirgacheffe (natural process):** Blueberry hits first, almost like a fruit punch. The body is syrupy rather than bright. Aftertaste lingers with dark chocolate and a faint floral note. Would not brew this as espresso — the sweetness would get muddled.

**Colombia Huila (washed):** Clean and classic. Brown sugar sweetness, mild citrus acidity, no surprises. Good everyday cup. Brewed well as a V60 with a 1:15 ratio and water at 93°C.

**Guatemala Huehuetenango (honey process):** Somewhere between the two others. A bit of stone fruit, mild brightness, medium body. Better than I expected for a honey process from this region, which I usually find polarizing.

For home brewing references I keep coming back to James Hoffmann's method descriptions at [https://www.jameshoffmann.co.uk](https://www.jameshoffmann.co.uk) — his cupping protocol is clearer than the SCA official one for casual use.

Next session I want to try the same three beans as cold brew to see how the flavor profiles shift. The Yirgacheffe especially should be interesting given how forward the fruit is when hot.

Total cost for all three bags: around $58 before shipping. Reasonable for specialty.
