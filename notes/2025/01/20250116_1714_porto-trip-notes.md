# Porto trip — day notes

Arrived. Booked a small guesthouse in the Bonfim neighbourhood, recommended in [Porto travel blog Devour Tours](https://devourtours.com/blog/porto-neighbourhoods/) as having more character and fewer tourists than the Ribeira waterfront.

## Day 1

Arrived late afternoon. Walked the Ribeira without a plan. The light at 4:30pm in January is low and golden and the azulejo tiles on the buildings catch it in a way that makes everything look slightly magical. The waterfront is predictably touristy — tour groups, restaurant touts — but the streets immediately behind it are quieter and more interesting.

Ate at a counter restaurant near the guesthouse. Caldo verde, grilled squid, a glass of house vinho verde. The squid was simply done: just oil, lemon, garlic, and the heat of the grill. Perfect. Cost for two with wine: €32.

## Day 2

Walked the Douro riverside from the Ribeira to the Foz do Douro where the river meets the sea. About 7km. The estuary in January is wide and grey and very beautiful. The abandoned boat hulls rusting on the south bank. Fishing boats still working.

Found a pastelaria that has been open since 1933 — dark wood, marble counters, older clientele. Had coffee and pastel de nata. The pastéis de nata debate (Belém in Lisbon versus everywhere else) misses the point: good ones are good regardless of postcode, and this was very good.

## Day 3

Took the train to Pinhão in the Douro Valley. One of the great railway journeys: the line runs along the river through terraced vineyards. The vines are bare in January but the landscape remains spectacular.

Tasted wines at a small quinta that doesn't have a website — found it by asking the guesthouse owner. Drank a white Douro (a revelation — I'd only known the reds and ports) and an aged tawny port with almonds.

This is what the trip was supposed to be.
