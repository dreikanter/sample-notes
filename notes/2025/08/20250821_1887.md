# Observations — the late summer light

The light in late August has a quality I haven't found a name for. It's not the sharp white light of June or the soft gold of early autumn. It's something in between — a fullness, a heaviness, as if the atmosphere is more saturated than usual.

The shadows are longer than in July. At 4pm the garden is already half in shade. The light catches the underside of the leaves when the wind moves them, which is something it does in spring too but seems more pronounced now.

I was sitting on the back steps for an hour this afternoon reading. The sun was on the right side of the garden wall, not on me. The heat was still there — around 24°C — but without the intensity. The tomato plants in the raised bed were casting their own shadows inward, and the skin of the riper tomatoes had a translucent quality where the light passed through from behind.

There were a few small white butterflies over the nasturtiums — small whites, probably. The fennel at the back of the bed had gone to flower weeks ago and the seedheads are fully formed now, pale and lacy. I let it go intentionally — fennel seeds for cooking through winter.

A wood pigeon was roosting in the ash tree at the end of the neighbor's garden, entirely still in the heat. Wood pigeons have a specific quality of stillness that other birds don't — they sit as if they have decided nothing more is required of them.

Being still in late afternoon in August is one of those things that is free and entirely available and that I don't do often enough.
