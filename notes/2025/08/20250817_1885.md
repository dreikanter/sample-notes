# Concert — string quartet, Schubert Death and the Maiden

Went to a chamber music concert last night at the old church on Regent Street — the acoustics there are excellent for small ensembles. The program was Schubert's String Quartet No. 14 in D minor, "Death and the Maiden," played by a young quartet I hadn't seen before.

The quartet is about 40 minutes of Schubert at his most serious. It's named for the theme he borrowed from his own song "Der Tod und das Mädchen" — used in the slow movement, a theme and variations that becomes by turns tender, violent, and exhausted.

The performers were very good. The first violinist was the obvious talent — her tone in the slow movement's first variation was luminous. The ensemble coordination was less settled: there were a couple of moments in the first movement where the second violin and viola seemed slightly out of sync, though they recovered quickly.

What I keep thinking about is the slow movement. Schubert was ill when he wrote this piece — he was 26 and probably already had syphilis. The variations move through the theme without obviously resolving it; each one illuminates a different facet. The final variation is a march that sounds like resignation rather than triumph.

The ending of the whole quartet — the Presto finale — was played almost too fast, but that speed creates a kind of vertiginous effect that works dramatically. The audience was silent for a full five seconds after the last note before anyone moved.
