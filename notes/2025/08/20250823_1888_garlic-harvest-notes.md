# Garlic harvest — curing and storage notes

The garlic came out of the ground in mid-June but I've been meaning to write up the storage notes properly. Three months in, here's how it's going.

**Curing**

After lifting, the bulbs were laid in a single layer on a slatted shelf in the shed — good air circulation, out of direct sun, warm. Cured for four weeks. The outer papery skins dried to a crackling papery texture; the stems dried from green to tan.

After curing, I plaited 8 bulbs together to hang in the kitchen (mostly decorative — garlic in a plait dries further over time and is usable for months). The remainder went into a mesh bag hung in a cool, dark cupboard.

**Three months in**

The plaited kitchen garlic: six of eight bulbs still firm and fully usable. The other two have started to show a green shoot beginning — they're at the edge of their life but still usable if I use the cloves soon. The flavor is still good, possibly even more mellow than fresh.

The stored mesh bag: all 10 remaining bulbs are firm. Storing cooler has preserved them better.

**Lessons**

Temperature matters more than I thought. The kitchen plait was exposed to more temperature variation (oven heat, etc.) than the cupboard storage. For longevity, the cool dark storage is clearly better.

The variety matters for keeping quality. Mine was a softneck variety ("Albigensian Wight") which typically keeps longer than hardneck types.
