# Late August bittern — finally

Finally got a bittern at Heron's Fen. After five months of hearing "possible boom" and coming up empty, I had a genuine sighting this morning.

I was in the main hide at 6:45am. The light was still low. I was watching a pair of gadwall near the island when I noticed something brown and heron-like moving in the reeds on the far side of the scrape. It walked — and bitterns walk with a very particular deliberate quality, almost mechanical — along the reed edge for about thirty seconds before disappearing back into the reeds.

In binoculars: the buff-brown streaking was unmistakable, the dagger bill, the way it froze with the bill pointed upward when it felt noticed. The camouflage is extraordinary — once it stopped moving and pointed upward into the reeds, it simply vanished even though I knew roughly where it was.

I stood very still for another 20 minutes hoping it would reemerge. It didn't, but the sighting was solid enough — perhaps 90 seconds in the open in total, multiple clear views. I'm confident.

I've been watching this reserve since March [[20250619_1853]] and [[20250807_1880]], and this was the bird I most wanted to see. They're rare enough that a local sighting is genuinely notable — probably fewer than 50 pairs breed in the country.

Went home, had breakfast, felt quietly good about the morning.
