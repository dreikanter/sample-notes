# Autumn wader watching — early August

Four visits to Heron's Fen this week, early morning each time. The wader passage is properly underway now — better variety than I expected for early August.

**Best sightings**

Green sandpiper: the standout of the week. Three individuals, all at the muddy edge of the main scrape on Monday morning. The green sheen on the upperparts and the stark white rump visible in flight make it unmistakable. Their call — a loud, explosive three-note whistle — sent them up twice when I moved clumsily.

Greenshank: two on Wednesday, feeding in the shallower water, constantly moving. Greenshanks are nervous birds, quick to flush. Better views after I stopped moving and waited.

Little ringed plover: one, consistently present at the same spot near the hide for three visits. Smaller than a ringed plover, faster movements, the prominent yellow eye-ring visible at moderate range.

Ruff: a juvenile in fresh plumage, dull and scaly-looking before it molts into adult plumage. Ruff are variable in size — this one looked noticeably larger than the dunlin nearby.

**Still wanted**

Wood sandpiper (possible, seen here in previous Augusts), curlew sandpiper (would be a county first for me), and the perpetually elusive bittern.

The passage peaks around late August and September. Planning to increase visits.
