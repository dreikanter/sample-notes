# Plum jam — first batch of the year

The market had local damson plums last Saturday, small and tart and beautiful. Bought 2kg with the intention of jam and here are the notes.

Damsons are high in pectin — you can make jam without added pectin if you use a good ratio of sugar. The traditional ratio is 1:1 sugar to fruit by weight. I used slightly less, 850g sugar to 1kg prepped damsons (stoned weight), which resulted in a jam that set firmly but not rubbery.

**Process**

Stone the damsons — this takes time and patience. A damson stone comes out more cleanly if you halve the fruit along its natural seam. Combine with sugar in a wide, heavy pan. Let macerate for an hour so the sugar draws out juice. Bring to a boil, stirring until sugar dissolves, then boil hard — no stirring — until setting point.

Setting point test: place a small plate in the freezer before you start. Drop a small amount of jam on it, let it cool for 30 seconds, then push with a finger. If it wrinkles, it's set. The jam was at setting point in about 18 minutes — damsons are efficient.

Yield: four 340ml jars.

**Flavor**

The tartness of damsons survives the jam-making process much better than dessert plums. The jam is intense, slightly astringent, deeply flavored — excellent with sharp cheese, good on sourdough with butter.

**Note for next time**

Stoning damsons is the bottleneck. Do it in advance the night before or budget extra time.
