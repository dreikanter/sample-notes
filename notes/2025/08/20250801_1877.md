# August begins — seasonal notes

August is the month where summer becomes aware of its own ending. The light has been changing for weeks — the angle lower in the evening, the golden hour longer and earlier — but August is when you can feel it as something other than astronomy.

The swifts left a week ago. They were over the streets until late July, screaming in groups at rooftop level, and then one morning they were gone. They winter in sub-Saharan Africa and won't be back until May. Their departure is an annual marker; I register it every year.

The garden is at peak production and starting to slow simultaneously. The tomatoes are still coming but the rate is tapering. The beans are becoming slightly coarser — still edible, not as tender as they were in July. The basil is in its element, producing more than I can use.

I've been making large batches of pesto — basil, pine nuts, good Parmesan, garlic, olive oil — and freezing in ice cube trays, then storing the cubes in bags. This is genuinely good through January: better than any commercial alternative.

The evenings close in noticeably at this time of year. A month ago we had daylight until past 9:30. Now it's 8:45 and changing fast — about two minutes per day.

What I like about August: the quality of the heat, which is dry and full rather than humid. The abundance of local fruit — plums are appearing at the market. The particular gold that appears in the late afternoon light.
