# Late summer garden

Third year with the raised beds. The tomatoes finally performed this year — two Cherokee Purples and one Brandywine, plus a row of Sungolds that produced so heavily from July onward that I ran out of uses for them and started giving bags away to neighbors.

The basil experiment worked: planted it densely between the tomato rows (six plants across a 4-foot bed) and harvested the outer leaves every five days to keep it from bolting. Lost one plant to what I think was fusarium wilt — stem turned brown from the soil line up over about four days. Pulled it immediately and the others were fine.

Cucumber failure. Started three plants from seed in May; they germinated well but two were taken out by squash vine borers in late June before I noticed the signs. The surviving one produced exactly seven cucumbers. Next year: row cover from the start, check stems weekly in June.

Kale continues to be indestructible. The Tuscan (lacinato) variety I planted in March is still going in late August. I've been making a simple preparation — torn leaves massaged with olive oil and salt, roasted at 375°F until the edges crisp, eaten warm or room temperature. The simplicity is the point.

Cover crop plan for September: winter rye and hairy vetch mix in the two beds that had tomatoes. The nitrogen fixation from the vetch should help next year. Seed source and mix ratios: https://www.johnnyseeds.com/c/cover-crops.html
