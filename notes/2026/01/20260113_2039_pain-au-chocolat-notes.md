# Pain au chocolat notes

The croissant dough from [[20260104_2031]], applied to pain au chocolat.

Same dough formula, same lamination process. The differences:

**Shaping**: Rectangles instead of triangles. Cut 4×5 inch pieces. Place one bar of dark chocolate near one edge, roll tightly to enclose, place seam-side down. Some recipes call for two bars; I tried both and preferred one bar of 68% chocolate. Two made them too sweet and structurally unstable.

**Chocolate**: Used Valrhona 68% baking bars cut into batons. The specific chocolate matters more than I expected — a previous test with grocery-store chips didn't work because chips contain stabilizers that prevent proper melting.

**Proofing**: 2.5 hours at room temperature. They should jiggle when you shake the tray but not slosh. Getting this right is the same challenge as croissants, possibly harder because the seam can open if they're under-proofed.

**Bake**: Identical to croissants. Egg wash twice — once before proofing, once just before the oven.

**Result**: Better than I expected given they were my first attempt. The chocolate sets firm as they cool, which is correct — you want slight resistance before it yields. The lamination showed clearly. Some asymmetry in the roll on a few, but that's technique that will improve.

Verdict: these are achievable at home if you've got the croissant dough working. Don't attempt before that point.

Reference for chocolate selection: https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-chocolate-for-baking
