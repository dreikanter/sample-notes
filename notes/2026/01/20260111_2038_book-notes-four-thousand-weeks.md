# Book notes: Four Thousand Weeks

Oliver Burkeman, *Four Thousand Weeks: Time Management for Mortals* (2021). Listed in my [[20251229_2020]] year-end reading log. Coming back to the notes now.

## Central argument

Four thousand weeks is roughly eighty years of life. The book argues that most "productivity" thinking is in denial about finitude — that the fantasy of Getting It All Done is not just impossible but actively harmful, because it stops you from choosing what to actually do with the time you have.

## Passages worth returning to

> "The real problem isn't that we have too little time. It's that we've unconsciously adopted a 'rendering unto Caesar' attitude toward it: we treat time as a resource to be managed, in service of future goals, rather than the very fabric of our lives."

> "You will never reach the end of your to-do list. This is not a failure of productivity. It's an implication of having any capacity for desire at all."

On the "efficiency trap": getting better at email generates more email. Getting faster at tasks generates more tasks to fill the time. The ceiling rises with you.

## What I actually changed

Stopped making daily task lists longer than five items. Accept that most things on a longer list don't get done and exist mainly to generate guilt.

Started asking: if I only get three things done today, which three? Then do those first.

## Criticism

The book occasionally substitutes eloquent resignation for useful direction. Some chapters feel like they're just saying "stop trying so hard" without accounting for real external pressures.
