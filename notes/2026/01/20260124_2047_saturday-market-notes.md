# Saturday market notes

First time at the winter farmers market this year. Smaller than the summer version — maybe a third the vendors — but the quality skews higher because only the serious ones come out in January.

**What I bought**

- Two bunches of lacinato kale from the same farm I buy from all summer
- A dozen eggs, brown, mixed sizes, the way eggs look when they're not sorted by machine
- A small wheel of aged goat cheese — firmer than fresh chèvre, rindless, slightly acidic
- Dried ancho chiles (three of them; a vendor I'd never seen before)
- Apple cider from an orchard about an hour north

**What I didn't buy but noted**

A vendor selling preserved lemon in various states — whole preserved in salt, sliced in oil, pureed. I've been meaning to make my own (they're trivially easy but take a month). Bought nothing; came home and started a jar with some Meyer lemons I had.

**On the eggs**: There's a meaningful difference between these eggs and supermarket eggs, and I think about whether it's real or placebo. The yolks are darker — more orange than yellow, which does indicate more xanthophyll from a more varied diet. Whether that translates to better flavor is contested. But cooking with them feels different. I'll accept that this might be partly irrational.

**Lunch made from today's haul**: Kale braised in the cider with garlic and anchovy, topped with the goat cheese crumbled over the top. The kind of thing that makes January tolerable.
