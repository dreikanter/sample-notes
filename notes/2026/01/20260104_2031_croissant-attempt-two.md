# Croissant attempt two

Attempt two. Kitchen at 65°F (opened windows, January helped). Butter kept at 59–62°F throughout lamination.

**Changes from attempt one**

- Rested dough in the freezer for 20 minutes between each fold (instead of fridge)
- Used European-style butter (84% fat, Plugrá) instead of standard 80%
- Rolled thinner: 3mm final thickness instead of the 4-5mm I had before
- Cut triangles larger: 8-inch height

**Result**

Visible layers. Not bakery-perfect — some puffed unevenly and one batch over-proofed while I was dealing with something else — but the honeycomb interior was there when I cut into them. The exterior shattered instead of compressing. The flavor had that faintly sour, buttery complexity that distinguishes a real croissant from a roll.

The ones that proofed correctly were genuinely good.

**What I learned**

Temperature is everything. The lamination only works if the butter stays plastic but not soft — it should bend without shattering (too cold) or squishing (too warm). The range is maybe 10°F wide. In a warm kitchen in July this would be very hard.

**Next steps**

Pain au chocolat variation once I'm consistent on plain croissants.
