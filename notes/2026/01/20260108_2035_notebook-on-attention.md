# Notebook: on attention

Reading Simone Weil's essay "Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies with a View to the Love of God" (available at https://wccm.org/publications/meditations/attention/) on a recommendation that's been sitting in my notes for two years.

The essay is about attention as a practice — not focus in the productivity sense, but the kind of receptive, patient waiting that Weil calls "negative effort." The student who is really paying attention is not forcing, not striving, but making themselves available to what is there.

The counterintuitive point: effort of will is not the same as attention. You can try very hard and still not be attending. Forcing your eyes to a page is not reading. Attention requires a different posture — something closer to receptivity, willingness, even waiting.

She uses geometry problems as her example. A student who can't solve a problem, who sits with the difficulty without frustration, without forcing, is exercising a faculty that transfers. The point of school study, she argues, is not the content learned but the training of this attention.

I find this useful against the productivity-culture habit of trying to maximize throughput. More isn't always more. There's something valuable in sitting with something longer than is efficient.

This pairs with things I've been thinking about re: reading pace. See also the year-end reading notes [[20251229_2020]] — the intention this year was to read more slowly.

The essay is only a few pages. Worth the fifteen minutes.
