# January cold snap

Three days of temperatures below 15°F, which is unusual for the city. The streets do something specific in the cold: they empty faster, people walk faster when they do appear, and sound carries differently — the usual ambient noise reduced to footsteps, an occasional bus, the hiss of a heating vent.

I walked to the hardware store on Tuesday morning, about six blocks. The cold pressed against my face in a way that required active attention. Not unpleasant, exactly, but demanding. You can't be elsewhere in your head when the air is this cold.

The hardware store was warm and smelled like rubber and wood and WD-40. An older man was asking about pipe insulation. The guy behind the counter knew exactly what was needed and had an opinion about the best brand. I bought the pipe insulation I came for and a bag of ice melt I didn't know I needed until I saw it.

At home: tea, the radiator ticking to life, the building settling in the cold. I read for two hours in the afternoon. The fiddle leaf fig dropped another leaf — stress response to the cold air coming from the window. Moved it away from the glass.

These cold snaps have a quality of compression to them. The world is smaller, and smaller can be clarifying. I notice I like the apartment more in cold weather. The warmth of it feels earned.
