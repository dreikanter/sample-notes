# Film notes: Fallen Leaves

*Fallen Leaves* (2023, dir. Aki Kaurismäki). Watched on a Sunday afternoon.

Kaurismäki has been making films with the same emotional register for forty years: deadpan, tender, working-class, Finnish. *Fallen Leaves* is late Kaurismäki, which means it has the pared-down quality of someone who has removed everything he doesn't need.

Two lonely Helsinki workers (Alma Pöysti and Jussi Vatanen) meet, lose each other's number, find each other again. That's the plot. The film is seventy-six minutes.

**On the visual style**

Kaurismäki uses color the way other directors use music. The protagonist's apartment is ochre; her friend's is blue; the workplace is institutional grey. These are not chosen randomly. The colors warm as the film progresses. By the final scene, the apartment is lit differently than it was at the opening.

**On the dialogue**

Minimal, literal, funny. "What's your name?" "Holappa." "And before that?" This is Finnish humor: deadpan delivered with total seriousness. The film made me laugh several times in the way that Kaurismäki films do — not jokes, just truthful observations about human absurdity delivered without inflection.

**The radio**

Throughout the film, a radio is on, reporting the news from Ukraine. The film is set in the present; the war is background noise that the characters live alongside. This is not sentimental. It's honest about what it's like to live in 2023.

Winner of the Jury Prize at Cannes. Deserved.
