# Bolognese – the long version

Saturday project. Started at 10 AM, ate at 2 PM.

**The meat**

Equal parts beef (chuck, coarsely ground) and pork (shoulder, coarsely ground). The butcher ground it fresh, which is different from prepackaged. The fat percentage is higher and the texture is coarser. 500g total.

**Soffritto**

One onion, two carrots, three stalks celery — all finely diced, not food processed. Cooked in butter over medium-low heat for 20 minutes until completely soft and starting to turn golden. This is the foundation.

**Building**

Added the meat, broke it up, cooked over medium-high until no pink remained. Added one cup whole milk, reduced to almost nothing (this is the Hazan technique — milk enriches and tenderizes). Then 1.5 cups dry white wine, same process.

Then: one 28-oz can whole San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand. One cup water. Reduce to lowest possible heat, partially covered, and cook for 3 hours minimum. Stir every 20–30 minutes. It should barely simmer.

At 3 hours: the fat has separated visibly, the sauce has reduced to a rich, thick paste with pockets of oil. Season with salt; it needs more than you expect.

**Served with**: fresh tagliatelle (see [[20260201_2050]]) cooked until just done, finished in the sauce with a splash of pasta water.

**Result**: the canonical version of the thing. Worth making once to understand what the shortcuts are approximating.

Reference: Marcella Hazan's version in *Essentials of Classic Italian Cooking*: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/166805/marcella-hazans-essentials-of-classic-italian-cooking/
