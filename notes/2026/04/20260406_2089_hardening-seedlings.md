# Hardening off seedlings

Starting the hardening-off process this week. Seedlings have been indoors their whole lives; they need gradual introduction to outdoor conditions.

**Why hardening is necessary**

Indoor seedlings grow under constant light, no wind, no temperature variation, and high humidity. Outdoors: direct sun is much more intense than grow lights, wind causes physical stress, and temperature swings are real. A seedling moved directly outside will typically sunscald, wilt, or simply die.

**Protocol**

Week 1: 1–2 hours outside in partial shade, sheltered from wind. Morning hours only.
Week 2: 2–4 hours in increasing sun.
Week 3: 4–6 hours including direct sun.
Week 4: full days outside, bring in only if frost expected.
After week 4: plant out permanently (assuming past frost date).

**What I watch for**

- Purple tinge on leaves (cold stress — too cold, reduce outdoor time or protect)
- White or tan patches on leaves (sunscald — too much direct sun too soon)
- Persistent wilting (wind stress — more shelter needed)

**Equipment**

A cold frame (a bottomless box with a clear lid) would make this much easier — essentially an outdoor nursery that buffers temperature and wind. On the list for next year.
