# Film notes — Conclave (2024)

Watched this on a Tuesday evening, which felt appropriate — it's not exactly a Saturday night film.

Edward Berger directed *All Quiet on the Western Front* (2022), and some of that film's controlled tension carries over here. *Conclave* is an adaptation of Robert Harris's novel about a papal election following the sudden death of the Pope. Ralph Fiennes plays Cardinal Lawrence, tasked with overseeing the conclave, who discovers unsettling information about one of the candidates during the process.

What works: the production design is meticulous. The film understands that ritual is a form of argument — the sequences inside the Sistine Chapel are shot with genuine gravity, not as spectacle. Fiennes is very good in a role that requires him to convey private doubt in a context where public performance is everything. Isabella Rossellini has a smaller role but uses it precisely.

What is less convincing: the plotting in the final act accelerates in ways that feel more like thriller mechanics than character logic. The twist is well-constructed but slightly at odds with the more measured tone of the earlier two-thirds.

Worth watching if you have any interest in institutions — how they maintain themselves, what they suppress, who is allowed to hold power and on what terms. The film is interested in faith as a pragmatic and political category, not a mystical one.

Roger Ebert's review archive doesn't have Berger coverage obviously, but RogerEbert.com ran a strong review: https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/conclave-2024

Compare with the institutional anxiety in Ishiguro — Stevens's problem in *The Remains of the Day* (see [[20260131_1109]]) is structurally similar to Lawrence's: loyalty to an institution that may not deserve it.
