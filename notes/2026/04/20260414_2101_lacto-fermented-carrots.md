# Lacto-fermented carrots

New item from the March backlog review (see [[20260330_2082]]): try lacto-fermentation for vegetables. Starting with carrots because they're forgiving and the result is immediately useful.

**Method**

Lacto-fermentation is salt preservation. The salt creates an environment where Lactobacillus bacteria (present on the vegetable surface) thrive and lactic acid is produced. No vinegar, no heating — just salt and time and beneficial bacteria.

**Formula**

2% brine: 20g salt per liter of water. I made 500ml brine (10g salt).

Carrots: peeled and cut into sticks. Added to a 1-quart jar with:
- 1-inch piece of fresh ginger, sliced thin
- A few slices of fresh turmeric (optional but available)
- 3 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
- 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes

Packed the carrots in tightly (they need to stay submerged), poured brine over to cover, weighted down with a small zip-lock bag of brine.

**Fermentation**

Day 1–2: room temperature (68–72°F), loosely covered. Check daily.
Day 3: bubbling should be visible. The brine gets slightly cloudy. This is right.
Day 4–7: taste daily from here.

**At day 5**

Sour, crunchy, faintly fizzy. The ginger comes through cleanly. The carrots are lightly soft on the outside but still have snap. Moved to the refrigerator to slow fermentation.

Better than I expected for a first attempt. Will try cabbage next (toward kimchi territory).
