# Miso soup variations

Finally made miso soup from a proper dashi base rather than the shortcut version (dashi powder dissolved in water). The difference is meaningful.

**Kombu dashi (cold steep)**

Place one 4-inch piece of kombu in 4 cups cold water. Refrigerate overnight. In the morning: remove kombu (don't discard — it can be used in a second steep or eaten). The water is now a light, savory, mineral-tasting dashi.

**Ichiban dashi (cooked)**

Bring kombu + water to 140°F (don't boil — it makes dashi bitter). Hold for 30 minutes. Remove kombu. Add a large handful of katsuobushi (bonito flakes), simmer 2 minutes, strain immediately. This is the gold-standard dashi — layered umami.

**Making the soup**

Heat dashi to just below simmering. Dissolve miso in a ladle of dashi first (prevents lumps), then add back. Never boil miso — it kills the live cultures and dulls the flavor.

**Variations I've tried**

*Classic*: silken tofu cubed, dried wakame (rehydrate separately, add at end), scallion.

*Winter version*: potato and leek, white miso (sweeter, less fermented).

*Mushroom*: fresh shiitake, enoki, white miso. The mushrooms add a second layer of umami.

*Breakfast miso*: very simple — just the dashi, red miso, a slice of daikon, a soft-boiled egg alongside.

**Miso types**

- Shiro (white): mild, sweet, short fermentation
- Shinshu (yellow): middle ground
- Aka (red): robust, salty, long fermentation
