# Reading notes: Borges, Labyrinths

Reading *Labyrinths* (1962), the New Directions translation. Picking stories at random, which seems appropriate.

## "The Garden of Forking Paths"

A Chinese spy in England during WWI, a spy thriller and a metaphysical puzzle. The "garden of forking paths" turns out to be a novel written by a fictional ancestor — a novel that is not a labyrinth in space but in time. Every time a character faces a choice, the novel follows all outcomes simultaneously. Every fork is taken. The garden is branching time.

The concept anticipates the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics (which Borges couldn't have been reading in 1941 — this was before Everett's 1957 paper) but also simply describes possibility space in a way that makes the physics feel more like metaphysics.

The plot machine is elegant: the spy's mission requires him to communicate a name, but he can't use conventional means. His solution is to murder someone whose name is the message. The news story of the murder will carry the name to those who know what to look for. A killing as a telegram.

## "The Library of Babel"

The universe is a library containing all possible 410-page books in all possible combinations of letters. It has therefore already been written somewhere — every truth, every lie, every translation of every truth. The library is infinite (or at least vast enough to be practically so) and the librarians spend their lives searching for the books that would explain the rest.

The story is a meditation on information, infinity, and the painful specificity of meaning. Any sufficiently large library contains every answer and therefore provides no answers.

The best five pages about meaning I've read recently.
