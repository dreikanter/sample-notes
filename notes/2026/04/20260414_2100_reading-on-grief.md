# Re-reading On Grief and Grieving

Kübler-Ross and Kessler, *On Grief and Grieving: Finding the Meaning of Grief Through the Five Stages of Loss* (2005). Listed in the year-end reading log [[20251229_2020]]. A re-read, the second time, prompted by a conversation this week.

## What the book actually says about the five stages

The "five stages" (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) are widely misunderstood as a linear progression, a checklist. Kübler-Ross and Kessler explicitly reject this in the book. The stages are *not* orderly, not sequential, not required. Some appear; some don't. You can cycle back. You can skip. The framework is not prescriptive; it's descriptive of a landscape.

The most important clarification: the stages describe not just grief after death but grief in any significant loss — relationship endings, health, identity, the life you thought you were going to have.

## What I find most useful

The chapter on depression as distinct from sadness. Depression in grief is not a malfunction; it's appropriate sadness at scale. It's the weight of actually reckoning with what is gone. Trying to talk someone out of grief-depression with positivity is not help.

The chapter on finding meaning (Kessler's addition to Kübler-Ross's original work). Not "finding meaning in loss" in the easy sense — not "it happened for a reason." Something more honest: what can be made from this, without diminishing what it was.

## On re-reading

The book reads differently at different times in life. This reading was more specific in application than the last. That's the sign of a book that's actually true about something.
