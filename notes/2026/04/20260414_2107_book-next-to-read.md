# Next book candidates

The Borges is winding down (nearly through *Labyrinths*). Starting to think about what's next.

**Fiction options**

*The Passenger* by Cormac McCarthy — his last novel, published 2022. Long, difficult, probably worth it. I've been avoiding it because I need to be in the right mood for McCarthy's particular brand of bleak.

*All Fours* by Miranda July — completely different register, contemporary, widely praised. A midlife crisis novel narrated by an artist who stops halfway through a road trip. Reviews suggest it's funny and strange and formally unusual.

*The God of Small Things* by Arundhati Roy — I've never read it. A gap. The prose is supposed to be extraordinary.

**Nonfiction options**

*The Dawn of Everything* by Graeber and Wengrow — a reassessment of early human history arguing that the familiar story (small bands → agriculture → hierarchy) is mostly wrong. Long, dense, fascinating premise.

*How Buildings Learn* by Stewart Brand — on the way buildings change over time and how they're designed to resist or accommodate change. Seems like it should be boring and apparently isn't.

**The shortlist**

Leaning toward *The Dawn of Everything* because the Harari book (see [[20260118_2043]]) made me want to interrogate its premises, and Graeber/Wengrow are doing exactly that.

*The Passenger* as the next novel.
