# Notebook: on slowness

There's a chapter in Milan Kundera's *Slowness* that frames the problem precisely: the man on the motorcycle, going fast, wants to forget; the man on the horse, going slowly, can remember. Speed and memory are in opposition. The faster you move, the less you retain.

I've been thinking about this in relation to the week I just had. By any objective measure, the week was full — work shipped, garden planted, cooking done, runs completed, notes written. But I have a vague sense that I moved through it too fast. That I was efficient rather than present.

The distinction matters. Efficiency is throughput. Presence is contact — the actual experience of what's happening, rather than the completion of the task.

The two slownesses I've managed to establish: morning coffee without a screen, and the weekly neighborhood walk without headphones. These are not dramatically slow, but they're slow enough. They function as stops in the week — places where speed isn't the point.

I want more of these. Not in a way that becomes its own project (ironic), but as a recognition that the weeks when I'm most functional are the ones with more of these stops.
