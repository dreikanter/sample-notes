# On writing notes

A meta-note. I've been using this note system for about four months now, and some things have become clear.

**What works**

Writing notes as if for a future reader — even if that reader is just future me who has forgotten. The discipline of actually explaining something rather than just jotting "see X" forces understanding. A note I can't explain is something I don't actually understand.

Cross-references work when they're meaningful. Linking [[20260201_2050]] to [[20260405_2088]] because they're both things I made this month is noise. Linking the fresh pasta note to the Bolognese note because the pasta was the vehicle for the sauce is useful.

Short notes are fine. Not everything needs 300 words. The sourdough log entry and the weekly reviews are useful at 150 words.

**What doesn't work**

Notes that are too abstract. I have a few notes from early in the year that say things like "think more about X" without any actual thinking. These are useless. A note is an output, not an intention.

Too many tags. I started with a controlled vocabulary of 20 or so; I now have more variation and it's harder to filter. Fixing this would require a pass through all the notes.

**The external links rule**

I added one external link per note as a constraint. This turns out to be useful — it forces a moment of "where does this information come from / what's the primary source?" and produces notes that are less self-referential.

**Net assessment**

The habit is working. It takes maybe 15–20 minutes per note. The total body of notes after a few months is actually useful to refer back to.
