# Winter running notes

I run in winter. Not enthusiastically, and not in the actual cold (below 20°F, I'm on the treadmill), but winter outdoor running has a quality that summer running doesn't.

**What works in cold weather running**

Layers over bulk. A thin base layer that wicks, a midlayer only if below 30°F, a wind-resistant outer shell. The mistake I made for years was over-dressing — you warm up faster than you think and the sweating starts early. Dress for 20° warmer than the temperature, or dress so you feel slightly cold for the first two minutes.

Hands and ears matter more than you'd expect given that they're small. A thin running glove and a headband covering the ears are non-negotiable below 35°F. Below 25°F: heavier gloves.

Footing on snow: the first few seconds on a slick patch matter. Shorten the stride, lower the center of gravity, accept slower pace. Microspike attachments (like Yaktrax) are genuinely useful on icy paths.

**The psychological element**

Winter running requires a different internal negotiation than summer running. Summer: the hardest part is putting on shoes. Winter: the hardest part is leaving the warmth. But the reward is also larger — fresh cold air, emptier paths, the city in a different register.

The light returning in February is visible on morning runs. Sunrise is earlier every week now. This is motivating in a concrete, not-metaphorical way.

**Current weekly distance**: 25 miles, down from 35 in October. Sustainable through winter.
