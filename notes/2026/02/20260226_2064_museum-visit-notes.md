# Museum visit notes

Spent three hours at the museum on a grey Thursday afternoon. Free admission on Thursday evenings (starts at 5 PM); arrived at 5:30.

The current special exhibition is on Japanese woodblock prints from the Edo period. I know very little about woodblock prints going in — this is the right frame, I think. Walking through knowing nothing lets you encounter things directly.

**What struck me**

The Hiroshige *53 Stations of the Tokaido* series in the second room. These are travel prints from the 1830s, depicting post-stations along the main road between Edo and Kyoto. What I didn't expect: the weather. Rain in at least six of the prints — diagonal lines suggesting downpour, the figures hunched under hats, the landscape reduced to blue-grey wash. Hiroshige was obsessed with transient atmospheric conditions.

One print showed a sudden rainstorm on a bridge at Shono: dark sky, crooked rain lines, travelers running. The composition is asymmetrical in a way that feels modern. This was 1833.

Also: a late Hokusai (not from The Wave series) of a winter mountain with sparse figures. The mountain is mostly negative space. The figures are tiny. The scale of the mountain to the figures is the entire point.

**Room I usually avoid**: contemporary photography. Still avoid it. Unchanged.

**On Thursday evening crowds**: smaller, more intentional. People who came with purpose, not to tick a box. Better company.
