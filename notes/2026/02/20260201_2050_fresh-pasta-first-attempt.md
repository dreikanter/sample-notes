# Fresh pasta – first serious attempt

February cooking project (see [[20260101_2026]]): fresh pasta. I've made it before but never correctly. This time I'm doing it right.

**The dough**

Classic Bolognese ratio: 100g 00 flour per egg yolk. Made 300g flour with 3 whole eggs + 1 yolk. No water. No oil. Just flour and eggs, worked together until smooth and elastic, then rested wrapped in plastic for 30 minutes at room temperature.

The resting period matters — it lets the gluten relax so the dough can be rolled thin without springing back. I skipped this step in previous attempts. You can feel the difference: post-rest dough is pliable and slightly tacky; pre-rest dough fights you.

**Rolling**

Used a pasta machine borrowed from a neighbor. Started at setting 1 (widest), folded the sheet in thirds and rolled again twice at this setting to develop more gluten structure. Then progressively thinner: 2, 3, 4, 5. Stopped at 5 for tagliatelle — thin enough to be silky, thick enough to have some texture.

**Cutting and cooking**

Floured the sheets, folded loosely, cut into ribbons about 6mm wide. Fresh pasta cooks in 2–3 minutes, much faster than dried. Salt the water aggressively — "like the sea" is the cliché but it's accurate.

**Served with**: browned butter, fresh sage, parmesan. Not subtle; entirely correct.
