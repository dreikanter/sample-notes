# Travel notes: Philadelphia weekend

Two nights in Philadelphia for a friend's birthday. February is not the city's finest month, but there are advantages to off-season travel: no lines, reduced prices, people who are actually from there.

**Saturday**

Arrived by train (Amtrak from Penn Station, 1h15m, always my preferred option over driving). Dropped bags, walked to Reading Terminal Market immediately because that's the only correct first move in Philadelphia.

The market is a covered food hall built in 1892 in the old train shed. It's chaotic, loud, smells like four cuisines simultaneously, and is exactly as good as its reputation. We ate: DiNic's roast pork sandwich (the correct order — sharp provolone, broccoli rabe, the pork that has been slow-cooked into submission), a Dutch apple pie slice from the Pennsylvania Dutch section, a small cup of the chowder from one stall whose name I immediately forgot.

Barnes Foundation in the afternoon. The collection is extraordinary and idiosyncratic — assembled according to Albert Barnes's own educational theories, which means paintings hang by principle rather than chronology. A Renoir next to a Cézanne next to a Matisse next to Pennsylvania Dutch ironwork, all demonstrating some formal point Barnes was making. It's an acquired approach but it works.

**Sunday**

South Street neighborhood in the morning. Coffee at a place on Bainbridge that took the coffee seriously without being precious about it. Wandered. Took the train home by 4 PM.

Philadelphia consistently surprises me with how livable it feels.
