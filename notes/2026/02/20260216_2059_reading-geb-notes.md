# Reading notes: Gödel, Escher, Bach

## What the book is doing

Hofstadter is building toward Gödel's incompleteness theorems — specifically, the claim that any sufficiently powerful formal system contains true statements that cannot be proven within that system. The path there is long and deliberate.

## Formal systems

The MU-puzzle and the pq-system are simple formal systems introduced early to build the reader's intuition. Rules are mechanical; strings are symbols; meaning is something we impose. The key insight: you can have a system that is *syntactically* valid (well-formed rules) but *semantically* complex in ways the rules don't determine.

## Isomorphism

One of the book's key concepts: an isomorphism is a structure-preserving correspondence between two systems. When you "see" a formal system as saying something about arithmetic, you're using an isomorphism between the system's symbols and arithmetic objects. The isomorphism is not inside the system; you're bringing it from outside.

Gödel's genius was to find an isomorphism between statements *about* a formal system and statements *within* it — letting the system talk about itself.

## Where I am

The Prelude, Ant Fugue is brilliant — a dialogue between Achilles, Tortoise, and Anteater about how colonies produce meaning that no individual ant intends. It's a concrete illustration of emergent meaning. The "message" of a colony is real even though no ant encodes it.
