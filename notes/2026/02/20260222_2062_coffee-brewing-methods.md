# Coffee brewing methods compared

I've owned all four of these and use three of them regularly. Notes for someone deciding what to get.

## Pour-over (V60, Chemex)

**Best for**: clean, clear cups; appreciating single-origin beans
**Ratio**: ~60g coffee per liter of water (adjust to taste)
**Time**: 3–4 minutes total brew time
**Grind**: medium-fine (coarser for Chemex due to thick filter)

The filter removes oils, producing a clean cup. Best way to taste the actual flavor characteristics of a bean. Requires more attention (pouring in stages, bloom, etc.) but rewards it.

## French press

**Best for**: full-bodied, oil-rich cups; low-effort brewing
**Ratio**: ~75g per liter
**Time**: 4 minutes steep
**Grind**: coarse

No filter means coffee oils stay in. Richer, heavier mouthfeel. Some sediment is normal. Common mistake: grind too fine (bitter, muddy) or steep too long (over-extracted).

## Aeropress

**Best for**: versatility, travel, concentrated coffee
**Ratio**: highly variable (50–100g per liter, depending on target)
**Time**: 1–2 minutes
**Grind**: medium to fine

Most forgiving of the four. Hard to make bad coffee with it. Produces a concentrated cup that can be diluted to taste. The inverted method gives more control over steep time.

## Moka pot

**Best for**: strong, espresso-adjacent coffee without an espresso machine
**Ratio**: fill basket, fill water to valve
**Time**: 5–7 minutes on stovetop
**Grind**: medium-fine (not espresso-fine — will clog)

Not technically espresso (lower pressure) but similar intensity. Good for milk drinks, not ideal for tasting delicate beans.
