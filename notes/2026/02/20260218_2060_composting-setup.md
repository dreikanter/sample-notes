# Apartment composting setup

Finally set up a real composting system after meaning to for a year.

**The setup**

Small countertop bin (2-quart) for kitchen scraps — vegetable and fruit peels, eggshells, coffee grounds, tea leaves, paper towels. No meat, dairy, or cooked food (apartment composting limitation).

The bin goes to a larger container in the freezer when full. Frozen scraps don't smell and can accumulate until drop-off day.

**Drop-off**

The city has a composting program with weekly drop-off at various parks. The nearest site is four blocks away, accepting organic material Saturday mornings. This is the piece that makes apartment composting actually viable — I don't need a backyard.

**What I've learned**

- Eggshells should be rinsed or they smell
- Coffee filters are fine, paper tea bags are fine, plastic tea bags (some brands) are not
- Citrus goes in, but slowly — it resists breakdown
- Avocado pits technically compostable but take a long time in commercial facilities; still drop them off

**What doesn't work**

Compostable plastics — cups, cutlery, bags — require industrial processing, not home or city compost. Don't add them unless your program explicitly accepts them. Mine doesn't.

**Impact sense check**

One small bin per week, 50 weeks, that's maybe 25 pounds of food waste diverted from landfill annually. Not nothing. The methane from landfill food waste is the reason composting matters.
