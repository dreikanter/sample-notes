# Film notes: La chimera

*La chimera* (2023, dir. Alice Rohrwacher). Watched at the revival cinema last weekend.

Josh O'Connor plays Arthur, a British archaeologist in rural Tuscany who has a strange gift: he can sense where ancient Etruscan tombs are buried, following an intuition that looks from the outside like sleepwalking or divination. He uses this gift in service of a band of tomb raiders selling antiquities on the black market. He's grieving something — a woman named Beniamina who may be dead or may be somewhere he's searching for underground.

The film is about thresholds. Between worlds: living and dead, Italian and foreign, present and ancient. Between states: Arthur half-belongs everywhere and completely belongs nowhere. Rohrwacher shoots on film in different formats and aspect ratios, sometimes within the same scene — 16mm cuts to Super 8 cuts to 35mm. The frame literally changes shape, suggesting that the world doesn't hold still.

What I found most interesting: the Etruscan artifacts in the film are real archival photographs of actual looted pieces, some of which ended up in museums and were later identified as stolen. The film is partly about the ordinary violence of the antiquities trade — how objects travel from burial to the Met, and what happens to the place from which they're taken.

O'Connor is extraordinary. The grief is worn in the body, not performed through expression.
