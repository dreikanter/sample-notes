# Fermentation notes: preserved lemons

Started these in late January (mentioned in [[20260124_2047]]). Six weeks later, checking in.

**What I did**

Meyer lemons (6, small), quartered but not all the way through (kept the base connected, like a flower). Packed with salt — roughly 2 tablespoons kosher salt per lemon, rubbed into the cut surfaces and packed into a sterilized jar. Pressed down, added the juice of two additional lemons to submerge. Sealed.

Left at room temperature for the first week (fermentation needs warmth), then moved to the back of the refrigerator.

**Six-week check**

The brine is thick and slightly syrupy — this is correct, it's concentrated lemon juice with dissolved salt. The lemons have softened and turned translucent. The peel, which is the part you use, has a concentrated, complex lemon flavor — salty, bright, slightly funky in a good way. Nothing like fresh lemon.

**How to use**

Rinse the piece you're using, scrape out the pulp (too salty to eat), and use the rind. Finely minced into grain salads, pasta, braised chicken, roasted vegetables. A little goes a long way.

I used some in a roasted cauliflower dish this week: florets, olive oil, preserved lemon rind, green olives, capers. The preserved lemon was the note that made it interesting.

**Next batch**

Regular Eureka lemons, more salt, longer ferment (3 months). Will compare.
