# Spring equinox

The equinox was yesterday — March 20, 11:01 AM. Equal day, equal night. This has been a remarkably mild winter, which I'm grateful for but also slightly suspicious of in the way that mild winters warrant suspicion now.

The signs of spring are concrete and specific in the city. The small redbud tree on the corner is pink, about three days ahead of last year. The green markets have ramps, which are the spring ingredient that people in food culture become briefly unhinged about — and I understand why, having first tasted them a few years ago. A ramp has the flavor of a spring onion crossed with garlic, and it only exists for about three weeks. That brevity is most of the point.

Seedlings are doing well (see [[20260316_2074]]). Tomatoes are in 4-inch pots now, looking sturdy. The basil is getting leggy under the light — I'll pinch it back next week.

The running route is getting crowded again. The casual runners have returned: people who run only in good weather, whom I respect rather than resent because they're smarter about the cold. The path along the water was almost empty in January; by April it will be impassable.

There's a specific quality of light in late March afternoons — low, golden, longer than you'd expected — that feels like a return rather than an arrival. Something familiar coming back. The word that comes to mind is *restoration*, which is maybe too dramatic for sunlight, but that's what it registers as.
