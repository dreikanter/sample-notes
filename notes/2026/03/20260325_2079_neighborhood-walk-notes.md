# Neighborhood walk notes

Wednesday afternoon, clear sky, 54°F. Walked about two miles with no destination.

**What I noticed**

The building on the corner with the scaffolding that's been up for eight months finally has it down. The facade is the original brick, cleaned. It looks newer and worse than it did with grime — the grime was part of the building's history.

A new laundromat opened on the block where the pharmacy used to be. The laundromat has large windows and good lighting; someone put a few plants in the window. It looks inviting. Laundromats can be inviting, this is underappreciated.

Three different people walking dogs, no interaction between them. One dog (small, white, furious) attempting to triangulate three separate smells simultaneously.

The hardware store had a cart of tools on the sidewalk — clearance on what looked like older stock. A very good handsaw for $8. I bought it. I don't need a handsaw. I have a handsaw. I now have two handsaws.

**On walking with no destination**

I do this once a week now. No headphones. No podcast. Sometimes a camera; usually not. The point is available attention — the Weil kind (see [[20260108_2035]]), or something like it. Looking at what's there.

What I find: the neighborhood is not a background to life. It's content. The same block on different days contains different things, and you notice them only if you're looking.
