# GEB completion notes

Finished *Gödel, Escher, Bach* on March 12. Started it in 2024. About time.

## What the book argues

At its core: self-reference is the engine of consciousness and meaning. Gödel showed that sufficiently powerful formal systems can make statements about themselves, and that this creates undecidable propositions — true statements the system cannot prove. Hofstadter's leap: this same structure appears in consciousness. The "I" is a strange loop, a self-referential pattern that perceives itself perceiving.

He's not claiming brains *are* formal systems. He's claiming the structure of self-reference — the way a system's output folds back and becomes input — is the thing that generates the subjective sense of being a self.

## What I'll actually keep

The formal system chapters were clarifying in a deep way. I now understand why undecidability is not a flaw in logic but a mathematical fact — not a gap in our knowledge but a limit inherent to the structure of powerful formal systems.

The Gödel numbering section was the high point: Gödel encodes statements about a formal system as numbers within that system, which lets the system "talk about" its own statements. A formal system can then construct a statement equivalent to "this statement is not provable." If provable, it's false. If not provable, it's true. Either way: the system is incomplete.

## Criticism

The book is 800 pages. Some chapters (particularly in the second half) could be shorter. The dialogues between chapters are sometimes more clever than illuminating. Hofstadter is clearly enjoying himself; occasionally more than the reader.

Still: worth it. One of the books that changes the kind of things you think about.
