# Ramp risotto

Bought a bunch of ramps (see [[20260321_2077]] for equinox notes where I mentioned ramps). Made risotto.

**The ramps**

Separated leaves from bulbs — they cook differently and both are useful. Blanched the leaves in salted water, squeezed out the water, set aside. Sliced the bulbs thin. Sautéed the bulb slices in butter at the beginning, building the base flavor.

**Risotto method**

This is the method I've landed on after years of varying approaches:

- Warm the stock and keep it warm throughout (cold stock slows the starch release)
- Toast the rice (Carnaroli, not Arborio — Carnaroli holds texture better) in butter 2 minutes
- First addition: white wine, a full splash, stir until absorbed
- Then: warm stock, one ladle at a time, stirring constantly until each addition is absorbed before the next
- Near the end: stir in the blanched ramp leaves (cut into ribbons) — they warm through and turn bright green
- Off heat: butter and parmesan in aggressively (the *mantecatura*), stir vigorously until the risotto "waves"
- Plate immediately; risotto waits for no one

**Result**

Pale green risotto from the ramp leaves, clean spring flavor, the faint garlic-onion character of the ramps integrating into the starchy richness. Good with a glass of Vermentino.

This is a dish that only works for about three weeks each year. That's part of what makes it work.
