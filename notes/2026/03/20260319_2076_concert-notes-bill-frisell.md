# Concert notes: Bill Frisell

Bill Frisell quartet at a small venue, around 300 seats. Reserved seating, excellent sightlines. Arrived early enough to hear sound check, which was better than I expected — he was working on a passage for fifteen minutes straight, micro-adjustments.

**The set**

Frisell has been making a kind of guitar music that doesn't fit cleanly into genre for forty years. It's jazz in the sense that there's improvisation and space, country in the sense that the melodic language is pentatonic and American, classical in the sense of compositional care. The effect is something like memory — specific musical phrases that feel half-remembered.

The quartet format (guitar, bass, drums, second guitar/steel guitar) was warmer than his solo recordings. The steel guitar added a Hawaiian quality to several pieces that shouldn't have worked and did.

Highlight: a slow version of "What the World Needs Now Is Love" that lasted about twelve minutes. Not schmaltz — genuinely tender, with the kind of space between notes that makes you hold your breath. The drummer brushed throughout, barely touching the snare. The room was very quiet.

Frisell rarely speaks between songs. He smiles, which is enough.

**On the venue**

Small jazz rooms are different from large ones. You can see the hands. You can hear when someone breathes in before a phrase. The music is not amplified into anonymity; it's still something happening in front of you.
