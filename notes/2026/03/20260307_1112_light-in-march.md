# Light in March

There's a specific quality to the light at around 5:30pm in early March that I always forget about until it reappears. It's not bright — the sun is low and slightly orange, the kind of light that makes long shadows — but it's light, genuinely present in a way that it isn't in February. It's the first sign that the year is turning in a direction that feels habitable again.

I walked home from the bus stop without the hood of my jacket up for the first time since maybe October. The temperature was around 8°C, which by any objective measure is still cold, but the light made it feel optional.

There's a concept in Finnish culture — the first day when you can eat ice cream outdoors again, which I've seen described online without a clean primary source, but the sentiment resonates: the light signals permission more than the temperature does.

What I noticed on the walk: the willows along the canal have started going yellow-green at the tips. Not leaves yet, just a shift in the colour of the bark. The ducks were more active than usual. A jogger in shorts, which seemed either optimistic or performative.

I find late autumn and January genuinely difficult in terms of mood, and I'm never quite sure how much of that is the light and how much is coincidental clustering of harder weeks. But the shift in March feels reliable. There's a piece in *Emergence Magazine* on phenology — the science of seasonal biological timing — that I've been meaning to read: https://emergencemagazine.org/essay/the-timing-of-things/

No particular conclusion here. Just wanted to note the day.
