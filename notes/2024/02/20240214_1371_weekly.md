## *Seeing Like a State* final notes

The last chapter on the failure of high modernist schemes (Soviet collectivization, Le Corbusier's urban planning, Tanzanian villagization) pulls everything together. The pattern: central planners optimize for legibility and control, sacrificing the local knowledge (*metis*) that made the original system work. The failures are not random — they follow a logic.

The software engineering analogy I keep reaching for: large-scale rewrites that discard the existing system's "defects" that were actually load-bearing adaptations. The strangest bugs in old systems are often there for a reason that nobody remembered to write down.
