# Chicken stock — method notes

Made stock from the accumulated bones in the freezer. About 1.5kg of bones — carcasses from three whole chickens plus various leg and wing bones from meal prep.

## Method

Roast the bones first. 220°C, 30–35 minutes until deeply browned. This is optional for a pale, clean stock but essential for a rich, full-flavored one. The Maillard reaction on bone and meat gives the stock its depth.

Into the largest pot with:
- Roasted bones
- 2 onions, quartered (don't peel — the skin adds color)
- 2 carrots, rough chop
- 2 celery stalks
- 1 head garlic, halved across the equator
- Bay leaves, peppercorns, parsley stems, thyme sprigs

Cover with cold water. Bring to a very gentle simmer — never a full boil. Skim foam for the first twenty minutes. Leave for 4–6 hours, barely trembling.

Strain through a fine-mesh sieve. Don't press the solids — just let it drain, which keeps the stock clear.

## What went well

Six liters from this batch, very rich, gelled solid in the refrigerator (which is the sign of good collagen extraction from the bones). The roasting step made a noticeable difference versus previous pale stock attempts.

## What I'll do differently

The garlic was too much — the stock has a slight garlic forward note that will read oddly in some applications. Reduce to four cloves next time. Also the simmer ran too hot once; there's slight cloudiness in one of the containers.

Reference: [Thomas Keller's stock method in The French Laundry Cookbook](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/301665/) — the gold standard for technique, though his scale is slightly absurd for home cooking.
