# Fresh pasta log — first attempts

February commitment: make fresh pasta once a week for the month. Week one done.

## Dough (for two servings)

- 100g 00 flour (Caputo Tipo 00)
- 1 whole egg + 1 yolk
- Pinch of salt
- Olive oil (optional, I added it, not sure it made a difference)

Mix flour and eggs until combined, knead 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Rest under plastic wrap 30 minutes. Roll thin with a pasta machine (setting 6 of 9 for tagliatelle).

## First attempt — tagliatelle with brown butter and sage

The pasta was slightly too thick because I stopped at setting 5 instead of 6 — it cooked in 2 minutes instead of 90 seconds and the texture was more chewy than silky. Still good, but not what I was after.

The brown butter and sage sauce was the simplest thing I know and the right test for the pasta — nothing to hide behind.

## Second attempt — pappardelle with ragu

Setting 6, one and a half minutes, significantly better. The pasta had the texture I was looking for: tender but with slight resistance, not soft.

The ragu was a three-hour beef and pork short rib version. Too much effort for a test of the pasta, but the results were excellent. The combination of fresh pasta and properly made ragu is a different dish from the dry pasta version — the pasta absorbs differently, the sauce clings differently.

## What to try next

Filled pasta — probably tortellini or some form of ravioli. The technique is different enough that I want to understand it properly. And an egg yolk pasta for something richer.

Reference: [Evan Funke's *American Sfoglino*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/601155/american-sfoglino-by-evan-funke-and-carol-the/) for the pasta technique fundamentals.
