# Cold frame build — February

Finally built the cold frame on Saturday. Six hours, one trip back to the hardware store, minor swearing over the hinge placement.

## Dimensions

4 feet × 3 feet × 12 inches at the back, 8 inches at the front. This slope catches winter sun from the south. The design is the standard box-and-glass version: nothing original.

## Materials

- 2×10 lumber (untreated cedar — rot-resistant, no chemical leaching concern near food plants)
- Old storm window I found at the salvage yard, $8
- Piano hinge, two lengths
- Prop stick for ventilation

Total cost: approximately $45 plus time. A commercial cold frame of similar size would run $120–180.

## What I'll use it for

Overwintering spinach and mache (corn salad) this season. Next fall I'll plant earlier and actually have winter greens rather than building the frame in February.

For now: the tomato seedlings are under grow lights and the cold frame will be used to harden them off in late March/early April before outdoor planting.

## What to do differently

The hinge screws are slightly too short — they'll hold but I should replace them with longer ones. The glass seal isn't perfect on the corners; a weather-strip tape application will help retain heat.

One useful technique I didn't know: black-painted water jugs inside the frame act as thermal mass, absorbing heat during the day and releasing it overnight, preventing sharp temperature drops.

Reference: [Eliot Coleman's four-season design](https://www.chelseagreen.com/product/four-season-harvest/) and [Johnny's Selected Seeds cold frame guide](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/).
