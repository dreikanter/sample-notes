# Music finds — February

Monthly log of new listens.

## Revisited

**Talk Talk — Spirit of Eden (1988):** This album is the origin point of post-rock without anyone knowing it at the time. Four long tracks, jazz improvisation structure, orchestral elements, field recordings. It was commercially disastrous on release and is now recognized as foundational. The label sued the band for not delivering a commercial follow-up. This fact makes the album more interesting.

What makes it work: the negative space. Long silences between musical events. The production lets sounds decay fully before the next one arrives. This is the technique that Brian Eno systematized in ambient music; Mark Hollis was doing it in rock.

## New to me

**Nala Sinephro — Space 1.8 (2021):** Jazz harpist from Belgium via London. The album is one long arcing piece of music across eight tracks. Meditative without being inert. The harp in a contemporary jazz context does something unusual — it has a specific weight (the attack and sustain pattern is unlike any other instrument) that anchors the more ambient textures.

**William Basinski — Disintegration Loops:** Not new but finally heard the full thing. Audio tapes decaying as they play; the music is the physical deterioration of the magnetic recording. Context (recorded on September 11, 2001, the dust from the towers visible from Basinski's Brooklyn studio) is inseparable from the effect. The most conceptually loaded piece of music I've heard.

Reference: [Bandcamp's ambient/electronic tags](https://bandcamp.com/) have been the most useful music discovery channel I've found since streaming ate everything.
