# Team offsite — February notes

Three days, rented house outside the city. Eight people. Notes while fresh.

## What worked

**The unstructured first afternoon** was the right call. No agenda, just arrival and a long dinner. By the time we started working on day two, people had already had the easy conversations that clear space for harder ones.

**The technical direction session** ran over by forty minutes, which was appropriate. The read replica discussion surfaced a disagreement about when to introduce a caching layer that I didn't know existed — Devlin and Marcus had opposite intuitions and hadn't aired it before. Three hours later we had an actual decision, not just deferred disagreement.

**Walking meetings:** Spontaneous, happened because the house had good hiking paths and some conversations started inside and ended outside. Two of the best conversations happened walking.

## What didn't work

The escape room on Tuesday evening was a mistake. Too competitive for a team that's been feeling some interpersonal stress. The better move would have been cooking dinner together, which Priya suggested and I overruled.

The Wednesday morning retrospective ran out of energy before we got through the whole agenda. People were ready to go home by 11am.

## What I'll take from it

The conversation between Devlin and Marcus was worth the entire offsite cost. Those disagreements that don't surface in normal work are the most expensive ones — they become implicit conflict that slows everything down.

The offsite helped. We should do it again before the summer, not after.

Reference for facilitation: [Liberating Structures](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/) for formats that avoid the "death by presentation" pattern I wanted to avoid.
