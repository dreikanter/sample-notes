# Cooking log — ghormeh sabzi

First attempt at ghormeh sabzi — the Persian herb and kidney bean stew that I've been eating at a restaurant near the office for two years without ever trying to make it myself.

## What I learned before starting

The dish is defined by three things: dried fenugreek leaves (*shanbalileh*), which provide the distinctive bitterness and funk; dried limes (*limoo amani*), which give the sour, slightly earthy depth; and the volume of fresh herbs (parsley, cilantro, fresh fenugreek if you can find it, sometimes spinach) that cook down to almost nothing but become the body of the stew.

## Ingredients (serves 4–5)

- 500g lamb shoulder, cut into cubes (can use beef)
- 400g fresh herbs (mostly parsley and cilantro, with some fresh spinach)
- 3 tbsp dried fenugreek
- 3 dried Persian limes, pierced
- 1 can kidney beans
- 1 large onion, diced
- Turmeric, salt, black pepper
- Lamb or beef stock, about 500ml

## Method

Fry the fresh herbs in oil until very dark and fragrant — this takes 20+ minutes on medium-low and is not a step to rush. The herbs should reduce to a fraction of their volume and turn almost black-green.

Brown the lamb with the onion, add turmeric. Add the fried herbs, dried fenugreek, limes, stock. Low simmer 1.5–2 hours until lamb is tender. Add beans, simmer 30 more minutes.

## Result

Good but not the restaurant version yet. The limes need more time to fully dissolve into the broth — I'll pierce them more aggressively next time. The herb quantity was right; the lamb was slightly overcooked and will benefit from the low-and-slow approach in the oven next time.

Reference: [Najmieh Batmanglij's *Food of Life*](https://www.mage.com/food-of-life/) is the definitive Persian cookbook in English.
