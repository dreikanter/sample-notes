# Winter solstice 2024

The shortest day. Sunrise at 8:01am, sunset at 3:55pm — about seven hours and fifty-four minutes of daylight, which doesn't feel real but is apparently the case.

I've been marking the solstices for about five years now, not in any ritual sense but as a deliberate acknowledgment of where we are in the year. There's something useful in having two fixed points in the calendar that don't move with work schedules or social demands — they just are what they are, astronomically inevitable.

The solstice has its own logic as a marker: not the start of winter in the cultural sense, but the hinge point from which the light returns. By the end of January, the difference will be perceptible — not dramatic, but there. By the February half-term the evenings will already have shifted noticeably.

I went outside at sunset (3:55pm, which still feels like early afternoon to my summer-calibrated sense of time). The sky was pink and grey and there were starlings doing something complex over the allotments to the north. A murmuration, though small — maybe two thousand birds rather than the tens of thousands you see in the famous videos. Still a strange and beautiful thing. The [RSPB guide to starling murmurations](https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/starling) says they gather for warmth and safety in numbers before roosting.

Made a simple meal: lentil soup with good bread. Lit candles at the table. Spent the evening reading rather than looking at screens. A quiet marking of the turning point.

Next solstice: June 20, 2025.
