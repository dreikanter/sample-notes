# Winter walk — December light

Moved the solstice walk forward to the 5th to take advantage of a rare clear day. The forecast showed a window of sun between 11am and 2pm — unusual for December — so I drove 25 minutes to the chalk downs and walked the ridge.

The light in mid-December in England is low even at noon. The sun barely cleared the ridge line to the south, and the shadows were long even at midday. Everything was in that golden-hour light that in summer you get only briefly and which in December lasts all afternoon. The frosted grass caught it and the whole hillside was luminous in a way that felt slightly unreal.

Walked about 9km along the ridge path. Saw: red kites circling (three or four, effortlessly using the thermals), a kestrel hovering and then dropping, flocks of fieldfare moving through the hawthorn scrub at the valley edge. The fieldfare are winter visitors from Scandinavia — chunky thrushes with a grey head and chestnut back that move in groups and clatter off noisily when disturbed.

No other walkers for most of the route. On the final section near the car park, a group with a dog. The dog was more excited about the day than anyone.

Ate the packed lunch sitting on a fence stile looking south: cheese and pickle sandwich, apple, flask of tea. Ate looking at the view rather than at a screen. The tea was perfect in the way that tea in the cold from a flask always is.

More information on the chalk downland habitats: [South Downs National Park wildlife guide](https://www.southdowns.gov.uk/wildlife/).

This is the part of the year I forget to enjoy properly. A reminder to myself: get outside on the clear days in winter, even when it's cold.
