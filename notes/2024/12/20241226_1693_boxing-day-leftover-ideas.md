# Boxing Day leftovers — what to do

The post-Christmas question: what to do with the inevitable surplus. Notes and ideas.

## What's in the fridge

- Half a turkey (substantial — 1.5kg or so)
- Leftover roast potatoes (about ten)
- Red cabbage braised with apple and caraway (lot of this)
- Gravy (about 400ml — very good gravy)
- Brussels sprout halves with pancetta
- Cranberry sauce (half a jar)
- Butter

## Immediate plans

**Turkey pie**: Make a quick white sauce, add turkey chunks and some frozen peas, top with ready-roll puff pastry. 30 minutes in a 200°C oven. The [BBC Good Food turkey pie recipe](https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/turkey-and-ham-pie) is a reliable template.

**Bubble and squeak**: Mash the roast potatoes with any remaining vegetables, form into a patty, fry in butter until deeply browned on each side. This is the primary reason to have leftover roast potatoes.

**Turkey broth**: Strip the carcass and simmer bones with onion, celery, bay, peppercorns for 3-4 hours. Strain and use as a base for a January lentil soup.

## What to do with the gravy

Don't dilute it — the reduced gravy is already at the right concentration. Freeze in a zip-lock bag flat for the next roast chicken. Or add it to the turkey pie as part of the filling sauce.

## What not to do

Don't try to replicate Christmas dinner. Boxing Day is for a different mode: simpler, looser, leftover-based cooking that doesn't pretend it's a fresh meal. The best Boxing Day food acknowledges that it's using what's there rather than pretending otherwise.

Bubble and squeak is genuinely better than the original roast potatoes, which is one of food's reliable surprises.
