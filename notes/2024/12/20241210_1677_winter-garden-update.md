# Winter garden — December update

Following up on the November garden notes [[20241106_1643]]. Coldest stretch of the year so far this week — overnight temperatures hitting -4°C.

The garlic is growing well despite the cold. The Solent Wight has put up 10–15cm of green growth; the Elephant is behind at about 6cm. Both are healthy and that difference may just be variety timing.

The broad beans finally emerged — took 18 days rather than the 10–14 the packet suggested, presumably because the soil was cold. About 70% germination which is reasonable. The plants are small and low, which is good — they're less likely to be damaged by frost if they haven't put on excessive growth before winter properly sets in.

The purple sprouting broccoli under the net is looking excellent. Dense, dark green, no signs of pest damage. It won't be ready until February or March but it's clearly happy.

The tulip bulbs are presumably doing their thing underground. No signs above soil yet, which is fine.

**Loss**: the pelargonium cuttings I took in October ([[20241018_1624]]) — about half of them. The unheated greenhouse is too cold. Should have kept them in the kitchen window. The survivors are leggy and pale. Will see if any root properly by spring but expectations are low.

**Gain**: the compost bins are looking healthy. Turning them regularly through autumn seems to have kept them active even in cold weather. There's visible worm activity right to the surface.

The [RHS winter vegetable guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/grow-winter-crops) is useful for understanding what the overwintering crops actually need — mostly it's "leave them alone and keep the birds off".
