# Christmas Day cooking log

Reporting back on the plan from [[20241218_1685]].

## What went well

The turkey was excellent — 3.5kg bird, took exactly 2 hours 20 minutes at 190°C fan. Rested 40 minutes under foil. The breast was moist, which is always the main risk. Putting butter under the skin and covering the crown loosely for the first hour makes a real difference.

The roast potatoes were the best I've made. Three things contributed: Maris Piper variety (waxy potatoes fall apart), duck fat (much hotter smoke point than vegetable oil), and par-boiling until just soft and then shaking to roughen the surface before roasting. The rough surface catches the fat and crisps better.

The gravy came together well from the pan drippings — added the turkey stock I'd made Christmas Eve, a splash of madeira, and reduced it properly. No cornstarch; thickened by reduction alone.

## What was hard

The timing. Coordinating six different dishes for a 2pm serving time requires actual kitchen skill I'm still developing. The parsnips were done ten minutes early and went slightly soft while resting. Next year: start them fifteen minutes later.

The bread sauce was too thick. Should have loosened with more warm milk before serving.

## The table

Eight people, two of whom I don't see often enough, good wine, long table. The meal lasted three hours from first mouthful to coffee. The chocolate tart [[20241208_1675]] finished it well — several people asked for the recipe.

A successful Christmas. The planning (see [[20241218_1685]]) definitely helped; the main value was not being stressed about what came next. The cooking was calm rather than chaotic, which is the real goal.
