# Advent music project — Handel oratorios

Working through some Handel I've never properly listened to as part of the advent project [[20241201_1668]].

## Saul (1739)

Started here because it's rarely performed and I knew almost nothing about it. The [recording by Paul McCreesh and the Gabrieli Consort](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/dc.asp?dc=D_SACDA67504) is superb — period instruments, clear textures, excellent soloists.

*Saul* is dramatically gripping in a way that surprised me. The title character is explicitly sympathetic in the early acts — generous, kingly — before his jealousy of David curdles into something pathological. Handel gives Saul music of genuine nobility even when the character is behaving badly, which makes the tragedy more affecting. The "Dead March" in the final act is one of the great pieces of music I've encountered; it moves from a kind of ominous restraint into something crushing.

## Israel in Egypt (1739)

Completely different character — almost entirely choral, very little solo work. The chorus is the protagonist. The [Westminster Abbey recording](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk) is the one I found.

The plagues sequences are extraordinary: the frogs (a kind of hopping, comic figure in the chorus), the darkness (thick and terrible), the hailstones (the basses doing something percussive and relentless). The double chorus writing — Handel splitting the choir and having the two halves answer each other — creates a spatial effect that must have been shocking in the original performances.

Less gripping narratively than Saul but musically more inventive. A different kind of experience.

Both are worth returning to. Planning to listen to *Jephtha* next.
