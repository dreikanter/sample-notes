# Advent calendar project — planning

Making a small daily advent project this year — not a calendar exactly, but one intentional thing each day in December leading up to the 25th. Trying to counter the usual experience of December being something that happens to you rather than something you participate in.

## The format

Each day: one small thing. Could be a recipe, a walk, a piece of music, a bit of reading, a phone call with someone I've been meaning to call. Nothing that takes more than an hour. Nothing expensive. The point is attention, not achievement.

## Some planned entries

- Dec 1: Make mulled wine — using a recipe from the [Guardian's wine column](https://www.theguardian.com/food/series/felicity-cloake-how-to-make-the-perfect) rather than the sachet
- Dec 3: Listen to a Handel piece I've never heard — working through the oratorios
- Dec 5: Write to someone I haven't contacted in over six months
- Dec 7: Cook something with winter vegetables only — no imported exotica
- Dec 10: Extended walk in a place I've never been before
- Dec 12: No screens after 7pm
- Dec 14: Bake — probably another sourdough attempt, building on [[20241103_1640]]
- Dec 18: Listen to a full album in one sitting with no other tasks
- Dec 21: Winter solstice — outside at sunset, whatever the weather

## The logic

Advent as a season has a long history of being a time of preparation and intention rather than consumption. The [Church of England's Advent resources](https://www.churchofengland.org/faith-action/faith/advent-and-christmas) frame it as a season of expectation. I'm not approaching it religiously, but the underlying structure — deliberate attention across a period of time — has its own value regardless.

## What I'm not doing

Not buying gifts in a stressed rush. Not attending every social event. Not watching Christmas films ironically until I actually dislike Christmas.
