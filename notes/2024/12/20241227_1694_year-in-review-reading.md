# Year in review — reading 2024

A partial accounting. I read more than I've tracked but the tracked list is what I have.

## Completed this year

- *The Dispossessed* — Le Guin [[20241014_1618]]. The alternating structure. The wall. The General Temporal Theory as philosophical argument. One of the best things I read all year.
- *Piranesi* — Susanna Clarke [[20241028_1634]]. Stunning small novel. The joy of Piranesi's cataloguing.
- *The Remains of the Day* — Ishiguro [[20241125_1662]]. The bench scene.
- *Atomic Habits* — James Clear [[20241116_1653]]. More useful than expected. The identity frame.
- *Gilead* — Marilynne Robinson [[20241216_1683]]. The most beautiful prose I read this year.
- *The Rings of Saturn* — Sebald [[20241224_1691]]. The form of associative walking and its relationship to decay and memory.

## Partially read / ongoing

- *Housekeeping* — Robinson (just started)
- Various essays and longform journalism

## What the list tells me

Strong in fiction; weaker in non-fiction this year (the habits book aside). The fiction skews toward the literary and the quietly devastating — I seem to have spent 2024 seeking out books that do something with time and memory. The Dispossessed, Piranesi, Remains of the Day, Gilead, Rings of Saturn — all preoccupied with the relationship between past and present, between the self that was and the self that is.

That probably says something about where I've been mentally this year. I'm not sure what.

## 2025 intentions

Read more non-fiction, specifically: economics, science, history. Read something I disagree with politically and engage with it seriously. Read at least two books in translation from languages other than French or German.

The [Backlisted podcast](https://www.backlisted.fm) has been the best reading recommendation source this year — they focus on overlooked older books and have put at least five things on my list.
