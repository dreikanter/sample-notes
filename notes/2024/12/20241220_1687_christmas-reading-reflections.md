# Christmas break reading reflections

The break starts today for most people; I've cleared my desk and am not opening work emails until January 2nd. The goal: read, cook, walk, rest, see people.

Finished *Gilead* [[20241216_1683]] two days ago and have been sitting with it since. The ending is exactly right — quiet, complete, not consoling in any obvious way but not bleak either. Ames's last words to his son are an act of blessing, which sounds saccharine but isn't, because Robinson has earned it.

Starting *Housekeeping* next, also Robinson. Want to see how her early work differs from the Gilead trilogy.

Also in the pile:
- *The Rings of Saturn* by W.G. Sebald — long on the to-read list, feels right for winter
- A collected essays volume on town planning I picked up secondhand
- The new translation of Dante's *Inferno* by Mary Jo Bang — dipped into it at a bookshop

The reading life feels more sustainable when the day has natural pauses — the walk before lunch, the long evening without screen pressure. It's possible this is just what holidays feel like and the lesson is that I need more of them, not that I've discovered some secret to sustained reading.

There's an [essay by Tim Parks on reading](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2011/06/09/reading-mindfully/) in The New York Review of Books about mindful reading versus skimming that's worth finding if you read fast and feel guilty about it. His argument is broadly: the quality of attention matters more than the quantity of pages, and that rushing through books to have read them is a specific kind of category error. I return to this argument periodically.

Looking forward to a long, unstructured ten days.
