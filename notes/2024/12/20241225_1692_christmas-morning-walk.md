# Christmas morning walk

Out at 8am while the household slept. The streets were empty in a way that streets only are at Christmas and at 5am — a quiet that isn't absence but something more like suspension.

Took the route along the river path and through the water meadows. The ground was firm with frost, the grass white and crunching underfoot. The river was high after a week of rain and the colour of pewter. No wind.

Birds: a kingfisher, which I always feel slightly guilty calling "a kingfisher" as though seeing one is routine — it is extraordinary every time. It flew upstream, electric blue, and then was gone. Robin singing from the hawthorn by the footbridge, apparently performing for no audience but itself. Later, a grey heron standing at the river bend with that improbable motionless patience herons have.

The light at 8am on a clear December morning is almost horizontal. Everything has long shadows and a slight orange warmth to it. The frost caught the light and the whole meadow was glittering in a way that seemed too beautiful for an ordinary day, which Christmas morning isn't, really, even for someone non-religious — there's still the sense that the calendar is doing something different on this particular day.

Home by 9am. Made tea and sat at the kitchen table with it while the house woke up around me.

The walk took 55 minutes and changed the quality of the whole day. See also the winter walk note [[20241205_1672]] for similar experiences.

The [Wildlife Trusts' guide to winter river wildlife](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/habitats/rivers) is good on what to look for at this time of year.
