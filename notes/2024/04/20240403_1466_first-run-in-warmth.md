# First run in real warmth

Temperature was 14°C at 6am, which is a first for the year. Went out in shorts and a long-sleeve shirt and was comfortable — the seasonal marker I'd been waiting for.

Route: the long loop through the park and around the reservoir. 8.2km, which is the longest since the knee issue in February. Felt easy enough that I held back from going further, which is the correct instinct at this stage of returning.

The park was different than the March walks — full leaf-out is still weeks away but the understory is visibly green now, the ground covered in spring ephemeral wildflowers. Identified (from memory, confirmed later):
- Trout lily in several patches, both yellow-flowered plants and the mottled leaves without flowers
- Bloodroot finishing up — a few white flowers still open, most already seedpods
- Wild ginger, the mottled heart-shaped leaves covering a slope under the beeches

The reservoir was still and the water was reflecting the pale sky and the bare upper branches of the trees at the edge. Two loons in winter plumage, which is the first loon sighting of the year — they stop during migration.

Running economy felt good. The 8km distance that required effort in January felt controlled at a pace that would have been an effort. This is the compound effect of consistent training even when it doesn't feel like it's going well.

Knee: fine during and after. No stiffness by evening.
