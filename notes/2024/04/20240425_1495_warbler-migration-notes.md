# Warbler migration notes

Peak warbler migration is beginning. The first arrivals showed up this week — generally the hardier species that can tolerate insect shortages, while the more exclusively insectivorous ones lag by one to three weeks.

**Confirmed this week:**

**Yellow-rumped warbler:** The expected first. Six or eight of them in the park, picking insects from the bark of old oaks. Still in patchy late-winter plumage on some individuals. These overwinter further south than most warblers and are among the first back. They're the ones birders slightly condescend to because they're abundant, but the breeding males in full plumage are striking.

**Palm warbler:** Tail-pumping along the ground at the edge of the field. Three birds total. A species I associate with Florida in winter, which is where they were last month.

**Pine warbler:** One male in the pines along the south trail. Very yellow. Came to the feeder briefly, which pine warblers do more than other warblers.

**Not yet:** Yellow warbler, Common yellowthroat, American redstart, Magnolia, Blackburnian (the ones worth waiting for).

**Heard but not confirmed ID:** Something in the shrubs along the reservoir that I thought might be a Nashville — the song didn't resolve into certainty before it stopped. Logged as possible.

This is the window when going out early (6:00-8:00am) makes a real difference. The birds are actively foraging and singing, light is good, and the park is quiet enough to hear the calls.
