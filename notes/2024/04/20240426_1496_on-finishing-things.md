# On finishing things

There's a class of project I'm noticing in my own behavior: things that reach 80% and stop there. The shelf that needs one more piece of hardware. The photo organization project. The audio recording idea from last year. These aren't abandoned — I still think about them, still intend to finish them — but they've entered a limbo where the remaining 20% requires something I keep not providing.

What's different about the remaining 20%?

Sometimes it's genuinely hard — the last part is the hardest part, which is why it's last. The audio recording project stalled because I got to the mixing stage and mixing requires skills I haven't developed. Restarting requires either learning something or accepting an outcome that doesn't match the intention.

Sometimes it's a supplies/resources problem with a small activation energy — the shelf needs one specific bolt I haven't bought. The barrier is almost nothing but it exists.

Sometimes it's that the project has done most of what it was going to do emotionally or practically, and the remaining 20% is about completion rather than value. The photo organization would be "done" in some meaningful sense if I stopped at where I am, even though it isn't technically finished.

The useful question is which category each stalled project is in. The mixing problem is a real barrier. The shelf is a bolt away. The photos might be fine where they are.

I think I'll buy the bolt this weekend. The rest needs longer reflection.
