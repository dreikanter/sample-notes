# Cold brew coffee method

After four years of occasional cold brew, have landed on a method I like and stopped experimenting much.

## Standard concentrate method

**Ratio:** 1:4 coffee to water by weight (concentrate — dilute 1:1 before drinking)
- 100g coarse-ground coffee
- 400g cold filtered water

**Grind:** Coarser than pour-over — similar to coarse sea salt. Too fine = astringent; too coarse = thin.

**Steep time:** 18-20 hours in the fridge. Room temperature steeps faster (12-14 hours) but I've found the fridge version is cleaner-tasting.

**Strain:** Through a paper filter in a pour-over dripper, or a fine mesh strainer + paper. Don't squeeze the grounds.

**Shelf life:** 10-14 days refrigerated. Quality starts dropping around day 10.

## Notes on coffee selection

Cold brew is forgiving of medium roasts that might taste slightly flat as hot brew. Fruity natural-process coffees become jammy and pleasant. Overly light roasts can taste thin and acidic even cold-brewed. Dark roasts work but can become bitter-adjacent.

Worst result: Colombia medium-roast from a gas station, which produced something that tasted like very cold weak instant coffee.

Best result: Guatemala Huehuetenango, medium-light, washed — the caramel notes intensified.

## Variations tried

- **Japanese-style** (brew hot directly over ice): more complex, less round, better for single-origin showcasing. More work.
- **Toddy system:** Produces a cleaner concentrate than DIY. Worth it only if you're making large batches regularly.
- **Nitro at home:** Bought a whipped cream charger and tried this. Acceptable but not as creamy as commercial nitro. The equipment is annoying to clean.
