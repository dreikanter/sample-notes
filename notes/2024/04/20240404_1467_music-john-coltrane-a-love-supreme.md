# Music notes — A love supreme

John Coltrane, 1965. Forty minutes in four parts: Acknowledgement, Resolution, Pursuance, Psalm. I've listened to this record dozens of times over many years but listened again tonight with the liner notes actually open.

The four-note bass motif in Part I ("A Love Supreme") is one of the most famous in jazz — it's almost a mantra, repeated and transformed. What I hadn't noticed (or hadn't consciously registered) before reading: Coltrane actually chants the title words over the motif at the very end of Part I. It's barely there, quiet, almost a private statement.

The quartet: Coltrane on tenor, McCoy Tyner on piano, Jimmy Garrison on bass, Elvin Jones on drums. The interplay between Tyner and Jones is the engine — Tyner's quartal harmony creates an open landscape without the pull of conventional chord progressions; Jones's polyrhythmic drumming generates momentum that feels like multiple tempos coexisting.

Part IV, Psalm, is Coltrane playing the written poem from the liner notes as a melody — each phrase of the prayer matched to a phrase of saxophone. It only makes sense when you read along. The ending resolves to near silence.

The conventional critical praise for this record is almost too familiar to be useful. What strikes me fresh: it was recorded in one day (December 9, 1964) in two takes per part. The feeling of sustained concentration is literal, not constructed in editing.
