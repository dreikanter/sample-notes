# Urban foraging notes

The idea of foraging in an urban or suburban environment sounds more radical than it is. Many of the most common "weeds" are edible, several are actively good, and identifying them requires modest effort with a reliable field guide.

**What I've eaten this spring from non-cultivated sources:**

**Garlic mustard (Alliaria petiolata):** Invasive, therefore guilt-free to harvest. The young leaves have a mild garlic-mustardy flavor, great in salads or pesto. As it flowers (now) it becomes more bitter but is still usable in cooked applications. Blanch briefly if the bitterness is too strong. Found in large patches at the edge of the park where the mowed area meets the unmowed.

**Dandelion greens:** The young leaves before flowering are excellent in salad or wilted with garlic. Once the plant is flowering the leaves are too bitter for most raw uses. I picked the crown (the central growing point at ground level) for the most tender leaves. The flowers can be made into wine or battered and fried.

**Wood sorrel (Oxalis):** The small clover-looking plant with heart-shaped leaves that grows through sidewalk cracks and in garden beds. Tastes unmistakably of lemon — it's high in oxalic acid. Use as a garnish or for a fresh acid note in salads. Don't eat large amounts.

**Not tried yet but in my range:**
- Stinging nettle (needs gloves to harvest, excellent cooked)
- Chicken of the woods fungus (the orange shelf fungus on certain trees — needs confident ID)

Always positive ID before eating. Forager's guide: https://www.wildfooduk.com/
