# Japanese bookstore visit

In Tokyo for four days on the tail end of a work trip. Spent most of the final afternoon in Junkudo in Ikebukuro — eight floors, labyrinthine, extremely well organized by systems I couldn't entirely decode.

The experience of being in a large bookstore where you're illiterate in the primary language is strange. You can't browse the usual way, can't read titles or blurbs, can't follow the section logic entirely. What you can do: look at design, pick up books based on physical appeal, find the English-language sections which were better stocked than I expected.

The manga section — maybe 40% of the whole store — was fascinating to walk through with no ability to read it. The variety of art styles visible from the covers alone was much wider than the manga that gets translated and exported. Some was obviously targeted at very young children; some was clearly for adult readers in ways that might not make it to translation. The sheer volume of ongoing series was staggering.

Found the craft and design section, which had several books I recognized (English-language originals, translated) and several I didn't. Bought a book about Japanese joinery that has no English in it except technical dimension labels — I wanted it for the photographs, and the photographs are superb.

Also bought: two issues of an architecture magazine, Jutakutokushu, whose name I had to ask the clerk to write down. Very beautiful.
