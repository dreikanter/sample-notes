# Spring ephemerals walk — April survey

Went back to the grove section of the park specifically to document what's flowering before the canopy leafs out and the light conditions change. These plants are racing to complete their entire above-ground lifecycle in the six weeks before they're shaded out.

**Documented today:**

- **Hepatica** — one small patch, lavender-blue flowers on hairy stems, leaves with the trilobed shape that gives it the name. About twelve plants in an 8m radius. This is a new find in this section.
- **Trout lily** — widespread, now fully flowering in most patches. The nodding yellow flowers close at night. The non-flowering plants (one leaf rather than two) far outnumber the flowering ones — trout lily takes years to flower for the first time.
- **Dutchman's breeches** — two clusters near the east-facing slope. The white pantaloon-shaped flowers are one of the most distinctive spring ephemerals. Pollinators need to be long-tongued bumblebees to access the nectar.
- **Wild blue phlox** — just beginning, a few flowers in the more open sections. Will be a carpet in another week.
- **Rue anemone** — delicate white flowers, the stems look too thin to hold them up.

**Not yet seen:**
- May apple (usually up but not flowering until later)
- Wild geranium
- Jacob's ladder

The timing this year is about a week ahead of last year. The warm March pushed everything forward.
