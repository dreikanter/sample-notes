# Mushroom risotto log

Made risotto properly for the first time in months. The standing-and-stirring requirement means it's a weekend or evening-when-not-tired dish.

**Mushrooms used:** Cremini (200g, sliced thick), dried porcini (15g, soaked in hot water — kept the soaking liquid), and some fresh oyster mushrooms (100g) that I found at a good price.

**Stock:** The porcini soaking liquid plus chicken stock, kept warm in a separate pan throughout. This is non-negotiable — adding cold stock drops the temperature and slows the process unevenly.

**Procedure:**
1. Rehydrated porcini in 250ml hot water, 20 min. Strained through paper towel, kept liquid.
2. Sautéed the cremini in butter over high heat until deeply brown — this is the flavor foundation and most people don't go dark enough.
3. Set mushrooms aside. In the same pan: shallot and garlic until soft.
4. Added Arborio (300g), stirred in the hot fat, toasted until edges translucent.
5. Added white wine (100ml), stirred until absorbed.
6. Added warm stock one ladle at a time, stirring constantly. This took 22 minutes.
7. At 18 minutes the texture was correct but I added more stock to loosen.
8. Off heat: stirred in butter and Parmigiano. Added the mushrooms. Adjusted salt.

**Texture:** Perfect — wave-like when you shake the pan (all'onda). Held the wave for about 90 seconds before it stiffened.

**Finish:** Truffle oil, which I know is controversial (most is synthetic). This one is real and used sparingly.

**Verdict:** The three-mushroom combination worked. The porcini added earthiness the others don't have.
