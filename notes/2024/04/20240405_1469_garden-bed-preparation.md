# Garden bed preparation — April

Ground finally workable throughout — the frost has left even the shadier sections. Did the main garden prep today over about two hours.

**What I did:**

Removed all the dead plant material from last year that I hadn't gotten to in fall. The tomato cages were still standing with the dried vines wrapped around them. Pulled everything, composted what was disease-free, bagged the one remaining pepper plant (which had powdery mildew at the end of the season — not worth risking the compost pile).

Added 4cm of finished compost over the entire bed. Last year's soil test showed adequate phosphorus and potassium but low-ish organic matter. The compost addresses this without adding synthetic fertilizer. I'll get another soil test in May.

Worked the compost in with a fork — not deep tillage, just enough to break the surface and integrate the top 5cm. The minimal-till approach has improved soil structure noticeably over three years: the aggregation is better, earthworm activity is much higher, the soil doesn't crust after rain the way it used to.

**Crop rotation plan for this year:**

- Bed A (north, shadier): squash — was tomatoes last year
- Bed B (center): tomatoes and peppers — was beans last year  
- Bed C (south, sunniest): beans — was squash last year
- Herbs stay in their permanent section

The garlic from last fall is now 10-15cm tall and looking strong. A few cloves came up later than the others and are only 5cm — might be worth marking these to see if they produce smaller bulbs at harvest.
