# Concert notes — symphony program

**Date:** April 16
**Venue:** Symphony hall, center section, row M
**Program:**
- Sibelius: Violin Concerto in D minor, Op. 47
- Beethoven: Symphony No. 7 in A major, Op. 92

**Conductor:** Visiting, unfamiliar to me
**Soloist (Sibelius):** Young violinist from Lithuania, name I'm blanking on

---

The Sibelius was the draw — I've heard recordings but never live. The concerto is peculiar: the opening violin line enters quietly and takes a long time to establish itself before the orchestra joins properly. The soloist had enormous technical facility without the showiness that sometimes goes with it. The slow movement (Adagio di molto) is the emotional center, and she played it with restraint that made it land harder than the more extroverted approach would have.

The cadenza in the first movement is famous for being one of the most demanding in the repertoire. She appeared completely calm during it. The audience knew what they were watching and the silence before the applause was a beat longer than usual.

Beethoven's 7th after intermission felt like a different energy — joyful and driving where the Sibelius had been interior. The second movement (Allegretto) is the famous one, the ground-pattern rhythm that seems to pull everything forward. The finale is genuinely exciting in a physical way — the pulse is insistent.

The hall acoustics are very good. The bass strings in the Beethoven had weight and warmth without muddiness.
