# Thoughts on perfectionism

A piece of feedback from Maya at our last one-on-one: "you sometimes hold PRs waiting for them to be cleaner than they need to be." She wasn't wrong.

I've had a complicated relationship with the word "perfectionism" because it's used as both a real criticism and a humble-brag. "My weakness is that I'm a perfectionist" is a cliché that means almost nothing. But there's a real dysfunction underneath it that's worth looking at honestly.

The actual mechanism, when I'm honest about it: there's a version of the work in my head that's better than the version that currently exists, and I'm reluctant to release the current version into the world because it makes the gap between intention and execution visible. The gap being private feels safer. This has nothing to do with quality standards and everything to do with avoidance.

This is different from actually knowing that a piece of work needs more time. Those cases exist too. The skill is distinguishing them — "this needs more work to be correct" versus "this needs more time so I can feel better about it."

The fix, as far as I can tell: set a release criterion before starting. Not "when it's ready" but "when X is true." Then honour the criterion.

Applied to the rate limiting project: criterion was "all unit tests passing and the load test hitting target numbers." That criterion is now met. Time to open the PR.

Adam Grant has written on this: [the costs of perfectionism](https://adamgrant.net/), though his framing is more optimistic than mine.
