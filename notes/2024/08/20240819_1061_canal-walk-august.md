# Canal walk — late August

Walked the towpath from the lock at Marsworth down to Berkhamsted, about nine miles. Started at 7 am to get ahead of the narrowboat traffic and the dog-walker density that peaks mid-morning on weekends.

The water was low enough that the silt showed in the shallows near the reedy sections. A few herons stood at intervals along the bank with the absolute stillness of things that have decided patience is the only strategy worth having. One fished successfully while I watched — the strike is faster than you expect, almost invisible, and then there's just the heron re-composing itself with a fish at an inconvenient angle.

The Cow Roast lock has a painted narrowboat permanently moored on the far side that someone clearly lives on — pots of herbs on the roof, a wind-chime that was audible from fifty metres, a terrier that made its opinion of walkers known without any conviction. I stopped to watch a pair working the lock manually, the choreography of it: one on the paddles, one walking the boat forward with a rope, everything slow and deliberate.

The towpath surface varies dramatically. From Marsworth to Tring it's compacted gravel, fine in any weather. The middle section goes to bare earth that would be ankle-deep in mud after rain. August was dry enough to be no problem.

Berkhamsted town centre is fine for a coffee and a train back. The Gatsby café near the station was quiet and the flat white was competently made.

Canal & River Trust has the towpath maps: [canalrivertrust.org.uk](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/enjoy-the-waterways/walking)
