# Recipe — slow-roast lamb shoulder

The recipe for occasions where you have five hours and a shoulder of lamb.

## Why shoulder over leg

Shoulder has more connective tissue and fat than leg. At low temperatures, these slowly convert into gelatin, which produces the self-basting, fall-apart texture that makes slow-roast lamb worth doing. Leg is for hot-and-fast; shoulder is for patience.

## Ingredients (serves 6)

- 1 whole lamb shoulder, bone in (around 2-2.5kg)
- 8 garlic cloves
- 3 anchovy fillets
- 2 tsp dried rosemary (or fresh sprigs)
- 2 tsp dried thyme
- Zest of one lemon
- Olive oil, salt, pepper
- 300ml white wine
- 1 onion, halved (for the roasting tin)

## Method

1. The day before (optional but good): make a paste of garlic, anchovies, herbs, lemon zest, and olive oil. Using a small knife, make incisions all over the lamb and push the paste in. Rub remaining paste over the surface. Refrigerate overnight uncovered.

2. Remove lamb from fridge 1 hour before cooking. Preheat oven to 160°C (fan).

3. Place onion halves in roasting tin, lamb on top. Pour wine into the tin.

4. Cover tightly with foil. Roast 4-5 hours until the meat pulls away from the bone without resistance.

5. Remove foil, increase heat to 220°C, roast 15-20 minutes to colour the surface.

6. Rest 20 minutes before serving.

The juices in the tin are the gravy. Skim fat, reduce if needed. Serve with anything soft — roasted aubergine, white beans, flatbreads.
