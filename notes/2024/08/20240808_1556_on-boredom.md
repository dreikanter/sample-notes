# On boredom

The no-news week I promised myself in August is now in day six. Some observations.

The 40-minute estimate was accurate in total but wrong in distribution. The time doesn't come back in big blocks — it comes back in the small moments when I would previously have reached for the phone to check headlines. These moments are more numerous than I expected. Dozens per day.

What have I done with these moments? Mostly: looked out of windows. Thought about what I was actually doing rather than reaching for a context switch. Let my mind wander before pulling it back. These are not impressive activities, but they seem to produce something useful — a kind of low-grade processing that you can't do while reading.

The odd thing is the quality of information I've retained. Events that filtered through — a friend mentioning something, a headline visible on a shop's TV — stuck in a way that things I actively sought out rarely do.

I don't think news abstinence is a serious civic position. Being informed matters and information has to come from somewhere. But the *rate* of information ingestion seems important and mostly unconsidered. We've designed delivery systems that maximize frequency and minimize reflection time, and then we're surprised that the information doesn't feel useful.

At the end of the week I'll go back. But probably not to the same consumption rate.

Related thoughts on media and attention: [Rolf Dobelli's *Stop Reading the News*](https://www.dobelli.com/en/books/stop-reading-the-news/) is the polemic version of this; worth reading critically.
