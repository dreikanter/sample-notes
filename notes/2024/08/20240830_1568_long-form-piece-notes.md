# Long-form piece — notes and outline

The piece I committed to writing in August. Topic decided: the way that software systems accumulate social assumptions in ways that become invisible over time. Not a technical piece — more an essay.

## The argument

Software encodes the assumptions of the people who built it at the time they built it. These assumptions become structural — they shape what's easy and hard, possible and impossible, for everyone who uses the system later. Most of this happens without intention or notice.

The interesting cases are the ones where the assumptions become visible: when a system built by one group is used by another group for whom the assumptions don't hold. Phone auto-correct trained on typing patterns from one demographic failing on names from another. Date systems that assume a particular calendar. Financial systems that assume credit history starts at a predictable age.

## Structure I'm drafting

1. Opening: a specific case (probably the phone example — concrete and relatable)
2. The general principle: Ursula Franklin on "practice" vs "production" technologies
3. Software as a crystallized moment in time
4. The problem with "neutral" — the absence of intentional bias is not neutrality
5. What the alternative looks like: not a solution, but a posture
6. Closing: not optimistic, not pessimistic — curious
