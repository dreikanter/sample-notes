# Fermented hot sauce — first attempt

Following the kimchi success [[20240712_1538]], trying lacto-fermented hot sauce. Different fermentation context but the same microbiology.

## What I made

- 400g mixed red chillies (cayenne and a milder Anaheim variety for body)
- 8g salt (2% of total weight)
- 3 garlic cloves
- ½ onion, roughly chopped

## Method

Blended everything together (intentionally rustic — not smooth). Mixed thoroughly, packed into a 500ml jar, pushed below the liquid line as best I could. The mix is too thick to have a true brine; I'll be relying on the natural moisture in the vegetables.

Left with a loose lid to allow gas to escape. The mixture is generating CO2 already at day 2.

## Expected timeline

Fermented at room temperature 5-7 days, then blend more thoroughly, strain through a coarse sieve, bottle.

## Why lacto-fermented vs fresh

Lacto-fermentation develops complexity — sourness, depth, a funkiness that raw blended chilli sauces don't have. Commercial examples include Tabasco (aged in oak barrels, 3 years) and Crystal.

## Day 2 observations

Bubbling visible when I pressed the top of the jar. Smells bright and slightly sour already. Colour has deepened slightly.

[Fermentation recipe at Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/fermented-hot-sauce-recipe) for comparison — their ratio uses more salt at 3%.
