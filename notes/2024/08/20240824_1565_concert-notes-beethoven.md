# Concert notes — Beethoven at the Barbican

Fifth and Seventh symphonies on the same evening. Orchestra was the LSO under a guest conductor I hadn't heard before — Mirga Gražinytė-Tyla, apparently the former chief conductor of CBSO.

## The Fifth

The Fifth is the most performed symphony in the western canon and carries that weight. Everyone has heard the opening four notes so many times that there's a real risk of hearing them as a cultural artefact rather than music. What Gražinytė-Tyla did — slower than I expected in the opening, pushing into the transition to the fourth movement rather than easing through it — made the familiar feel reoriented.

The brass in the fourth movement were very close to the edge of what the room could hold. This was not a mistake.

## The Seventh

Less famous but possibly the better piece. Wagner called it "the apotheosis of the dance." He was not wrong. The second movement (Allegretto) is one of the most precisely engineered pieces of music I know — the rhythmic pattern is established and then elaborated with complete inevitability, like watching a proof.

The finale is nearly unhinged in its energy. At the tempo Gražinytė-Tyla chose, it felt like the orchestra was barely holding on, which is the right feeling.

## General

The Barbican Hall has mixed acoustics by reputation. I was in the stalls and found it excellent. The seats are not comfortable for 2.5 hours but you forget.
