# Late May garden update

Two weeks since the transplants went in. Running assessment.

**Tomatoes:**
All nine plants survived transplanting — this is better than my usual 80% survival rate after transplanting, probably from the extended hardening-off period. The Romas have set their first true flower clusters. The beefsteak types are still in vegetative growth but look healthy.

First sign of early blight on the lower leaves of two plants — tiny yellowing spots with dark borders. Removed the affected leaves (bagged, not composted), applied copper fungicide spray as a preventive. This always shows up eventually; catching it early is what matters.

**Peppers:** Growing slowly but visibly. Peppers are more sensitive to cold nights than tomatoes and we had two nights at 6°C in the second week. They yellowed slightly but are recovering. Added a fleece cover that I leave on until 10am.

**Garlic:** Now 45cm tall. The scapes were removed last week (used in pesto — [[20240509_1507]]). The leaves are yellowing from the bottom up, which is normal at this stage — the plant is directing energy into the bulb. Harvest is probably 4-5 weeks away when 60% of the leaves have browned.

**Spinach:** Beginning to bolt (send up flower stalks) in the warmer temperatures. Harvested all remaining leaves. The plants will get bitter and tough quickly once bolting starts. Will pull and replant with something heat-tolerant.

**Radishes:** First harvest — seven radishes, crisp and sharp. The variety is French Breakfast type. Replanted immediately in the same space.

Good resource on disease management: https://www.extension.psu.edu/tomato-diseases
