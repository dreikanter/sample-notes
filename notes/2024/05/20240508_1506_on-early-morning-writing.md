# On early morning writing

Been doing this semi-consistently for two months now, so I have some actual data rather than theory.

What works: the window from 6:00 to 7:30am, before the first work messages arrive and before the day's shape is determined. The writing in this window is different from evening writing — less polished, more exploratory, occasionally more honest. I think the difference is that the brain hasn't yet been organized by the day's inputs. In the evening I'm recapping what I thought; in the morning I'm actually thinking.

What doesn't work: having a specific subject in mind. The morning writing sessions that begin "I'm going to write about X" often collapse quickly. The ones that begin with a loose observation ("the light is doing something interesting") or a question ("why did that conversation feel wrong?") tend to go somewhere.

The notebook vs. digital question: I've been doing this on paper, which feels important but I can't fully explain why. Maybe the permanence of ink — you can't delete, only cross out, which commits you to the thought even when it's half-formed. Digital writing has more editing anxiety even when you tell yourself not to edit.

Length: 400-600 words feels natural. Less feels like warming up; more starts to feel like work. The stopping point is usually when I've gotten somewhere I wasn't expecting to get.

The connection to the notes practice (see [[20240316_1433]]): morning writing is generative; evening notes are archival. They do different things and interact interestingly — morning thoughts occasionally become evening notes, but they're not the same impulse.
