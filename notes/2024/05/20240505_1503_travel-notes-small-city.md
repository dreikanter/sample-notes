# Travel notes — visiting a smaller city

Two nights in a city I've passed through but never stopped in — population about 180,000, a former manufacturing center that made some kind of successful transition in the 1990s that most comparable cities didn't. The evidence of this everywhere: a renovated waterfront, several good restaurants, a thriving small museum.

What struck me about the scale: you can walk from one end of the meaningful part to the other in forty minutes. The city is compact enough to be comprehensible as a spatial object in a way that larger cities aren't. After a day I had a rough mental map. After two days I felt like I understood the neighborhood structure.

The food scene is interesting specifically because the absence of food media attention means that the restaurants haven't calibrated their ambitions to external audiences. A Peruvian place in a strip mall near the university was extraordinarily good and clearly not performing for anyone. The lamb dish was a grandmother's recipe from a region I'd never heard of and the owner was happy to explain it for ten minutes.

The hotel was a renovated mid-century building that had been a law office. The room had original terrazzo floors and large windows. Slightly overpriced for what it was but worth it for the character.

The museum had a permanent collection that was ambitious for the size of the institution: a floor of regional art from 1880-1960, a changing exhibition of contemporary work. The contemporary show was by a local artist I had no frame of reference for, and I found it genuinely absorbing.

The danger of cities like this: arriving with expectations from larger cities and missing what they offer on their own terms.
