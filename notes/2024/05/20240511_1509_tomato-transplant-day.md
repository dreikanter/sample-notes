# Tomato transplant day

Last frost date is technically May 15 here, but the forecast shows overnight lows staying above 7°C for the next two weeks and I've been hardening the seedlings for a week now. Transplanted today — May 11.

**What went in:**
- 6 Roma tomatoes, staggered between the wire cages from last year
- 3 beefsteak (saved seed type), positioned to the south end where they'll get more sun
- 4 sweet peppers — these go in later than tomatoes optimally, but the temperature looks right

**Method:**
Each plant went in deep — buried to within 5cm of the first true leaves, which on the tallest plants meant going 20cm down. Dug each hole, added a handful of compost, a teaspoon of bone meal, watered the hole before planting, set plant, backfilled, watered again. The "water the hole" step feels fussy but keeps the soil from pulling moisture from the transplant during establishment.

Staked the peppers immediately (they're floppy seedlings). Will stake tomatoes when they're 30cm and more in need.

**The hardening-off effect:** These seedlings were outside in a sheltered spot for 5-6 hours a day for the past week, increasing outdoor time gradually. The stem color has changed — purple-tinged from UV exposure, which indicates the stress responses are active and the plant is ready for outdoor conditions. Unhardened transplants would show transplant shock; these looked barely disturbed within an hour.

**Watering:** Will water deeply twice this first week, then reduce frequency to encourage deeper rooting.
