# Music — ambient listening notes

Went through a run of ambient music in the evenings this week. Notes on what I found.

**Brian Eno — Ambient 1: Music for Airports (1978):** The ur-text. Eno's liner note explains the intention: music that "can be either actively listened to or ignored, depending on the state of mind of the listener." The recordings are tape loops of different lengths, layered so they shift in phase with each other. Simple piano notes and processed voices repeating with variation. It creates a temporal texture rather than progression — you can listen for five minutes or an hour and not feel like you've missed or finished something.

**William Basinski — The Disintegration Loops (2002):** This one has a famous origin story: Basinski was digitizing old magnetic tape recordings when the oxide began flaking off the tape as it played, progressively destroying the recording as it was made. The result: loops that degrade in real time, becoming shorter, more corrupted, finally just noise. The loops started as a kind of melancholy beauty and ended as silence. Recorded on September 11, 2001 — Basinski watched the World Trade Center burn from his rooftop while the tapes played.

**Grouper — Dragging a Dead Deer Up a Hill (2008):** The most immediately emotional of the three. Liz Harris's voice treated with heavy reverb until it becomes an instrument blending with the guitar. The lyrics are barely legible, which is probably intentional — the overall effect is more like weather than music. Sad in a diffuse way.

All three are best experienced without parallel tasks competing for attention. Not background music but music that changes what you're present to.
