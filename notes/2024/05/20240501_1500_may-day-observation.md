# May day observation

May 1 and it's warm enough to sit outside with coffee for the first time this year. The maple in the back has leafed out almost completely in the past four days — the acceleration at this point in spring is remarkable. A week ago it was ten percent leaves; today it's eighty.

The quality of birdsong in early May is different from March or April. In March you have a few species establishing territory; in May the sound is layered and constant. From the back step this morning I could distinguish at least eight species without moving: Robin, Cardinal, House Wren (arrived yesterday apparently), Warbling Vireo from somewhere in the maple, a Yellow Warbler in the shrubs along the fence, Song Sparrow, Common Grackle, and Red-winged Blackbird from the distance.

The garden is looking good. The spinach and radishes planted outside two weeks ago are three centimeters tall and obviously viable. The radishes in particular are fast — I can watch them change day to day. The garlic has reached 35cm and a few of the bulbs are sending up scapes, which is early.

Made coffee with the new coffee and let it sit on the back step until it was cool. The season-transition moment where you stop wanting everything hot is difficult to place exactly, but today might be it.

What I notice on the first real outdoor morning: how completely inside-adapted my body has become. The sounds and smells of outside are briefly overwhelming in a pleasant way before the adjustment happens.
