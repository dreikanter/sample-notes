# Celery seed ferment experiment

Tried a fermented celery recipe from a book I borrowed — the idea being that lacto-fermented celery produces a natural source of the flavor compounds in celery seed but with additional brightness from the lactic acid.

**Batch:** 4 stalks celery (roughly 400g), sliced 2cm on the diagonal, with leaves.
**Brine:** 2% salt by weight (8g to 400g water — submerged weight method).
**Additions:** 1 tsp celery seed (yes, adding celery seed to celery), 1 clove garlic.

Packed into a 1L jar, covered with brine, weighted down with a small jar filled with water. Left at room temperature (20°C).

**Day 2:** Bubbling actively. Smells clean and faintly of celery. The color has shifted from bright green to a slightly yellowed green.
**Day 4:** Tasted — pleasantly sour, still with crunch, the celery flavor concentrated and complex. Slightly more salt than I'd want to eat much of on its own.
**Day 5:** Moved to fridge.

**Uses tested:**
- Chopped into potato salad: excellent, replaced both celery and the vinegar
- In a tuna salad: good, the sourness brightened everything
- Plain as a snack: fine but the saltiness limits how much I want
- In a braise: added with the vegetables in a braised chicken, gave a background complexity

Verdict: worth making occasionally. The brine is also useful — a few tablespoons in a Bloody Mary was very good.

Lacto-fermentation reference: https://www.wildfermentation.com/introduction-to-lacto-fermentation/
