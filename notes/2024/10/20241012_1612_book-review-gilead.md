# Book review — Gilead by Marilynne Robinson

Read over the past two weeks. First book on the autumn list [[20241012_1597]] and I didn't expect it to be this.

## What it is

A letter from a dying Iowa minister, John Ames, to his young son. Written in 1956 in the voice of a man who knows he won't see his son grow up. The form is chosen perfectly — the letter to a child who can't yet read it means the narrator is simultaneously direct and free from the anxiety of the reader's response.

## What it does

The novel doesn't have plot in any conventional sense. It has movement — toward death, toward a reckoning with an estrangement, toward an understanding of grace. Robinson is a Calvinist theologian as well as a novelist, and the theological thinking in the book is not decorative. Ames thinks carefully and seriously about predestination, grace, and the problem of a just God allowing suffering, and these aren't set pieces — they're character.

## What surprised me

The humor. Ames is gentle and funny in a dry way that I didn't expect from a book described primarily in terms of its gravity. The story of his grandfather and his father is almost picaresque in places.

## The prose

Sentence by sentence, this is among the most carefully written novels I've read. Not ornate — the opposite. Sentences are usually simple in structure and absolutely precise in weight.

**Rating:** 5/5. Going immediately to the shelves next to *The Dispossessed* [[20240708_1534]].

[Robinson's Paris Review interview](https://www.theparisreview.org/) is essential reading alongside the book.
