# Coffee roast level comparison notes

A friend who works at a specialty roastery gave me a three-way sample: the same Ethiopian Yirgacheffe beans roasted to light, medium, and dark. This is a useful comparison to do at least once — the roast variable is normally confounded by variety and origin.

## Light roast (18-22% weight loss, ~195°C)

**In the cup:** Bright, high acidity, floral. The famous Yirgacheffe blueberry note is very present. Also jasmine, light stone fruit.

**Mouthfeel:** Light-bodied, almost tea-like at this extraction.

**Verdict:** Requires a specific kind of attention. Best at morning with nothing competing.

## Medium roast (~20-24% weight loss, ~205°C)

**In the cup:** Some acidity retained but darker fruit comes forward — dried cherry, a hint of chocolate. More balanced than the light.

**Mouthfeel:** Fuller body, more comfortable to most drinkers.

**Verdict:** The "correct" roast level for this origin, in my opinion. Everything works together.

## Dark roast (~26-28% weight loss, ~220°C+)

**In the cup:** Roast flavors dominate — bitter, smoky, low acidity. The origin characteristics almost gone.

**Mouthfeel:** Full, slightly oily.

**Verdict:** For this origin, a mistake — you're paying for Yirgacheffe and tasting roast. Dark roast can be excellent for other purposes (espresso blends, French press) but it hides what makes single-origin interesting.

[Sweet Maria's](https://www.sweetmarias.com/) has excellent educational resources on roast levels if you want the technical detail.
