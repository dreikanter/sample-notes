# Coffee brewing variables

Notes from experimenting with a Hario V60 over the past few months. Trying to systematise what actually changes the cup.

## The main variables

**Grind size** is the most impactful. Too coarse → sour, underdeveloped. Too fine → bitter, astringent. With medium-roast single origin I'm landing around 25 on a Comandante C40 for V60 (20g coffee, 300g water).

**Water temperature** matters more than I expected. The conventional 92–94°C range is a reasonable default, but lighter roasts extract better at higher temps — closer to 96°C. Darker roasts can go lower. The [James Hoffmann blog](https://www.jameshoffmann.co.uk/coffee) has some useful breakdowns on this.

**Bloom**: 40–60g of water for 30–45 seconds. Lets CO2 off-gas so extraction is more even. Fresh coffee blooms dramatically; older coffee barely stirs. The bloom tells you a lot about roast freshness.

**Pour pattern**: Concentric circles outward from centre, then back in. I've tried aggressive pours and gentle spirals — the difference is real but smaller than grind and temp.

**Total brew time**: 2:30–3:00 for a 15:1 ratio V60. If it's running long, grind coarser. If it's draining too fast, go finer.

## Current favourite recipe

- 20g medium-light roast (currently Ethiopia Yirgacheffe from a local roaster)
- 300g water at 94°C
- Bloom 60g / 40s
- Second pour to 180g at 1:00
- Final pour to 300g at 1:45
- Drain by 3:00

The result is bright, with stone fruit and a clean finish. Same coffee as espresso is much harder to dial in.
