# Autumn foraging notes

Spent three hours this afternoon in the woods east of town. The rain last week seems to have triggered a late flush of oyster mushrooms on a fallen elm I've been watching since August. Pulled about 400 grams — enough for a decent pasta. The fronds were pale grey with that faint anise smell that's a reliable tell.

Saw what looked like giant puffballs near the meadow edge but left them alone since I wasn't certain enough. [First Nature's puffball guide](https://www.first-nature.com/fungi/calvatia-gigantea.php) is worth reviewing before next time. Brought home three clusters of wood blewit too, identified by the lilac gills and the sweet, faintly floral scent.

The hawthorn berries are still hanging in good numbers. They're fully ripe now — picked a carrier bag full and will try making hawthorn ketchup this weekend following a recipe I bookmarked from the River Cottage handbook online. Last year's batch was too thick; I'll add more apple cider vinegar this time.

Notes for next visit:
- Check the big beech near the stream for beech tuft clusters
- Bring paper bags rather than plastic — mushrooms sweat badly in plastic
- Start earlier; light was going by 4:30 pm

Weather is turning cold faster than expected this year. Might only have two or three good foraging windows left before the first hard frost closes things down for the season.
