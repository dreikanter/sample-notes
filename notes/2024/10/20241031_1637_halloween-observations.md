# Halloween observations

First year in the new flat and the first year I've actually had trick-or-treaters in about a decade. Bought a bag of mixed sweets, put a light on the porch — had maybe fifteen kids over the course of two hours, 6–8pm.

The costumes were mostly conventional: a few vampires, a skeleton, two witches, one very committed spider. One child was dressed as what appeared to be a photocopier, which is either a profound statement or a parent running low on ideas at 10pm the night before.

The social choreography of trick-or-treating is interesting. The youngest kids come with parents and barely speak — they just hold out their bags. The 9–11 cohort have usually been coached to say "trick or treat" and "thank you" and do so with a kind of earnest formality. The older ones, 12–13, have the self-conscious air of people who know this is slightly silly but haven't quite outgrown it.

Halloween in the UK has always been imported and slightly uneasy — the [history of Halloween in Britain](https://www.historic-uk.com/CultureUK/Halloween-in-Britain/) is genuinely complicated, involving a palimpsest of Celtic Samhain, Catholic All Hallows, and American commercial influence. The result is something that feels simultaneously ancient and entirely modern.

Made a simple dinner: roasted pumpkin soup with the flesh from the carved lantern. Mixed results — lantern pumpkins aren't bred for flavour, they're bred for size and wall thickness. Next year I'll get a Hokkaido or Crown Prince for eating and a lantern pumpkin separately.

The evening felt neighbourly in an unexpected way.
