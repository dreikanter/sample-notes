# Film review — Past Lives

Director: Celine Song. Watched on a Sunday evening, feeling the right kind of melancholy for it.

*Past Lives* is a film about the distance between the lives we live and the lives we might have lived, and it handles that premise with a restraint that most films about longing can't manage. The central triangle — Nora, Hae Sung, Arthur — is never framed as a love triangle in any conventional sense. Arthur knows what he represents to Nora and Hae Sung, and his conversation with Hae Sung near the end is one of the more generous and psychologically real scenes I've seen in a drama in years.

Greta Lee is extraordinary throughout. The scene at the airport at the end of the first act is doing so much work with so little.

The "in-yeon" concept — the Korean idea that people who encounter each other are connected across many past lives — is introduced without being overexplained, which is the right call. The film doesn't commit to it as literal metaphysics but uses it as a frame that gives the characters a language for something they couldn't otherwise say.

What I found most surprising was how the film made me think about immigration as a kind of temporal as well as geographical rupture. The version of Nora that Hae Sung knows stopped existing when she left Seoul at 12. The woman he meets in New York is continuous with that girl but is also someone else. That's not a metaphor — it's something real about what leaving a place does to a person.

More on the film: [Roger Ebert review by Sheila O'Malley](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/past-lives-2023)

A beautiful, quiet film. Would watch again.
