# Mushroom foraging walk — October

Went on a guided foraging walk this morning in Epping Forest. About twelve people in the group, led by a mycologist named David who carries a field guide he's been annotating for twenty years.

## What we found

**Ceps (Boletus edulis):** Three specimens, found by a woman in the group who spotted the characteristic shape under leaf litter. David demonstrated the key identification features — pores rather than gills, white pore layer not yellow, no blueing when cut. These are among the best edible mushrooms in Europe and I've been walking past them for years without recognizing them.

**Chanterelles (Cantharellus cibarius):** Several clusters. The egg-yolk orange is striking once your eye is trained. False gills (forked ridges rather than true gills) is the key distinguishing feature from the lookalike jack-o'-lantern mushroom.

**Shaggy inkcap (Coprinus comatus):** Don't eat with alcohol apparently — produces a compound that reacts with alcohol to cause unpleasant symptoms. Interesting to know.

**Fly agaric (Amanita muscaria):** Classic red with white spots. Toxic. Several in the group photographed it; no one touched. David mentioned it's rarely fatal in adults but makes you very unwell.

## What I took home

Three ceps and a good handful of chanterelles. Sautéed in butter with garlic and thyme tonight. The cep has a depth of flavour that cultivated mushrooms don't have.
