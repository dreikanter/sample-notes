# Journal — late October

The light changes so fast at this time of year. By 5pm it's fully dark, and even at midday the sun barely clears the roofline across the street. This is the thing about autumn I can never quite remember when summer is long and endless: the contraction is fast. One week you have afternoon light; the next week you don't.

I've been feeling a kind of productive restlessness that I associate with this season. Something about the shortening days makes the hours feel more finite, which ought to be discouraging but instead tends to sharpen focus. There's academic work on seasonal effects on cognition — the [Seasonality of Mood research](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/seasonal-affective-disorder) at NIMH suggests it's not just anecdote — but I'm not sure whether what I'm experiencing is affective or just a change in how I'm using the evening hours.

Reading more. Cooking more elaborate things. Going to bed earlier. The sourdough project ([[20241015_1620]]) has been satisfying in a way that is partly about the bread and partly about having a slow, physical thing to attend to alongside the abstract work of writing and debugging.

One thing I've been thinking about: the relationship between maintenance and creation. A lot of what keeps a life running well is maintenance — physical, relational, domestic, technical. It doesn't generate anything new but it's not nothing. I think I used to undervalue it. Right now the maintenance feels like the texture of things rather than an obstacle to some cleaner life beyond it.

Going to make a proper Sunday roast tomorrow. Chicken, roasted root vegetables, gravy from scratch.
