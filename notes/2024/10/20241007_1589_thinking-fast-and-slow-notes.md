# Reading notes — Thinking, Fast and Slow

Finally done. Third attempt, successful. The middle sections (on judgment heuristics) are genuinely slower going but worth it.

## The core framework

System 1: fast, automatic, associative, emotional, unconscious. Generates impressions and intuitions.

System 2: slow, effortful, deliberate, rule-governed. Monitors, endorses, or overrides System 1.

The crucial insight: System 2 is lazy. It often endorses System 1's output without checking. This is how cognitive biases operate — not as occasional errors but as the default mode.

## The parts that changed my thinking

**The planning fallacy.** We systematically underestimate how long projects take because we construct detailed inside-view narratives (everything goes well) rather than using base rates (how long have similar projects actually taken?). The fix: reference class forecasting — ask how long comparable projects took, not how long this one seems like it should take.

**Anchoring.** Even clearly arbitrary numbers affect subsequent estimates. Someone asked to spin a wheel before estimating a quantity is influenced by the wheel's result. This is uncomfortable because it applies to me, in situations where I think I'm thinking.

**The experiencing self vs the remembering self.** We evaluate experiences by their peaks and their ends, not their duration. A short vacation ending on a high is remembered better than a longer one that ended badly. This has implications for how I should actually structure good experiences.

## What I'd say to someone considering reading it

The best parts are the first 200 and last 100 pages. The middle is worthwhile but you have permission to read it slower.
