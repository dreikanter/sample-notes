# Sourdough starter log

Documenting the state of the starter after reviving it from the fridge. It had been dormant for about six weeks.

**Day 1 (Oct 13):** Discarded all but 50g. Fed with 50g whole wheat flour and 50g water at room temp (20°C). Smell was quite acidic — cheesy and sharp. No visible activity by evening.

**Day 2 (Oct 14):** Slight bubbling by morning, maybe 10% rise. Fed again at the same ratio. Still a bit flat by afternoon. The [Tartine Bakery blog](https://www.tartinebakery.com/stories/country-bread) recommends patience here — a cold-dormant starter can take 3-4 days to come back.

**Day 3 (Oct 15):** Much better. Starter doubled between the 6am and 12pm marks. Smell has shifted from acidic to yeasty and fruity — a good sign. Did a float test: a small spoonful floated easily. Ready to bake.

**Notes on the float test**: Controversial. Some bakers swear by it; others say it's unreliable. My experience: it works well as a rough indicator but doesn't account for hydration. A 65% hydration starter will float more easily than an 80% one at the same level of fermentation.

**Planned bake**: Basic country loaf. 450g bread flour, 50g whole wheat, 375g water, 100g levain, 10g salt. Autolyse 45 minutes, then add levain and salt. Bulk ferment at room temp, four stretch-and-folds, overnight cold proof.
