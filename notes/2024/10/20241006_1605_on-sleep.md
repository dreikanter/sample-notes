# On sleep — what I've changed and why

Read Matthew Walker's *Why We Sleep* about four months ago. Applied some things, ignored others, formed a more nuanced view than the book itself takes.

## What I changed

**Consistent wake time.** Even on weekends. The research on social jet lag is compelling — your circadian rhythm anchors to wake time more than to bedtime. I now wake at 6:45am seven days a week. Going to bed varies by up to an hour; the wake time doesn't.

**Temperature.** The bedroom is cooler than it was. Sleep onset and quality improve with lower core body temperature. I'd been sleeping in a slightly warm room for years.

**No screens 30-40 minutes before bed.** I was skeptical but have to admit this has made a difference. The substitution is reading physical books, which I'd been doing less of. Two birds.

## What I tried and abandoned

**No caffeine after 2pm.** This made me miserable in the afternoons without clear sleep quality improvement. Walker is emphatic about this; I'm skeptical that the half-life of caffeine affects everyone equally. Moved the cutoff to 3:30pm as a compromise.

## What I think Walker gets wrong

The book reads as anxiety-maximizing in places. The evidence that occasional poor sleep is acutely catastrophic is weaker than the book implies. Chronic sleep deprivation is clearly harmful; stressing about a night of bad sleep (which makes sleep worse) seems like the wrong takeaway.

[Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/) for evidence-based basics without the hype.
