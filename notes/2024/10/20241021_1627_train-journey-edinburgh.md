# Train journey — Edinburgh to London

Took the LNER East Coast Main Line for the first time. Left Edinburgh Waverley at 9:30, arrived King's Cross at 14:05, so roughly four and a half hours with one unscheduled stop near Newcastle for track maintenance. The scheduled time is closer to four hours fifteen on a good day.

The journey north of Newcastle is genuinely beautiful. The train hugs the coast for a stretch, and on a clear October morning the North Sea is gunmetal with these long white wave-lines. Worth a window seat on the east side heading south.

Working on a train for four-plus hours is an acquired skill. I did about 90 minutes of useful focused work, then 45 minutes of drifting, then gave up and read for the last hour and a half. No regrets — the Le Guin was good enough that surrendering to it felt like the right call.

Food: the on-board catering is expensive and mediocre in the way of British train food. Brought my own: cheese sandwich, apple, dark chocolate. Ate the chocolate first. Classic error.

Cost: £62 advance ticket booked three weeks out. The walk-up fare was listed at £187 when I checked, which is roughly criminal for a domestic rail journey. The [Trainline fare comparison tool](https://www.thetrainline.com) is worth checking against directly booking with LNER — prices are usually identical but occasionally one has a sale the other doesn't.

The seat reservation system at Waverley was slightly broken — my booked seat was occupied and it took 20 minutes of awkward passenger diplomacy to sort out. Small grievance. Would still take the train over flying for this route any day.
