# Weekend market notes — October

October at the farmers' market is the height of the autumn season. Notes from this morning.

## What was there

**Root vegetables in abundance.** Varieties of carrot I don't normally see — Chantenay (the stubby round ones, very sweet), black carrots from one grower who had six colours laid out, purple and white. Bought a mixed kilo for no practical reason other than the colours.

**Squash and pumpkins.** The November wave is early. Crown Prince squash (the blue-grey ones with dense orange flesh) are excellent right now. The market price for these is dramatically better than supermarkets. Got a large one for £2.50.

**Apples.** Peak apple season. An elderly couple were selling directly from their orchard — about twenty varieties with handwritten labels. Bought Blenheim Orange (sharp, complex, brilliant for eating) and a bag of Cox's for the week. The Cox's were three days off the tree.

**Last of the summer things.** One stall still had courgettes and a few late peppers. These feel valedictory at this point in October.

## What I made with the market find

Crown Prince squash: halved, seeds removed, roasted face-down with olive oil for 45 minutes, then flesh scooped out and made into soup with ginger, garlic, and coconut milk. Best squash soup of the year. The Crown Prince variety has a density that cheaper pumpkins don't have.

Carrots: raw with hummus immediately. Will roast some later in the week with cumin and honey.
