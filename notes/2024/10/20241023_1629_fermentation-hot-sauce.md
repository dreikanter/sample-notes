# Fermented hot sauce — first attempt

Made a lacto-fermented hot sauce using the season's last capsicums from the garden. Roughly following the method in [Serious Eats' fermented hot sauce guide](https://www.seriouseats.com/how-to-make-fermented-hot-sauce).

## Ingredients used

- 400g mixed hot peppers (mostly cayenne, a few scotch bonnets from a friend's plant)
- 3 cloves garlic
- 1 small white onion, roughly chopped
- 3% brine by weight (so 12g salt dissolved in 400g water)
- Pinch of sugar to help kickstart fermentation

## Process

Everything went into a clean 1-litre jar after rough chopping — no need for fine cuts since it'll be blended. Submerged with a small zip-lock bag filled with brine acting as a weight. Covered with a cloth secured with a rubber band. Left at room temperature (around 19°C currently in the kitchen).

## Fermentation notes

**Day 1**: No visible activity. Brine is clear.  
**Day 2**: Tiny bubbles forming around garlic pieces. Smell is still raw pepper.  
**Day 3**: Active bubbling. Brine has gone slightly cloudy. Smell has shifted — tangy, yeasty, with the heat of the peppers still coming through.  
**Day 5**: Fermentation slowing. Tasted the brine — pleasantly sour, warm.  
**Day 7**: Blended everything including brine. Passed through a coarse sieve. Colour is deep orange-red. Added a tablespoon of apple cider vinegar to stabilise and brighten.

## Result

Hot, tangy, with a complexity that fresh hot sauce doesn't have. The fermentation adds something slightly funky and rounded. The scotch bonnets give real heat that builds slowly. Made about 350ml — will store in the fridge for up to six months.
