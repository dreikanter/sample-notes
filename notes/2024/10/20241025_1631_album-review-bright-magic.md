# Album review — Bright Magic by Anna von Hausswolff

Listened to this three times in a row on a rainy afternoon. *Bright Magic* (2023) is a live album recorded at the Gothenburg Concert Hall on a massive pipe organ — specifically the Acke organ, one of the largest in Scandinavia. The scale of the instrument defines everything.

Von Hausswolff has been building toward something like this for a while. *Dead Magic* (2018) was the pivot point where she leaned fully into the cathedral-drone space, but this recording has a physical presence that studio work can't replicate. The low-frequency content on the opening piece is genuinely unsettling — not in a horror-film way but in the way that some natural sounds are, the kind that your body registers before your brain does.

The programme mixes original compositions with pieces that feel like they've been excavated from some tradition that doesn't quite exist. There's a structural similarity to certain minimalist composers — Arvo Pärt's tintinnabuli technique comes to mind — but with more shadow in it.

The recording quality is exceptional. You can hear the hall's acoustic response, which becomes almost a second instrument. The reverb tail on some pieces runs to twenty seconds or more.

The [Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/anna-von-hausswolff-bright-magic/) is worth reading, though they underplay the physical-music-as-event quality that makes the live recording the right format for this.

Recommended listening context: headphones, dark room, no other tasks. Not background music. This demands attention and rewards it.
