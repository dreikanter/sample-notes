# Hot sauce result — tasting notes

Follow-up to [[20240828_1567]]. The hot sauce completed fermentation and has been processed.

## What happened during fermentation

Fermented 7 days at room temp. The surface got a thin white layer on day 4 — kahm yeast, not mould. Slightly alarming to look at but harmless. Scraped it off.

The smell evolved: day 1-2 was bright and raw, day 4-5 became noticeably sourer and slightly funky in a good way. By day 7 it smelled like something you'd want to eat.

## Processing

Blended smooth, pushed through a coarse sieve. Produced about 300ml of sauce. Added:
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar (for brightness and extra preservation)
- ½ tsp salt (tasted underseasoned after processing)

Bottled into a clean glass bottle with a tight cap.

## Tasting notes

**Immediate:** Bright, acidic, hot but not overwhelmingly so. The sour note is clearly present and distinct from vinegar — it's more rounded, deeper.

**Heat level:** Medium-hot. The cayenne provides a quick front heat; the Anaheim contribution keeps it from being one-dimensional.

**Complexity:** Yes, genuinely. Raw blended chilli sauce doesn't have this. The fermentation added a back note that's hard to describe but makes it more interesting.

**Uses so far:**
- On eggs (excellent)
- In mayo (transformed)
- On the shakshuka leftovers (correct decision)

**Rating:** Much better than expected for a first attempt. Will make again with adjustments — more garlic, try adding a roasted red pepper for body.
