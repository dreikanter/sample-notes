# Essay first draft — notes and problems

First complete draft done as of last night. 2,400 words. Too long for what it is but the right amount to have said what needs cutting.

## What's working

The opening case study is strong. The specific and concrete is better than I'd feared. Readers who've experienced the exact thing will recognize it immediately, and the specificity makes the general argument land harder.

The Ursula Franklin material (section 3) integrates well. The holistic/prescriptive distinction does useful work.

The pacing in the middle is correct — the argument builds in logical order.

## Problems identified

**Section 2 is doing too much.** It's trying to define "neutral technology" and simultaneously argue against the concept. These should be separate movements. The reader needs to understand the claim before it can be refuted.

**The ending is weak.** I land on "curiosity rather than optimism or pessimism" which is true but gestures at something rather than saying it. The final paragraph needs to be rewritten around a more specific image or example.

**The Ruha Benjamin material isn't integrated.** I read *Race After Technology* partly for this essay and then... didn't use it in any way that's visible. Either I need to use it or acknowledge that it shaped my thinking without appearing in the text. The latter is acceptable; the current muddle isn't.

**Word count:** 2,400 words is too long. Target is under 1,800 for the purpose.

## Process note

This draft took eleven days from outline to completion. Six of those days I wrote nothing. The writing happened in three concentrated sessions of 2-3 hours each. This is roughly normal for me and I should stop planning otherwise.
