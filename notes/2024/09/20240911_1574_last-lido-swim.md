# Last lido swim of the season

Made it. September 11th, 8am. The temperature board said 16°C and I believed it.

Thirty people in the water, maybe — the regulars plus a few others who'd had the same instinct about the season closing. The atmosphere at the lido in mid-September is different from July. Everyone in the water seems aware of being in the last stretch. Conversations happen between lengths that don't happen in peak summer; there's more acknowledgment between strangers.

The water at 16°C is qualitatively different from 19°C. The cold catches your breath on entry in a way that makes you immediately more present. Cognitive white noise disappears. You have to pay attention to what you're doing.

Did fifteen lengths, which is more than my usual twelve. Didn't want to stop. Got out when the cold in my hands became uncomfortable rather than bracing. Stood in the September sun with a towel, watching others in the water. A small child was shrieking with laughter in the shallow end. An elderly woman was doing a steady breaststroke with her head fully out of the water, moving through the lane with complete serenity.

The café was full. Flat white. Sat outside with it for twenty minutes, damp, warm enough.

This is one of the experiences where the anticipation is good and the thing itself is better, and the memory will be better still. This is the rarest kind.

[Serpentine Lido](https://www.royalparks.org.uk/parks/hyde-park/things-see-and-do/serpentine-lido) stays open a bit later — into October some years.
