# On autumn light

The equinox was yesterday. The days are mathematically equal to nights and then, from here, shorter. The light knows before the calendar does — it's been coming at a lower angle for weeks, making long shadows in the late afternoon that weren't there in July.

The autumn light in London has a particular quality: warmer in tone than summer, more golden at the edges of the day. It does something interesting to brick buildings — makes the red bricks look deeper, more saturated. Walking through the Victorian terraces in the early evening at this time of year is genuinely beautiful in a way that's easy to not notice when you live here.

I keep a small record of light. Not formally — just a note when the quality of it arrests me. Today: 5:30pm, cycling back from the allotment, sun about 15 degrees above the horizon, the rail bridge over the road caught the light on its steel girders and threw a kind of copper reflection onto the road beneath. Lasted maybe three minutes before the angle changed.

There's a whole literature on this, from Turner's obsession with light across water to the Japanese concept of *komorebi* (sunlight filtering through leaves). But the experience is simpler than literature — you either notice it or you don't, and noticing it costs nothing and makes the day sharper.
