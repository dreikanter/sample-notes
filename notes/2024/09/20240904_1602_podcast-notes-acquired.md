# Podcast notes — Acquired TSMC episode

The [Acquired podcast](https://www.acquired.fm/) did a multi-hour episode on TSMC and I've been listening in sections over the past week. Notes on what was most interesting.

## What TSMC actually does

TSMC is a "pure-play foundry" — it only manufactures chips, it doesn't design them. This sounds like a limiting model and turned out to be the entire point. By committing never to compete with its customers, TSMC built trust with every major chip designer in the world. Apple, AMD, Nvidia, Qualcomm — all TSMC customers.

## The Morris Chang story

Morris Chang founded TSMC in Taiwan in 1987 at age 55, after being passed over for the top job at Texas Instruments. The foundry model was considered eccentric at the time — conventional wisdom was that chip companies needed to do both design and manufacturing. He was right that the industry would separate.

## Why Taiwan specifically

Not an accident. Taiwan's government in the 1980s was making deliberate industrial policy decisions to develop advanced manufacturing capability. The combination of government support, trained engineering talent, and the foundry model created a cluster effect — suppliers, engineers, and customers all co-locating around TSMC.

## The geopolitical dimension

TSMC manufactures approximately 90% of the world's most advanced semiconductors. If production were interrupted for any reason — conflict, natural disaster, political disruption — the effect on the global electronics industry would be catastrophic within months. This fact is now structuring national policy in the US, EU, Japan, and elsewhere.

## What stuck with me

The "silicon shield" concept — the argument that Taiwan's semiconductor dominance is its own form of deterrence, because harming Taiwan means harming the global tech economy. Whether this actually works as deterrence is debated.
