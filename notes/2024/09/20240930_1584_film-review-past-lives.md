# Film review — Past Lives

Watched this alone on a Sunday evening. This was probably the right conditions for it.

Celine Song's first film is an exercise in the genre of restrained feeling — films where the significant thing is what's held back rather than what's expressed. The tradition includes Lean's *Brief Encounter*, Koreeda's films, and now this.

The premise: two Korean childhood friends reconnect online as adults, then in person in New York twenty years later. She is now married to an American man. The old friend has come to see her.

What Song does that's unusual: she films the American husband not as an obstacle but as a full human being in an impossible situation. The scene where all three sit in a bar late at night — the former childhood friends speaking Korean across the table, the husband sitting outside their language — is one of the most devastating things I've seen in a film in years, and it's devastating because of what the camera does with his face. He understands exactly what's happening. He's not angry. That's the worst part.

The concept of *in-yeon* (fate, connection across lives) is introduced early and given full weight by the end without the film pretending to resolve anything.

Running time: 105 minutes. Every one is used.

**Rating:** 5/5. A necessary film.
