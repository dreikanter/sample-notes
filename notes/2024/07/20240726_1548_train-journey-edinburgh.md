# Train journey to Edinburgh

Five and a half hours on the east coast mainline. One of the better uses of a Friday.

I've been on this route enough times that I know the sightlines — when to look east for the sea at Berwick, where the viaducts are, the moment the landscape shifts unmistakably into Scotland somewhere north of the border.

The carriage was quiet until York, when a school group boarded and arranged themselves loudly, then became completely absorbed in their phones and were quiet again. A woman across the aisle was doing needlework — I couldn't see the design but the motion was rhythmic and calming to watch. An elderly man in the seat behind me was telling someone on the phone about a dispute with his neighbour over a fence panel, in considerable and patient detail.

I read for about two hours and then did the thing I do on trains which is look out the window and think loosely. The coast north of Newcastle, when it appears — a sudden glint to the east — is one of the more unexpectedly moving things I know about Britain. The sea appears like a fact you'd forgotten.

Edinburgh Waverley sits in its valley below the castle. Every time I arrive I think it's the most dramatically situated station in the country, and I continue to think it's probably right.

Staying three days. Agenda light — two planned things and the rest unscheduled.
