# Fringe show notes — selected picks

Continuing from [[20240703_1057]]. These are rough impressions written the same evening, not considered reviews.

## What I saw

**Róisín Gallagher — "Adjacencies"** (Summerhall, Fri 5 Aug)
Forty-minute monologue about misidentification and bureaucratic error. Performed entirely seated. The stillness was deliberate and effective — the accumulating absurdity landed harder because she never played for laughs. Recommended without reservation.

**"The Cartographers" — unnamed devised company** (C Venues)
Three performers, no dialogue, maps as the central metaphor. Visually inventive in the first half; the second half didn't earn its emotional conclusion. Worth seeing if you like physical theatre and can forgive an unresolved ending.

**Stand-up showcase, late night slot at The Hive**
Mixed bill. The compère was better than three of the five acts. The standout — a woman whose surname I didn't catch, first name possibly Dervla — did ten minutes on GP waiting times that felt both locally specific and completely universal. No recording I can find online.

**"Soil"** (Traverse Theatre)
New writing, Irish production. About inheritance and what people assume land means to a family. Strong performances, slightly over-long, the final image was striking enough that I'm still thinking about it a week later. Transferred from a Dublin run earlier in the year.

## What I skipped that I probably shouldn't have

A show at Summerhall called "Negative Space" kept coming up in conversations. By the time I tried to book, sold out for the remaining run.
