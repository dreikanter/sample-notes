# Reading notes — The Dispossessed

Finished *The Dispossessed* by Le Guin last night. Started it as a book on my summer list [[20240625_1516]] and ended up reading the last 150 pages in a single session.

## What it's doing

This is a physics novel wearing science fiction clothing wearing political philosophy clothing. The protagonist Shevek is a physicist trying to complete a unified theory of time (the General Temporal Theory), and Le Guin uses the structure of the novel — alternating chapters between Shevek's past on Anarres and his present on Urras — to literally enact simultaneity as a theme. Form and content are doing the same work.

## The politics

Anarres is an anarchist society and Le Guin refuses to idealize it. The book's honesty is in showing how social pressure, conformity, and a different kind of scarcity can constrain freedom as effectively as state power. Shevek's enemy on Anarres isn't the government — it doesn't have one — but collective inertia and the committee of peers who've decided his work is dangerous.

Urras is capitalism/state socialism and Le Guin shows both as corrupt but not cartoonishly so. The intellectuals of Urras who help Shevek are genuinely well-meaning and still complicit.

## Best passage

The sequence where Shevek realizes he's been sponsored by the military-adjacent foundation. His crisis is quiet and very true.

## Rating

5/5. One of the best SF novels I've read. Immediately going on the permanent shelf.
