# Reflections on remote work — three years in

Three years since I moved to fully remote. The things I thought would be problems weren't; the things I didn't anticipate were.

**What I got wrong initially:**

I thought I'd miss the office environment for collaboration. I don't. Async communication, when done well, is actually better for collaborative thinking because it forces you to write things down. Written proposals get proper scrutiny in a way that whiteboard sessions sometimes don't.

I thought I'd struggle with boundaries. This was true in year one and not true now. The calendar disciplines you into it eventually.

**What I didn't anticipate:**

The slow erosion of ambient professional identity. In an office you're visibly a professional all day. At home you're just a person who sometimes works intensely and then makes tea in their kitchen. This isn't bad — but it requires conscious effort to maintain a sense of professional trajectory when you don't have the social scaffolding of a workplace.

Also: the absence of physical movement between contexts. Commutes are bad but they serve a transition function. I've replaced mine with a short walk before starting work, which helps.

**What works well:**

Deep focus is genuinely better. I can get into a state of concentration that was simply unavailable in an open-plan office. My best technical work has been done in the last three years.

Cal Newport's [writing on deep work](https://www.calnewport.com/books/deep-work/) became practically relevant to me in ways it wasn't when I read it before going remote.
