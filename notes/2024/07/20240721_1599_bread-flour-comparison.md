# Bread flour comparison notes

Following up the sourdough troubleshooting note from June [[20240625_1517]]. The new Shipton Mill flour arrived and I've been baking with it for two weeks. Documenting the differences.

## Flours tested

**Supermarket bread flour (previous):**
- Protein: 11.2%
- Price: approximately £1.20/kg
- Milling: roller-milled
- Performance: adequate but required longer fermentation, less extensible dough

**Shipton Mill No. 4 Strong White Flour:**
- Protein: 13.5%
- Price: approximately £2.10/kg (direct from mill at 5kg)
- Milling: stone-ground (partially wholegrain content retained)
- Performance: significantly better

## Observable differences

The dough with the Shipton Mill flour is more extensible without tearing. I can do the stretch-and-fold stages longer without the gluten breaking. This translates to better gas retention and more open crumb.

Fermentation timing is more predictable — the starter behaves more consistently with a higher-protein flour. The bulk fermentation runs 4-5 hours at 22°C now instead of the 6-8 I needed before.

The crust has more colour with the partial wholegrain content — the Maillard reaction products are more complex.

## Cost-benefit

The premium flour costs 75% more per kg. Given that a single loaf uses 450g, the cost difference per loaf is about 40p. Given the quality improvement, this is obviously worth it. I'd been applying false economy.

[Shipton Mill](https://www.shipton-mill.com/) sells direct and the quality is consistent.
