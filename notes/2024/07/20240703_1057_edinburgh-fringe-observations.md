# Edinburgh Fringe — first-timer observations

Arrived on day three of the Fringe, which apparently is still early enough that the city hasn't fully buckled. The Royal Mile is already a gauntlet of leaflet-thrusters. Some act immediately interesting, most clearly following a script written by someone who hates both the audience and themselves.

A few observations worth keeping:

**On choosing shows.** The star-rating system is nearly useless for theatre and spoken word — reviewers attend previews, often on comps, and the scoring compression means anything below three stars probably still has merit. Better heuristics: check whether the blurb actually describes the show's form, look at the venue (Summerhall and Traverse skew better on average), and trust word-of-mouth from people who dislike the same things you dislike.

**Timing.** Shows before noon tend to be either extremely confident acts who know their audience will seek them out, or desperate ones filling slots nobody else wanted. The 6–9 pm window is crowded but has the most variety. Late night after 10 pm is reliably weird in ways that are occasionally rewarding.

**The free Fringe.** PBH Free Fringe and Laughing Horse operate on a bucket-at-the-end model. The arrangement matters less than you'd think — I saw two of my favourite shows of the trip this way. Donations feel more honest than a £15 ticket for something that turns out to be a work-in-progress.

The Pleasance Courtyard is pleasant as a social hub but the bar prices are punitive. The Meadows is better for sitting between shows.
