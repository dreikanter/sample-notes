# Edinburgh notes

Three days. Two planned things (National Museum and a gallery in Leith), the rest improvised.

## National Museum of Scotland

Free entry and enormous. The Scotland gallery traces the country from geological formation through to devolution — ambitious scope, mostly well executed. The Jacobite section has the actual Bonnie Prince Charlie relics, which are in a glass case and somehow both smaller and more affecting than expected. An old man standing near me said quietly to his wife "this isn't his, they got this wrong" and then they moved on without elaboration. I've been thinking about that since.

The Lewis Chessmen are in London (British Museum claims them) but there are a few replica pieces here with good explanatory panels. The originals being disputed is its own story.

## The gallery in Leith

Found a small contemporary gallery that wasn't on my original list — passed it walking from the port. A show of large-scale textile work by a Ghanaian-Scottish artist named (based on the signage) Clara Boateng. Very good. The relationship between the weaving patterns and digital pixel grids was explicit in the artist statement and visible in the work.

## Odds and ends

- Arthur's Seat at 7am is completely different from Arthur's Seat at noon. The former has a dozen committed walkers; the latter has hundreds of tourists. Both versions of the hill exist simultaneously.
- Holyrood Palace is underrated as architecture. The tower on the far end from the main entrance is from the 15th century and sits slightly uneasily with the later baroque addition. I find this conjunction honest.

[VisitScotland](https://www.visitscotland.com/) has the official visitor guides but the real gems are in the smaller listings.
