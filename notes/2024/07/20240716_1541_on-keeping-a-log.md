# On keeping a log

It's been about fourteen months since I started keeping these notes consistently. Some observations on what's changed and what hasn't.

What's changed: I have a much better record of what I was actually thinking rather than what I thought I was thinking. This turns out to be quite different. Reading notes from October last year, I'm surprised by concerns that felt enormous at the time and resolved themselves without intervention, and surprised to find small observations I've since completely forgotten that would have been lost otherwise.

What hasn't changed: I still write in bursts. There are weeks with ten entries and weeks with one. The rhythm is irregular and I've stopped trying to fix that. The alternative — writing when I have nothing to say because the streak says I should — produces entries that are embarrassing to read later.

The best entries are observations: something I noticed, something I cooked, something that confused me about a piece of code. The worst are the ones that are trying to be profound. You can tell when a journal is performing.

I've been writing in plain text files for the whole fourteen months. The format forces brevity. There's no formatting bar to stare at, no font to choose. [Markdown](https://daringfireball.net/projects/markdown/) plus a good text editor is the whole stack. This won't suit everyone but it suits me.

The question I can't answer: whether the act of writing changes what I notice, or whether I'm just a person who notices things and the writing is secondary. Probably both, probably inseparable.
