# Short review — Piranesi by Susanna Clarke

Read this in three sittings. My sister was right.

The premise is disorienting in a way that's best not explained before you start — the less you know, the better. The novella-length format (295 pages) is exactly right: long enough to build real strangeness, short enough to read before the world breaks the spell.

Clarke's achievement here is doing more with voice than almost anything I've read recently. The narrator's journals are written in a tone that is simultaneously precise, innocent, and gradually, heartbreakingly strange. By the time you understand what you're reading, you're already implicated in caring about it.

The house — a vast, impossible architectural structure with statues, tides, and mysterious rules — is described with the loving detail of someone for whom it is simply home, not a mystery. That perspective reversal does a lot of work.

A note: if you read *Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell* first (Clarke's earlier novel), this will feel like a companion piece with a different emotional register. If you haven't read Strange, Piranesi will send you there. Either way is correct.

**Rating:** 5/5. A very rare thing: a novel that does exactly what it intends to do and does it completely.

[Susanna Clarke's website](https://www.susannaclarke.com/) has a small amount of context if you want it after reading.
