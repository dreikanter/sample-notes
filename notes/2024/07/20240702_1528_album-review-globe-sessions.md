# Album review — The Globe Sessions by Sheryl Crow

Revisited this record after not hearing it in probably fifteen years. It holds up better than I expected.

Released in 1998, it came out during that strange moment when Crow had figured out exactly what she wanted to sound like. The debut had its cult following, the second album made her enormous, and this is the third — the one where she could do anything and mostly chose restraint.

What works: the opening pair of "My Favorite Mistake" and "There Goes the Neighborhood" establishes two completely different versions of the same artist and lets both exist. The production (Crow handled most of it herself at her home studio) has a warmth that album-from-a-click-track records of that era almost never achieved.

"Riverwide" is the one I'd forgotten about. Seven minutes, barely a chorus, more Celtic folk than anything on her other records. It doesn't belong on the album and is probably the best track on it.

What doesn't quite work: the middle section drags slightly. "Anything But Down" is a quality song I've heard so many times in supermarkets that I can no longer hear it properly.

Best for: driving long distances in summer. It was made for this.

[Pitchfork's retrospective on Sheryl Crow's catalog](https://pitchfork.com/) is actually generous and worth reading if you grew up dismissing her.
