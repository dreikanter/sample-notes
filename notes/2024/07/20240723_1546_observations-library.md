# Observations from the central library

Went to the reading room to work for the day — the apartment has been too hot and I needed somewhere with air conditioning and a reason not to talk.

The reference room has a particular quality of silence that's different from the silence of a forest or an empty building. It's a cooperative silence, produced by many people who have all implicitly agreed to it. You can hear people turning pages, the squeak of a chair, someone's phone vibrating against a hard surface two tables away. But these sounds exist inside a sustained agreement to be quiet, and somehow they're more restful than actual absence of sound.

The reading room has carved wooden desks that have been used for decades. The wood has a patina from thousands of hands in the same spots. My particular desk has an old pencil groove where someone used to rest their pencil — worn smooth by the pencil and then by subsequent fingertips finding the same groove.

I got three hours of focused work done, which I haven't managed in weeks.

The library's catalogue is still partly physical — there's a bank of wooden card drawers near the entrance that are now used for local history materials rather than general cataloguing. I spent ten minutes going through the cards for materials about the industrial development of this neighborhood. Found a reference to a pamphlet from 1931 about the canal extension — almost certainly the same canal I walked along last week [[20240626_1519]].

Checked the pamphlet out. It exists. Reading it tonight.
