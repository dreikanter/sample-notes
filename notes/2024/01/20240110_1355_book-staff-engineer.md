# Book notes — Staff Engineer by Will Larson

Read over three evenings in the first week of January. Larson interviewed thirty or so people with staff engineer titles and synthesized what the role actually involves across different companies. The variety is striking — the title means very different things in different organizations.

## The archetypes

Larson identifies four:

1. **Tech lead:** Most common, embedded in a team, accountable for technical direction on a specific roadmap
2. **Architect:** Responsible for technical direction across multiple systems or the whole org
3. **Solver:** Dropped into hard problems, solves them, moves on
4. **Right hand:** Operates as a force multiplier to a senior engineering leader

The value of naming these is that they're different jobs. Failure in the role often comes from performing one archetype while your organization needs another.

## Useful ideas

"Work on what matters" sounds obvious and is genuinely hard. Larson's framework: distinguish between boxes of prestige (visible, rewarded) and boxes of actual leverage (less visible, higher impact). Staff engineers are at risk of optimizing for the former.

"Stay aligned with authority" is uncomfortable to read but accurate — a staff engineer who can't influence their skip-level has limited leverage, whatever their technical ability.

The writing sprints chapter is practical and I'm experimenting with it now.

## Reservation

The book is most useful if you're in an organization large enough to have staff-level roles. For smaller teams, the archetype framework doesn't map cleanly.
