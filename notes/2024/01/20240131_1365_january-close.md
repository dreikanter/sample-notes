# January close — month summary

January was better than expected. Usually the first month of the year has an inertia problem — the year hasn't established momentum yet — but this one moved.

## Work

The read replica architecture is defined and reviewed. Implementation starts in sprint two of February. The alert audit is closed. The Q1 priorities document has the team's buy-in.

Still no progress on writing a proper on-call runbook. This is the thing that will bite us the next time someone new takes on-call rotation.

## Reading

Finished *The Dawn of Everything*, *Entangled Life* (both in the middle of the month). Started *Blood Meridian* and stopped after forty pages — not the right mental season for it. Started *Seeing Like a State* instead and immediately found it was the right book at the right time.

Scott's central argument — that "legibility" (making populations measurable and controllable by the state) destroys local knowledge and produces failures — is one I want to apply to software system design. The analogy is inexact but generative.

## Cooking

Made the tajine again with lamb this time (see [[20240120_1360]] for the chicken version). Better: the lamb shoulder has more fat and the flavor is deeper. Cooked an hour longer.

## Garden

The garlic is showing half-inch shoots. The ground is still frozen solid but the mulch has insulated enough for the roots to establish. On track.

## Personal

The morning phone experiment from November (see [[20231120_1316]]) has finally stuck. Five weeks of mostly phone-free mornings. The 10am–noon window is measurably better.

Reference: [Scott's *Seeing Like a State* at Yale Press](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300078152/seeing-like-a-state/).
