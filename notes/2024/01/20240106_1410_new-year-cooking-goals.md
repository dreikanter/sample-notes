# Cooking goals for 2024

A list I'll revisit in December. Specific enough to be measurable, not so restrictive that it becomes a compliance exercise.

## Skills to develop

1. **Fresh pasta, properly** — not occasional, but fluent. At minimum: comfortable with flat pasta, one filled shape, and the sense of when the dough is right.

2. **Stock from scratch** — stop buying cartons. Chicken stock from bones once a month is achievable.

3. **One Asian cuisine more deeply** — I cook broadly but shallowly across Asian traditions. Japanese or Korean, pick one and spend six months going deeper. Leaning Japanese because the fundamental technique (dashi, proper rice, knife work) is transferable.

## Things to try for the first time

- Mole (the month-long project version, not the shortcut)
- Dim sum at home — at least two types
- Fermented hot sauce
- Proper croissants (expecting failure, aiming for education)

## Habits to build

- Cook dinner without a recipe once a week
- Write up cooking experiments as I do them (already doing this, maintain the habit)
- Keep the pantry stocked at the foundational level: the ingredient gaps that force recipe compromises

## What I'm explicitly not adding

No fancy equipment purchases. The Dutch oven, cast iron, and sheet pans cover everything I actually cook. The tagine was a good gift, not a gap I'd identified.

Reference: [Samin Nosrat's cooking philosophy](https://www.saltfatacidhe.at/) remains the best framework for cooking without constantly following recipes.
