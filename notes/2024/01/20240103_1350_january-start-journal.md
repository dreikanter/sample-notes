# January start — back to work journal

Back at the desk on Wednesday after a long break. The return had that familiar disorientation — the muscle memory for the workflow is intact but the context is temporarily missing. It took most of the morning to reconstruct what I was in the middle of before December 22.

The team is gradually returning through the week. The Slack channels are quiet, which is how you know the holiday actually happened this year. Usually someone posts a "quick question" by December 27.

**What I'm noticing about the re-entry:**

The gap gives you an involuntary outside view of the work. Things that felt urgent in December look different from the first week of January. The alert audit (still owed) looks less like a technical problem and more like a communication problem — we have too many alerts because nobody ever removed the ones that stopped mattering. The solution is not engineering.

The Q1 priorities document is already in draft. I'm resisting the urge to make it comprehensive. The best planning documents I've seen are short and hold one or two real bets, not fifteen reasonable incremental improvements.

**Reading in January:**

Continuing *The Dawn of Everything* — still about two-thirds through. Starting *Staff Engineer* this week for work context.

**Logistical note:** The faucet washer has been replaced. The dripping silence is remarkable.

Reference: [Kourosh Dini on returning from breaks](https://www.kouroshdini.com/) has useful framing on re-entry anxiety and how to use the transitional state productively.
