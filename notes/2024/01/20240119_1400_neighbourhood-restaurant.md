# Neighbourhood restaurant notes — new Yemeni place

Opened three months ago on the street behind the hardware store. I walked past it four times before going in, which is a failure of curiosity I'm noting here.

Went for lunch on Saturday, alone, with a book that I didn't open.

## What I ordered

**Saltah:** The national dish — a lamb-based stew with fenugreek froth (hulba) stirred in at the table, served with flatbread. The froth changes the texture of the entire dish; it becomes slightly foam-y, herbaceous, and somehow more filling than it was before the stirring. I've never had this before and it required a minute of adjusting expectations.

**Bint al-sahn:** A honey and ghee pastry served as dessert. Almost impossibly sweet in the best way — the kind of sweet that works because the dough itself is barely sweet and the honey is doing everything. Layered, flaky, eaten warm.

**Adani tea:** Cardamom, ginger, milk, tea, very sweet. The restaurant is Adeni (from Aden, in the south), and the spice balance in the tea reflects that — more cardamom-forward than the Northern Yemeni style apparently.

## Notes on the space

Six tables, cash only, the owner taking orders and serving. A television on the wall showing Al Jazeera on mute. The smell of the kitchen reached the dining room in a way that made you want to know what was coming.

I was there for ninety minutes and ordered nothing I'd had before. This is the whole point of having a neighborhood.

Reference: [Yemen Food by Lamees Abdullah](https://www.yemenfood.com/) for background on the cuisine.
