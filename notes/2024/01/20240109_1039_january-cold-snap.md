# January cold snap — observations

Three days into a proper cold spell. The thermometer on the north wall read -6°C this morning, which is unusual here — we typically sit at 2–4°C through January even in harder winters.

The bird feeders have been extraordinarily busy. Goldfinches, which I rarely see in January, have been working the nyjer seed feeder in groups of six or seven. A brambling appeared on the second morning, which was a first for the garden — it must have come down from the hills where the beech mast is buried under snow. Robins are on the ground almost continuously, which I understand is a stress indicator; they normally defend territories fairly aggressively but the cold seems to override that.

The pond iced over by yesterday afternoon. Left a tennis ball floating in it before the freeze to keep a small hole open for the frogs still in the mud at the bottom. This is apparently important for gas exchange — without it, methane builds up under the ice and can suffocate overwintering amphibians. Read about this technique via the Wildlife Trusts pages: https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/actions/how-help-pond-wildlife-winter

The hazel catkins, which had started to elongate before the cold snap, are now hanging stiff and pale. They'll probably resume once it warms — catkins are remarkably freeze-tolerant since they've already reached that stage of development.

Inside: made a big pot of barley soup to use up the root vegetables before they go soft. Parsnip, celeriac, leek, a dried bay leaf. Cooked low and slow for two hours. The kind of cooking that makes sense in a house that's reluctant to warm up.
