# Coffee brewing notes — January experiments

I've been using a V60 pour-over for about a year and varying one variable at a time. These are the current findings.

## Current baseline recipe

- Dose: 20g coffee
- Water: 320g at 93°C
- Grind: medium-fine (13 on the 1Zpresso JX-Pro)
- Time: 3:00–3:30 total brew time
- Technique: 60g bloom for 45 seconds, then three pours of ~87g each at 30-second intervals

This produces a balanced, slightly bright cup that works for most origins.

## Variables tested this month

**Grind size:** Coarser (15 on the grinder) produced a faster, flatter cup — less complexity, slightly sour. Finer (11) choked the drain and produced bitter, over-extracted result. The 13 setting is genuinely the right place for this kettle and this V60.

**Water temperature:** Dropped to 88°C for a natural process Ethiopian I received as a gift. Lower temps preserved the fruit and fermentation notes that were disappearing at 93°C. Origin-appropriate temperature adjustment is something I hadn't taken seriously before.

**Bloom time:** Extended to 90 seconds for the same Ethiopian. The degassing is real — you can see it in the bloom bubble activity. Longer bloom, longer contact, clearer flavors.

## Coffees worth noting

A washed Guatemalan from a local roaster was the cleanest, most chocolate-forward thing I've had this winter. Boring in a good way — reliable, unpretentious, good at 6am.

The natural Ethiopian is better as an afternoon coffee. At 6am its complexity reads as confusion.

Reference: [James Hoffmann's V60 recipe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AI4ynXzkSQo) was my starting point; I've moved away from his technique slightly but the underlying logic is still useful.
