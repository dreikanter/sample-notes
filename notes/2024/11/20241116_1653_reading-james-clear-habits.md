# Reading notes — Atomic Habits

James Clear, 2018. Read in about five days in fragments — appropriate for a book about small consistent actions.

The core model (cue → craving → response → reward) is not new — it's drawn substantially from Charles Duhigg's *The Power of Habit* and behaviour psychology before that, which Clear acknowledges. The book's value is in the accumulation of practical applications rather than conceptual novelty.

What I found most useful:

**Identity-based habits**: framing habit change as "I am the type of person who..." rather than "I want to..." is a genuine psychological reframe. The example of "I don't smoke" versus "I'm not a smoker" is small but lands. The identity frame shifts the reference point from a desired outcome to a desired self-concept.

**The two-minute rule**: any habit can be started in two minutes. This is the "getting to the gym is the habit, not working out" concept. The insight is that the barrier is initiation, not continuation. See [[20241024_1630]] where I wrote something similar about deep work and the transition moment.

**Environment design**: this is probably the most underrated idea in the book. The story about the hospital that reduced alcohol use among medical staff by making it slightly inconvenient is striking. Most of what we think of as willpower is actually friction management.

What I found least convincing: the optimism about compounding. One percent better every day is a nice model but life doesn't compound linearly. Bad weeks happen. The book's relentless positivity about systems occasionally reads as bypassing the genuine difficulty of change.

[James Clear's website](https://jamesclear.com) has the core ideas well-summarised if you want the condensed version.
