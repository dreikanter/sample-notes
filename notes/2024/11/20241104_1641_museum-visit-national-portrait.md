# Museum visit — National Portrait Gallery

First visit to the NPG since the reopening after its renovation. The refurbishment is substantial — the flow between galleries is much better and the natural light in the new roof extension transforms what had been some of the grimmer rooms.

The Tudor section remains the most immediately engaging. The Holbein portraits in particular have a quality of presence that reproductions don't convey — the texture and density of the paint on panel, the way the jewel colours hold their saturation across centuries. Henry VIII looks exactly as he should: implacable and somehow both powerful and slightly ridiculous.

The 20th-century galleries are stronger than I remembered. A set of Lucian Freud portraits I hadn't seen in person before — his 1950s work before the paint got so thick — showed him at a particularly unsettling clarity. Every subject looks trapped.

The contemporary section is always the most contested territory. Some of it feels like magazine photography given a different context by the institution. Some of it is genuinely moving — there was a series of photographs by a British-Sudanese artist whose name I didn't write down, and I'm annoyed at myself for that.

Spent about two and a half hours. Could have spent longer in the Tudor and 20th-century rooms. The cafe is improved and not overpriced, which is not a given for Central London.

More on the collection: [NPG website](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections).
