# Concert review — City Sinfonia, November programme

Saw the City Sinfonia at the Barbican for a mixed programme: Brahms Piano Quartet in G minor (op. 25), Bartók String Quartet No. 4, and after the interval Schubert's "Death and the Maiden" string quartet.

The Brahms was a revelation. I've listened to the recorded version with Pollini and the Amadeus Quartet dozens of times but the live performance made it viscerally different — the piano in particular. The first movement has this rolling, restless energy that comes alive in a room in a way recordings flatten. The pianist (name regrettably not written in my programme note) brought a kind of muscular patience to it.

Bartók after the interval was tough in exactly the right way. His fourth quartet is spiky, percussive, angular — the pizzicato sections have this violent snap to them. It's one of those pieces that benefits from being slightly confrontational in person. The audience settled into it slowly; by the third movement the restlessness had resolved into real attention.

The Schubert landed heavily. Coming after the Bartók, the beauty of it felt slightly precarious, which is probably the right response to *Death and the Maiden* — something lurking in even the most lyrical passages. The slow movement was superb. The cellist held the main theme in the final variation with real intensity.

The Barbican's acoustics in the main hall are sometimes criticised but I find them well-suited to chamber music at this size. The dryness that hurts orchestral concerts helps chamber transparency.
