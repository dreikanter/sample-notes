# Rye bread notes — first attempts

Rye behaves completely differently from wheat. I assumed my sourdough intuition would transfer. It mostly did not.

## What caught me off guard

**Dough consistency.** 100% rye dough isn't dough in any familiar sense — it's closer to a very stiff paste. You can't fold it, you can't develop gluten through stretching. You shape it by pressing it into a tin and smoothing the top with a wet hand.

**Fermentation speed.** Rye ferments faster. The same starter at the same temperature was visibly active in four hours instead of the usual eight. On a warm day, the dough had over-proofed before I noticed.

**Crumb density.** A fully rye loaf will never have an open crumb. Compact and even is what you're aiming for — not a sign of failure.

## What finally worked

- Autolyse the rye flour and water for 45 minutes before adding starter and salt.
- Proof in a greased tin, covered, at room temperature for 3-4 hours only.
- Bake covered at 230°C for 20 minutes, uncover, reduce to 200°C for 30 more.
- Do not cut for at least 12 hours. The crumb needs time to set or it's gummy.
