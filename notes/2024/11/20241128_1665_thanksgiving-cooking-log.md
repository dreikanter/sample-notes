# Thanksgiving cooking log

Not American but have American friends and was invited to an expat Thanksgiving dinner as a contributing cook. My assignment: two side dishes and a dessert.

## What I made

**Roasted sweet potato with brown butter and sage**  
Cut three large sweet potatoes into wedges. Roasted at 200°C for 35 minutes until caramelised. Made brown butter by cooking 100g butter until the milk solids turned nutty and golden. Tossed the roasted wedges in brown butter with fresh sage leaves crisped in the same pan. Simple but worked well — the brown butter adds something that plain olive oil can't.

**Green beans with toasted almonds and lemon**  
1kg green beans blanched for 3 minutes, shocked in ice water. Finished in a pan with butter, sliced almonds toasted until golden, zest of one lemon, salt. Classic American prep. The ice water step keeps them bright green and snappy — skipping it gives you khaki beans.

**Pumpkin pie**  
The one item I'd never made before. Used the Serious Eats recipe which calls for roasting a real pumpkin (a Hokkaido, not a pie pumpkin) rather than using tinned. The [Serious Eats pumpkin pie guide](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-pumpkin-pie-recipe) recommends a blind-baked all-butter crust and a custard cooked to 175°F internal temperature.

Result: flavour was excellent — more complex than tinned, slightly earthy. The texture was slightly softer than I wanted; should have baked longer. The crust was the best part, which is not a bad problem to have.

The dinner itself was loud, warm, and generous in the best American social tradition.
