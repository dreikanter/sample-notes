# Kimchi — first proper attempt

Followed a version of the traditional recipe from [Maangchi's kimchi tutorial](https://www.maangchi.com/recipe/tongbaechu-kimchi) with some modifications based on what I could get at the local Korean grocery.

## Ingredients (made a 2kg batch)

- 2 medium napa cabbages (about 2kg)
- 100g sea salt for salting
- Gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes): 5 tbsp — went conservative for a first batch
- Fish sauce: 3 tbsp (used anchovy sauce from the Korean shop)
- Fermented shrimp paste (saeujeot): 2 tbsp — found this at the Korean shop in Peckham
- Spring onions: 8, cut into 5cm lengths
- Garlic: 8 cloves, minced
- Fresh ginger: 2cm piece, grated
- Sugar: 1 tsp

## Process

Salted the cabbage in layers overnight (about 8 hours), weighted down with plates. It lost a significant amount of water — the leaves were completely pliable, no crunch.

Rinsed three times and tasted — still slightly salty, which is correct since it will dilute during fermentation. Let drain for 30 minutes.

Mixed the gochugaru paste: gochugaru, fish sauce, shrimp paste, garlic, ginger, sugar, plus about 3 tbsp of the salted cabbage liquid. Tasted before applying — it was aggressively spiced, funky, and hot in exactly the right way.

Mixed paste into cabbage with gloved hands. Packed tightly into sterilised jars, pressing down to remove air pockets and leave some headroom for fermentation.

## Fermentation plan

Room temperature for 24–48 hours (depending on bubble activity), then refrigerate. Will taste at day 5 and day 14.
