# November garden notes

First hard frost last night — woke up to white ground and the dahlias finally, definitively done. They had been hanging on even through several light frosts, putting out a few ragged flowers that were more touching than beautiful. The frost finished them cleanly.

The garlic went in on 25th October, 120 cloves in two rows. Checked the bed this morning — a few have already sent up their first thin shoots, which is impressive given it's been cold. Solent Wight seems to establish faster than the Elephant variety based on what I can see.

The brassica net is doing its job. A gang of wood pigeons has been patrolling the plot all week, looking aggrieved at being denied access to the purple sprouting broccoli. The plants themselves are looking healthy — dense and compact in the way that suggests they hardened off well before the cold.

The broad beans I sowed direct in the last week of October haven't emerged yet. The packet says 10–14 days; I'm now at 12 days with nothing. Not worried yet. Germination is slower in cold soil and the soil temperature has been low.

Tulip bulbs are all in — 200 bulbs across three beds, roughly 15cm deep and 10–12cm apart. Mixed a handful of grit into the planting holes to improve drainage. Crown imperials went in at the border because they're tall enough to hold their own there.

The [RHS winter garden guide](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-inspiration/garden-types/winter-garden) suggests this is the time to start thinking about winter structure. Note to self for next year: more evergreen interest, more plants with good seed heads.
