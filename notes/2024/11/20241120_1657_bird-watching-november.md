# Bird watching notes — November

Went to the local wetland reserve on a cold, still morning. Peak time for winter visitors.

The highlight was a bittern — heard the characteristic "pumping" call from the reed bed and then saw the bird itself, briefly, walking at the reed bed edge. A bittern in the open, even for fifteen seconds, is rare enough to be genuinely exciting. They're incredible at staying invisible — their vertical freeze posture in reeds is textbook. The [RSPB bittern page](https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/bittern) notes that winter is when they're most visible as freezing conditions force them to the water's edge.

Also notable:
- **Bearded reedlings**: a group of six moving through the reed tops, making that distinctive "ping" call. The males have striking black moustaches — a tiny, improbable bird.
- **Water rail**: squealing from deep in the reeds, briefly visible at a gap. Their call is sometimes compared to a piglet.
- **Stonechat**: perched on a fence post at the entrance, obligingly still. Males have the striking black head and orange-red breast.
- **Marsh harrier**: two females/juveniles (brown with cream crown) hunting low over the reed bed in mid-morning.

More common birds on the water: teal in good numbers, shoveler, gadwall, coot, great crested grebe. The grebe was diving repeatedly and coming up with small fish.

Spent two hours. Binoculars: 8×42 Swarovski EL (borrowed from a friend — the difference in glass quality from my budget pair is remarkable). Weather: 4°C, no wind, overcast, perfect for birding.
