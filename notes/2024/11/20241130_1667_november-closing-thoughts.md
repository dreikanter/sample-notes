# November — closing thoughts

The month that feels simultaneously very long and too short. By the end of November, the year has compressed to the point where December starts to feel like an afterword rather than a chapter.

What happened this month that mattered:

The kimchi is now genuinely excellent — week-three kimchi has a depth that week-one doesn't, and I've been eating it with almost everything. The fermentation project has become something I'm genuinely invested in; there's something satisfying about the slow, low-intervention nature of it.

The async standup experiment at work is working better than expected. Four weeks in and the synchronous standup has been replaced entirely. Morning rhythm is noticeably better — no 9am call means the first hour of the day is actually productive. Will write up the experience properly at some point.

Finished *The Remains of the Day* [[20241125_1662]]. The kind of novel that changes how you see certain other things.

The bird watching trip [[20241120_1657]] was the best morning of the month. The bittern. The reedlings. Cold still air and the reed bed sounds.

**Reading this month**: The Dispossessed (finish), Atomic Habits, The Remains of the Day. Three books of quite different kinds, none of which left me unmoved.

**What I want in December**: more cooking, less scrolling, one long cycle ride if the weather allows, to finish setting up the flat properly rather than living around boxes.

The [advent season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent) has its own logic — the gathering-in quality, the shortening light, the way December's social calendar suddenly fills. I'm going to try to approach it as a season rather than a countdown.
