# Reading notes — Anna Karenina

Finally reading this properly, not in the abridged version I worked through in school. The Pevear and Volokhonsky translation. About halfway through.

The thing nobody warned me about is how much of the novel belongs to Levin. I expected Anna. Levin's sections — the farming, the philosophical restlessness, the excruciating proposal scene — are what I keep thinking about between sessions. There is something almost unbearable about reading a character who overthinks in ways that feel genuinely contemporary.

The social machinery is rendered with a precision that doesn't feel like historical re-enactment. The gossip networks, the way information travels across households, the calibrated embarrassment of being seen in the wrong company — it maps onto things I recognise more than I expected.

A few passages I marked:

> "He soon felt that the fulfilment of his desires gave him only one grain of the mountain of happiness he had expected."

That's Levin after the proposal is accepted. The gap between anticipated and actual happiness treated so plainly and without irony — no moralising, just observation.

The pacing is long but not slow. There is a difference. Tolstoy doesn't hurry anything, but there is almost never a scene that feels like it's marking time.
