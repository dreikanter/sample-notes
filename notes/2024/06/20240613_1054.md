# Light at 9pm

The light here at this time of year is disorienting in the best way. At nine in the evening the sky is still a pale blue over the roofline to the northwest, and the street is in that ambiguous state where it's too dim for sunglasses but bright enough that turning on a lamp inside feels premature. The shadows are long and going somewhere decisive.

I walked the long block down to the main road and back. The linden trees are in bloom — I always forget that they bloom so late, long after everything else has finished, and that they smell like that. Sweet and slightly medicinal. One tree in front of the corner house was loud with bees, the sound audible from across the street.

Three things noticed:

1. Someone has started a small front-yard garden where there was grass before. Tomatoes in cages, basil in between, some kind of vining thing on a trellis against the fence. The soil looked freshly amended — dark and crumbly.

2. A crow on a wire watching the road with what felt like professional detachment.

3. A kid on a bike going much faster than necessary down the gentle slope, arms out for a second before grabbing the bars again. Controlled recklessness.

The linden timing and bloom chemistry is interesting — there's a short overview in [this piece from the Garden Museum](https://gardenmuseum.org.uk/). The scent compound is primarily linalool, same as lavender, which explains the overlap in what they do to the nervous system.

Didn't bring a notebook. Writing this from memory an hour later.
