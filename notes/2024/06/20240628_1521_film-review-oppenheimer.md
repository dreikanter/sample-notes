# Film review — Oppenheimer

Finally watched this properly rather than on a laptop screen — caught a late screening at a proper cinema. Three hours passes faster than I expected given how dense the material is.

What Nolan does well: the texture of scientific culture in the 1940s, the way ideas feel exciting and dangerous simultaneously. The Los Alamos sequences have genuine energy. Cillian Murphy plays Oppenheimer as a man who thinks in equations and metaphors, which feels accurate to everything I've read about the historical figure.

The intercutting structure between timelines is bold. The black-and-white Strauss hearings anchor the color timelines effectively, though I think the film lets Strauss off the hook slightly — his pettiness reads as almost comical by the end when it should be terrifying.

Where it loses me slightly: the treatment of Kitty Oppenheimer. Emily Blunt is excellent in every scene she's given, but those scenes are too few. Her complexity gets compressed into a handful of confrontational moments.

The Trinity test sequence is worth the price of admission alone. Knowing what's coming and still feeling dread during the countdown — that's craft.

Rating: 4/5. Ambitious and largely successful. More of a portrait than an argument, which is both its strength and a kind of evasion.

[Roger Ebert's historical essay on the Oppenheimer story](https://www.rogerebert.com/) is worth reading alongside this.
