# Fermentation basics — lacto-fermented vegetables

Started my first intentional lacto-fermentation batch after reading through the Noma Guide to Fermentation. The chemistry is straightforward: salt draws water out of the vegetables via osmosis, the resulting brine drops pH as Lactobacillus bacteria produce lactic acid, and that acidic environment outcompetes harmful microbes.

## Salt ratio

The critical variable is salt concentration by weight. I used 2% for a simple cabbage kraut and 3% for a radish brine. Below 1.5% you risk mold and off-flavours; above 3.5% fermentation slows dramatically because it becomes hostile to the bacteria too.

## Process notes

- Pack vegetables tightly into a clean glass jar. Mason jars work fine.
- Weigh the veg, calculate salt, dissolve salt in the smallest amount of water needed if the veg doesn't release enough liquid on its own.
- Keep everything submerged below the brine line. A small zip-lock bag filled with brine works as a weight.
- Burp the lid daily for the first three days — CO₂ production is vigorous.
- Taste after day 4. Fully sour and shelf-stable somewhere around day 7–10 at room temperature (20°C).

## Results

The cabbage kraut at day 8 had good tartness and crunch. The radish turned an alarming pink within 24 hours — totally normal, just anthocyanins reacting to the pH shift.
