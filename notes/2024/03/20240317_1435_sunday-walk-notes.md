# Sunday walk notes

Three hours, roughly 11km. Started at the reservoir and looped back through the park and the neighborhood behind the library. Did this route once before in November and the difference is obvious: cattails brown and matted in November, now showing new green growth at the base; ice completely gone from the reservoir edges; red-winged blackbirds calling from every stretch of marsh (arrived early, given the temperatures).

The herons were back. Four great blue herons stationed around the north end of the reservoir at intervals, the spacing roughly equal as if they'd arranged it. One flushed when I got within fifty meters and went to stand on the far bank with the dignity that herons maintain even when obviously startled.

The park section was muddier than I'd dressed for. Water pooled in low spots where the frost was still leaving the ground. The trail through the grove has deteriorated since last year — should report it to the parks department, there are two significant erosion channels that will get worse if not addressed.

Found a small cache of morel mushrooms under the elm tree at the northwest corner of the grove. Six of them, small and fresh. Picked them all. Sautéed with butter and garlic for dinner with an egg on top. The elm morel association is well-documented — elms dying from Dutch elm disease provide the particular substrate. This tree has the look of something stressed.

Nothing else notable except the light at 5pm which was properly warm for the first time, a real seasonal shift rather than a trick of the angle.
