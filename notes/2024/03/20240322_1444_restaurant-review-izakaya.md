# Restaurant review — Izakaya Tanuki

**Visited:** March 22, dinner
**With:** two friends, sat at bar by choice
**Bill:** ~$85 per person with drinks

This place has been open about eight months and I'd been meaning to visit since a friend mentioned it in passing last November. It's in a space that used to be a French bistro — the bones are similar (tight tables, tiled floor, open kitchen) but the lighting is dimmer and the menu is on a handwritten board that changes nightly.

**Ordered:**
- Chicken karaage: excellent. Thighs, fried to order, the crust almost translucent. Served with kewpie mayo and a wedge of yuzu. One of the better versions I've had outside Japan.
- Agedashi tofu: soft interior, crispy coating (held for the two minutes it should, then started getting soggy — this is inherent to the dish). The dashi broth was appropriately light and clean.
- Yakitori skewers: ordered four varieties. The tsukune (chicken meatball with tare glaze) was the standout — dense, charred, the egg yolk dipping sauce was creamy and rich. Negima (chicken and green onion) second favorite.
- Uni toast: on the specials board, not cheap ($22), worth it. Santa Barbara uni on milk bread toast with a little cultured butter. Simple and correct.

**Drinks:** Japanese whisky highball made with Suntory Toki — refreshing, not sweet. Two bottles of Asahi by the end.

**Service:** Knowledgeable about the menu, honest about what was good that night. No upsell pressure.

**Would return:** Yes, specifically for the karaage and whatever the uni special is.
