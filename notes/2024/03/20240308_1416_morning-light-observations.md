# Morning light observations

Woke at 6:15 to find the kitchen filled with an unusual amber tint. March mornings have this quality that August never quite manages — a low angle that catches dust motes and turns the steam from the kettle into something theatrical.

The sun rose at roughly 6:42 here in the northern latitude. I've been tracking this loosely since January, noting that each week the sunrise moves back by about twelve minutes. The math is satisfying. By June we'll have almost sixteen hours of daylight, which feels genuinely strange to anticipate while standing in the cold kitchen in a wool sweater.

The neighbor's oak still hasn't leafed out. Its bare branches made a precise grid against the pale sky — I counted fourteen intersections visible from the window. There's an argument that deciduous trees are more beautiful in winter when you can see their actual architecture. Fully leaved they're just a green cloud.

I made oatmeal with dried tart cherries and a spoonful of tahini, which sounds wrong but works. The bitterness of the tahini cuts the sweetness of the cherries. Recipe modified from a post I found on Serious Eats: https://www.seriouseats.com/oatmeal-recipes

Temperature was -3°C at 7am. By the time I left for the bus at 8:30 it had climbed to 1°C. That two-hour window in early spring when the world is technically above freezing but feels absolutely identical to when it was below — those are the hours when you understand why people move to warmer climates.
