# Pasta filling notes — ricotta variants

Made filled pasta three times now. Each time the filling has been a variant on ricotta. Notes on what I've learned.

## The base

The recipe that works reliably:
- 250g whole milk ricotta, drained overnight (essential — too much moisture wrecks the texture)
- 1 egg yolk (binds, enriches)
- 50g Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated
- Nutmeg, white pepper, salt

Taste before filling. This is the only chance to adjust seasoning. Fresh pasta is nearly flavorless and the filling is carrying the whole dish.

## Variants tried

**Spinach and ricotta (classic):** Add 100g cooked spinach, squeezed very dry, roughly chopped. The most forgiving version, hard to get wrong.

**Brown butter and sage (as a sauce, not filling):** Works better as the finishing sauce for plain ravioli than as a component of the filling. Adding sage to the filling muddles it.

**Pea and mint:** Replace spinach with blanched peas, mashed coarsely. Add 1 tbsp finely chopped mint. Seasonal, lighter than the spinach version. Works well in spring.

**Lemon and herb:** Zest of one lemon, tablespoon of parsley and chives. Makes a filling that cuts the richness of the pasta — better with a lighter sauce (olive oil and white wine rather than butter).

## The sealing problem

The consistent difficulty: getting all the air out before sealing. Any trapped air becomes a steam pocket that forces the seal open in the pot.

Solution: don't over-fill. Use half as much filling as you think you need. Then use a slightly damp finger to run the border before pressing. Press firmly, working from the filling outward.

Reference: [Marcella Hazan's *The Essentials of Classic Italian Cooking*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/167396/) for the foundational technique.
