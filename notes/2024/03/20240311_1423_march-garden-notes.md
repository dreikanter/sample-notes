# March garden notes

Ground is still mostly frozen below 3cm but the top layer thaws by mid-afternoon on sunny days. Crocuses are up in the south-facing bed — purple and yellow, maybe 15 of them, about 8cm tall. The snowdrops that appeared last week are already starting to look tired.

Started seeds indoors today: tomatoes (two varieties — a Roma-type and a beefsteak I saved from last year's volunteer plant), basil, and the slow-germinating parsley. Everything in 72-cell trays under the grow light on the back porch. The porch stays around 12°C overnight which might be too cold for tomatoes — I should check germination temps. Tomatoes want soil temp of 21-27°C, so the heat mat is non-negotiable.

Noticed the garlic planted in October is showing green tips. About 30 of the 36 cloves appear to have survived the winter. The six that didn't make it were in the shadier part of the bed, where drainage is worse. Mental note to reroute that section before fall planting.

The compost pile is not hot enough to be doing anything — it's been a solid mass of frozen material since December. Once temperatures reliably stay above 5°C I'll turn it and add some nitrogen material (kitchen scraps have been going in but layering got abandoned).

Planning this year's layout: want to move the cucumbers out of the spot where squash was last year. Crop rotation matters most for nightshades, but moving things around seems worth the small effort.

Good seed-starting guide: https://extension.umn.edu/planting-and-growing-guides/starting-seeds-indoors
