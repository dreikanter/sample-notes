# Film review — Past lives

Celine Song, 2023. Two hours. Watched on a Tuesday evening when I expected nothing in particular, which is probably the ideal condition.

The film tracks Nora and Hae Sung across three time periods: childhood in Seoul, young adulthood via Skype calls between Seoul and New York, and a week in New York twenty-four years after they last saw each other in person. Nora has married Arthur, an American writer she met at a residency.

What the film understands that most romance films don't: the person you didn't choose is not a counterfactual self, they're a real person with a real life that continued without you, and meeting them again is meeting a stranger who carries the memory of a version of you that no longer exists. The concept of in-yeon (Korean: fate, or the invisible thread of connections across lifetimes) is introduced early and pays off emotionally without being schematic.

The cinematography by Shabier Kirchner is restrained. Most of the emotional work is done in close-ups of faces reacting rather than speaking. The scene in the bar where Arthur watches Nora and Hae Sung speak in Korean he can't understand — while Hae Sung can't speak to Arthur in English — is filmed with unusual patience.

Greta Lee is extraordinary. She carries twelve years of a character's decisions in posture alone.

Not a tearjerker in the manipulative sense. The ending is sad but earns the sadness.
