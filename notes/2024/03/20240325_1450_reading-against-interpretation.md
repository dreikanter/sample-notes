# Reading notes — Against interpretation

Susan Sontag, 1966. The title essay argues against the critical practice of treating art as a text to be decoded — asking "what does it mean?" as if the meaning were separable from the work itself. Her counterproposal: "In place of a hermeneutics we need an erotics of art." By which she means: an approach that deals with the sensory experience of the work, its form, its immediate presence.

The argument lands differently fifty-plus years on. She's reacting against a particular mid-century tendency to reduce everything to allegory or symbol — a Kafka story as a symbol of bureaucracy, a Bergman film as a metaphor for modern alienation. This kind of interpretation was genuinely reductive when it was the dominant mode.

Now the problem might be different: not that we over-interpret but that we under-experience. We consume cultural objects quickly and at volume and experience is rationed accordingly. Whether Sontag's erotics of art is an answer to this problem rather than her original target is unclear.

The essay on camp is what everyone cites. Camp as appreciation of things-as-they-are-rather-than-what-they-intend. Her fifty-eight numbered notes on camp is an odd form choice — the form itself is slightly campy. "Camp sees everything in quotation marks."

Less cited but more interesting: "The artist as exemplary sufferer" on the cultural mythology that suffering produces great art, and the self-defeating logic this creates for artists.

Would read: the essays on Godard and Resnais, and "Notes on 'Notes on Camp'" by Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick as counterpoint.
