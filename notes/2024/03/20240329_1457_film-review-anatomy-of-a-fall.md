# Film review — Anatomy of a fall

Justine Triet, 2023. 151 minutes. Palme d'Or at Cannes. Watched with subtitles — it's filmed in French and English, which is thematically significant.

The film begins with a man found dead in the snow below the attic window of his chalet. The question that structures the next two-and-a-half hours: did he fall, did he jump, or did his wife Sandra push him? The answer is not definitively given, and the film is more interested in what the question reveals than in answering it.

What the trial sequences do brilliantly: show how legal proceedings translate a complex relationship into a narrative that can be adjudicated. The prosecution's story of Sandra is coherent and damning; the defense's counter-narrative is also coherent. Neither is the relationship. The audio recording played in court — a domestic argument the day before the death — is the film's centrepiece, nine uncomfortable minutes that reveal a marriage's private language.

Sandra Hüller gives an extraordinary performance. The character is written as deliberately opaque — a woman who has learned to present herself strategically — and Hüller plays this without letting it become likeable opacity. You don't know whether to believe her because she herself knows you're watching.

The child, Daniel, carries much of the film's emotional weight without the film making his experience the moral center. He has to decide something. The decision he makes is not certain either.

The long title is perfect — a fall, anatomized, turns out to be irreducibly ambiguous.
