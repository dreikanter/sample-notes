# Finishing Blood Meridian

Finished at midnight on March 6. Took two and a half months across three attempts spanning December, January, and March.

The last section, when Tobin and the Kid are hunted through the desert, is the most sustained piece of writing in the book. McCarthy drops almost all dialogue and renders the desert as the primary presence — the heat, the light, the absence of water, the growing distance from everything human. The narrative voice becomes nearly impersonal, as if reporting geological facts.

The Judge's appearance in the final pages is the worst and right kind of horror — not surprising, not explicable, simply final. His speech about dancing — "whatever in creation exists without my knowledge exists without my consent" — is the thesis the entire book has been building toward. He is not evil in any human sense; he is something older and more systematic.

## What I'll think about

The violence in the book is numbing by design. The reader's response to the five-hundredth atrocity is different from the response to the first, and that change in the reader is itself part of what the book is doing. By the end, I found I was reading the violent passages like weather reports — accurately, with diminished affect. McCarthy forces you to notice yourself becoming accustomed.

The Kid is the novel's structural mystery: the character who refuses the Judge's worldview without articulating a counter to it. His refusal is wordless and achieves nothing. The book doesn't punish him for it any less than it punishes everyone else.

Whether this is nihilism or something more like tragic realism is a question I'll be working on for a while.

Rating: 10/10. One of the handful of books that genuinely changed what I thought literature could do.
