# Miso soup variations

Base recipe is fixed: dashi stock, miso dissolved off heat, serve immediately. What varies is what you put in it and which miso you use.

## The stock problem

Instant dashi (hondashi) is fine. I've made proper kombu-and-katsuobushi dashi and the result is better but the difference is smaller than the effort difference, especially for weeknight soup. Where it matters is when the soup has nothing else in it — plain tofu and wakame soup with good dashi is significantly better than with instant.

## Miso types

- **White (shiro):** Sweet, mild, good for lighter soups and spring/summer use
- **Red (aka):** Stronger, saltier, better in winter with heartier add-ins
- **Mixed (awase):** Compromise, what most restaurants use
- **Mugi (barley):** Earthy, my current preference — a sweetness that's different from shiro

## Combination ideas

**Classic:** silken tofu, dried wakame, sliced scallion, white miso
**Heartier:** firm tofu, sliced shiitake, enoki, red miso
**Winter:** daikon matchsticks, burdock root, carrot, red miso — this is close to tonjiru without the pork
**Unexpected:** roasted sweet potato (precooked), white miso, mirin — almost dessert-adjacent

## Temperature note

Miso should never boil after it's added — the beneficial bacteria die above ~60°C and the flavor turns bitter. Dissolve miso in a small amount of the hot stock before adding to the pot, and serve immediately. Never reheat.
