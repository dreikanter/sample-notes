# Train to Edinburgh — journey notes

Left King's Cross at 09:00. Window seat on the east side, which gives the North Sea coast from around Berwick onwards. This was deliberate — last time I sat on the west side and missed it entirely.

The flat land north of Peterborough always surprises me. The fields are vast in a way that feels almost Central European, broken only by drainage ditches and the occasional stand of poplars. Some of the farms seem to go on for kilometres without a hedgerow.

Durham came and went faster than expected. The cathedral is visible for about 40 seconds from the line — a glimpse of grey stone above the town, then gone. It must be one of the great brief views from any rail route in Britain, and completely unremarked in the journey.

Crossed the Royal Border Bridge at Berwick just before 13:00. The Tweed is wide here, dark and moving fast. This is always the moment the trip feels like it's arriving somewhere.

North of Berwick the track runs close enough to the coast that on clear days you can see the Farne Islands offshore. Today was overcast but the sea was a convincing grey-green, and a line of gannets was working low over the water heading north.

Edinburgh arrived at 15:50, five minutes early. Waverley station is still one of the better city arrivals in Britain — you emerge directly into the Old Town rather than some peripheral interchange.
