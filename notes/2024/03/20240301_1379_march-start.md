# March start — opening the month

March feels like a pivot month. The light is noticeably returning — the 6pm sunsets are back and the difference from the 4:16pm December solstice is visceral. The tomato seedlings are three inches tall. The garlic is fully up and putting on growth.

This is the month where the garden becomes active work rather than passive waiting. The cover crop winter rye needs to be turned under in the next three weeks before it gets too established to incorporate easily. The cold frame spinach is ready to harvest — got my first handful last Saturday.

At work, the big infrastructure bet has landed (see [[20240219_1373]]) and the next several sprints will feel different — more incremental, more maintenance-y. There's something slightly deflating about this after the focused effort of Q1, but it's the right mode. The system needs to stabilize before we add the next ambitious thing.

**What I want to do this month:**

Run the 5k on April 7. The training is on track. Ran 4.5km on Saturday without stopping, which is 90% of the race distance. 

Finish the on-call runbook. It's been a carried item since November.

Try at least one thing I've never cooked before.

**Reading:**

Currently between books for the first time in months. I'm going to let this last a day or two before picking something up — the gap is interesting. There's a different quality to thinking when no active narrative is competing for mental space.

Reference for the seasonal shift: [Robert Frost's poem "Two Tramps in Mud Time"](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/53380/two-tramps-in-mud-time) — the March description is accurate to this latitude.
