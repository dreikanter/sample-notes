# Train journey notes

Four-hour train from the city heading northeast. The route goes through industrial zones before opening into agricultural land around the ninety-minute mark. I've made this trip maybe eight times in various directions over the years.

What I always notice on trains that I don't notice driving: the backs of things. Houses show their utilitarian side to the tracks — decks, garages, storage, the real shape of a property before the landscaped front. Industrial buildings seen from rail have this quality of honest infrastructure. Pipes, loading docks, ventilation equipment. The front-of-house performance is only for roads.

The agricultural stretch today had a particular quality — enormous fields still brown from winter with snow lingering in the shadowed furrows between rows. The geometry from this height is more abstract. The tractor lines from last fall's planting are visible as faint parallel impressions across the whole field.

Seat neighbor was a retired teacher returning from a conference. We talked for forty minutes about the quality of attention in students versus twenty years ago. His take: it's not worse, it's different. Students are better at managing multiple streams of information but struggle with sustained single-subject focus. I'm not sure he's right but it was a more nuanced position than I expected from the opening sentence.

Arrived twenty minutes late. The late-winter light at the destination was extraordinary — horizontal gold across the platform, everyone briefly bronze-tinted.
