# On keeping notes

I've been doing this in some form for about eight years — paper notebooks before that, digital since around 2016. The question that comes up occasionally is whether any of it is actually useful or whether it's a form of productive procrastination that creates the feeling of thinking without the substance.

Honest answer: both. The notes that get revisited are maybe 15% of what I write. The other 85% functions as a write-to-understand exercise where the act of writing clarifies thinking that then sits in my head rather than in the file. This doesn't mean the file was wasted — the output was the clarity, not the text.

The revisited 15% tends to be reference material (code patterns, recipes, technical specifics) rather than reflections or reading notes. This is slightly disappointing from a philosophical standpoint. The dream of a personal knowledge system where insights connect and compound is appealing but probably overrated. Most of what matters is either learned deeply enough to internalize or forgotten because it wasn't important enough to matter.

What does seem to work: keeping notes during or immediately after experiences (readings, conversations, observations). Notes written from memory two days later are already half-reconstructed narratives rather than records.

Cross-referencing these notes with older ones happens rarely but when it does it's genuinely interesting — [[20240314_1428]] on rereading is relevant here, the idea that you read differently at different ages applies to old notes too.
