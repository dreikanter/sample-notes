# Bread scoring experiment

Ran a controlled-ish comparison on two identical 850g boules from the same batch of dough. Same hydration (76%), same cold proof time (14 hours at 4°C), same bake temperature (250°C with steam first 20 minutes).

**Loaf A:** Single curved score at 30° to the surface, following the ear technique from the Tartine Bread book. Scored quickly with a stiff lame.

**Loaf B:** Cross-hatch pattern, four cuts at 45°, less angled blade.

Results after bake:
- Loaf A had dramatic ear, excellent oven spring, good internal structure. Crust color mahogany.
- Loaf B spread rather than rose. The cross-hatch didn't give the dough a clear direction to open. Crust still tasty but appearance disappointing.

The ear on Loaf A is almost entirely about giving the gas an escape route in a specific direction. When you score at a shallow angle, the top "flap" peels back during baking and the gas pushes up through the slot. Cross-hatching divides the surface tension without directing it, so the loaf exhausts expansion uniformly rather than dramatically.

Interior crumb on both was acceptable. The 76% hydration gives a fairly open crumb but not the wild irregular holes you see in photos — that probably requires higher hydration and better gluten development than I'm achieving.

Next test: comparing 4-hour room temp proof vs 14-hour cold proof for crumb structure.

Reference for scoring technique: https://www.theperfectloaf.com/guides/how-to-score-bread/
