# Attention and reading — a note to self

I've noticed over the past few months that I start more books than I finish. Not because the books are bad — I abandoned *The Dispossessed* twice before reading it cover to cover last December (see [[20231208_1038]]) and it turned out to be exactly the book I needed. The problem seems to be elsewhere.

What I notice: I read two or three pages and then check something. Not always my phone — sometimes it's just looking up from the page, letting my mind wander to whatever is unresolved. The reading is interrupted at the sentence level, not just between sessions.

Some of this is probably ordinary life pressure. But I think there's also a habit loop that's been reinforced by screen reading — where links, scroll depth, and notification edges have all trained a kind of restless scanning rather than settling.

Things that seem to help, based on recent observation:

Reading in the morning before opening any screen, even briefly, seems to set a different rhythm for the day. The brain hasn't been switched into scan mode yet.

Physical books over ebooks for anything I want to actually absorb. The absence of affordances — no links, no brightness slider to fiddle with, no battery indicator — seems meaningful.

Taking notes while reading slows me down in a useful way. Not summaries — actual observations and responses, like the Le Guin note referenced above. It makes reading feel like a conversation rather than consumption.

The research on reading and attention is worth engaging with: Maryanne Wolf's work on deep reading is a good entry point — https://www.maryannewolf.com/reader-come-home
