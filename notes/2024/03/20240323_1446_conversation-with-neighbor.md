# Conversation with neighbor about the old orchard

Ran into Marguerite while checking the mail — we ended up talking for forty minutes on the sidewalk, neither of us dressed for it. She's lived on the street for thirty-one years and knows the history of properties going back further through an elderly neighbor who has since moved to assisted living.

The empty lot on the corner that I've always assumed was a failed development was apparently an apple orchard until the 1970s. The property belonged to a family that ran a farm stand from the 1940s onward, selling produce from a small wooden structure at the road. The house was torn down in 1979 when the last family member who'd been living there died. The lot sat for about fifteen years in a dispute over whether it would be subdivided, eventually settled when a developer bought it and then went bust in the early 1990s recession. It's been vacant since, owned by a holding company.

Marguerite says the old apple trees were still standing in the mid-1990s when she moved in. Three of them survived until 2003 or so when they were removed — she thought they might have had fire blight. There's no visible sign of orchard now but she pointed out a slight rise in the land at the north end of the lot where she thinks the farm stand was.

I'd walked past that lot probably a thousand times. Knowing this changes how it looks — I keep expecting to see something that isn't there.
