# Fermentation log — kimchi batch three

**Start date:** March 20
**Napa cabbage:** 1.8kg before salting, 1.4kg after
**Salt (kosher):** 60g for initial brine, applied by hand in layers

## Day 0 — prep

Quartered the cabbage, salted between leaves, let it sit for two hours turning occasionally. The goal is wilting without cooking — osmosis draws out the water, making the cabbage pliable and concentrating flavor. Rinsed three times, squeezed hard.

**Paste:** 3 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes, not generic chili), 1 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tbsp salted fermented shrimp (saeujeot), 4 cloves garlic minced, 1 tsp fresh ginger, 1 tsp sugar. Added 4 scallions cut to 5cm lengths, 1 medium daikon in matchsticks.

Mixed paste into cabbage by hand (gloves essential — gochugaru stains everything). Packed tightly into two 1L jars, pressing until liquid covered the vegetables. Left 2cm headspace.

## Day 1

Bubbles visible in the jar liquid. Burped the jars once. Taste: still very salty, raw-tasting. Put in cool spot (18°C) rather than room temperature to slow fermentation.

## Day 3

Good sourness developing. The shrimp paste flavor is integrating. Ready for the fridge now — cool fermentation continues slowly. This is the style I prefer over sharply sour kimchi.

## Comparison to batch 2

Batch 2 had too much garlic and was overwhelmed by it through the whole fermentation. Reducing from 6 to 4 cloves was the right call. Batch 2 also used regular hot pepper flakes which lack the fruity quality of gochugaru.
