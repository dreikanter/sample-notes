# Reading notes — The Dispossessed

Started this on the recommendation of a friend who said it was the most accurate fictional depiction of academic life she'd read. She's a physicist. The comparison is interesting because the book is also one of the central works of anarchist political philosophy in fiction.

Le Guin's setup: two worlds in binary orbit — Anarres, a resource-poor moon settled by anarchist exiles; and Urras, the lush, capitalist (in one of its nations) home planet. Shevek is a physicist on Anarres who travels to Urras and the novel alternates between his past on Anarres and his present on Urras.

## What strikes me so far (page 120)

The anarchist society on Anarres is not utopian in Le Guin's telling. It has its own social pressures — conformism, conservative resistance to novelty, the soft coercion of collective disapproval replacing the hard coercion of state power. The author's honesty about this is what makes it literature rather than polemic.

The physics — Shevek is working on a unified theory of time — functions as a structural mirror for the novel's own temporal structure. Sequency versus simultaneity. The past chapters and present chapters exist simultaneously in the reader's experience.

## The comparison to *The Dawn of Everything*

Both are interested in the same question: is radically different social organization possible? Le Guin answers via fiction; Graeber and Wengrow answer via archaeology. The fictional version has advantages — it can show what living inside a different set of assumptions feels like, not just that such assumptions existed.
