# Reading attempt — Blood Meridian

Third attempt at this book. Stopped at forty pages in December, stopped again at thirty pages in January. Started again on March 3 and something clicked at around page sixty.

The prose is the first obstacle: it arrives like geology — long sentences without punctuation, Biblical cadences, archaic vocabulary that you can't skip over. McCarthy is not accommodating. You match his pace or you fall behind.

What changed: I started reading slower. One chapter per sitting, maximum. Not pushing through to build momentum. The book rewards attention rather than pace.

## What I'm seeing now

The violence is not the point of the book, though it accumulates in the first hundred pages beyond anything I've read. The point seems to be the Judge — the seven-foot hairless philosopher-killer who gives long speeches about war and knowledge and the nature of reality between murders. He is the only character who makes the violence legible, and his ledger contains everything — he makes notes and sketches of everything he observes, then destroys the originals. Possession by knowledge, then erasure of the thing known.

The landscape is a character. The Chihuahuan desert as moral condition.

I'm 120 pages in and can't quite look at the book and can't stop. This is the intended effect.

Reference: [Harold Bloom on Blood Meridian](https://www.nytimes.com/1985/06/02/books/among-the-rarest-of-birds.html) — his introduction is the best critical framing I've read for first-time readers.
