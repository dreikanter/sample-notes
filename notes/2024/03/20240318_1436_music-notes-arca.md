# Music notes — Arca

Went through the Arca discography in rough order over two evenings. Started with *Xen* (2014) and ended with *Kick i* (2020) and selected tracks from the later Kick series.

*Xen* is the one to start with. The production is deconstructed club music — structures that almost resolve into recognizable forms and then fracture or collapse. The title track opens with something like a beat that gets bent until it's unrecognizable. There's a quality of listening to a building settle and shift rather than to music.

*Mutant* (2015) is harsher. Less melodic, more abrasive. The percussion sounds designed to be unpleasant in specific ways — almost medical in the precision of its discomfort. Harder to return to.

*Arca* (2017) was the transitional record — voice appears seriously for the first time, more conventional song shapes, more emotional legibility. "Piel" is beautiful in an almost pop sense. The identity themes are present throughout the album but never didactic.

*Kick i* represents the synthesis: hyperpop-adjacent in some tracks, deep club influence, Arca's own voice as an instrument. "Time" is genuinely affecting. The reggaeton-influenced sections land better than they should given how eclectic the context is.

What the work shares across the discography: the body as material. Sound that seems to have physical weight and texture. Music that you process somatically as much as aurally.
