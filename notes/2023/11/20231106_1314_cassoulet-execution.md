# Cassoulet — execution notes

Following the planning note from last week [[20231102_1311]]. This is what actually happened.

## Day 1 — duck confit and beans

**Duck confit:**

Rubbed two duck legs with salt, black pepper, fresh thyme, and a bay leaf. Left overnight in the fridge. Rinsed, dried, placed in a small oven-safe pot completely covered with rendered duck fat (bought, not homemade — I have limits). Cooked at 90°C for 8 hours. The legs were tender enough that the bone slid out cleanly.

Let cool in the fat. Refrigerated overnight. This is now a building block.

**Beans:**

Soaked Royal Corona beans overnight. Cooked the next morning with a halved onion, bay leaf, and pork rind (bought a package from the butcher — traditional and worth it). Cooked to almost-tender, not fully cooked. They'll finish in the cassoulet.

## Day 2 — assembly and baking

**Pork shoulder:**

Braised a 600g piece with white wine, aromatics, and chicken stock. Two hours in the oven. Shredded into large chunks.

**Assembly:**

Layered beans, pork shoulder pieces, sliced kielbasa, and the duck confit legs (skin side up) in a Dutch oven. Poured the bean cooking liquid over everything to just cover.

Breadcrumbs made from stale sourdough [[20230825_1244]], mixed with parsley and garlic oil. Spread over the top.

**Baking:**

210°C, 45 minutes. When the crust formed, broke it down into the beans with a spoon, added a small amount of stock, another layer of breadcrumbs. Repeated twice.

**Result:**

Exceptional. Rich, savory, deeply satisfying. The duck skin crisped in the final bake. Served to three people with a simple green salad. The bean and confit combination is one of the better things I've cooked this year.

Worth the effort. Will do it again.

For duck confit technique reference: https://www.seriouseats.com/duck-confit-recipe
