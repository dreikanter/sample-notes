# Film review — Past Lives

Celine Song's debut is the kind of film that refuses to be summarized without losing what makes it work. The plot — two Korean childhood friends separated by emigration who reconnect as adults — sounds like the skeleton of a dozen other things. But the emotional architecture is unusual: it is almost entirely about the weight of unlived lives.

Greta Lee and Teo Yoo are remarkable together precisely because they convey so much through restraint. The final scene at the curb — twenty-four years of feeling compressed into a few seconds of looking — is the best closing shot I've seen in years.

What struck me most on reflection: the film doesn't treat New York and Seoul as symbols of different selves but as actual places with specific textures. The scenes in the Korean bars feel grounded in a way that's rare in diaspora films, which often aestheticize the country of origin into something too tidy.

The concept of "in-yeon" — the Korean notion that you are linked to someone by having brushed their sleeve in a past life — gives the film its metaphysical hinge without ever becoming mystical. It stays right at the edge of real feeling.

Minor criticism: the husband character (John Magaro) is given a late-night conversation scene that threatens to tip the film into a more conventional triangle. Song pulls it back, but just barely.

Rating: 9/10. More information at [A24's page for the film](https://a24films.com/films/past-lives).
