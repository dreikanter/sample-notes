# Cassoulet project — planning notes

Cassoulet is the kind of dish I've been avoiding for years because the authentic versions involve three days and multiple trips to specialty butchers. Making peace with an approximation.

Reference: Paula Wolfert's cassoulet chapter in "The Cooking of Southwest France" — the standard English-language treatment. https://www.amazon.com/Cooking-Southwest-France-Recipes-Traditions/dp/0471286648

## What cassoulet requires (technically)

- Duck confit (legs, slow-cooked in their own fat)
- Garbure sausage or Toulouse sausage (coarse-ground pork, garlic-forward)
- Some combination of lamb or pork shoulder, slow-braised
- Tarbais beans (a specific French white bean, very hard to source)
- Time: the bean-meat-breadcrumb-crust cycle is traditionally repeated three times over two to three days

## What I'm planning (honest approximation)

- Duck confit: make my own. Two duck legs, rendered duck fat, aromatics. This takes 24 hours but most of it is unattended.
- Sausage: find a good butcher sausage — the German kielbasa at my market is close in style to Toulouse
- Pork shoulder: braise in advance, shred
- Beans: Rancho Gordo Royal Corona beans — similar to Tarbais, large and creamy. https://www.ranchogordo.com/
- Two-day process instead of three

## Timeline

Day 1 (next Saturday): make duck confit, soak beans
Day 2 (Sunday): cook beans separately, braise pork, assemble and bake for 2–3 hours with crust
Day 2 evening: eat

This is an honest adaptation, not a recreation. Still more involved than most things I make.
