# Morning coffee thoughts on attention

There's something quietly decisive about the first hour of the day. I've been reading Cal Newport's work on deep work for the second time — not because I forgot it, but because I keep catching myself violating every principle he lays out. The phone within arm's reach. The inbox opened before breakfast. The slow leak of attention before the day has really started.

This morning I left my phone on the charger across the room and sat with the coffee and a notebook. No agenda, just thirty minutes of thinking without a prompt from an algorithm. It felt uncomfortable for roughly the first eight minutes and then something loosened.

The interesting thing Newport doesn't fully address is the role of *texture* in a morning. It's not just about avoiding distraction — it's about putting something real in place of it. The weight of a ceramic mug. The specific sound of a French press plunger. These small anchors seem to do something neurological.

I want to test a hypothesis: that five consecutive mornings of phone-free first hours will measurably change how I approach the 10am–noon window, which is historically when I do my best writing. The baseline is pretty clear — lately I've produced very little before lunch.

Reference: [Newport's blog on rituals](https://www.calnewport.com/blog/) has a few relevant posts from 2022 that are worth returning to.
