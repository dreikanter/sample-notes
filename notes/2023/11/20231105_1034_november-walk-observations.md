# November walk — canal path observations

Left around ten in the morning when the mist was still sitting low over the water. The towpath was soft underfoot — a week of rain had turned the clay sections into something that gripped boot soles. Not unpleasant; just slow.

The willows along the south bank have dropped most of their leaves but retain a kind of yellow-green haze at the tips of long trailing branches. Against a pale sky this produces a colour that doesn't quite have a name — not gold, not green, somewhere between the two and slightly grey.

Counted three grey herons over the two-hour stretch. One perched motionless on a mooring post for so long I thought it might be a carved decoy until it turned its head. They seem entirely indifferent to passing boats and joggers.

The locks at the midpoint of the walk were being operated by a couple moving a narrowboat south. The procedure takes about fifteen minutes if you're efficient: close the upstream gates, open the sluices to drain, wait, open the lower gates, motor through, reverse everything. Simple hydraulics, but satisfying to watch.

Passed a section where the bank had been reinforced with new sheet piling. The smell of disturbed mud lingered there, organic and slightly sulphurous. A notice from the Canal & River Trust indicated ongoing bank restoration work — their restoration projects are documented at [https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/enjoy-the-waterways/canal-and-river-network/restoration-projects](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/enjoy-the-waterways/canal-and-river-network/restoration-projects).

Back home by noon, damp around the collar, feet warm. Good use of a grey morning.
