# Mushroom foraging notes — autumn hunt

Went out to the mixed woodland on the north edge of the reservoir with K. this morning. Ground was damp from two days of rain, which made conditions good. We covered maybe four kilometres of trail in three hours.

Finds: a decent flush of oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus) on a fallen beech — took about 400g. A few scattered Laccaria amethystina, which are pretty but not worth eating in quantity. One spectacular but untouchable Amanita muscaria near the larch stand, bright cap still mostly intact.

Nothing in the Boletus family today, which was a disappointment. The season feels like it shifted late this year — we had a dry September that probably delayed the mycelial activity.

K. brought a copy of Roger Phillips' field guide, which remains the most practical reference I've used. The photography is unmatched for identification confidence: https://www.rogersplants.co.uk/product/mushrooms/

We debated the hen-of-the-woods (Grifola frondosa) season. The literature suggests it fruits September to November in the UK, usually at the base of old oaks. We've found it twice in the past but not today.

Worth revisiting the oak stand on the south slope in ten days if the weather holds mild. Also want to try the plantation edge where we found chanterelles in 2021.

Basic rule of thumb that's kept us safe: if you can't positively identify a find from at least two independent features, leave it. Spore prints are underused by beginners and take only a few hours.

Cleaning tip: dry brush first, then a barely damp cloth for stubborn soil. Never submerge oyster mushrooms — they waterlog fast.
