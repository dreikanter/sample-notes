# Lisbon travel notes — day two

Alfama in the morning before the tourist groups arrive is a different city. The narrow streets smell of last night's rain and something faintly floral I can't identify. An old man sweeping a doorstep, a cat asleep on a wall in a square that isn't marked on any map I've seen.

Walked from the Miradouro da Graça down to the waterfront, then took the 28 tram as far as Campo de Ourique before it got too crowded to breathe. The tram is genuinely useful but also genuinely a tourist trap by 10am. Worth it before 8:30.

Lunch at a tasca near the Mercado da Ribeira — salt cod croquettes and a glass of Vinho Verde that cost four euros. The croquettes were better than anything I'd had in Lisbon so far: crispy outside, almost creamy inside with enough salt and parsley that they didn't need sauce. Made a note to find the recipe.

Afternoon in the Museu do Azulejo. Tile museum sounds like an afternoon of obligation but the building is a converted convent and the collection is genuinely beautiful. The large panel of 18th-century Lisbon (before the 1755 earthquake) is remarkable — the city before the catastrophe, preserved in blue and white.

Fado in the evening at a small house in Mouraria. The fadista was perhaps sixty, unannounced, dressed without ceremony. She sang for forty minutes. I do not speak Portuguese and it didn't matter.

City guide reference: [Time Out Lisbon](https://www.timeout.com/lisbon) has surprisingly reliable coverage that avoids the more obvious tourist pitfalls.
