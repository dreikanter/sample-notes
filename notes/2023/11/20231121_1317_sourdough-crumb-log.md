# Sourdough crumb log — November bake

Third bake this month, first one that cracked open with anything resembling the open crumb I've been chasing. Key variables changed from last week: hydration bumped from 75% to 78%, bulk ferment extended by forty minutes (total 4h 20min at 24°C), and I finally shaped more aggressively on the bench.

The ear came up well. The crust had that hollow sound when tapped. Interior: irregular but present holes, not the gummy paste of bake two.

What I think helped most was the lamination step. Stretched the dough out thin on a damp counter around hour two of bulk, folded it letter-style, rested ten minutes, then shaped. The gluten felt entirely different afterward — tighter, more responsive.

Still problems: the bottom darkened faster than expected, probably from the cast iron. Next time I'll try parchment under the dough rather than greasing. Also the score slipped a bit — I need a sharper lame or at least a fresher blade. The current one has done about twelve bakes.

Flour: 90% King Arthur bread flour, 10% whole wheat (Farmer Ground)
Starter: 100% hydration rye, fed 10h before use
Salt: 2%

Resources I keep returning to: [The Perfect Loaf](https://www.theperfectloaf.com/) has a hydration calculator and detailed bulk fermentation notes that match my kitchen temps almost exactly. Maurizio's approach to the windowpane test is the clearest explanation I've found.
