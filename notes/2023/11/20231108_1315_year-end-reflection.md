# Year-end reflection — early November draft

Writing this now, six weeks before the year ends, because I've found that December reflections are colored by proximity to the holidays and by resolution-setting pressure. November feels clearer.

## What I set out to do this year

Not comprehensive, but the things I remember caring about in January:
1. Get more consistent with fitness — 3x per week, something sustained
2. Finish a significant technical learning project
3. Read more widely than previous years
4. Be more present at work rather than half-attending while thinking about the next thing
5. Start cooking more seriously (not just eating well, but the cooking itself as a practice)

## What happened

**Fitness:** Consistent for February through May, then sporadic during the summer, then picked back up in October. The pattern is familiar. The daily walks have been more reliable than the formal workouts and probably do more for my mental health.

**Technical learning:** The Rust project [[20231011_1290]] is ongoing. The Kubernetes knowledge [[20230917_1267]] was applied in a real migration [[20231016_1295]]. This was more successful than previous years when learning stayed theoretical.

**Reading:** Much better. More fiction, more diverse — Butler, Le Guin, Robinson, Rulfo [[20231104_1313]], Ishiguro [[20230918_1268]], Pirsig. The habit of taking notes while reading [[20230803_1219]] made the reading more meaningful.

**Presence at work:** Inconsistent. The habit of writing meeting notes immediately after helped. Still drift during long meetings.

**Cooking:** This was the year's genuine success. The fermentation experiments [[20230805_1222]], the sourdough troubleshooting [[20230825_1244]], the stock-making [[20231020_1299]], and especially the cassoulet [[20231106_1314]] — cooking became a real practice rather than something I did to eat.

## What I actually care about that I didn't plan for

The notes habit. Starting and sustaining these notes has been more valuable than I expected [[20231019_1298]]. The cross-referencing and the act of writing both improved the underlying thinking.

## What I want to carry forward

The cooking, obviously. The daily walks. The reading practice with notes. The habit of finishing things — the sourdough troubleshooting session [[20230808_1227]] and the cassoulet planning [[20231102_1311]] both taught me that following through on a project past the difficult middle is where the value actually is.

A good year, imperfect in the ways years are imperfect.
