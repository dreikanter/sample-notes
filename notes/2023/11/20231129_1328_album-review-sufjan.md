# Album review — Sufjan Stevens, Javelin

I waited a few weeks before writing anything about this record because I wanted to hear it enough times that I was responding to the album rather than to the context (the dedication to his late partner, Evans Richardson, is disclosed in the liner notes and shapes everything you hear once you know it).

The album is quieter than *Carrie & Lowell* in arrangement but not in feeling. Where that record felt like forensic documentation, *Javelin* feels like something more unguarded — less constructed grief, more ongoing presence of grief. The title track is one of the most restrained things he's recorded. The production by Stevens and his collaborators keeps instruments close and dry; nothing is reverbed into distance.

"Will Anybody Ever Love Me?" is the obvious centerpiece and probably should be. It's a direct question in a way Sufjan rarely allows. The harmonics on the acoustic guitar at the end of the track go somewhere unexpected.

The choral elements on "Everything That Grows" recall *Illinois* without being nostalgic — the orchestration is in service of the feeling rather than demonstrating range.

My only reservation: two or three tracks in the second half feel like they belong to a different, slightly more conventional album. They're not bad but they dilute the record's specificity.

Rating: 8/10. More at [Pitchfork's review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/sufjan-stevens-javelin/).
