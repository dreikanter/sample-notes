# Observation — Berlin Tempelhof field

Spent two hours at Tempelhof this afternoon. It's one of the more unusual spaces I've visited: a former international airport turned public park, the runways still intact, used by cyclists and rollerbladers and kite flyers, the old terminal building looming at one end.

The scale is disorienting at first. You walk onto the field and there's nothing to stop your eye for several hundred meters in any direction. The Berlin sky opens up in a way it doesn't in the neighborhoods. In a dense city, this amount of deliberately unbuilt space feels almost transgressive.

There are community gardens along one edge — long narrow plots separated by low fences, each with its own character. Some are tidy and productive, others are intentional wildflower meadows, one had a small solar panel running what looked like a water pump. There's something compelling about watching urban gardeners claim a strip of airport tarmac.

The history is complicated. The Luftbrücke airlift flew from Tempelhof in 1948–49. Before that, the Nazis used the terminal building as a detention site early in the regime. The building itself was designed by Ernst Sagebiel under Albert Speer's direction, and it's massive in that specific fascist-monumental way — meant to project power at scale. It now houses a refugee shelter and a museum.

The juxtaposition is very Berlin.

More about the site: https://www.thf-berlin.de/en/

I left when the wind picked up and the kite flyers got more aggressive about claiming territory. Tomorrow: Gemäldegalerie.
