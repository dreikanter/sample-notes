# Album review — Sufjan Stevens "Javelin"

Sufjan Stevens released "Javelin" in October 2023 (I'm getting to it late — writing this after a first full listen on a Berlin afternoon). It's his most stripped-back record in a decade: mostly voice, piano, and strings, with occasional electronic texture.

The album was written after the death of his partner Evans Richardson IV, which contextualizes the overwhelming tenderness and grief in the lyrics. It's the kind of record that doesn't explain itself — you feel the loss in the space between notes before you understand it from the words.

Standout tracks for me on a first pass:

**"Goodbye Evergreen"** — opens with a fingerpicked guitar pattern that becomes a kind of anchor for the whole album. The line "I don't need to know what happens next" lands differently the second time you hear it.

**"So You Are Tired"** — the most direct the album gets. Just piano and voice. No arrangement to hide behind.

**"A Running Start"** — the one track that opens up sonically, with layered harmonies and what sounds like lap steel. A little light in an otherwise quiet record.

It's not an easy listen, not because it's difficult musically but because it asks for your full attention. This is not background music.

Album page: https://sufjanstevens.com/music/javelin/

I want to hear it again in a different context — probably at home at night, not through headphones on a plane.
