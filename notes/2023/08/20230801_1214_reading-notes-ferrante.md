# Reading notes — My Brilliant Friend (Ferrante)

Elena Ferrante, *My Brilliant Friend* (2011, trans. Ann Goldstein). First volume of the Neapolitan quartet. Late to this — it's been on the list for years.

The novel is narrated by Elena in retrospect, writing about her childhood friendship with Lila. The retrospective framing is important: Elena always knows, and we always know she knows, what happened to these people over the decades after the events she describes. She withholds. The novel is both the story and a performance of selective memory.

What Ferrante does unusually well is the social granularity of the neighbourhood. The hierarchy of the rione — which families are the good families, which are allied to which, who owes what to whom, where power actually sits — is mapped in the first hundred pages with extraordinary density. And this isn't decorative: the novel's conflicts all flow from this structure.

Lila is the more compelling character to read about precisely because Elena is doing the reading. The brilliant friend is always mediated — we see her through eyes that are simultaneously admiring, envious, and protective. Whether we're seeing Lila accurately is an open question the novel never resolves.

The prose in translation is direct without being flat. Goldstein's choices seem right to me, though I can't check them against the Italian.

Going to read all four. The sense of implication — that everything in this volume is the first chapter of a very long sentence — is its own kind of propulsion.
