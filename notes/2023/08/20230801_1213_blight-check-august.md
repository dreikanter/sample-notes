# Blight check — early August

The risk period is here. Checking the tomatoes carefully.

**Conditions**

August is high-risk for blight (Phytophthora infestans) because the nights are warm enough to maintain humidity while the days are still hot. The conditions that trigger blight (Beaumont periods): temperature above 10 °C for 48+ continuous hours with relative humidity above 75%.

**Inspection today**

All three plants checked leaf by leaf.

Plant 1: Some yellowing on lower leaves — this is normal senescence, not disease. Lower leaves removed as a precaution. No dark lesions, no white mould on undersides.

Plant 2: Healthy. One slightly curled leaf on the upper stem from heat. No blight signs.

Plant 3: Possible concern — two leaves with small brown patches on the upper surface. Checked for the diagnostic signs: brown patches with a water-soaked margin and white mould on the underside. **White mould absent.** The patches look more like scorch (dry heat on foliage) than blight. Removed those leaves anyway and bagged them separately (not compost, just in case).

**Action**

No spray intervention yet — I prefer to avoid copper fungicide unless I can confirm blight. Removed all affected/senescent lower foliage. Improved air circulation by cutting one lateral shoot that had been touching an adjacent plant.

Will check again in 4 days. If the white mould appears on underside lesions, that's confirmation and I'll act immediately.
