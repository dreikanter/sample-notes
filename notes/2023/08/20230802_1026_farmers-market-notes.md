# Farmers market notes — late summer

Arrived just after seven when the best vendors still had full tables. The heirloom tomato selection was exceptional this week: Cherokee Purple, Green Zebra, and a squat yellow variety the grower called Taxi. Bought two pounds of the Taxi tomatoes to roast slowly with garlic and thyme — they hold up better than the Cherokees, which collapse beautifully in salads but turn watery under heat.

The mushroom vendor had chanterelles for the first time this season. Priced at $18/lb, which felt steep but not unreasonable given how wet the past two weeks have been. Took a quarter pound to fold into scrambled eggs tomorrow morning.

A new bread stall appeared near the east entrance: a couple baking out of a wood-fired oven they built themselves. Their miche had a tight, slightly chewy crumb with a pale beige crust that crackled when squeezed. Nothing fancy on the label — just "whole wheat sourdough, 1.5 kg." Paid $12 and it was worth every cent. Reminds me of the loaves described in the Tartine method. See [Tartine Bakery's philosophy](https://www.tartinebakery.com/about) for context on long-fermented doughs.

Picked up a bundle of lemon verbena from the herb section. No specific plan — likely a simple syrup for iced tea, or muddled into a cold gin drink this weekend.

Got there early enough to avoid the crowds, but by eight-thirty the narrow aisles were impassable. Worth the early alarm.
