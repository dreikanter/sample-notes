# Book short review — "The Dispossessed" by Ursula K. Le Guin

Re-read for the third time. Still one of the best novels about political philosophy I know of, which is saying something because it's nominally a science fiction novel about twin planets.

Anarres is an anarchist moon colony that broke from Urras 180 years before the novel begins. Shevek, a physicist, travels to Urras and confronts the contradictions between his own society's ideals and its actual practice, and between Urras's abundance and its oppression.

Le Guin structures the book in alternating chapters: even chapters follow Shevek's present on Urras, odd chapters trace his past on Anarres. This creates a continuous comparison. Anarres is free but impoverished and subject to social conformism that operates through shame rather than law. Urras is prosperous but stratified and violent. Neither is the answer. Both are the answer.

The famous line: "You cannot buy the revolution. You cannot make the revolution. You can only be the revolution." It's the kind of sentence that sounds like a poster until you've read the novel and understand what it cost Shevek to arrive at it.

The physicis subplot about Shevek's Simultaneity Theory is actually necessary — the idea that past and future coexist, that time isn't a sequence but a field, gives the dual-timeline structure thematic weight rather than just formal cleverness.

Read it alongside Graeber's work on mutual aid for interesting resonances.
