# Film review: "Past Lives" (2023)

Celine Song's debut feature is one of the more quietly devastating films I've seen in years. It follows Nora and Hae Sung across three time periods — Seoul as children, a Skype relationship in their mid-twenties, and a reunion in New York when both are in their early thirties — and asks what it means to live the life you chose by also living with the life you didn't.

The film is almost entirely dialogue and reaction. There are no big confrontations. What makes it work is the commitment to ambiguity: Nora has built a life with Arthur that is real and good, and Hae Sung represents something that might also have been real and good, and neither option cancels the other out.

John Magaro as Arthur has a scene near the end where he sits at a bar while Nora and Hae Sung are outside, and you watch him wrestling with the fact that his wife loves someone who isn't him, in some form, and that this doesn't mean she loves Arthur less. He doesn't resolve the feeling. He just holds it. I found this more affecting than most explicitly emotional performances.

The concept of "in-yeon" (인연) — the idea that chance encounters carry the weight of prior lives — is introduced early and hangs over the whole film without being over-explained.
