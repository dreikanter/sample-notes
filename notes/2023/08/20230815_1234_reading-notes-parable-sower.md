# Reading notes — "Parable of the Sower" by Octavia Butler

Butler's 1993 novel is set in a near-future California (2024–2027 in the narrative) that has collapsed under climate change, water shortages, poverty, and political dysfunction. Lauren Olamina is a teenager in a walled neighborhood in Robledo who has a condition called hyperempathy — she physically feels the pain and pleasure of others — and who spends the novel surviving the collapse of that neighborhood and building a new community around a philosophy she's developing called Earthseed.

**What works:**

The matter-of-fact tone. Lauren keeps a diary and the entries are clinical about violence and loss in a way that makes the horror more effective, not less. Butler doesn't linger. The worldbuilding is delivered through detail rather than exposition — you understand how bad things have gotten before it's stated outright.

Lauren's hyperempathy is interesting as a device. It could be a metaphor, but Butler grounds it in physical mechanics and shows how it's a liability in a violent world while also being the source of her ethical clarity.

**Earthseed:**

The religion Lauren invents is: "God is change. Shape God." It's a pragmatic theology, not a comforting one. The community that forms around it is chosen on the basis of shared values and survival need, not ancestry or belief.
