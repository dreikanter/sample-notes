# Reading notes — "Burnout" by Nagoski and Nagoski

Emily and Amelia Nagoski's 2019 book draws on stress physiology research to make a distinction I hadn't thought about clearly before: the stressor (the external cause of stress) and the stress (the physiological response) are separate things, and eliminating the stressor does not automatically complete the stress cycle.

The example: you escape a lion. The threat is gone — the stressor is resolved. But your nervous system is still running the emergency program. To complete the cycle, you need to discharge the accumulated activation somehow. The book argues this is why it's possible to solve a problem at work and still feel terrible — the problem went away but the stress response didn't get to finish.

**Ways to complete the stress cycle:**

The authors recommend physical activity as the most reliable method. Not necessarily intense exercise — even a 20-minute walk signals "we survived" to the nervous system. Other options: crying until done (not just starting), creative expression, and connection with people you trust.

**The Human Giver Syndrome:**

One section that hit hard is about the cultural expectation that some people (mapped historically and currently onto women and caregivers) should consider giving their attention and resources to others as a primary obligation rather than a choice. The "human giver" exists to provide rather than to receive. This expectation contributes to burnout by making self-maintenance feel selfish.

Recommended without reservations.
