# Reflection — three months working from home full time

It's been twelve weeks since the office converted to optional attendance and I've been working entirely from home. Some notes on what's changed.

**What's better:**

- No commute (was 45 minutes each way). That's 7.5 hours a week returned to me. I've been using it for morning walks and reading.
- Deep work blocks are easier to protect. I can set a three-hour focus window without anyone walking by to ask a question.
- Cooking lunch is both cheaper and better than the office options, which were a rotating cast of sad salads and expensive sandwiches.

**What's worse:**

- The boundary between work and rest has collapsed somewhat. I find myself checking Slack at 8pm in a way I wouldn't if leaving the office meant leaving.
- I miss spontaneous conversation. The ambient knowledge you get from overhearing someone debug a problem, or grabbing coffee and talking about nothing in particular — that's hard to replicate.
- My back hurts. The office chair was better than my desk chair at home, which I've been meaning to replace for eight months.

**What I've changed:**

- Hard stop at 6pm. Phone goes on do-not-disturb. This has been the single most important adjustment.
- A short "end of day" ritual: close all work tabs, write three lines about what I did and what tomorrow starts with.
- Bought a standing desk mat. Helps more than I expected.

Article that influenced the ritual idea: https://www.bbc.com/worklife/article/20200506-coronavirus-wfh-work-from-home-ending-remote-work-day

Still adjusting. Ask me again in three months.
