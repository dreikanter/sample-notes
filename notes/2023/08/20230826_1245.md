# Observations on notification fatigue

I turned off almost all notifications on my phone six months ago and I've been tracking the effect informally. Some findings.

**What I turned off:**
- All social media (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram)
- Email push notifications (I check email twice daily at scheduled times)
- Most app badges
- Group chats except one family group

**What stayed on:**
- Direct messages from specific contacts
- Calendar reminders
- SMS (but I muted most group texts)
- My bank (fraud alerts)

**What I noticed:**

Within the first week, I had a low-grade anxiety that I was missing something. By week three this faded. By month two, checking email twice daily felt completely natural, and I no longer felt the reflex to reach for my phone during conversations or while reading.

The interesting secondary effect: when I do check email at the designated times, I engage with it more intentionally instead of skimming and swiping. Response quality is better.

**The tradeoff:**

I did miss one time-sensitive message from a colleague during a minor incident at work. It resolved without me, but I was later asked why I hadn't responded quickly. Had an honest conversation about the policy. My manager was skeptical but supported it.

Whether this model works depends heavily on your role and the expectations of people around you. It requires negotiation.

A piece that influenced my approach: https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/04/technology/notifications-smartphone-update.html

The notifications didn't go away. I just stopped letting them interrupt me.
