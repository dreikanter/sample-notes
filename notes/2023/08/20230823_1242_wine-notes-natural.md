# Wine notes — natural wine exploration

Been exploring natural wines more seriously after a conversation with a sommelier at a wine bar in Berlin last week. Here are notes on three bottles I've tried since returning.

**2021 Domaine Mosse "Moussamoussettes" — Loire, Grolleau**

Pale red, almost rosé. Slight haze. Smell: red fruit, something faintly mineral, a bit of earth. Taste: really light, refreshing, the tannins are barely there. Low alcohol (10.5%). Perfect for a weeknight. Not a wine you contemplate — it's just good to drink.

**2020 COS "Pithos Rosso" — Sicily, Nero d'Avola**

Fermented in terracotta amphorae, which gives it a textural quality that's hard to describe — slightly porous, almost oxidative but clean. Dense red fruit, dried herbs, a long finish. This needed 20 minutes of air and a good pairing; I had it with grilled eggplant and it made sense.

**2022 Gut Oggau "Winifred" — Austria, Welschriesling/Grüner Veltliner blend**

The Gut Oggau labels are famous for their illustrated characters. The wine lives up to the branding — light and floral, very food-friendly. Had it cold on a warm evening and it was exactly right.

Natural wine resource I've been using: https://www.rawwine.com/

The "natural wine is flawed wine" critique is sometimes true and sometimes a category error. These three were excellent by any standard.
