# Coffee tasting log — August session

Ran a quick cupping session this morning with three bags I've been meaning to compare: a Yirgacheffe natural from Onyx, a Colombian washed from Intelligentsia, and a Sumatra wet-hulled from a local roaster called Bottomless Cup.

**Yirgacheffe natural (Onyx):** Overwhelming blueberry on the nose before the crust broke. Once stirred, chocolate and dried cherry came forward. Very sweet finish, almost dessert-like. Not great for milk drinks — it just disappears.

**Colombian washed (Intelligentsia):** Clean, bright, slightly savory. Tasted like red apple and brown sugar. The kind of coffee that makes you want a second cup not because it's exciting but because it's just right.

**Sumatra wet-hulled (Bottomless Cup):** Earthy and full-bodied as expected. Notes of cedar and dark cocoa. Some people find this style funky, but there's something comforting about it on a humid morning.

I've been using the SCA cupping protocol loosely: https://sca.coffee/research/protocols-best-practices

Grind was 400 microns on the EK43, water at 93°C, 8.5g per 150ml. The Colombian scored highest by my informal rubric but the Yirgacheffe was the most memorable.

Next session I want to try side-by-side processing comparisons from the same farm. Counter Culture's La Palma y El Tucán does exactly that and I've been eyeing their releases.
