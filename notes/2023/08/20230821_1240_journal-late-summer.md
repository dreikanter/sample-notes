# Journal — late August slowdown

There's a particular quality to late August that I find myself looking forward to each year: the light is changing, still warm but lower in the sky, and it hits surfaces at angles that make ordinary things look significant. The apartment in the early morning. The garden mid-afternoon.

Work has been slower this week, which I'm trying to accept rather than fill. The backlog will refill itself. Some years I've treated any lull as an invitation to plan the next thing, and the planning becomes its own kind of busyness that prevents any actual rest.

Read a piece by Pico Iyer on the value of stillness that's been sitting with me: https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2012/jan/01/pico-iyer-value-of-sitting-still — he talks about how the people who seem to get most out of speed are those who practice stillness. I'm not naturally still. I'm aware of this.

The tomato season is winding down. I'll probably pull the San Marzano plants this week and let the bed rest before fall garlic goes in. The Cherokee Purples are still producing and I'm picking every day. Made a slow-roasted tomato sauce yesterday — four hours at 150°C with olive oil, garlic, and fresh thyme — and froze most of it in 200ml portions.

Had dinner with V. on Saturday. It's been a good friendship to have, the kind where you can go several weeks without contact and then talk for three hours without it feeling strange. We went to that Thai place on Morrison Street that I've been meaning to try for months.

A good week, overall. Not in spite of the slowness.
