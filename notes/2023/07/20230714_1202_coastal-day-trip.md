# Coastal day trip — Dungeness

Finally made the trip to Dungeness that's been on the list for two years.

Dungeness is a shingle headland in Kent — technically the largest cuspate foreland in Europe, though you would not know this standing on it. What you notice is the flatness, the enormous sky, and the buildings scattered across the shingle with no apparent plan. The nuclear power station at the far end completes the dissonance.

Derek Jarman's garden is still maintained and worth visiting. He planted drought-resistant plants directly into the shingle in the years before his death — sea kale, valerian, gorse, curly kale — and put found objects among them: driftwood, chain, old tools, flint. The aesthetic is more considered than it looks on first approach. The scale is cottage-garden small against the vast grey backdrop.

The RSPB reserve on the point is one of the best in the south-east for passage migrants. We had curlew on the beach, dunlin, a single grey plover that stayed long enough to properly identify. The shingle habitat itself is rare — only a few places in Europe have it at this scale.

Lunch at The Pilot Inn — good chips, unremarkable fish, excellent view of the power station. The beer garden is on gravel and faces the sea directly.

Train from London Bridge to Appledore, then bus (unreliable) or taxi. The whole trip takes a day but a short one.
