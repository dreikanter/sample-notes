# First tomato — Gardener's Delight

Picked the first three cherry tomatoes today. Gardener's Delight, from the plant I potted into the old garlic container ([[20230610_1181]]).

Small clusters of three — deep red, slightly blushed orange where they were more shaded. The skin blistered at the top in one case, which is usually a sign of inconsistent watering. Otherwise perfect.

Ate them still warm from the vine. The flavour was sweet and sharp simultaneously — more flavour than any supermarket cherry tomato, partly because they were eaten immediately, partly because they're allowed to ripen fully on the plant rather than being picked green.

This is the reason people grow tomatoes. The fruit you get after 90 days of feeding, watering, staking, pinching, and vigilance for blight is substantially better than anything you can buy at a normal price point.

**State of the three plants**

- **Plant 1 (east container, original 30 cm pot):** Three ripe clusters, six more still green. Most productive so far.
- **Plant 2 (east container, shared):** Two ripe clusters. Good plant, slightly smaller than plant 1.
- **Plant 3 (old garlic container, repotted):** The one producing today. One more truss visible and developing.

**Blight watch**

No signs. The dry, warm summer has helped — blight likes humidity. But August is when it usually arrives.

**Succession**

Going to let the plants run until first frost. Have not started new seedlings — no space.
