# Canal walk observation — July 22

Walked the towpath from the lock at the northern end down to where the canal widens into the basin, about 4 km total. Started at 7:15 before the temperature climbed.

The water level is lower than I've seen it this summer — you can see the silt ledge that's normally submerged. Two narrowboats were moored together near the third bridge; one had a small wood-burning stove going despite the heat, the chimney pipe painted in the same red as the roof panels.

A pair of grey herons on the east bank, one of them standing completely still for the entire stretch I could see it. Herons are ambush hunters — that stillness is functional, not decorative. They'll wait up to four hours in the same spot. I looked it up: the [grey heron on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_heron) notes they take prey up to the size of a small rabbit. This seems impossible given the beak geometry until you watch one swallow a large eel.

The vegetation on the towpath verge is largely unmanaged. Willowherb, nettles, some hogweed further along — the usual suspects. There's one stretch where the bank is reinforced with gabion baskets and the plant community is different, more ruderal, probably because the drainage is better there.

Noticed a stretch of the towpath where the brick coping is missing for about 8 metres, replaced with a rough concrete pour that's cracking. Reported to the canal trust website, though I've done this before with no visible result.

I've walked this route enough times that I take it for granted. Today I tried counting locks — there are 7 in the 4 km section, which means the total elevation change is significant even though the path feels flat. Each lock chamber drops roughly 1.2 to 1.8 metres.
