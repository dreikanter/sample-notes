# Observation — Saturday in the park

High summer. Everyone in the park is horizontal.

The park is a large one, common type — Victorian, former private estate, opened to the public in the 1880s. The paths describe an efficient circuit that allows you to see most of it without backtracking. On a day like today, Saturday, 28 °C, the paths are beside the point. People are on the grass.

Groups have claimed territory with remarkable efficiency and almost no visible negotiation. Each group maintains a comfortable distance from the next. The invisible radius of each group's space is honoured by arrivals looking for a spot; they walk until they're past the edge of someone's implied area and then they stop.

Observed for about ninety minutes from a bench near the rose garden. The rose garden itself was nearly empty — too formal, too exposed, too much work to maintain the fiction of appreciating it in that heat. The shade of the large plane trees on the north side drew the largest concentrations of people.

A couple had set up a badminton net, poorly. It sagged in the middle. They played for about fifteen minutes with increasing frustration, then abandoned it and lay down next to it like a small defeat.

The dog-walkers move through the park in a different relationship to it than everyone else: purposeful, upright, on a schedule. Their dogs are the only things in the park that are clearly working.
