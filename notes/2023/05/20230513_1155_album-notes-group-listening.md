# Album notes — group listening session

Three of us, deliberately no talking during the albums. Tomás's flat. Listened to two full records.

**Talk Talk — Spirit of Eden (1988)**

Second time I've heard this properly. The studio process was apparently extreme — three years, ambient environment, musicians often improvising without knowing what the final album would be. The result sounds like a record made by a group listening to each other very carefully in a large dark room.

What I notice this time: the use of silence. The album has more silence than most ambient records. Individual instruments emerge, interact briefly, disappear. Nothing sustains too long. It creates a patience in the listener — you start attending to tiny sounds because that's where the movement is.

Side two opens into something stranger than side one. The organ-and-brass ensemble that appears in "Inheritance" is the most unexpected texture on the record. Very beautiful.

**Grouper — Dragging a Dead Deer Up a Hill (2008)**

This follows Spirit of Eden well. Both records use lo-fi recording as a compositional choice rather than a limitation. Grouper's guitar and voice are so processed with reverb they become ambient objects. Individual songs are almost beside the point — it's about sustained immersion.

Less surprised by this one than by Talk Talk — Grouper I've heard more. But hearing it in a room with other people changed it slightly. We were all quiet at the end.
