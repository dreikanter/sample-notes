# Journal — slow Thursday

No meetings until noon. Used the morning to do actual work, which is rarer than it should be.

The thing I keep noticing: I do my best thinking between about 8:30 and 11:30, before the social/reactive part of the brain gets switched on by email and conversations. This isn't a novel observation but it keeps being confirmed. The code I write in the morning is cleaner, the writing is more direct, the decisions are less second-guessed.

Went for a longer walk at lunchtime because the weather was genuinely good for the first time in weeks. Took the longer route through the park rather than the canal path. The chestnuts are in full bloom now — the candles (is that what you call them? the upright flower clusters) are at peak. There's something gaudy and excessive about a horse chestnut in full bloom that I find entirely likeable.

Thought about the systems thinking reading while walking. Specifically about delays: how many of the things I'm anxious about are lagging indicators of situations that have already resolved? The anxiety arrives on a delay after the stressor, persists after the stressor has gone, and is treated as information about the present when it's actually information about the past.

Not a novel observation either. But useful to be reminded of it while standing under a very full chestnut tree in the middle of the day on a Thursday in May.
