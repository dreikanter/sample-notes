# Reading notes — Debt (Graeber)

David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011). Reading slowly, about 50 pages a week.

Through Part III. The central argument is that the myth of barter — the idea that money evolved from barter economies — has no anthropological evidence. No society has ever been found that used barter between strangers as its primary mode of exchange before money. Instead, complex credit systems appear before coinage, often operating through social obligations that look nothing like market transactions.

**The section on primordial debt**

Graeber's treatment of the "primordial debt" theorists (you are born into debt to society, which you must repay) is interesting. He reads this as a theological structure that precedes economic formalism — the idea that life itself is a debt owed to the cosmos or to one's ancestors. Different civilisations have different answers to this, all of them generating different kinds of economics.

**On violence**

The book keeps returning to the connection between debt and violence. The threat underlying a debt relationship is always ultimately physical — you can be imprisoned, enslaved, or harmed for non-payment. Graeber argues that modern finance has never entirely shed this structure; it's just more distant.

**Reservations**

The breadth is part of the argument but also a vulnerability. It's hard to evaluate the anthropological claims without specialist knowledge. I'm reading this alongside the [LRB review by Keith Hart](https://www.lrb.co.uk) which is a useful corrective.
