# Reading notes — Der alte König in seinem Exil

Arno Geiger, *Der alte König in seinem Exil* (2011). Found in Berlin ([[20230425_1135]]). Reading in German, which is slow but worth it — the prose loses something in translation.

The book is a memoir about Geiger's father August, who has Alzheimer's. It's not what I expected: not primarily about loss or grief, though those are present, but about the logic of a consciousness reorganising itself around what it can still hold.

The title comes from something the father says during one of his clearer intervals. He describes himself as a king living in exile in his own country — present but displaced, recognisably himself but no longer at home in his own mind.

Geiger's approach is to take his father's statements seriously rather than correcting them. When the father talks about the farm of his childhood as if he is still there, Geiger follows the conversation rather than orienting him back to the present. This reads as kindness but the book implies something more — that the father's inner coherence, even at a remove from shared reality, is worth respecting.

There's a passage where Geiger reflects that we've been thinking about dementia wrong: not as the erosion of a person but as the emergence of a different, more provisional kind of self. Whether or not this is medically defensible, it's ethically generative.
