# Systems thinking — introductory notes

Working through Donella Meadows's *Thinking in Systems* alongside the Meadows Institute's free online materials. Notes on the core concepts.

**Stock and flow**

A stock is anything that can be measured at a moment in time: water in a tank, money in an account, knowledge in a population. A flow changes stocks over time: water in, water out. Understanding a system means understanding what changes stocks and at what rate.

**Feedback loops**

Reinforcing loops amplify change (compound interest, population growth). Balancing loops seek a goal state (thermostat, predator-prey dynamics). Real systems have both interacting, which produces the counterintuitive behaviour that makes systems hard to manage.

**Delays**

The time lag between cause and effect. This is where most systems management goes wrong — people respond to what they see now, not to what is happening in the underlying stocks. Classic example: shower temperature adjustment. You turn up the hot water and wait, don't feel the change yet, turn it up more, then get scalded.

**Policy resistance**

When you push on a system, it often pushes back. Subsidising fuel lowers prices → more consumption → prices rise. Expanding road capacity → more driving → more congestion. This is not a failure of policy but a feature of complex systems.

**Most useful insight so far**

Systems often produce the outcomes they were designed to produce — just not the outcomes their designers intended. Behaviour emerges from structure.
