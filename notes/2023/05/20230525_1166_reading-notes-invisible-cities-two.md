# Reading notes — Invisible Cities, finished

Finished *Invisible Cities* — third reading complete. See [[20230416_1119]] for notes on the first half.

The second half shifts the balance between the city-descriptions and the Khan/Polo dialogue. The dialogue becomes more philosophical, almost more Platonic in structure — Polo and the Khan are working out together what the cities are *for*, what the exercise of imagining them is for.

The final city description — Berenice, the city that contains its own opposite — is the most structurally interesting. Berenice the unjust contains within it the seeds of Berenice the just, which contains the seeds of Berenice the unjust. It refuses the idea that imagining a good city gets you closer to a good city. What it gives you instead is a better map of what you're dealing with.

**What I took from this reading**

The book works better as a philosophical text than as a work of narrative fiction — though it's dressed as the latter. The frame narrative (Marco and Kublai) is the least interesting part, but without it the city-descriptions would feel like prose poems with no stakes.

I'm thinking about this alongside the Graeber reading ([[20230518_1160]]): both are books about the invisible structures underneath visible things. Cities are built on memory and desire and sign systems, just as economies are built on violence and obligation and myth.
