# Reading — "The Age of Surveillance Capitalism" by Shoshana Zuboff

About 150 pages into this. It's a long book (600+ pages) and I'm reading it in sections rather than straight through.

Zuboff's central claim: that the dominant firms of the digital economy have developed a new economic logic she calls "surveillance capitalism." The logic is: behavior is extracted as raw material, refined into prediction products (what you will do next), and sold to advertisers who want to influence that behavior.

The important shift from earlier capitalism: the product isn't produced from land, labor, or capital in the traditional sense. The raw material is behavioral surplus — the data you produce as a byproduct of using services, which is more than the company needs to improve those services, and which is therefore available to monetize.

**The Google case:**

The specific origin of this logic, Zuboff argues, is the development of behavioral targeting at Google after 2001. The engineers discovered that the data people generated while searching (not just the queries, but timing, dwell time, rephrasings) predicted the effectiveness of ads. This realization — that behavioral data could be converted into financial value — became the template.

**Where I'm skeptical:**

The framework is coherent and the history is well-researched. My hesitation is about agency. Zuboff treats users as purely passive subjects of this system, and I think the picture is more complicated. People make choices — imperfect, often poorly informed, sometimes coerced — but choices.
