# Bread — whole grain experiment

Tried substituting 30% of the white flour in my usual sourdough formula [[20230825_1244]] with freshly milled whole wheat (Emmer, from a local mill). Notes.

**Formula (750g total flour):**
- 525g high-extraction white flour (70%)
- 225g whole Emmer wheat flour (30%)
- 150g mature starter (20% inoculation based on flour weight)
- 615g water (76% hydration — dropped from 75% as the whole grain absorbs more)
- 17g salt

**Process changes:**
- Extended autolyse to 60 minutes — whole grain needs more time to hydrate fully
- Bulk fermentation was faster (about 45 minutes shorter than my usual 4.5 hours) — whole grain accelerates fermentation
- Final dough temperature ran 1.5°C warmer than usual

**Observations:**

The shaping felt different — the dough was slightly less extensible, more resistant to stretching without tearing. This is the bran cutting the gluten strands. I was gentler than usual.

**Result:**

The crumb is tighter than my standard white sourdough — fewer large holes, more even distribution. This is expected and not a failure. The flavor is noticeably more complex: nutty, slightly earthy, with the characteristic tang amplified. The crust is a deeper brown.

This is a better everyday loaf than my white bread. More interesting to eat. Will experiment with 40% whole grain next.

Emmer wheat resource: https://www.ancientgrainwheat.com/emmer-wheat

The mill is about 25 minutes away and does Saturday pickups. Worth building into the Saturday routine.
