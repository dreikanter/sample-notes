# Visiting the art museum — October afternoon

The city art museum is free on Thursday afternoons, so I've started making it a monthly habit. This month I spent most of the time in the 17th-century Dutch and Flemish painting galleries, which I've walked through before but never spent real time with.

What I didn't expect: how funny some of the genre paintings are. There's a painting of a woman laughing at a man who's clearly drunk, her expression caught between contempt and enjoyment, and it's just — very human, very recognizable, across 380 years.

The still life paintings are extraordinary when you get close. I spent a long time in front of a Pieter Claesz — a breakfast table with a peeled lemon, a glass of wine, an oyster, a loaf of bread, a knife with a bone handle. The lemon peel, spiraling off the edge of the table, is painted with a specificity and care that makes the lemon feel more real than anything I'd seen that day outside the museum.

The Vermeer (one, borrowed for the exhibition) had its own crowd. People waiting their turn to stand in front of it in a way that doesn't happen with anything else in the museum. I waited. The painting — a woman reading a letter by a window — delivers something in person that reproductions don't. The quality of the light, the feeling of the room.

Resource on Dutch Golden Age painting: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/rijksstudio

I've been thinking about starting to draw again. Being around paintings this carefully made is the right provocation.
