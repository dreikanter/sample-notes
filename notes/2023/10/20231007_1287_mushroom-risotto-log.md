# Mushroom risotto — cooking log

Used the remainder of the maitake from the market [[20230916_1266]] plus a handful of dried porcini I'd been saving. The combination — fresh maitake for texture, dried porcini for depth — is more interesting than either alone.

## Setup

**Mushroom stock (made the day before):**
- Soaked 20g dried porcini in 500ml boiling water 30 minutes
- Reserved the soaking liquid (strained through cloth — very fine grit in porcini liquid)
- Chopped rehydrated porcini and reserved
- Combined porcini liquid with 500ml chicken stock — this is the risotto stock

**Day-of mise en place:**
- 200g maitake, torn into rough pieces
- 1 shallot, minced fine
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 150g Carnaroli rice (I prefer Carnaroli over Arborio — slightly firmer finish)
- 100ml dry white wine (Vermentino)
- 40g cold butter, cubed
- 40g Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated fine
- Good olive oil

## Method

1. Sauté maitake in a wide pan over high heat with olive oil until golden and beginning to crisp at the edges. Season with salt. Set aside.

2. In a heavy-bottomed pot, soften shallot in butter + oil. Add rice, toast 2 minutes until translucent at edges. Deglaze with wine.

3. Add warm stock ladleful by ladleful, stirring constantly, waiting for each addition to absorb. 18–20 minutes.

4. Off heat: add cold butter and Parmesan. Stir vigorously to emulsify (the "mantecatura"). Rest 2 minutes.

5. Top with sautéed maitake and chopped porcini. Finish with a few drops of good olive oil.

Result: very good. The porcini liquid gave depth without any off-flavors.

Reference on mantecatura: https://www.greatitalianchefs.com/features/how-to-make-perfect-risotto
