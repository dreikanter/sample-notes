# Autumn leaves — observation log

The maples on my street have gone first, as they always do. The sequence in my neighborhood:

1. **Norway maples** (the invasive street trees) — yellowing starting in mid-September, peak color by the first week of October. A uniform yellow with some orange, no red.

2. **Red maples** (a few in the park) — starting now, mid-October. Living up to their name. The red is intense, especially backlit in afternoon sun.

3. **Oaks** — barely started. They'll hold their leaves the longest, often into November, turning a dry brown that's less spectacular but has its own character.

4. **Ginkgos** (there are two on Morrison Street) — these turn last and drop all at once. One week the tree is full, the next the sidewalk is covered in golden fans. The tree itself is bare in a day. I've watched this happen twice now and it still seems like a magic trick.

5. **Ash trees** — if there are any left after the emerald ash borer, they turn a subtle purple-yellow. I only know one on my block. It looks like the borer got it last year; half the crown is dead.

The color is primarily carotenoids (yellows and oranges, always present but masked by chlorophyll) and anthocyanins (reds, produced in autumn specifically). The bright reds mean the tree is actively producing new pigment — not just revealing what was hidden.

Useful explainer on leaf color chemistry: https://www.nature.com/scitable/knowledge/library/why-do-leaves-change-color-in-fall-13509869/

I've been photographing the same three trees every week. The progression is noticeable day to day if you pay attention.
