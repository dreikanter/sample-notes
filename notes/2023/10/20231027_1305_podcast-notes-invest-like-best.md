# Podcast notes — "Invest Like the Best" episode on capital allocation

Listened to an episode of Patrick O'Shaughnessy's podcast featuring a conversation about capital allocation decisions in private companies. The episode was a few months old but worth catching up on.

Podcast: https://www.joincolossus.com/episodes

## Key points I noted

**The five choices of capital allocation:**
1. Reinvest in the business (organic growth)
2. Acquire other businesses (inorganic growth)
3. Pay down debt
4. Return capital via dividends
5. Buy back shares

The guest argued that most company founders default to reinvestment (because it's what got them to their current size) and have under-developed intuitions about the other four. This leads to capital being reinvested at declining marginal returns long past the point when alternatives would have been superior.

**The "maintenance capex vs. growth capex" distinction:**

Maintenance capex keeps the business running. Growth capex expands it. Conflating them in financial statements obscures real earning power. A business that is "profitable" but requires constant reinvestment to maintain that profitability is fundamentally different from one that generates cash it doesn't need to spend.

**On acquisitions:**

The episode was skeptical of acquisitions as a growth strategy, citing research that most acquisitions destroy value for the acquirer's shareholders. The exceptions tend to be small "acqui-hire" style acquisitions of talent and technology rather than full business integrations.

**What I took from this:**

Not investing advice, but the mental models translate. The maintenance vs. growth spending distinction maps directly to technical debt vs. new feature investment in an engineering context — a useful frame.
