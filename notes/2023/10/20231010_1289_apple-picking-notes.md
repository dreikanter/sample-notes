# Apple picking and variety notes

Went to an orchard about 40 minutes outside the city. It's a small operation — maybe three acres of mixed varieties, some heirloom — run by a couple who moved out of the city eight years ago. They do a U-pick from August to November and have a small shop selling cider and jam.

**Varieties available:**

*Honeycrisp* — ubiquitous, popular for a reason. Good balance of sweet and tart, exceptional crispness. I find it a bit one-dimensional for anything other than eating fresh.

*Esopus Spitzenburg* — Thomas Jefferson's favorite apple, apparently. Excellent: complex, tart, dense, good for both eating and baking. The skin is tougher than Honeycrisp; worth knowing before serving to children. I bought a large bag.

*Calville Blanc d'Hiver* — a French heirloom variety, pale and a bit lumpy-looking. The flavor is extraordinary — deeply aromatic, a bit like floral anise mixed with apple. Not a keeper variety; needs using within 2 weeks. Best eaten out of hand.

*Roxbury Russet* — classic russet type, rough brownish-green skin. The flesh is dense and dry. Good for cider blending or baked applications. Not appealing raw.

**Cider:** They press single-variety ciders in small batches. Bought a gallon of the Spitzenburg hard cider, which was slightly fizzy, dry, and smelled genuinely of apples rather than the generic cider sweetness you get from commercial products.

Orchard finder resource: https://www.pickyourown.org/

I need to plant an apple tree. The orchard owner said Spitzenburg does well in our climate zone.
