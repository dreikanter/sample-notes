# Music — building a record collection in a streaming age

I started buying records again about a year ago, tentatively, after a decade of only using streaming services. I now have about 60 LPs. Some reflections on what this has changed.

**The listening is different.** With a record, you commit to a side. The act of putting a record on has a physical ritual to it — choosing, cleaning, lowering the needle — that primes attention in a way queuing a playlist doesn't. I listen more actively.

**The collection has an editorial quality.** Every record I own I chose to buy. My streaming library is accumulated more casually — adds and saves that don't all represent real enthusiasm. The physical collection is more curated by necessity (space, money). I know what's in it.

**Discovery works differently.** With records, you find things slowly: used record shops, recommendations that come with physical objects, following one artist to another through liner notes. It's less efficient and more interesting.

**What I've bought recently:**

- John Coltrane, "A Love Supreme" (reissue, original release 1965) — the album that made me want records again after I played it off a turntable at a friend's house
- Loraine James, "Reflection" (2021) — found in the electronic section at a shop in Portland, hadn't heard of her, bought on instinct. Excellent.
- Prefab Sprout, "Steve McQueen" (1985) — a reissue of a record I'd known from streaming; the vinyl version sounds notably different in the high end

**The turntable:** Pro-Ject Debut Carbon EVO with an Ortofon 2M Red cartridge. Not audiophile-grade but significantly better than what records deserve to be played on.
