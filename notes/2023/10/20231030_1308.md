# Interview process retrospective — October hiring

Wrapped up three interviews this month for a senior backend engineer role. Reflecting on the process before we make the decision.

## The candidates

We saw eight resumes, screened four by phone, brought three to the full interview loop (technical screen + system design + behavioral + culture fit).

**Candidate A:** Strong on system design — the distributed caching discussion was impressive and he caught a subtle consistency issue in my proposed architecture before I'd flagged it. The behavioral portion showed some concerning patterns around disagreement with managers — he described two situations where he felt he was right and his manager wrong, and in both cases he went around the manager. The instinct may have been correct, but the approach is a red flag at senior level.

**Candidate B:** Best communicator of the three. Excellent at articulating tradeoffs and asking clarifying questions. The coding screen showed some hesitation — she mentioned she doesn't do LeetCode-style practice and it showed, but the actual logic was sound once she got past the initial orientation. This is the candidate I'm most interested in.

**Candidate C:** Strong technical background in a somewhat different stack (more Go than Node). The willingness to learn was evident, and the questions he asked about our architecture suggested he'd actually thought about what challenges he'd encounter. A longer ramp-up time than the role ideally needs, but high ceiling.

## Process observations

The technical screen caught actual depth differences. The system design round was more revealing than I expected — it's much harder to fake distributed systems intuition than coding problem fluency.

What I want to discuss in the debrief: do we optimize for the candidate who can contribute fastest (A or B) or the candidate with the highest long-term potential (B or C)?
