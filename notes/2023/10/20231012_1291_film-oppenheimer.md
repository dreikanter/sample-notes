# Film notes — "Oppenheimer" (2023)

Second viewing, this time in IMAX as intended. Some thoughts updated from first viewing.

**What improves on second watch:**

The non-linear structure is easier to navigate when you're not tracking plot. You can attend to performance and composition instead. Cillian Murphy's eyes — the film is built around them, and on second watch you notice how much Nolan holds on them while Oppenheimer is listening rather than speaking.

The Strauss sections (in black and white, in the Senate hearing) snap into place once you know what they're building to. The deliberate misdirection of where the film's emotional climax actually falls becomes clear in retrospect.

**What I'm still uncertain about:**

The Trinity test sequence. It's one of the most technically accomplished sequences in recent blockbuster filmmaking — shot without CGI on practical effects. And it's terrifying in the right way. But I'm not sure the film has the moral seriousness the sequence asks for. The horror passes and the film moves on into political procedural fairly quickly.

**Murphy's performance:**

Exceptional. He makes Oppenheimer's arrogance and ambivalence coexist without resolving either into the other. The scene in the office after Hiroshima — the auditory distortion, the fragmented crowd, his face — is what the whole film is in miniature.
