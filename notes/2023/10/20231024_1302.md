# Journal — late October, first frost

First frost last night — the forecast called for 1°C but it may have gone a little below. The arugula in the garden bed is fine; arugula is frost-tolerant and the cold actually improves the flavor somewhat. The spinach has a few damaged outer leaves but the crowns are intact.

The garlic bed [[20231014_1293]] is mulched under 3 inches of straw and should be entirely protected. Won't know for certain until spring, but I feel good about it.

Spent the morning doing what I always do in late October: moving tender plants inside (the rosemary in the terra cotta pot, a small bay tree I've been nursing for two years), getting the garden hose drained and stored, stacking the tomato cages in the shed.

The light was extraordinary this morning — very low, very gold, the frost making the grass look like frosted glass. I stood in the garden for a few minutes just looking. This is the kind of thing I try to write down because I know I'll forget the specific quality of it.

Work: the EKS pilot [[20231016_1295]] has been running clean for eight days. Load tests passed yesterday. The call to expand to the analytics service is probably happening next week.

Evening: made a long-cooked beef ragu from scratch. Five hours, low heat, the apartment filling with steam. Served it over tagliatelle with a generous amount of aged Parmigiano. The kind of meal that justifies the whole season.

Reading this week: finished "Gilead" [[20231005_1285]], started "Home" (the companion novel, following Jack Boughton). Robinson's world is absorbing.
