# Concert notes — live performance

Saw William Basinski perform at a small venue downtown last week. About 150 people, seated, in a converted warehouse space. The room was set up with chairs in a loose grid rather than rows — the kind of arrangement that signals this isn't a traditional concert.

Basinski performed what I believe was a section of the "Disintegration Loops" — the hour-long tape loop recordings made as the master tapes deteriorated, particles of oxide falling away with each playback. The ambient music becomes, over the course of each loop, progressively degraded — holes and silences accumulating. The piece Basinski performed for tonight was about 45 minutes.

The experience of listening to this in a room with other people is different from listening alone. There's a collective attention that forms — I could feel it in the audience. People weren't looking at their phones. The room was doing what music rooms used to do before they became venues competing with other stimulation.

About 20 minutes in, the degradation was noticeable and then impossible to ignore. The music was disappearing from the inside.

I kept thinking about the Ishiguro novel I'd just finished [[20230918_1268]] — about collective forgetting, and whether the thing you forget still happened.

No setlist — this isn't that kind of concert. The performance was the piece.

Basinski's site: https://www.mmlxii.com/

One of the more affecting music experiences I've had in years.
