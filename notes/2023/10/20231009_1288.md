# Mental models — a few I return to

Not a comprehensive list. These are the ones I've actually found useful and thought about rather than ones I read in a listicle and forgot.

## Inversion

Instead of asking "how do I achieve X?" ask "what would guarantee X fails?" Then avoid those things. Useful when you're optimizing for the wrong outcome — working backward from failure surfaces constraints you might miss working forward.

Applied this recently in the infrastructure migration discussion [[20230909_1259]]: "What would guarantee this migration fails?" Produced a better list of prerequisites than "what do we need to succeed?"

## Second-order effects

Every decision produces first-order effects (the intended consequences) and second-order effects (the consequences of the consequences). Good decisions often have poor first-order effects but excellent second-order effects, and vice versa.

Example: code reviews slow down individual PRs (first order, negative) but catch bugs before production and transfer knowledge across the team (second order, positive). Removing them would be locally optimal and systemically damaging.

## Chesterton's Fence

Before removing something that seems unnecessary, understand why it was put there. The rule, the convention, the piece of code with no obvious purpose — it may have been placed by someone who solved a problem you've forgotten about.

I've violated this twice in production systems and paid for it both times.

## Goodhart's Law

When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. Optimizing for the metric that represents a thing is different from optimizing for the thing.
