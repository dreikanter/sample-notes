# Blog post published — context switching

Published the context switching post today after two rounds of editing. ([[20230515_1157]] for original draft.)

**What changed in editing**

The original draft had too much preamble. The argument about "transition time" (as opposed to the switch itself) is the thing worth saying — I was burying it in three paragraphs of "here's what everyone else says about this topic." Cut all of that.

The three practical suggestions at the end were too close together — they need white space and a short explanation each. Added a sentence of context to each.

The closing was weak in the draft ("hope this helps"). Rewrote it to be more direct: the post now ends on a claim rather than a valediction.

**Reception so far**

Posted to the usual places. A few replies, mostly on the "written note at end of block" point — apparently this is something people have done independently and found useful. Two people mentioned doing a "shutdown ritual" for a similar purpose, which points to [Paul Graham on the subject](http://paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html) (different but related framing).

One reader said it missed the structural point — that context switching isn't primarily an individual problem, it's an organisational one imposed on people by meeting culture. Fair criticism. Worth a follow-up post.

**Lesson for next time**

Start writing earlier. The draft sat for two weeks before I edited it, which is too long. The ideas went cold. Will try to revise within three or four days of completing a draft.
