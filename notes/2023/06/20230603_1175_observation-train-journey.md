# Observation — train journey north

Two-hour train to visit family. Sat in the quiet carriage, which is aspirationally quiet rather than actually quiet.

The man across the aisle is reading a hardback with the dust jacket removed. This is a specific kind of reader — someone who either doesn't want to be seen reading what they're reading, or has the practised habit of protecting covers on long journeys. He turned pages every six or seven minutes. Literary fiction pace.

The landscape on this route changes in distinct bands. First an hour of suburb giving way to industrial estate giving way to retail park, then a sudden cut to farmland that feels too abrupt, as if the city gave up and left rather than trailing off. Then the farmland itself, which I always underestimate. You can go quite a long time in England thinking it's all city and then realise the cities are actually small.

A child of about four in the seats ahead spent forty minutes in a state of elaborate imaginary play involving a small plastic crocodile and a paperback copy of a Thomas the Tank Engine book (used as a platform, not read). The parent next to her was asleep. The crocodile survived several elaborate disasters.

Outside now: a village built around a church, then fields, then a large warehouse complex that goes on for twenty minutes. The warehouse complex is unbranded from the outside. Thinking about distribution logistics.
