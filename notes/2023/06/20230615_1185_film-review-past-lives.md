# Film review — Past Lives

Celine Song, 2023. Watched at the cinema.

There are films that are technically accomplished and emotionally inert, and there are films that achieve something harder: restraint in service of feeling rather than as a substitute for it. Past Lives is in the second category.

The structure is three acts separated by twelve-year gaps. A childhood friendship in Seoul, a reconnection in New York via video call, a reunion in New York in the present. The film is about what the first relationship becomes over time — not what it was, but what it turns into in memory and imagination.

Song's directorial instinct is to hold the camera still and let the actors fill the frame. There are long takes of faces in which very little happens, and in which the audience is forced to project interior states. This is either a trust exercise between director and viewer or an imposition, depending on your patience. I found it a trust exercise.

Greta Lee's performance is the best thing in the film. She is playing someone who has chosen her life but has not resolved all the alternatives — and she conveys that without ever becoming brittle or sentimental. The final sequence at the taxi is formally brave: the camera stays outside the car, outside the apartment, watching from a distance.

The film is about grief for a life not lived. It doesn't argue for or against the choices made. It just holds them.
