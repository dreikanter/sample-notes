# Reading notes — Debt (Graeber), finished

Finished *Debt: The First 5,000 Years*. First notes were in [[20230518_1160]].

**The argument as a whole**

Graeber's thesis holds together better than I expected given how much territory it covers. The core claim — that credit relations precede coin relations, that the social/moral dimension of debt underlies the economic one — is well-supported by the anthropological examples even if individual cases can be disputed.

The most compelling part of the book is the final section on the current debt crisis (written 2011). He's essentially arguing that the financial system is enforcing payment of debts that were never legitimate — that the "we all must pay our debts" moral claim is applied selectively (banks don't repay debts; they get bailouts) and that this selectivity is itself a political choice with an ideological function.

**What I'll take from this**

The distinction between debt and obligation is useful. Not all obligations are debts. Some things I owe to people cannot be quantified, cannot be discharged, and were never intended to be. The problem is when the logic of quantifiable debt colonises relationships that were supposed to be outside it.

**Critical reservations**

The historical narrative is grand and the evidence base is not uniformly strong. Some of the anthropological reconstructions are speculative. The polemic in the final chapters is productive but not always fair to opponents.

**Overall**

One of the more generative books I've read this year. The ideas keep coming back.
