# Reading notes — Slow Productivity (Newport)

Cal Newport, *Slow Productivity* (advance copy). Newport is reliable: the books are well-researched, the argument is usually more nuanced than the title suggests.

The core claim: modern knowledge work has defaulted to "pseudo-productivity" — visible busyness as a proxy for valuable work. The alternative he proposes is structured around three principles:

1. **Do fewer things.** Reduce simultaneous commitments. The overhead of managing parallel projects is underestimated.
2. **Work at a natural pace.** Seasons, rhythms, varying intensity — these are historically normal. Sustained uniform output is a modern industrial expectation badly fitted to creative and cognitive work.
3. **Obsess over quality.** Take the time to make things genuinely good. Quality compounds in a way that volume doesn't.

**What I found useful**

The historical examples are the strongest part. Newport traces knowledge work through Newton (long periods of nothing, then intense focused production), John McPhee (works very slowly on very few things), and several other cases. The point is that "slow" doesn't mean lazy — it means refusing the metrics of pseudo-productivity.

**What I'm skeptical of**

The book implicitly addresses people with significant autonomy over their work. The advice to "do fewer things" is harder to operationalise in jobs where commitments are imposed rather than chosen.
