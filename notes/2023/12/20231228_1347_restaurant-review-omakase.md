# Restaurant review — omakase dinner

A birthday dinner at a small omakase counter in the neighborhood — twelve seats, a single chef working in front of you, fifteen courses over two hours.

The format is disarming. You give up menu control entirely and the meal becomes collaborative in a one-sided way: you learn what someone else thinks is worth your attention tonight, in this season, with this fish. There's something relieving about it.

**What I'll remember:**

The kinmedai (golden-eye snapper) with yuzu kosho was the simplest preparation on the menu and the one I'm still thinking about. The fish was seared only on the skin; the flesh remained almost raw. The yuzu kosho provided heat and citrus without covering anything. The rice underneath was seasoned correctly, which sounds like a baseline but isn't.

The hokkaido uni was the expected highlight — sweet, oceanic, no bitterness. The wagyu bite was the one I was most skeptical of going in; fatty beef as nigari topping felt gimmicky. It worked. I don't fully understand why.

The meal ended with a house-made tamago that was a deliberate palate reset: sweet, eggy, dense, like the cleanest possible conclusion to a very complex sentence.

**Reservation:** Three months in advance on Resy. Worth planning for. The room seats only twelve, so the price reflects real scarcity, not constructed scarcity.
