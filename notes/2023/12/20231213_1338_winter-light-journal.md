# Winter light journal entry

December light at 47° north is strange and specific. By 4pm it's gone, not fading — gone, as if someone turned a dial past a threshold. The quality in the middle of the day is horizontal in a way that makes everything look longer and more serious than it did in July.

I've been noticing how the light changes what I read. The same paragraph feels different in summer afternoon light versus this thin December slant. There's probably a practical explanation involving pupil dilation and contrast, but the subjective experience is of the words being different words.

The apartment gets about three hours of direct sun in winter, which I've started mapping obsessively. The southeast corner at 10am; the kitchen table between noon and 1pm; that's it. The plants have been reorganized accordingly. The pothos moved to the kitchen window and is doing better. The monstera gets what it can.

I read somewhere that Scandinavian design historically developed partly in response to the need to make the most of limited winter light — pale walls, reflective surfaces, minimal obstruction of windows. This matches my experience of feeling better in the apartment after I moved two bookshelves away from the windows.

The short days make time feel contracted in an interesting way. There's something about having darkness arrive at 4pm that makes the evening feel longer rather than shorter — like a second day begins at the lighting of lamps.

More on light and psychology at [the Center for Environmental Therapeutics](https://www.cet.org/), which has good resources on seasonal light exposure.
