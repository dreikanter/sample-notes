# Wine notes — Burgundy introduction

Attended an informal tasting hosted by a friend who works in wine retail. Six bottles, all from different parts of the Côte d'Or, all between ten and fifteen years old. I knew almost nothing going in.

## What I understood afterward

Burgundy's complexity comes entirely from geography. The same grape (Pinot Noir for red, Chardonnay for white) grown a hundred meters apart produces detectably different wine because the soil composition shifts. This is what the French mean by *terroir* — not mysticism, but a real set of chemical and hydrological differences that the vine registers.

The hierarchy (Village → Premier Cru → Grand Cru) maps to specific plots, not producers. A Grand Cru designation means the land, not the winemaker. This is backwards from how I thought about wine, where producer reputation dominated.

## Specific bottles

The Gevrey-Chambertin villages wine from Rossignol-Trapet was the most accessible — red fruit, some earthiness, nothing intimidating. The Chambolle-Musigny premier cru (Mugnier) was where things got interesting: more floral, lighter body, mineral finish that lingered.

The Vosne-Romanée was the one that made me understand why people spend absurd money on Burgundy. There was something happening in the glass that I couldn't reduce to a description.
