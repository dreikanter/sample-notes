# Solstice observation 2023

Sunrise today at 7:54am, sunset at 4:16pm. Eight hours and twenty-two minutes of daylight. The shortest day, though the earliest sunset was a couple of weeks ago — an asymmetry caused by the equation of time that I have to re-explain to myself every December.

I've been marking solstices and equinoxes for a few years now, not ceremonially but as a practice of noticing. The year gains a skeleton. Without these four points, time collapses into an undifferentiated forward movement. With them, you're always somewhere in a cycle — past the halfway point, approaching the turn, descending again.

The ancient significance isn't mysterious. If you depend on agriculture and don't have accurate calendars, knowing when the light turns back matters enormously. We've inherited the calendar without the urgency, but the body still registers something at these pivot points. Seasonal affective disorder is real; so is the particular quality of relief that comes when the days noticeably lengthen again, usually around late January at this latitude.

Marked it this year by a walk at 4pm — just before dark — through the park near the apartment. The light was doing that horizontal December thing again, turning everything amber-bronze. Two crows in a bare tree. A dog running in circles for reasons of its own. The specific, unrepeatable texture of this particular solstice.
