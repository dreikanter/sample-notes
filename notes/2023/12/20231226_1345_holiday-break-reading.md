# Holiday break reading log

Five days off and I read more than I have since August. Notes on what I got through.

## The Dawn of Everything — Graeber & Wengrow

Sixty pages further. The core argument (that human prehistory was far more politically varied and experimental than the standard Rousseau/Hobbes narrative allows) is compelling and the archaeological evidence they cite is often striking. The writing is occasionally self-congratulatory in its debunking — you can feel the authors enjoying certain takedowns more than serves the argument.

The chapter on Indigenous critiques of European society as the actual origin of Enlightenment political thought was the most mind-bending section. The "native informants" weren't passive subjects of European curiosity; they were active interlocutors whose arguments about freedom and equality shaped European intellectual history.
