# Year-end reflection 2023

Not a structured review with categories, just things I want to name before the year closes.

**What changed:** Moved from an individual contributor role to something adjacent to technical lead. The role isn't formal — no title change — but the expectation shifted around April. I'm less the person who fixes the hardest technical problem and more the person who decides which problems are worth fixing. Different skill set. I'm slower at it than I'd like.

**What worked:** The sourdough practice. Learning to bake consistently over eighteen months produced results that didn't exist before, and the practice itself is grounding in a way that I didn't anticipate when I started. There's something about a craft that respects fermentation timelines — you cannot rush it, and that makes you feel the hours differently.

**What didn't:** I read fewer books than any year I can remember. The reading had to compete with longer-form projects and, if I'm honest, with shorter-form internet reading that felt like information but wasn't. Changing this in 2024 is a genuine priority, not a polite aspiration.

**The work question:** The system we've built over the past eighteen months is actually pretty good. Better than I expected it to be at this stage. This has created a new uncertainty: what's the interesting next problem? I've been sitting with that question for the last month and don't have an answer.

**Things I'd keep:** The monthly cooking experiments. The weekly review practice. The solstice and equinox walks. The habit of writing notes like this.

[Austin Kleon's notes on year-end reflection](https://austinkleon.com/) is where I found this format, loosely.
