# Gift cooking notes — holiday batches

Made three types of edible gifts this year. Notes on what I'll repeat and what I won't.

## Shortbread (made twice, gave to six people)

Classic recipe: 250g butter, 120g caster sugar, 350g plain flour, pinch of salt. Beat butter and sugar until pale, add flour, mix until it just comes together. Press into a pan, mark into fingers, bake 150°C for 40 minutes. Let cool completely before cutting.

Result: universally liked, keeps for two weeks in a tin, scales easily. The only gift I'll definitely make every year.

One variation that worked: added two tablespoons of finely chopped fresh rosemary to one batch. The herbal bitterness against the sweet butter was genuinely interesting. Polarizing but the people who liked it really liked it.

## Flavored salt (gave to four people)

Mixed sea salt with lemon zest and thyme. Dried overnight, jarred, labeled.

Result: cheap, easy, looks good, slightly underwhelming as an experience. The recipient doesn't quite know what to do with it. Better as an addition to a gift than a standalone.

## Preserved lemons (gave to two people who cook)

Takes a month of lead time but requires five minutes of work. Quarter lemons, pack in salt in a jar, cover with lemon juice. Leave on the counter for four weeks. Done.

Result: excellent if the recipient uses them. Not everyone does. Only give to people you know cook Moroccan food or are actively curious cooks.

Reference: [David Leibowitz on edible gifts](https://www.davidlebovitz.com/category/sweets-desserts/candy-and-confections/) has reliable, tested ideas.
