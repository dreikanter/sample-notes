# December garden notes

The raised beds are done for the season. Pulled the last kale plants this weekend — they'd held on through two light frosts but the heavy one on Wednesday finished them. Good kale, better than last year's: I think the switch to transplants from seed worked, and the spacing was more generous.

The garlic went in two weeks ago (November 20), six inches apart in rows twelve inches apart. Planted Music and German Red from the seed stock I bought at the local feed store. Mulched with about three inches of straw. The ground is still unfrozen so they should establish some root before hard winter. First harvest expected late June or early July.

**Cover crops:** Broadcast winter rye over the two empty beds. Already germinated and about two inches tall. Will till it in mid-March before direct sowing the cool-season crops.

**Soil notes:** Ordered two yards of compost for spring incorporation. The bed that grew tomatoes all summer is clearly depleted — pale yellow in a soil test where the others showed darker readings.

**What to do differently next year:**
- Succession sow lettuce more aggressively in August/September (always regret not having fall salad)
- Try direct-sowing carrots earlier — the June sow produced well but they could have gone in May
- Consider a cold frame for overwinter spinach

Reference: [Four-Season Harvest by Eliot Coleman](https://www.chelseagreen.com/product/four-season-harvest/) is the best thing I've read on extending the season in colder climates.
