# Sourdough — 20% whole wheat experiment

Following up on [[20231121_1317]]. Tried the higher whole-wheat ratio today and also swapped the cast iron combo cooker for a Dutch oven with a parchment liner.

## Variables

- 80% King Arthur bread flour, 20% Farmer Ground whole wheat
- Hydration: 76% (dropped from 78% because whole wheat absorbs differently)
- Bulk ferment: 5h at 23°C (cooler kitchen this month)
- Same starter, same salt percentage

## Results

The crust was better — lighter color on the bottom, no burning. The parchment made scoring easier because I could pick up the whole thing and move it without disturbing the shape.

The crumb is slightly denser than the white flour version, which I expected. The flavor is noticeably more complex — nuttier, slightly earthier, with a hint of something almost sweet in the crust. I prefer it.

Problem: the dough was noticeably stickier during shaping. Whole wheat absorbs slowly, so the dough felt underdeveloped during lamination but was actually fine by the time it went into the fridge. Hard to trust the feel when you're used to lower whole-wheat ratios.

Next experiment: try 30% whole wheat, and also test cold retard of 16 hours vs 12.

Useful resource for whole wheat percentages and their effect on fermentation: [Full Proof Baking on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@fullproofbaking) has done controlled comparisons that are exactly what I needed.
