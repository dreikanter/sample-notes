# Tonkotsu broth — home attempt notes

Spent a weekend on this. Not exactly a weeknight recipe but the result was worth it. Based loosely on the Serious Eats method with some modifications.

Reference: https://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/the-food-lab-definitive-tonkotsu-ramen.html

## Day 1: bones and blanching

- 1.5kg pork trotters (gelatinous, key to body)
- 500g pork neck bones
- 300g chicken backs

Blanched all bones in boiling water 10 minutes, rinsed, scrubbed. This removes the grey scum that would cloud the broth and add off-flavors. Don't skip this.

## Day 1: long boil

Covered with cold water, brought to a rolling boil. Not a simmer — tonkotsu is intentionally turbulent. The emulsification of fat and collagen into the water creates the milky, opaque broth.

Boiled 12 hours. Strained. Chilled overnight.

## Day 2: seasoning

**Tare (seasoning concentrate):**
- 150ml soy sauce (I used a mix of dark and light)
- 50ml mirin
- 2 tbsp sake
- 2 tbsp sugar
- Kombu soaked in the mix

Simmered 20 minutes to reduce slightly. A small amount of tare (2–3 tbsp) seasons each bowl.

**Broth result:** Deep ivory, viscous, rich, porky but not funky. Better than any store option.

## Assembly

- Broth + tare
- Fresh ramen noodles (bought, not made — next project)
- Chashu pork belly (braised separately)
- Soft-boiled marinated eggs (soy + mirin brine, 7 minutes)
- Menma, nori, sliced scallion
