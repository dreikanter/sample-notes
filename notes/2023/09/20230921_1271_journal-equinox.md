# Journal — autumn equinox

The equinox is one of those occasions where the astronomical fact corresponds with something I actually feel. Today the light and dark are balanced, and from here until the solstice in December the dark takes over. I've never found this depressing in the way some people do — there's something clarifying about the season ahead: longer evenings, indoor activities, the kitchen getting more use.

Pulled the last of the Cherokee Purple tomatoes this morning. They were still mostly good but the plants looked spent — yellowing leaves, the fruit smaller than earlier in the season. Composted the vines and turned the bed with a fork. First time the soil has been fully open since May.

The maitake mushrooms from the market [[20230916_1266]] were roasted last night: high heat (230°C), olive oil, thyme, salt. They came out in dense crispy clusters with a savory depth that was genuinely surprising. Better than most preparations of meat I've eaten recently. Had them over polenta with a soft-poached egg.

Reading in the evenings has been consistent this week — Proust in the evening, a bit of the Pirsig [[20230915_1265]] before bed. Different registers: Proust demands everything, Pirsig asks for a looser engagement. Both productive in their way.

I've been thinking about a longer writing project — something that isn't notes or reviews but an actual piece of sustained prose. Not sure what yet. The thought keeps arriving uninvited, which usually means it's time to start paying attention.

Weather: 14°C, overcast, leaves just beginning to turn on the street maples. Perfect.
