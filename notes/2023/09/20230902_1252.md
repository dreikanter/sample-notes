# Autumn preparation — garden notes

First day of September and the shift is already happening. Morning temperatures are dropping to 14–15°C. The light is noticeably different by 7pm — the garden is in shadow an hour earlier than it was in July.

**Current beds:**

*Tomato bed (main):* Still productive. Cherokee Purples are the last holdouts — picking every two or three days. I'll let them go until the first frost warning. San Marzanos are done; pulled the vines and left the roots in place to decompose. The nitrogen-fixing root nodules are a bonus.

*Herb bed:* Basil is bolting — went to flower over the heat wave. Cut it all back, stripped the leaves, made three batches of pesto and froze them in ice cube trays. The thyme, rosemary, and sage are entering their winter form: woody, concentrated, durable.

*Squash patch:* Two butternut squashes are curing in place — I'll harvest after the skin resists a fingernail. Delicata are done; four squashes in the cellar now.

**Compost:**

The main bin is almost full. Need to start the second bin and let the first continue breaking down. This is a good time to add the carbon material that's been accumulating — cardboard, dry leaves from last autumn that I'd stored, some straw from the tomato mulch.

**Fall planting window:**

Spinach and arugula can still go in before the end of the month. They'll germinate fast in this soil temperature and survive the first frosts.
