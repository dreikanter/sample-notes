# Coffee origins — Ethiopia Yirgacheffe

Picked up a 250g bag of the washed Yirgacheffe from the roaster on Elm Street. They roast on Thursdays and this was bagged the day before, so it'll need another four or five days of degassing before it's at its best.

**Roast profile noted on bag:** light, 200°C peak, 11-minute total roast time.

**Brewing parameters I've been using:**

- Ratio: 1:15 (15g coffee, 225g water)
- Grind: medium-fine on the Comandante (around 22 clicks)
- Water temp: 93°C
- Method: V60, single pour with 45-second bloom

**Tasting notes after day 6:**

Bright and clean in a way that's almost citrus-forward — specifically lemon peel rather than orange. There's a floral note that appears as the cup cools, faintly jasmine. The finish is short and dry, which I prefer. No muddiness or earthiness, which sometimes shows up in washed Ethiopians that are slightly over-extracted.

Day 8 was noticeably better than day 6. The floral note peaked and the lemon softened into something closer to peach. This is consistent with what I've read about high-altitude washed coffees peaking later in their rest window.

Worth comparing against the Kenyan AA I had in January — completely different profile, more blackcurrant and tomato-skin character. The SCA has a useful flavour wheel at [https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-tasters-flavor-wheel](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-tasters-flavor-wheel) for calibrating language around these distinctions.

Will try a French press next week with the same beans, coarser grind, to see what body the method adds.
