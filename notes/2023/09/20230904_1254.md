# Short film thoughts — "An Oversimplification of Her Beauty" (2012)

Came across Terence Nance's debut feature again while reading about experimental cinema. Watched it for the second time, this time paying more attention to the structural choices.

The film begins with Nance waiting for a woman (Namik Minter) who doesn't show up. From there it expands outward through multiple visual registers: animation, documentary footage, spoken narration, stream-of-consciousness text. It's about romantic disappointment but it refuses to be a film about romantic disappointment. It's more interested in documenting the particular texture of the experience — how anticipation feels, what longing does to perception, how narrative shapes itself after the fact.

The animation sequences have a kind of loose energy that matches the emotional register — hand-drawn, imperfect, not cleaned up into something commercial. The style signals that this is someone's actual internal process, not a polished product.

Nance went on to make "Space Is Only Noise" after this and eventually the TV series "Random Acts of Flyness," which is even more formally adventurous.

What strikes me most revisiting it: the film trusts that the specificity of one person's experience will feel universal, without generalizing it. There's no attempt to explain why this matters or to make the emotional situation relatable. It just shows what it is.

For anyone interested in self-referential personal cinema — Agnès Varda's "The Gleaners and I" (2000) covers some of the same territory in documentary form.
