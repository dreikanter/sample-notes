# Reading notes — Proust, volume 2, "In the Shadow of Young Girls in Flower"

Slower going than "Swann's Way" — the first volume was sustained by a single arc (Swann's obsessive love for Odette) that gave momentum to even the most digressive passages. Volume 2 is more diffuse, following Marcel's adolescence and first summer at Balbec.

The prose is denser and more deliberately paced than I expected even from Proust. The Lyon translation (Penguin Modern Classics) handles the sentence length well — it's readable without being simplified.

**What's working:**

The portrait of the grandmother is extraordinary. She appears in volume 1 as a gentle, somewhat abstract figure. Here she becomes fully particular — her character, her relationship with Marcel, her idea of what's good for him versus what pleases him. There's a scene where she walks Marcel in the rain because she thinks sea air and cold are healthy that captures something about loving, opinionated grandmothers exactly.

The passages on memory and habit — Proust's characteristic territory — feel less like digressions in this volume and more like the actual subject. Marcel's difficulty adjusting to the unfamiliar hotel room at Balbec, his slow recognition that habit protects us from feeling and therefore from experience, is a more honest account of travel than most travel writing.

**What's difficult:**

The Albertine sections. She hasn't fully emerged as a character yet, but already the possessive attention Marcel directs at her and the other girls has a quality that's uncomfortable in ways that aren't resolved by their historical context.
