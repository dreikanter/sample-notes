# Strange Loop 2023 — selected talk notes

Strange Loop was held in St. Louis, September 2023. I attended remotely via the recording releases. Notes on a few talks.

https://www.thestrangeloop.com/

## "A History of Programming Languages for 2 Voices" — Shriram Krishnamurthi and Joe Gibbs Politz

A playful but substantive run through language history presented as a conversation rather than a lecture. Key point: every "new" language is a response to perceived failures of existing languages, and understanding what problem each language was solving makes you a better programmer regardless of what language you currently use.

The observation that Scheme (designed to teach) and C (designed to be close to hardware) have more in common than their adherents typically acknowledge was interesting and not immediately obvious.

## "Visualizing Distributed Systems with Cartography" — talk on Cartography tool for AWS infrastructure

Demonstrated how graph databases (specifically the Cartography tool from Lyft) can map AWS infrastructure into a queryable graph. Security questions like "which EC2 instances are publicly accessible and connected to RDS instances with sensitive data" become Cypher queries instead of manual audits.

Tool: https://github.com/lyft/cartography

## Notes on the format

Strange Loop's eclecticism — mixing PL theory with infosec, distributed systems with music and art — is unusual for a technical conference. The cross-pollination between domains seems genuine and not forced. Several talks referenced ideas from fields unrelated to software.

Sad to hear this was the last Strange Loop. The format and culture were something specific.
