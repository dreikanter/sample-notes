# Short review — Criterion release, "Mirror" (Tarkovsky, 1975)

Re-watched after a long gap, this time using the Criterion Blu-ray transfer, which is significantly better than the version I'd seen before.

"Mirror" is a film built from fragments: wartime memories, the director's childhood, newsreel footage, dream sequences, scenes set in the present with the same actress playing both the narrator's mother and his ex-wife. There is no conventional plot. The experience is closer to how memory actually works — associative, non-linear, emotionally coherent even when chronologically scrambled.

What strikes me this viewing: the attention to physical surfaces. Grass moving in wind. Water dripping from a woman's hair. Fire catching straw. Tarkovsky's camera lingers on these with a patience that asks you to look rather than to follow. The long takes feel like they're waiting for something to happen within the frame rather than cutting to make something happen.

The recurring image of the burning barn — which appears in multiple forms, at different points, associated with different emotional registers — accumulates meaning across the film in a way that's impossible on first viewing and rewards repeated watching.

The music (Bach, Purcell, Pergolesi alongside Artemiev's electronics) is used sparingly and never to tell you what to feel.

Criterion page: https://www.criterion.com/films/27418-mirror

Not for every mood or every viewer. When it's the right time, there's nothing else like it.
