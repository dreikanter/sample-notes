# Reading notes — The Dispossessed

Finished the first six chapters on the train down to the coast. The alternating timeline structure — Anarres present, Urras past — creates a strange recursive quality where every scene is shadowed by its inverse. Shevek arriving on Urras (opulent, hierarchical, beautiful) is filtered through memories of Anarres (spare, egalitarian, harsh), and neither comes off cleanly heroic.

Le Guin's Anarres functions as a thought experiment rather than a utopia. The society works at a subsistence level; there's genuine solidarity but also quiet coercion through social shame. The term "archist" as an insult on Anarres mirrors the way "anarchist" functions on Urras — each world uses the other as a cautionary label.

What strikes me most so far is the physics subplot. Shevek's work on the Principle of Simultaneity is handled with enough narrative weight that it feels like it matters without requiring the reader to understand temporal mechanics. The theory parallels the novel's form: past and present aren't sequential but simultaneous, each informing the other.

The prose is unhurried in a way that 1970s SF sometimes allowed itself. Long interior passages, careful sensory description of the desert landscapes of Anarres. Reminds me stylistically of some of the Borges stories I read in July — see [[20230719_1005]] — though the aims are entirely different.
