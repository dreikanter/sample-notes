# Observations — farmers market in September

The market has its best selection right now — the brief window where summer and autumn overlap, and both sets of produce are present simultaneously.

Bought today:
- A flat of the last summer peaches from the stone fruit vendor — small, intensely sweet, starting to spot. These need eating by Tuesday.
- Two heads of lacinato kale (the dinosaur kale, dark and wrinkled) from the farm I've been buying greens from all summer
- A bunch of Chioggia beets — pink and white striped when cut, slightly milder than the red ones
- Six ears of dry-farmed corn from the Hmong farmers in the northeast corner — always the best corn at this market
- A small jar of raw honey, buckwheat varietal, dark as molasses
- Half a dozen late-season figs, Turkish variety, soft and warm from sitting in the sun

The fig vendor is the one I talked to about natural fermentation last spring. He remembered me and gave me an extra two figs as a sample, unprompted.

There's a vendor I've been watching for three weeks who sells mushrooms — lion's mane, maitake, oysters in several colors. Got a bag of the maitake to roast with butter and thyme this week.

The corn I'll eat tonight, simply boiled and with good butter. The peaches will become a small batch of jam and the rest eaten plain. The beets will be roasted and served over labneh with walnuts and herbs — a recipe roughly adapted from Yotam Ottolenghi: https://ottolenghi.co.uk/recipes

Good morning. The best kind of Saturday errand.
