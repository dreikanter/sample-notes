# Listening notes — black midi "Hellfire"

Returning to "Hellfire" (2022) after a gap. First listen was overwhelming in a way that prevented engagement. This time, I came in knowing what to expect.

The album is structurally theatrical — each track is closer to a scene in an opera than a conventional song. The production is dense and precise, and the playing (particularly Cameron Picton's bass and Morgan Simpson's drumming) has a controlled violence to it.

**Notable tracks:**

"Hellfire" (title track): A soldier's confession, delivered as a narrative with shifting time signatures and dynamics that mirror the emotional escalation. The arrangement goes from sparse to catastrophic and back. Geordie Greep's vocal performance is commitment in the extreme.

"Eat Men Eat": The one track on the album that suggests they could be an accessible art-rock band if they wanted to. Chorus is almost anthemic. The lyrics undercut this immediately.

"The Race Is About to Begin": Begins like a ballad and ends like a disaster.

**What I find difficult:**

The lack of breathing room. Every moment is occupied. This is clearly a choice — suffocation is part of the affect — but it requires effort that some albums don't demand. You have to decide to engage.

Pitchfork review: https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/black-midi-hellfire/

Better than "Cavalcade" for me, which was itself remarkable. Looking forward to whatever comes next.
