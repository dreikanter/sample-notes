# Coffee tasting log — April

Ran a small cupping session this afternoon with three bags picked up from the farmers market last weekend.

**Ethiopia Yirgacheffe (natural process):** Opened with a strong blueberry jam aroma once ground. In the cup it was sweet but not cloying — more dried fig than fresh fruit. Body was medium, finish was clean with a faint winey note. Temperature drop brought out something almost floral. Enjoyed it most at around 70 °C.

**Colombia Huila (washed):** Bright and citrus-forward. Tasted like orange peel with a mild dark-chocolate aftertaste. More acidic than the Ethiopian. Would work well as a cold brew concentrate. The bag says 1,850 m elevation, which tracks.

**Guatemala Antigua (honey process):** The middle ground. Stone fruit on the nose, caramel on the palate. Less acidity than the Colombian, more body than the Ethiopian. Very approachable. Good candidate for espresso.

Brew method: I used the standard [SCA cupping protocol](https://sca.coffee/research/protocols-best-practices) — 8.25 g per 150 mL, four-minute steep, break at 3:30, skim then taste at 11 and 15 minutes.

The Yirgacheffe was the clear winner today. Planning to order more before the season ends. Also curious to try the same three as pour-overs side by side to see if the rankings shift.
