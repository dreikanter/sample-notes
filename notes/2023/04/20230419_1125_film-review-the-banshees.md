# Film review — The Banshees of Inisherin

Martin McDonagh, 2022. Watched for the second time.

The film works better on a second viewing once you've stopped wondering whether Colm is going to relent and started watching what the island does to everyone in it. It's a film about the violence implicit in withdrawal — how the act of refusing connection can be as destructive as any direct harm.

The four central performances are all doing slightly different things. Colin Farrell plays Pádraic as genuine rather than stupid — his incomprehension is the film's moral compass. Brendan Gleeson is managing Colm's self-disgust carefully enough that you understand his logic even as you watch it destroy him. Barry Keoghan is doing something stranger: Dominic is the only character who is honest about what everyone else is feeling.

The landscape does considerable work. Inis Mór (doubling for Inisherin) is photographed as beautiful and claustrophobic simultaneously. The ferry crossing in the background of several shots keeps reminding you that the island is permeable but nobody quite leaves.

What stays with me: the ending is a refusal. Pádraic refuses to be the one who forgives first. The film refuses to resolve this. The music refuses to offer consolation. It's one of the more formally honest endings I've seen in recent Irish cinema.
