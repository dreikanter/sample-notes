# Music notes — Sufjan Stevens, Javelin

Listened to the advance stream that leaked early — this is a pre-release listen, so treat this as provisional.

The record sounds like it was made at very low volume. Everything is close-miked and quiet — piano, strings, voice, and occasional guitar in arrangements that leave a lot of air. The production is by Sufjan himself and it's restrained in a way that his bigger orchestral work (*Illinois*, *Carrie & Lowell*) is not.

Opening track is piano and voice only, which sets up an expectation the album mostly fulfils. The strings, when they appear, are chamber-sized and used sparingly. There's a moment mid-album where the arrangement briefly opens up into something more expansive before pulling back — feels deliberate, like a door cracked open and then closed.

Lyrically the album is about grief, or something in that neighbourhood — loss, winter, staying in place. The images are concrete without being anecdotal: windows, rooms, cold. This is different from the confessional specificity of *Carrie & Lowell*, which was named after real people and talked about real events. This record keeps more distance.

Best track on first listen: track four, though I don't have titles from the leak. The piano figure in the final thirty seconds of that one is going to stay with me.

See [Pitchfork's Sufjan discography overview](https://pitchfork.com/artists/671-sufjan-stevens/) for context.
