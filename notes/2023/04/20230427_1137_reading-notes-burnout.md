# Reading notes — Burnout (Nagoski & Nagoski)

Emily and Amelia Nagoski, *Burnout: The Secret to Unlocking the Stress Cycle* (2019).

The central claim: stress is a biological cycle with a beginning, middle, and end. The modern problem is that we complete the stressor (deal with the email, survive the meeting, manage the crisis) without completing the stress cycle itself. The body remains in a state of activation with nowhere to go.

**Completing the cycle**

The authors list physical discharge methods that are actually evidence-based: vigorous exercise, creative expression, crying to completion, laughter. The key word they use is "completing" — not managing or suppressing the stress response but running it to its natural conclusion.

**The Human Giver Syndrome**

A term for the cultural expectation that some people (disproportionately women) exist to give rather than to have needs. The book is explicitly feminist without being preachy — they connect the wellbeing research to structural conditions rather than treating burnout as a personal failing.

**What I found useful**

The distinction between stressor and stress. I'd confused these for most of my life. You can remove the stressor without completing the stress cycle, which is why solving problems doesn't necessarily make you feel better.

The workout commitment is real. I've noticed this myself but never had the framework to articulate it.
