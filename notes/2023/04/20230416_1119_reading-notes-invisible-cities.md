# Reading notes — Invisible Cities

Italo Calvino, *Invisible Cities* (1972, trans. William Weaver). Re-reading for the third time — first time since 2015.

The book's architecture holds up better than I remembered. Each chapter frames a conversation between Marco Polo and Kublai Khan, with the city-descriptions as insets. The city-categories (Cities and Memory, Cities and Signs, etc.) cycle in a pattern that becomes clear only once you've gone through the whole book and look at the chapter-title grid. It's a formal game but not a cold one.

Polo says at one point: *"I have also thought of a model city from which I deduce all others."* That single sentence does a lot of structural work. All the cities might be Venice. Or all cities might be the same impossible city dreamed from different angles.

The best cities in this reading: Zaira (memory is in its walls), Tamara (you read only signs, never things), Eudoxia (a carpet maps the city or the city maps the carpet — unclear which).

What I'm taking away this time is the Khan's function. He's the reader proxy: exhausted by empire, unable to visit everything, relying on reports he can't verify. His scepticism and Polo's evasiveness produce the book's tension.

Found [this essay by Umberto Eco on Calvino's relationship to semiotics](https://monoskop.org/Umberto_Eco) useful as context.
