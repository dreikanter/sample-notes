# Zettelkasten — what actually works for me

Eighteen months into a zettelkasten-adjacent practice. Not the orthodox Luhmann method — I'm not using a physical card box and my links are softer than a true zettelkasten. But the core idea holds: each note should contain one idea, be written in my own words, and connect to other notes.

**What I actually do**

I write notes in plain Markdown with wiki-style links. The discipline I've internalised is resisting the urge to summarise or transcribe — instead I try to write what I think about what I've read. This is harder than copying quotes but the notes are more useful later.

I use tags loosely (three per note maximum) and only link when I have an actual reason to — a genuine conceptual relationship, not just topic overlap.

**What I stopped doing**

- Daily notes: too much friction, ended up being a TODO list with extra steps
- Fleeting notes: every "quick capture" I made was never processed and just became clutter
- Obsessive structuring: I spent more time maintaining the system than using it

**What surprised me**

The notes that get the most links tend not to be the ones I thought were most important when I wrote them. Small observational notes often turn out to be more generative than comprehensive summaries.

More context at [zettelkasten.de](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/overview/) — I disagree with some of their prescriptions but the core articles are essential reading.
