# Berlin travel notes — day two

Rain all morning. Took the opportunity to go to the Hamburger Bahnhof. The permanent collection is arranged more generously than I remembered — they've given the Beuys installation its own large room rather than cramming it alongside everything else.

The Cy Twombly canvases on the upper floor were what I came for. They're one of those things that genuinely does not translate to reproduction. The brushwork has a quality — velocity and hesitation alternating — that you can only read in person. The scale matters too: these aren't things that should be seen at screen size.

Lunch at Rogacki on Wilmersdorfer Straße — old Berlin deli, been there since 1928. Herring, potato salad, a glass of beer. The room is all surfaces that look like they've absorbed decades. Ate at the counter.

Afternoon: walked from Charlottenburg through the Tiergarten, entered near the Bellevue and walked south toward the memorial to the murdered Jews of Europe. Got there at about 16:30 when the light was starting to flatten. The forest of concrete stelae is a more successful piece of architecture than I'd expected — it does something to the body as well as the mind. The ground rises and falls under the slabs in a way that disorients without being manipulative.

Picked up a copy of Arno Geiger's *Der alte König in seinem Exil* from a street box near Potsdamer Platz.
