# Observation — Saturday market

Spent forty minutes at the farmers market before the crowd got thick. Trying to get better at just noticing things without immediately reaching for my phone.

The flower seller at the south entrance has an assistant now — a teenager who wraps the bouquets and handles the card machine while the seller does the actual selling. The teenager has the practiced-disengaged look of someone doing a job they didn't choose but haven't decided to resent either.

Two stalls in from the entrance, someone was selling preserved lemons they'd made themselves. Hand-labelled jars, three sizes, priced with small handwritten cards. I didn't buy any but spent longer looking at them than I expected to. There's something appealing about the specificity of a handmade product — you can see the decisions in it.

The cheese stall had a queue that started blocking the path to the vegetable stalls. This seems to happen every week in roughly the same place. Nobody has put up a sign asking people to queue to the side. I wonder whether the cheese seller benefits from the queue as a kind of advertisement.

Bought: radishes (for the balcony container, not to eat — I want to see how they do), a bunch of watercress, half a dozen eggs from a farm I hadn't tried before. The eggs were considerably more expensive than supermarket but the yolks were a deep orange that felt like proof of something.
